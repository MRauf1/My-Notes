<!-- Recurring gap types and weak prerequisites, updated when a type shows up 3+ times. -->
- `notation` (10, as of 2026-11-04): mostly *which distribution an expectation is taken under, and what is random*: $\mathbb{E}[\cdot \mid s_1 = s, \pi]$ as the trajectory law, $\widehat{\mathbb{E}}$ as the empirical law, and the time index in $i_t$. Also operator families: a superscript $\pi$ means "for a fixed policy" ($\mathcal{T}^\pi$, $Q^\pi$), while $\star$ or no superscript means optimal ($\mathcal{T}$, $Q^\star$). Habit: for every $\mathbb{E}$, name the measure and the random variables before reading on. Review: [[Empirical Distribution Function]], [[Conditional Expectation]], [[Trajectory]].
- **Good events and the union bound** (3 entries across `prior-result` and `quantifiers`, as of 2026-11-04): turning "all estimates are accurate" into a probability bound. The steps are the complement $G^c = \bigcup_i G_i^c$ (De Morgan), then $P(G) = 1 - P(G^c)$, then [[Boole's Inequality]] on the *bad* events, and subtracting the upper bound flips it into a lower bound: $P(G) \geq 1 - \sum_i P(G_i^c)$. Habit: write the direction of every inequality next to each step. Review: [[Bonferroni's Inequality]], [[Probability of Complement]].
- **Infinities** (self-reported, as of 2026-11-18; cross-cutting, spans `construction`, `hidden-lemma`, `quantifiers`): intuition built on finite objects misfires once an infinite process appears. Logged instances: Cantor's diagonal set (2026-11-04), moving $\lim$ inside $P$ (2026-11-16), and rearranging or regrouping a series in the LOTUS proof (2026-11-18). He also names: no largest number or cardinal, probability-0 events that occur and probability-1 events that fail, Gödel incompleteness, and $\sum n$ "$=-\tfrac{1}{12}$". Habit: write every infinite object as a limit of finite ones, name the order of limits, and cite the licence for each interchange (nonnegativity, absolute convergence, domination, uniformity). He wants quizzes themed on infinities when he asks for them (interchange of limits, cardinality, countable additivity, the counterexample zoo). Review: [[Riemann Series Theorem]], [[Regrouping (Series)]], [[Cantor's Theorem]], [[Unordered Sum]], [[Continuity Theorem of Probability]], [[Dominated Convergence Theorem]].
- `hidden-lemma` (7, as of 2026-11-20): mostly a fact Hogg uses silently in a probability proof that comes from real analysis: existence of a Stieltjes integral, continuity of $P$ along monotone events, and the supporting line of a convex function (Jensen). Review: convexity via chord slopes and supporting lines ([[Convex Function]]), and the monotone-limit/continuity facts of measure ([[Continuity Theorem of Probability]]).
