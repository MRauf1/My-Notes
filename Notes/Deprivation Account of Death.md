# Definition
> [!info] Deprivation Account of Death (Nagel)[^1]
> Death is bad for the person who dies not because being dead is an unpleasant state (it is no state of experience at all) but because it deprives them of the goods that continued life would have contained. Hence the fact that one shouldn't *fear* death does not imply that death isn't *bad*.

# Properties
- Responds to the [[Epicurean Argument Against Fearing Death]]: no matter how persuasive Epicurus is against fearing death, it doesn't follow that death is not bad.[^1]
- The badness lies in losing the benefits of living, not in being dead.[^1]
- From Nagel's essay "Death" (1970).
