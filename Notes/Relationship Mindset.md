# Definition
> [!info] Relationship Mindset[^1]
> The application of [[Mindset|mindsets]] to relationships, where there are three things one can believe fixed or developable: one's own qualities, the partner's qualities, and the relationship itself (inherently good or bad, "meant to be" or not). The [[Growth Mindset|growth mindset]] holds that all three can grow and change.

# Properties
- **Rejection**: fixed-mindset people feel permanently labelled ("UNLOVABLE!") and their number one goal is revenge; growth-mindset people aim to understand, forgive, learn, and move on.[^2]
- **Fixed-mindset belief 1, "if you have to work at it, it wasn't meant to be"**: the ideal is instant, perfect, perpetual compatibility, with problems solved magically by love; this robs relationships of the effort they need. It includes expecting partners to read each other's minds and to share all views, so even minor discrepancies feel threatening. Growth-minded couples "work happily ever after".[^3]
- **Fixed-mindset belief 2, "problems indicate character flaws"**: conflicts are blamed on a trait (one's own or, more often, the partner's), producing anger, contempt, and dissatisfaction with the whole relationship, or else denial of problems. Growth-minded people see imperfections and still have a fine relationship. Choosing a partner is choosing a set of problems (Daniel Wile).[^4]
- Belief that a partner *can* change differs from belief that they *will*: they must want, commit to, and act on change.[^5]
- Forgiving is hard in the fixed mindset since exonerating the other implies being the one at fault; the growth mindset lets one rise above blame, control one's own half of the relationship, understand the problem, and fix it together.[^6]
- Fixed-mindset partners may compete over who is smarter or more likable; in good relationships partners feel on the same side and help each other become who they want to be.[^7]
- **Ideal mate**: fixed-mindset people want to be put on a pedestal and worshipped; growth-mindset people want someone who sees their faults and helps them work on them, challenges them, and encourages new learning, without undermining self-esteem.[^8]
- **Friendship**: friends can foster each other's growth and validate each other; fixed-mindset people may diminish others ("the lower you are, the better I feel") or use them as mirrors of their excellence.[^9]
- **Shyness**: fixed-mindset people are more likely to be shy; shy growth-minded people treat social situations as challenges, relax after the first minutes, and come to look like non-shy people, whereas shy fixed-minded people stay nervous and awkward.[^10]
- **Bullying** is about judging who is more worthy: bullies gain a self-esteem rush and status from fixed-mindset thinking, fixed-mindset victims fantasise about violent revenge, and prolonged bullying can instill a fixed mindset. Growth-minded victims see it as the bully's problem and aim to educate and forgive; schools can reduce bullying by replacing judgment with collaboration and effort praise (Stan Davis).[^11]
