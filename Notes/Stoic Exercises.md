# Definition
> [!info] Stoic Exercises[^1]
> Daily practical exercises (*askēsis*) by which Stoics train the [[Three Disciplines (Epictetus)|three disciplines]], turning doctrine into habit. Only exercises that serve the goal (unimpeded desire and aversion) count as training.

# Types
- **Questioning impressions** - tell each disagreeable impression, "You're an impression, and not at all what you appear to be", then test it first by whether it concerns what is up to us; "Wait a moment, impression … let me put you to the test", and substitute a fine impression for an impure one.[^2]
- **Pausing** - when irritated or tempted by pleasure, don't be carried away: bide your time, compare the pleasure with the later regret and with the satisfaction of abstaining.[^3]
- **Attachment training (graded)** - tell yourself what kind of thing you love, starting with the least important: a cup, then a toga, a dog, a horse, land, then your body and family; "if you kiss your child, tell yourself you're kissing a human being".[^4]
- **Negative visualisation** (*premeditatio malorum*) - picture death, exile, and other terrors daily; murmur while kissing your child "Tomorrow you will die". It removes contemptible thoughts and excessive craving and prevents surprise ("I knew my son was mortal").[^5]
- **"I've returned it"** - never say "I've lost it"; treat possessions "as travelers treat an inn".[^6]
- **Same attitude to one's own misfortune as to others'** - when your cup breaks, react as you would to someone else's.[^7]
- **Two handles** - "everything has two handles, one of which makes it bearable, the other unbearable" (cognitive reframing).[^8]
- **Difficulties as training** - a difficulty is God pitting you against a tough training partner; an insulter trains your patience, a bad neighbour your fairness; "This is what I've been training for."[^9]
- **Turning to one's resources** - in every situation ask what means you have for dealing with it (self-control against temptation, endurance against pain).[^10]
- **Counting days** - to weaken a habit such as anger, count the days without it; after thirty, "make a sacrifice to God".[^11]
- **Evening review** (Pythagorean verses) - before sleep, review the day: "Where did I go wrong? What did I do? What duty did I leave undone?"[^12]
- **Voluntary hardship** - practise living like an invalid (fasting, water only) in private, never for show.[^13]
- **"Bear and forbear"** (*anechou kai apechou*) - the two vices are lack of fortitude and lack of self-control.[^14]
- **Constant attention** (*prosochē*) - relaxing attention makes inattention and procrastination habitual; nothing goes better inattentively.[^15]
- **Accepting fate** - when facing a seer, remember the future is not up to you and so is neither good nor bad; you can make good use of whatever comes.[^16]

# Properties
- Every habit is confirmed by corresponding actions (walking by walking); each lapse strengthens a vice like fuel on a fire and eventually forms a "callus"; see [[Habit]].[^11]
- Principles must be kept "at hand" day and night, written down, read, and discussed.[^17]
- Grounded in the [[Dichotomy of Control]]; many anticipate techniques of [[Cognitive Behavioral Therapy]].
