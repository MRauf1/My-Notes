# Definition
> [!info] Air–Water Interface (Cryo-EM Sample Preparation)[^1]
> Before [[Vitrification]], the sample is a film of protein solution only a few hundred Å thick. Proteins diffuse to its surfaces many times within the seconds before freezing. There, a protein can:
> - **denature**: its hydrophobic interior is exposed to the hydrophobic air–water interface, and it unfolds;
> - **aggregate**: exposed hydrophobic patches of neighbouring proteins coalesce into amorphous aggregates;
> - **adsorb in a fixed orientation**, which causes [[Preferred Orientation]].

# Properties
- **Timescale.** A protein's diffusion constant is about $10^{10}$ Å$^2$/s (about $100$ μm$^2$/s). It crosses a $100$ Å film in roughly $L^2/D \approx 1$ μs, so it meets the interface vast numbers of times before freezing. The source quotes $\sim 10^{13}$ Å$^2$/s, which looks like an extraction error; the conclusion is the same.
- **Remedies**:
  - a solid support that anchors proteins away from the interface, such as a single-carbon-layer graphene oxide film, which scatters very little. Making such grids reliably is still an art.
  - surfactants or pH changes;
  - imaging in situ, inside cells.
