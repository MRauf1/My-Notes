# Definition
> [!info] False Growth Mindset[^1]
> A misunderstanding of the [[Growth Mindset|growth mindset]], which is specifically the belief that people can develop their abilities. [[Carol Dweck|Dweck]] identifies three common misunderstandings.

# Types
- **Growth mindset as good traits** - labelling what one likes about oneself (open-mindedness, flexibility) a growth mindset, or calling it an "open mindset".[^1]
- **Growth mindset as effort only** - the process to be valued includes hard work *and* trying new strategies when one isn't working *and* seeking help or input from others; praising absent effort, or effort that yields no learning, is counterproductive (see [[Process Praise]]).[^2]
- **Growth mindset as "you can do anything"** - empty reassurance that puts the onus entirely on the student and may make them feel a failure; growth comes from helping them gain skills and find resources to progress.[^3]

# Properties
- Nobody has a pure growth mindset: everyone is a mixture of both, and one does not get a growth mindset by proclamation but by a journey ([[Mindset Change]]).[^4]
- Blaming students for having a fixed mindset is itself misguided; educators are responsible for creating growth-mindset-friendly environments.[^5]
- Adults often fail to pass on the growth mindset in their heads because their overt actions (praise, reactions to setbacks) do not line up with it ([[Mindset Transmission]]).[^6]
