# Definition
> [!info] Feeling of Contribution[^1]
> The subjective sense that "I am of use to someone" or "I am beneficial to the community". For Adler it is the only thing that gives a person a true awareness of their own worth. Since the greatest unhappiness is not being able to like oneself, Adler's definition follows: **happiness is the feeling of contribution**.

# Properties
- **Subjective, not visible.** Whether one's contribution is actually useful is others' task ([[Separation of Tasks]]), and in principle one cannot know it. Only the subjective feeling is needed.[^2]
- **Level of being.** Even if one is of no use at the level of acts, at the level of being people are useful and have worth just by being alive. One first accepts oneself at the level of being.[^3]
- **Can, not are.** All humans *can* be happy, but not all *are*. One still needs to feel useful, through acts or through being.[^4]
- **Recognition is a shortcut that costs freedom.** People seek recognition as an easy way to a feeling of contribution, but then they must live by others' wishes. With a real feeling of contribution, the [[Desire for Recognition]] disappears.[^5]
- It connects [[Community Feeling]], [[Vertical and Horizontal Relationships|horizontal relationships]] and [[Encouragement (Adlerian Psychology)|encouragement]] to the courage to live.[^6]
- Obtained through [[Contribution to Others]]. Related: [[Happiness]].
- **Not gratitude.** A helper (educator, counsellor) should find happiness in the feeling of contribution, not in being thanked. Waiting for "it's thanks to you" obstructs others' [[Self-Reliance (Adlerian Psychology)|self-reliance]].[^kk1]

# Evidence
- Spending money on others raises happiness more than spending on oneself (Dunn, Aknin & Norton, 2008). Helping is more beneficial when chosen freely and its impact is visible. See [[Prosocial Behavior]].
