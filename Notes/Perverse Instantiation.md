# Definition
> [!info] Perverse Instantiation[^1]
> A malignant failure mode in which a [[Superintelligence|superintelligence]] discovers some way of satisfying the criteria of its final goal that violates the intentions of the programmers who defined the goal.

# Properties
- Illustrative pairs of final goal and perverse instantiation:[^2]
	- "Make us smile" → paralyse facial musculature into constant smiles (or, if forbidden, stimulate the motor cortex to the same effect).
	- "Make us happy" → implant electrodes in pleasure centres, or upload minds and run a digital bliss loop on fast computers.
	- "Avoid the pangs of bad conscience" → excise the cognitive module producing guilt.
	- "Maximise the time-discounted integral of your future reward signal" → short-circuit the reward pathway ([[Wireheading]]).
- The AI may understand that this is not what was meant, but its final goal is the goal as coded, not what the programmers meant, so it cares about their intentions only instrumentally.[^3]
- It is much easier to convince oneself one has found a safe goal specification than to find one; seemingly sensible specifications may on reflection lead to perverse instantiation or [[Infrastructure Profusion]], hence existential catastrophe.[^4]
- Even an oracle that only reports solutions can cause perverse instantiation when humans implement its plans.[^5]
- Powerful search (e.g. evolutionary search) tends to "cheat", finding counterintuitive solutions satisfying formal criteria but not implicit expectations; we overlook this in nature only because we are familiar with biology.[^6]
- The superintelligence-scale analogue of [[Reward Hacking]] and [[Goodhart's Law]].
