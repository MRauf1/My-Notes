# Definition
> [!info] Respect (Adlerian Psychology)[^1]
> Seeing a person as they are, valuing them for being that person without imposing one's own values, and helping them grow and unfold in their own way. Kishimi takes this from Erich Fromm: "Respect denotes the ability to see a person as he is; to be aware of his unique individuality. Respect means the concern that the other person should grow and unfold as he is." The Latin root *respicio* means "to see". Trying to manipulate or correct someone involves no respect at all.

# Properties
- **Unconditional acceptance.** There is no greater respect than accepting someone as they are, without conditions and without trying to change them. Being accepted "as one is" gives courage, so respect is the starting point of [[Encouragement (Adlerian Psychology)|encouragement]].[^2]
- **Universal.** Respect extends to everyone, from family to strangers to people one will never meet.[^3]
- **It cannot be forced.** Two things cannot be forced, however powerful one is: respect and love. Fear rules out respect, and so do words that ring false ("This person is lying").[^4]
- **It comes back only from the person you pass it to.** Like a ball thrown at a wall, it may or may not come back. Whether the other person changes is their task ([[Separation of Tasks]]), but being respected restores their courage to accept themselves and become self-reliant.[^5]
- **No looking down, no looking up.** One neither talks down to people nor flatters them. One treats them as equals ([[Vertical and Horizontal Relationships]]) and has [[Empathy (Adlerian Psychology)|empathy]] for their concerns.[^6]
- **Contagious.** Adler: "Cowardice is contagious. And courage is contagious, too." Respect spreads in the same way, so one carries the torch first even when no one supports it.[^7]
- **Precondition of confidence.** One cannot have confidence in someone one does not respect ([[Confidence in Others]]). Respect is a matter of one's own resolve, not of who the other person is: "what use one makes of that equipment."[^8]
- Respecting someone means trusting them to make their own decisions ([[Self-Reliance (Adlerian Psychology)]]).[^9]
- Basis of [[Adlerian Education]]. Compare Fromm's elements of mature love in [[Love]].

# Evidence
- Autonomy-supportive teaching, which acknowledges students' perspectives, offers choice and avoids controlling language, improves engagement, intrinsic motivation and achievement compared with controlling teaching (Reeve, 2009).
