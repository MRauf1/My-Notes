# Definition
> [!info] Stoic Theology[^1]
> The Stoic view that God is identical with the rational, providential order of the universe (Nature): "mind, knowledge, right reason". The human soul, in its rational aspect, is a fragment or splinter of God, so rationality is our birthright and the essence of goodness is to be sought in reason.

# Properties
- [[Epictetus]]'s Stoicism has a strongly religious flavour; God is often called Zeus or "the father of both human beings and gods".[^1]
- Because our souls are fragments of God, God perceives every movement of them; we should consider ourselves children of God and never think ourselves base.[^2]
- God has made the most important thing (use of impressions) up to us and unimpeded, and placed good and bad among the things up to us, while giving other things to the governance of the universe; we should be "of one mind with God" and gladly surrender what the universe needs.[^3]
- The universe is a single city made of a single substance, with cyclical replacement of things (including a periodic conflagration); we are parts of this whole, "as an hour is part of a day".[^4]
- Everything that happens moves one to praise providence, given the ability to see particulars in the context of the whole and a sense of gratitude.[^5]
- Life is compared to a festival God has laid on: one should be a grateful spectator and participant, and leave when led away, making room for others.[^6]
- Grounds [[Cosmopolitanism]] and [[Living in Accord with Nature]]; its religious language is not essential to modern [[Stoicism]].
