# Definition
> [!info] Scientific Workflow[^1]
> A precise description of a scientific procedure: a multi-step process coordinating multiple tasks, acting like a sophisticated script, typically supported by a workflow system with an execution platform, a visual design suite, and a development kit.

# Properties
- Provide (1) systematic, automated analysis across datasets and applications; (2) capture of the process so results can be reproduced and methods reviewed, validated, repeated, and adapted; (3) visual scripting without low-level programming; (4) an integration platform for independent resource providers.[^2]
- Liberate scientists from routine data processing; can be reconfigured and repurposed as templates to accelerate experimental design.[^3]
- Combining workflows and execution records with published results yields systematic, transparent, comparable research whose outcomes carry their provenance.[^4]
- Affect research in four ways: shifting practice (data-driven hypotheses tested in the lab), empowering scientists to author their own pipelines, producing comparable and attributable data, and establishing the *primacy of method* alongside the primacy of data ("method deluge").[^5]
- A core technique of [[Data-Intensive Science]]; compare [[Laboratory Information Management System]].
