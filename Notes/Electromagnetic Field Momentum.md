# Definition
> [!info] Electromagnetic Field Momentum[^1]
> The [[Electromagnetic Field]] itself carries [[Momentum]], distinct from the momentum ($m\mathbf{v}$) of any charged particle; a complete accounting of [[Conservation of Momentum]] for interacting charges must include this field momentum, not just the particles' momenta.

# Properties
- Needed because electromagnetic interactions are not instantaneous: a change in one charge's position takes time (propagating at the speed of light) to affect another charge. During this delay, the first charge can already have gained momentum while the second has not yet lost any, so the particles' momenta alone would not sum to a constant; the missing momentum resides in the field itself during that interval.
- Manifests as radiation pressure: light striking an object delivers momentum to it at a certain rate, which is equivalent to a (typically very small, but measurable) [[Force]] on the object.
- Illustrates that a field is not merely a bookkeeping device for forces between particles, but possesses physically real properties of its own — energy and momentum — in the same way that particles do.
