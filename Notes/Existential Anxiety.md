# Definition
> [!info] Existential Anxiety[^1]
> Anxiety arising from awareness of the [[Ultimate Concerns]]. Following Kierkegaard, *fear* is fear of some *thing* (locatable in time and space, and so manageable), whereas *anxiety* (dread) is fear of *no thing*, of losing oneself and becoming nothingness; it cannot be located, and so begets helplessness and further anxiety.

# Properties
- **"Anxiety seeks to become fear"**: we combat anxiety by displacing it from nothing to something, against which a self-protective campaign can be mounted; e.g. death anxiety after a near-fatal accident is quickly "secularised" into lesser concerns such as self-esteem, rejection, or humiliation. Primary anxiety is always transformed into something less toxic; that is the function of the whole system of defenses.[^2]
- Conversely, anxiety *increases* when a fear of something is understood for what it truly is, a fear of no thing.[^3]
- Strong anxiety does not remain conscious: it is repressed and processed by isolating affect from object, so one can think of death with only moderate discomfort and feel displaced anxiety with few clues to its source.[^4]
- Freud: anxiety is the original reaction to helplessness, reproduced later as a signal of danger.[^5]
- **Ur-anxiety**: many existential philosophers regard the anxiety of *groundlessness* (from freedom and self-constitution) as the most fundamental anxiety, deeper than death anxiety, which may symbolise it; unlike death anxiety it is not evident in everyday experience.[^6]
- Therapy aims to reduce anxiety to comfortable levels, not eliminate it: "Life cannot be lived nor can death be faced without anxiety"; anxiety is a guide as well as an enemy, pointing toward [[Authenticity (Existentialism)|authentic]] existence.[^7]
- Humans abhor uncertainty: naming and explaining something gives a sense of mastery, even when what one understands is that one is helpless and alone before cosmic indifference. Secondary anxiety from uncertainty can do more havoc than a disease itself (Jerome Frank).[^8]
- Therapist certainty has a curvilinear benefit: too little prevents trust, too much becomes rigidity and avoids the core truth that uncertainty exists.[^9]
- Special case: [[Death Anxiety]]. Compare [[Anxiety Disorder]].
