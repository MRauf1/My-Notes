# Definition
> [!info] Cold Welding[^1]
> Two pieces of the same pure, uncontaminated metal, brought into contact with their surfaces perfectly clean, fuse together into one piece rather than sliding against each other, with no melting or heating involved.

# Properties
- Occurs because atoms at the contact interface cannot "know" they belong to different pieces of metal once no foreign atoms (oxides, grease, or other contaminants) separate them; the interatomic forces that hold a solid together operate identically across the former boundary.
- Explains why the ordinary coefficient of [[Friction]], $\mu$, cannot even be defined for two surfaces of a truly pure, identical metal: once the contaminants that normally let the two surfaces "know" they are distinct are removed, the surfaces do not slide at any finite applied force — they are simply one piece of metal.
- A practical concern in engineering contexts with extremely clean surfaces and vacuum conditions, such as satellite and spacecraft components, where ordinary lubricating contamination is absent.

[^1]: [The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. I, Ch. 12: Characteristics of Force](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_12.html)
