# Definition
> [!info] Mindset Change[^1]
> The gradual journey from a [[Fixed Mindset|fixed]] toward a [[Growth Mindset|growth mindset]]: a change from a judge-and-be-judged framework to a learn-and-help-learn framework. Old beliefs are not removed but new beliefs take their place alongside them and, as they strengthen, give a different way to think, feel, and act.

# Types
Steps of the journey:[^2]
1. **Accept** that everyone has both mindsets; one cannot eliminate the fixed mindset within, only how often it shows up and how much havoc it wreaks.
2. **Recognise triggers**: e.g. taking on a big challenge, hitting dead ends, decisive or public failure, meeting someone better in one's area of pride, pressure, anxiety, criticism, self-doubt.
3. **Name the fixed-mindset persona** and observe, without judging, how it makes one feel and act and how it affects others.
4. **Educate the persona**: it was born to protect you, but in limiting ways; don't suppress it, let it settle, then explain how you plan to learn from the setback, and take it along on the journey.
5. **Keep setting growth goals** and help others on their journeys.

# Properties
- Understanding that everyone has a fixed-mindset persona gives compassion for those seen as incompetent or defiant who merely lack conditions to thrive; teams that understood each other's triggers and personas raised morale.[^3]
- Merely learning about the growth mindset can mobilise people; e.g. a workshop teaching that learning moves knowledge from [[Working Memory|working memory]] to [[Long-Term Memory|long-term memory]] raised adolescents' grades.[^4]
- **Beyond talking to yourself**: in the fixed mindset (and in most cognitive therapies, cf. [[Cognitive Therapy]]), restoring self-esteem ends the job; in the growth mindset it is only the first step, followed by learning and self-improvement.[^5]
- Growth-oriented action should follow a concrete [[Implementation Intention|plan]] regardless of feelings: "The plan is the plan."[^6]
- It is not a set of tricks: using growth strategies from inside a fixed mindset backfires, since once things improve people stop doing what helped (expecting good people and relationships to "just act good"), and the relapse is fiercer.[^7]
- **Willpower** is not a fixed thing one has or lacks: it needs help from learned strategies, maintenance habits, and treating lapses as lessons rather than a battle between the bad and good you (see [[Willpower]]).[^8]
- Hard parts include giving up the idea of superiority and accepting that effort guarantees nothing; it takes long before effort becomes enjoyable.[^9]
- Opening up to growth makes one more oneself, not less.[^10]
