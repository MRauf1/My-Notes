# Definition

> [!abstract] Theorem 1 ([[Monotonic Function]] [[Discontinuity]] Countable Theorem)[^1]
> Let $f$ be monotonic on $(a, b)$. Then the set of points of $(a, b)$ at which $f$ is discontinuous is at most [[Countable Set|countable]].

# Properties
- Follows from [[Monotonic Function One-Sided Limit Theorem]]: every discontinuity is a [[Jump Discontinuity]].
- The discontinuities need not be [[Isolated Point|isolated]]: given any countable subset $E \subseteq (a, b)$, even a [[Dense Subset|dense]] one, there is a function monotonic on $(a, b)$ that is discontinuous at every point of $E$ and at no other point of $(a, b)$.[^1]
- Generalized by: the set of [[Simple Discontinuity|simple discontinuities]] of any real function on $(a, b)$ is at most countable.

[^1]: [Principles of Mathematical Analysis](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/3BD27IHF?page=105)
