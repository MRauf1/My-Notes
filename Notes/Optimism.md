# Definition

General tendency to look on the bright side of things.[^1]

Linked to better longevity, healthier behaviors, fewer postsurgical complications, better [[Immune System]] functioning, better treatment adherence, less pain, better physical condition.

# Evidence and Limits (Pausch)
Pausch argued that optimism helps a cancer patient "endure brutal chemo, or keep searching for late-breaking medical treatments."[^2] The evidence supports the behavioral route he describes, not a direct cure.
- Optimism predicts about 35% fewer cardiovascular events and lower all-cause mortality (meta-analysis, Rozanski et al., 2019), and modestly better physical health overall ($r \approx 0.17$; Rasmussen, Scheier and Greenhouse, 2009).
- Optimism did *not* predict survival in lung cancer (Schofield et al., 2004). Telling patients to "stay positive" can add guilt when the disease progresses.
- Its benefits run largely through coping and health behavior: persistence, treatment adherence and seeking information.
- "Behave as if you're going to be around awhile."[^3] Compare [[Tragic Optimism]].
