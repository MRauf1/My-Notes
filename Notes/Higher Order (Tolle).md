# Definition
> [!info] Higher Order (Tolle)[^1]
> The claim that the universe is an indivisible whole in which everything is interconnected, nothing exists in isolation, and every event has its necessary place and function. Thinking fragments reality by isolating events and labeling them "good" or "bad". The deeper interconnectedness makes those labels ultimately illusory.

# Properties
- It cannot be understood by thinking, since anything thought about is content, while the higher order comes from formless consciousness. But in inner stillness it can be glimpsed as "a hidden harmony" and consciously aligned with.[^1]
- The sage accepts what is instead of judging it, knowing the mind often cannot see a seemingly random event's place in the whole: "there are no random events".[^2]
- Knowing yourself only through content makes you think you know what is good or bad for you, which is a fragmented perception.[^3]
- Tolle's claim that "life will give you whatever experience is most helpful for the evolution of your consciousness" belongs here.[^4]
- These are metaphysical, teleological claims, unfalsifiable rather than empirical. Compare the Stoic acceptance of fate ([[Stoic Determinism]]).
