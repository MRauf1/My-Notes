# Definition
> [!info] Apatheia[^1]
> The Stoic ideal of complete freedom from [[Passion (Stoicism)|passion]]. A dispassionate person is not an unfeeling zombie ("unfeeling as a statue") but someone who has changed his mind about the value of passions and experiences only sane feelings ([[Eupatheia]]).

# Properties
- Notorious in antiquity as now.[^1]
- Compatible with fulfilling one's duties to kin and caring for one's body, without thinking them ultimately important.[^2]
- Freedom from passion is the result of the first of the [[Three Disciplines (Epictetus)]].
- Compare [[Mindfulness]] and Buddhist non-attachment.
- **Not indifference (Robertson).** *Apatheia* means the absence of irrational or excessive passion (*pathos*), not of all emotion or desire. Seneca thought the Latin *impatientia* misleading and preferred to describe a mind "invulnerable" or "above all suffering". Preferring external goods (food, wealth, praise) over pain and poverty is rational as long as they don't harm mental health; the Stoic just doesn't see them as inherently important.[^rob1]
- The sage's indifference is not a lack of interest but a redirection of attention away from what monopolises other people (Hadot; [[Amor Fati]]). The Stoic is no "cold fish": he prefers what is genuinely in his interest over what is appealing but harmful.[^rob2]
- Encouraged by the [[View from Above]].
