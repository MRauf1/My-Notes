# Definition
> [!info] Anomaly (Philosophy of Science)[^1]
> The recognition that nature has somehow violated the paradigm-induced expectations governing [[Normal Science|normal science]]; the starting point of scientific discovery.

# Properties
- **Structure of discovery**: discovery is an extended episode, not an isolated event. It begins with awareness of anomaly, continues with exploration of the anomalous area, and closes only when the paradigm theory is adjusted so that the anomalous becomes the expected; until then (until the scientist learns to see nature differently) the new fact is not quite a scientific fact.[^1]
- Characteristics: prior awareness of anomaly, gradual and simultaneous emergence of observational and conceptual recognition, and consequent change of paradigm categories and procedures, often with resistance. As in the anomalous playing-card experiment, novelty emerges only with difficulty against a background of expectation; the same pattern seems built into perception itself.[^2]
- Discovery cannot be unequivocally attributed to one individual and one moment (as the contested priority for oxygen shows); the image of discovery as a simple act like seeing is misleading. The value placed on a discovery varies with how far it violated paradigm expectations.[^3]
- Discoveries occur by accident (X-rays) more often than scientific reporting admits; even discoveries not implicated in theoretical upheaval can change existing fields and deny previously paradigmatic instrumentation its status, necessitating paradigm change for part of the community.[^4]
- Anomaly appears only against the background of a precise paradigm, and only to one who knows precisely what to expect; resistance to change ensures scientists are not lightly distracted and that anomalies penetrating to the core lead to change. Simultaneous discovery in several laboratories reflects how thoroughly normal science prepares its own change.[^5]
- Not every anomaly warrants scrutiny; when one comes to seem more than a puzzle and attracts the field's most eminent researchers, the transition to [[Crisis (Philosophy of Science)|crisis]] begins.[^6]
- Recognised anomalies, those stubbornly refusing assimilation, are the only type of phenomena that gives rise to new theories; paradigms give every other phenomenon a theory-determined place.[^7]
