# Definition
> [!info] Locus of Control[^1]
> (Rotter) The generalised belief about where control over one's outcomes lies. *Internals* have an internal locus of control and feel they control their personal destiny; *externals* place control outside themselves and look outside for answers, support, and guidance.

# Properties
- Internals are more independent, achieving, politically active, and power-seeking; acquire, retain, and use more information; are less suggestible and judge information on its merit rather than its source's prestige; and are more likely to delay gratification.[^1]
- Depressed individuals tend to have an external locus of control, with helplessness from a perceived breakdown between behaviour and outcome (cf. [[Learned Helplessness]], [[Major Depressive Disorder]]).[^2]
- Antecedents: consistent, warm, responsive families foster internality, and inconsistent ones externality; first-borns are more often internals.[^3]
- Improving hospitalised patients shift toward internality, but the evidence is correlational, so the direction of causation is unknown.[^4]
- An empirical correlate of existential [[Responsibility (Existential Psychology)|responsibility]].
