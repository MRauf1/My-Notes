# [[Viktor Frankl]]'s Perspective[^1]
Frankl believes that love is the ultimate and highest goal a man can aspire to. Would imagine his wife and talk to her.
Love goes beyond the physical self. It finds its deepest meaning in the spiritual being, the inner self. Frankl kept going with love even though he didn't know whether his wife was dead or alive.

# Mature Love (Yalom, Fromm, Maslow)[^2]
- **Need-free love**: at its best a relationship involves individuals relating in a need-free fashion; it is a mode of relating to the world, not a magical exclusive bond ([[Existential Isolation]], [[I-Thou Relationship]]).
- **D-love vs B-love** (Maslow): deficiency love is selfish love-need; being-love is unneeding, admiring, non-possessive, and fosters the other's growth ([[Deficiency and Growth Motivation]]).
- **Fromm**: "Immature love says, 'I love you because I need you.' Mature love says, 'I need you because I love you.'" Love is active, a "standing in" rather than a "falling for"; giving is an expression of abundance, not depletion. Its elements are giving, concern, responsivity, respect, and knowledge; the most fundamental form is brotherly love.
- **Yalom's characteristics of caring**: selfless (no observing third party), knowing the other fully, caring for the other's growth, active, a way of being in the world, flowing from richness rather than need, reciprocal, and rewarding, though the rewards "ensue but cannot be pursued".
- Patients complain of being unloved, but the productive work lies in their inability to love.
