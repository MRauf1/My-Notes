# [[Viktor Frankl]]'s Perspective[^1]
Frankl believes that love is the ultimate and highest goal a man can aspire to. Would imagine his wife and talk to her.
Love goes beyond the physical self. It finds its deepest meaning in the spiritual being, the inner self. Frankl kept going with love even though he didn't know whether his wife was dead or alive.

# Mature Love (Yalom, Fromm, Maslow)[^2]
- **Need-free love**: at its best a relationship involves individuals relating in a need-free fashion; it is a mode of relating to the world, not a magical exclusive bond ([[Existential Isolation]], [[I-Thou Relationship]]).
- **D-love vs B-love** (Maslow): deficiency love is selfish love-need; being-love is unneeding, admiring, non-possessive, and fosters the other's growth ([[Deficiency and Growth Motivation]]).
- **Fromm**: "Immature love says, 'I love you because I need you.' Mature love says, 'I need you because I love you.'" Love is active, a "standing in" rather than a "falling for"; giving is an expression of abundance, not depletion. Its elements are giving, concern, responsivity, respect, and knowledge; the most fundamental form is brotherly love.
- **Yalom's characteristics of caring**: selfless (no observing third party), knowing the other fully, caring for the other's growth, active, a way of being in the world, flowing from richness rather than need, reciprocal, and rewarding, though the rewards "ensue but cannot be pursued".
- Patients complain of being unloved, but the productive work lies in their inability to love.

# [[Eckhart Tolle]]'s Perspective
- True love is a [[States of Being|state of Being]] with no opposite. Love that alternates with attack is ego attachment and addictive clinging ([[Relationship as Spiritual Practice]]).[^3]

# Romantic Love (Manson)
- Manson likens intense romantic love to cocaine.[^manson] Early-stage romantic love does activate dopamine-rich reward regions (ventral tegmental area, caudate) also implicated in addiction (Aron et al., 2005; Fisher et al., 2010).
- He contrasts unhealthy love (using each other to escape problems) with healthy love (each person addressing their own problems with the other's support).[^manson]

# Adlerian Perspective (Kishimi & Koga)
- **Built, not fallen into.** Love is neither fated nor spontaneous: we do not "fall" in love, we build it from nothing by will, which is why the task of love is difficult. "Falling in love" is often just a desire to possess and to triumph. Being loved is hard, but loving someone is far harder.[^kk1]
- **From "me" to "us".** Love is neither self-interested ("my happiness") nor other-interested ("your happiness"). It builds the happiness of an inseparable "us". The subject of life changes from "me" to "us", so love is *liberation from "me"*. That "us" eventually widens to the whole community and humanity ([[Community Feeling]]).[^kk2]
- **Loving makes one an adult.** Only by loving, rather than waiting to be loved, does one leave the childhood "lifestyle for being loved" and become self-reliant ([[Self-Reliance (Adlerian Psychology)]]). "It is only by loving another person that we are liberated from self-centredness… achieve self-reliance… arrive at community feeling."[^kk3]
- **Fear of loving.** Fromm: "While one is consciously afraid of not being loved, the real, though usually unconscious fear is that of loving. To love means to commit oneself without guarantee… Love is an act of faith, and whoever is of little faith is also of little love." Adler would say *courage* instead of faith. Not loving because "I have no outstanding traits" is an [[Inferiority Complex]]. Waiting for someone who will love "this me" looks only at oneself; only parents love unconditionally.[^kk4]
- **Separation of tasks.** Loving is your task. How the other person responds is theirs. Just love first ([[Separation of Tasks]]).[^kk5]
- **No destined one.** Adler rejects destiny in love. The fantasy of a destined partner serves to "eliminate all the candidates" and avoid deeper relationships ([[Life-Lie]]). Marriage is not choosing a target but choosing a way of living. "We are able to love anyone." Fromm: "Loving someone is not simply an intense emotion. It is a decision, it is a judgement, it is a promise." Destiny is made with one's own hands, like a dance of the now with the partner in front of you ([[Energeial Life]]).[^kk6]
- **Meeting in order to part.** Time is limited, so every relationship presupposes parting. One devotes oneself to the best possible parting.[^kk7]
- **Evidence.** "Destiny beliefs" (a relationship is meant to be or not) predict breakup when early satisfaction is low, while "growth beliefs" predict working through conflict (Knee, 1998). Arranged marriages report love and satisfaction comparable to love-based marriages (Regan, Lakhanpal & Anguiano, 2012). Commitment, a decision, is a distinct component of love (Sternberg, 1986). This supports the view of love as a decision. "We can love anyone" is an overstatement, since compatibility and attraction still matter.
