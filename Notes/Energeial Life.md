# Definition
> [!info] Energeial Life[^1]
> A life lived as a **series of moments** ("now"), in which each act is complete in itself, like a dance whose goal is the dancing. It contrasts with a **kinetic (dynamic) life**, which aims at a destination and treats everything before arrival as an incomplete, "en route" preparatory period. The terms come from [[Aristotle]]'s distinction between *energeia* (activity complete at every moment) and *kinēsis* (motion incomplete until its end).

# Properties
- **Life is dots, not a line.** Under a magnifying glass, a chalk line is a series of dots. Treating life as a line (a mountain climb or a story with a summit) links to Freudian etiology and turns most of life into a "tentative life" led by a "tentative me".[^2]
- **The journey.** A journey to the pyramids is a journey from the moment one leaves home, even if one never arrives.[^3]
- **Arrival as a by-product.** One dances here and now. Looking around, one realizes "I've made it this far". As long as you are dancing, you will get somewhere. There is no need to compete and no use for destinations.[^4]
- **Always complete.** With an energeial view, life is complete at every moment. A life ending at twenty and one ending at ninety are both complete.[^5]
- **Don't postpone life.** Treating the present as preparation ("I'll do it when the time comes") postpones life into dull monotony. Studying a little each day for a distant exam is itself the real thing: "this is what I did today".[^6]
- **Spotlight on now.** Living earnestly here and now leaves past and future invisible. Seeing them clearly means living in dim twilight. The **greatest [[Life-Lie]]** is not living here and now.[^7]
- Life is simple and need not be taken too seriously. Live earnestly, but not seriously.[^8]
- When one has danced here and now to the full, the meaning of one's life becomes clear ([[Contribution to Others]]).[^9]
- Related: [[Relationship with the Present Moment]], [[Psychological Time]], [[Mindfulness]], [[Teleology (Adlerian Psychology)]].

# Evidence
- **Correction: planning works.** "A well-planned life is impossible" is too strong. Goal setting (Locke & Latham, 2002) and [[Implementation Intention|implementation intentions]] (Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006) reliably improve goal attainment. The supported kernel is about orientation, not about abandoning plans: process-focused, intrinsically motivated activity is more satisfying than activity valued only as a means to an end ([[Intrinsic Motivation]]).
