# Definition
> [!info] Stoic View of Death[^1]
> ([[Epictetus]]) Death is an [[Indifferent (Stoicism)|indifferent]]: it comes to everyone, is merely the dissolution of our constituent elements (the separation of soul and body), does not touch the self (which is not the body), and is a natural part of a good universal order, i.e. God's will.

# Properties
- "Now it's time for your matter to revert once more to the components of which it's made"; death is "a change … not to what is not, but to what is not now", and you will be replaced by something the universe needs.[^2]
- It makes no difference by which road one dies (fever, roof tile, sword, drowning); "all I can do is do what I can".[^3]
- What is dreadful is not dying but dying in disgrace; death can even be turned to good account (Menoeceus preserved his honour by it).[^4]
- Wish to be found, when death comes, doing something proper to a human, or at least working on oneself, and to depart grateful: "The amount of time I've had … is enough for me. Now take them back."[^5]
- People "want neither to live nor to die": they blame the gods for early death and for old age, yet cling to life.[^6]
- Epictetus allowed departure from life ("the door is open") only when a life in accord with nature is impossible, as a signal from God, not "for no good reason".[^7]
- Fear of death is the root of servility; take it away and "you'll find out how much calm and stillness there is in your command center".[^8]
- Cf. [[Epicureanism]] and [[Existentialism]].
