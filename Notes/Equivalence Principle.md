# Definition
> [!info] Equivalence Principle (Weak Form)[^1]
> Gravitational [[Mass]] (the mass appearing in [[Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation]]) is exactly proportional to inertial mass (the mass appearing in [[Newton's Second Law]]). Consequently, all objects in a given gravitational field undergo identical accelerations and follow identical trajectories, regardless of their mass or composition.

# Properties
- Explains weightlessness in orbit: an orbiting object and its container (e.g. an astronaut and their spacecraft) fall along exactly the same trajectory under gravity, so the object appears to float freely relative to its surroundings rather than settling to one side.
- Verified experimentally to be exact within $1$ part in $10^9$ by the Eötvös experiment (1909) and later, more precisely, by Dicke's experiment.
- The foundation later used by Einstein to formulate general relativity, which reinterprets gravity as curvature of [[Spacetime]] rather than a [[Force]] acting between masses.
- Equivalently, within a small region, gravity's effects cannot be distinguished from those of a [[Pseudo Force]] arising from a uniformly accelerating [[Frame of Reference]]; unlike an ordinary pseudo force, however, gravity cannot be eliminated everywhere at once by a single change of frame, since it varies from place to place — the obstruction that led Einstein toward a curved-spacetime description instead of a single global pseudo force.

[^1]: [The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. I, Ch. 7: The Theory of Gravitation](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_07.html)
