> [!abstract] Theorem 2 (Second Derivatives Test, Two Variables)[^2]
> Suppose the second partial derivatives of $f$ are continuous near $(a, b)$ and $f_x(a, b) = f_y(a, b) = 0$ ($(a, b)$ is a critical point). Let $D = f_{xx}(a, b) f_{yy}(a, b) - [f_{xy}(a, b)]^2$, the determinant of the [[Hessian Matrix]].
> - $D > 0$, $f_{xx}(a, b) > 0$: [[Local Minimum]].
> - $D > 0$, $f_{xx}(a, b) < 0$: [[Local Maximum]].
> - $D < 0$: [[Saddle Point]].
> - $D = 0$: the test gives no information.

Absolute extrema of a continuous $f$ on a closed bounded set are found by comparing values at critical points and on the boundary ([[Closed Interval Method]], [[Extreme Value Theorem]]).
