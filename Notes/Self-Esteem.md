# Definition

...

Healthy self-esteem comes and grows from overcoming challenges you fear through action and realizing what you're capable of.

# Evidence
Pausch: self-esteem is "not something you can give; it's something they have to build." His coach gave kids something they couldn't do and let them work until they could.[^1] The research supports him.
- **Boosting self-esteem directly doesn't improve performance.** A major review found that high self-esteem is mostly a *consequence* of good performance, not a cause. Programs that boost self-esteem directly did not improve grades or job performance (Baumeister et al., 2003).
- **Self-esteem still matters.** Longitudinal data show that self-esteem modestly predicts later relationship, work and health outcomes (Orth and Robins, 2014).
- **Mastery builds both.** Mastery experiences are the strongest source of [[Self-Efficacy]].
- **Critique of global self-rating.** [[Rational Emotive Behavior Therapy|REBT]] argues for unconditional self-acceptance instead: rate acts, not the self. Positive self-affirmations make people with low self-esteem feel worse ([[Ironic Process Theory]]).[^antidote]
- **Flattery doesn't.** Inflated praise can backfire in children with low self-esteem (Brummelman et al., 2014). See [[Process Praise]].
- Manson: self-worth is better gauged by how one relates to one's *negative* aspects than by positive feelings; unearned positive self-regard drifts into [[Psychological Entitlement]]. See also [[Contingencies of Self-Worth]] and [[Self-Verification Theory]].
