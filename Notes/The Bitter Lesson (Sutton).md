# Review[^1]

Sutton argues that the most effective methods to have come out have mostly been due to larger compute. It ultimately dominates human's domain knowledge as well. While they do not have to be mutually exclusive in theory, in practice, they oftentimes are as niche and complex algorithms do not necessarily merge well with large scale compute like the general methods do.

In particular, the two best classes of techniques for leveraging large amounts of compute is search and learning.

The second point is that reality is unbelievably complex. Thus, instead of trying to simplify the reality and feed that into the model, we should let the model learn and approximate all of the complexities of reality the way we humans do as we grow up and mature. Machines should not just learn what we humans have learned. They should be able to learn for themselves and develop their own unique perspectives.

However, even though human [[Inductive Bias|inductive biases]] are frowned upon, the mathematical inductive biases that describe the universe itself may in fact be useful and beneficial to the deep learning models as the real world data itself comes about and is generated through these mathematical laws. And in particular, some of these may even enable scaling laws, such as the translation-invariance of CNNs or permutation-invariance of Transformers.

In the terms of [[Generalization]]'s three tools (data, priors, hypothesis space), Sutton's argument can be read as a claim about which tool ages best: a strong, hand-designed prior is a soft constraint that encodes a fixed, human guess about the solution, whereas scaling up data and using a broad, general-purpose hypothesis space lets the learning algorithm itself discover structure that may go beyond what any human prior anticipated. Since priors only help when they are good guesses, and since compute keeps making the data-driven alternative cheaper, the bet on hand-crafted priors tends to lose out over time to the bet on more data and more general (less constrained) hypothesis spaces.
