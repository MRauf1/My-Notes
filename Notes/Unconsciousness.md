# Definition
> [!info] Unconscious Mind
> The unconscious mind is a popularly cited estimate for the portion of daily decisions, actions, and brain activity (around 90%–95%) that occur below the threshold of [[Consciousness|conscious]] awareness, leaving only a small remainder (around 1%–5%) to the [[Consciousness|conscious mind]].[^1]

These figures are widely repeated estimates rather than precisely measured biological constants. Neuroscientists caution that the brain operates as a single interconnected network rather than as two cleanly separable compartments, so the exact percentages should be treated as current, evolving approximations rather than settled fact — they may be revised as research improves.[^1]

# Properties
- Autopilot Mode: handles routine tasks, habits, and involuntary body functions without requiring active attention.
- Fast Processing: takes in and processes very large amounts of information per second, drawing on memory, instinct, and environmental input.
- Neural Preparation: underlying neural activity associated with a decision can be detected before the decision reaches [[Consciousness|conscious]] awareness, suggesting the unconscious mind often "prepares" a choice ahead of conscious recognition of it.
- The conscious mind frequently generates a [[Confabulation|post-hoc narrative]] to explain a decision after the fact, even when the unconscious processing actually drove the choice.

[^1]: Popular science synthesis of psychology/neuroscience estimates on conscious vs. unconscious processing (exact primary sources, including the cited PubMed-indexed brain studies, were not resolvable from the source material).
