# Definition
> [!info] Human Dignity (Frankl)[^1]
> The unconditional value of each person, paralleling the unconditional meaning of life: it rests on the values a person has realised in the past and is not contingent on their present usefulness. An incurably psychotic individual may lose usefulness yet retain the dignity of a human being.

# Properties
- Achievement-oriented society adores the successful, happy, and young, blurring the difference between being valuable in the sense of dignity and in the sense of usefulness (usually defined as functioning for society's benefit).[^2]
- If value stems only from usefulness, one owes it only to inconsistency not to endorse "mercy" killing of the socially useless, as in Hitler's euthanasia programme; this confusion traces back to contemporary **nihilism**, which states not that there is nothing but that everything is meaningless.[^3]
- Therapists should immunise trainees against nihilism rather than inoculate them with cynicism, itself a defense against the therapists' own nihilism.[^4]
- A humanised psychiatry sees "the human being behind the disease" rather than treating the mind as a mechanism.[^5]
- Grounded in [[Permanence of the Past]].
