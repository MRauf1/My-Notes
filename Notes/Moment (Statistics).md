# Definition
> [!info] Moment of a Random Variable[^1]
> For a [[Random Variable]] $X$ and positive integer $m$, the $m$th moment (about $0$) of $X$ is
> $$
> \begin{align}
> E(X^m) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x^m f(x)\,dx \quad \text{or} \quad \sum_x x^m p(x)
> \end{align}
> $$
> whenever the integral or sum converges absolutely. The $m$th moment about $\mu$ (central moment) is $E[(X - \mu)^m]$.

The name comes from mechanics: if $f$ is a mass density on a line, the first moment locates the center of mass and the second central moment is the moment of inertia about it. These integrals are generated by the [[Moment Generating Function]] via $M^{(m)}(0) = E(X^m)$.

**What the $n$th moment measures.** Only the first four moments have standard names: location, scale, asymmetry ([[Probability Distribution Skewness|skewness]]), and tail weight ([[Kurtosis]]). The central moment $E[(X-\mu)^n]$ weights each deviation by $|x - \mu|^n$, so as $n$ grows the moments become increasingly tail statistics. Odd $n$ measure the imbalance between the two tails and even $n$ how far the mass reaches. In the limit, $(E|X|^n)^{1/n}$ increases to $\operatorname{ess\,sup}|X|$, the $L^n \to L^\infty$ limit of the [[p-Norm]].

Taken together, the moments have a clean meaning. By [[Taylor's Theorem]] about $\mu$,
$$
\begin{align}
E f(X) = f(\mu) + \sum_{n \geq 2} \frac{f^{(n)}(\mu)}{n!}\,E[(X - \mu)^n],
\end{align}
$$
for $f$ analytic with a convergent expansion. So the $n$th central moment is the coefficient of the $n$th-order behavior of a test function in its average. The second moment is the leading correction to $E f(X) \approx f(E X)$, which is the local form of [[Jensen's Inequality]]. Equivalently, the moments are the values of the functional $g \mapsto E\,g(X)$ on the monomials $1, x, x^2, \dots$, i.e. the distribution's coordinates on that basis. They determine the distribution when the mgf exists, but not always otherwise ([[Moment Problem]]). [[Cumulant Generating Function|Cumulants]] are the coordinates in which the orders separate: the $n$th cumulant isolates $n$th-order departure from normality.

# Types
- First moment: [[Expectation|mean]] $\mu = E(X)$.
- Second central moment: [[Variance]] $\sigma^2 = E[(X-\mu)^2]$.
- Third standardized moment: [[Probability Distribution Skewness|skewness]] $E[(X-\mu)^3]/\sigma^3$.
- Fourth standardized moment: [[Kurtosis]] $E[(X-\mu)^4]/\sigma^4$.
- $m$th factorial moment: $E[X(X-1)\cdots(X-m+1)]$, generated by the [[Factorial Moment Generating Function]].
- Cumulants, generated by the [[Cumulant Generating Function]].

# Properties
- [[mth Moment Existence Theorem]]: existence of the $m$th moment implies existence of all lower moments.
- Moments need not determine the distribution: the lognormal shares all of its moments with other distributions ([[Moment Problem]]).
- A distribution may have no moments at all beyond some order (e.g. the [[Cauchy Distribution]] has no mean), in which case no mgf exists; the [[Characteristic Function (Probability)|characteristic function]] always exists.
