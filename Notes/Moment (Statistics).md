# Definition
> [!info] Moment of a Random Variable[^1]
> For a [[Random Variable]] $X$ and positive integer $m$, the $m$th moment (about $0$) of $X$ is
> $$
> \begin{align}
> E(X^m) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x^m f(x)\,dx \quad \text{or} \quad \sum_x x^m p(x)
> \end{align}
> $$
> whenever the integral or sum converges absolutely. The $m$th moment about $\mu$ (central moment) is $E[(X - \mu)^m]$.

The name comes from mechanics: if $f$ is a mass density on a line, the first moment locates the center of mass and the second central moment is the moment of inertia about it. These integrals are generated by the [[Moment Generating Function]] via $M^{(m)}(0) = E(X^m)$.

# Types
- First moment: [[Expectation|mean]] $\mu = E(X)$.
- Second central moment: [[Variance]] $\sigma^2 = E[(X-\mu)^2]$.
- Third standardized moment: [[Probability Distribution Skewness|skewness]] $E[(X-\mu)^3]/\sigma^3$.
- Fourth standardized moment: [[Kurtosis]] $E[(X-\mu)^4]/\sigma^4$.
- $m$th factorial moment: $E[X(X-1)\cdots(X-m+1)]$, generated by the [[Factorial Moment Generating Function]].
- Cumulants, generated by the [[Cumulant Generating Function]].

# Properties
- [[mth Moment Existence Theorem]]: existence of the $m$th moment implies existence of all lower moments.
- A distribution may have no moments at all beyond some order (e.g. the [[Cauchy Distribution]] has no mean), in which case no mgf exists; the [[Characteristic Function (Probability)|characteristic function]] always exists.
