# Definition
> [!info] Socioemotional Selectivity Theory (Carstensen)[^1]
> When time horizons are perceived as open-ended (typically in youth), people prioritise goals that gather information and expand horizons. When time is perceived as limited (with age, illness or endings), they prioritise emotionally meaningful goals and become more selective about whom and what they invest in.

# Properties
- **Evidence.**
	- Older adults have smaller social networks made up of closer partners, and they report emotional well-being that is as good as or better than younger adults' (Carstensen, Isaacowitz and Charles, 1999; Carstensen et al., 2011).
	- The shift follows perceived time, not age itself: young people facing an ending (e.g. a move, or HIV-positive men) show the same preferences (Fredrickson and Carstensen, 1990).
	- Older adults attend to and remember positive information more than negative (the *positivity effect*; meta-analysis: Reed, Chan and Mikels, 2014).
- **Manson's "maturity".** With experience and seeing time slip by, we notice that most things have little lasting impact, become selective about what to care about, and accept ourselves; this simplification makes us consistently happier.[^1]
