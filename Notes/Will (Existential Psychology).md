# Definition
> [!info] Will (Existential Psychology)[^1]
> The mental agency that transforms awareness and knowledge into action, the bridge between desire and act: responsibility *assumption* as opposed to responsibility *awareness*. It has been called the mental state preceding action (Aristotle), the "organ of the future" (Arendt), the power of spontaneously beginning a series (Kant), the "responsible mover" (Farber), the "trigger of effort", and the "mainspring of action".

$$\begin{align}
\text{wish} \longrightarrow \text{decision} \longrightarrow \text{action}
\end{align}$$

# Properties
- Therapeutic change must be expressed in action, not in knowing, intending, or dreaming; "to know and not to act is not to know at all"; refraining from habitual action is also action. "Response-ability" is the ability to respond.[^2]
- Motivation can influence but not replace will; explaining behaviour by motives alone absolves responsibility. Will is inextricably bound with freedom.[^3]
- "Will power" is only a thin first layer of willing. Farber's first realm of will is unconscious: important choices are often inferred only after the fact.[^4]
- One initiates through **wishing** and enacts through **decision** (choice). Wishing requires access to feeling; wishes based on deliberation or morality become "shoulds". Wish-blocked individuals (who distrust wishes, deaden feeling, or expect caretakers to read their wishes) become boring and parasitic on others' wishes; impulsively enacting all wishes is the opposite disorder.[^5]
- If wishing occurs without action there is no genuine willing; action without wishing is merely impulsive.[^6]
- The therapist cannot create or infuse will, only liberate it by removing encumbrances; the future is the proper tense of change.[^7]
- Change is a gradual series of decisions, see [[Decision (Existential Psychology)]] and [[Insight (Existential Psychotherapy)]].
- Compare the Stoic [[Prohairesis]].
