# Definition
> [!info] Letting It Happen (Burkeman)[^1]
> Often the real challenge in building an accomplished life is not making things happen through willpower, but being willing to stand out of the way and let them happen. "Not everything that is more difficult is more meritorious." (Thomas Aquinas)

# Properties
- **Effort fallacy.** Believing that meaningful tasks always require exertion casts you as someone who must be pushed and prodded. Daily life then becomes a battle between who you want to be and the backslider you fear you are. It also produces the reverse inference, "whatever takes effort must matter", which is bogus.[^2]
- **Ask "What if this might be a lot easier than I'd been assuming?"** Or decide to experience a daunting challenge as easy. This is not a mystical command but a reminder not to add complications or unpleasant exertion where none need exist. Stress about a project often only signals that you care about it.[^3]
- The point is not to deny that life is hard, but to avoid turning that fact into a judgment of your inadequacy.[^3]
- **The inner critic.** Met socially, this fault-finder would seem boring and cruel, like someone living in the fallout of a catastrophe. "Inspiration is for amateurs" can turn into the command that you should be able to work on demand or else you are worthless.[^4]
- **Following pleasure.** Doing what you feel like lets desires fuel action instead of draining energy to overcome them. Mindless scrolling often follows from forcing yourself against your wants. The belief that you would become a layabout if you treated yourself decently comes from the past and is usually false (Susan Piver).[^5]
- **Generosity.** You can't make yourself a more loving person; you let it happen by finding where you already feel warmth (Chögyam Trungpa). Most people already have generous impulses but block them through perfectionism, wanting to be *optimally* kind. Act on a generous impulse the moment it arises: "generosity never leads to remorse."[^6]
- Wanting to "become a better person" is a little confused: only someone with morals can berate themselves for lacking them. Better to locate the generosity already in you and not get in its way.[^7]
