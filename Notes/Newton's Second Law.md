# Definition

> [!info] Definition 1 ([[Isaac Newton]] Second Law)[^1]
> For [[Force]] $\mathbf{F}$, [[Mass]] $m$, [[Acceleration]] $\mathbf{a}$,
> $$
> \begin{align}
> \sum \mathbf{F} = m \mathbf{a}
> \end{align}
> $$

> [!info] Definition 2 (General, Momentum Form)[^2]
> $$
> \begin{align}
> \mathbf{F} = \frac{d\mathbf{p}}{dt} = \frac{d(m\mathbf{v})}{dt}
> \end{align}
> $$
> where $\mathbf{p} = m\mathbf{v}$ is [[Momentum]]. This is Newton's original formulation; it reduces to $\mathbf{F} = m\mathbf{a}$ under the (Newtonian) approximation that mass $m$ is constant.

# Properties
- Specifies not only how much the velocity changes for a given [[Force]] and [[Mass]], but also that the direction of the resulting change in velocity is the same as the direction of the force — a change in velocity (an [[Acceleration]]) includes speeding up, slowing down, and changing direction, e.g. [[Centripetal Acceleration]] for a sideways force.
- Combined with [[Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation]] near a planet's surface, reduces to the constant vertical acceleration $g$ responsible for free fall — see [[Equations of Motion for Constant Acceleration]].
- Combined with [[Hooke's Law]], governs [[Simple Harmonic Motion]].
