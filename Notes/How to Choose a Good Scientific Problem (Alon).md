# Review[^1]

First and foremost, science is done to nurture the student into a proper scientist and into a better human being, not to publish lots of high-impact papers and other external reasons.

Ideally, one would pick relatively easy/viable research problems that can significantly increase the knowledge of the researcher and advance them forward.

When picking a research problem, make sure to take your time and not pick the very first one that comes to mind. While it may take additional time, it can save you months compared to picking a bad research problem and struggling on it with no results. For example, in the author's lab, new students are expected to not select a research problem in the first 3 months, but to explore the literature and field before committing to an idea.

Listen to your inner self when deciding on a problem because listening to your inner voice makes you more likely to choose a problem that will satisfy you in the long run. In particular, ask yourself what problem you would choose if you were the only human in the universe. 

This inner voice can be guided and strengthened with the help of proper mentors/advisors.

Another good sign are ideas and questions that keep coming back again and again throughout months/years.

This inner voice, inner expression, comes from our own unique perspectives and values. Thus, nourish your inner world and ponder about what matters to you.

In the end, basing your research on your inner self typically results in work that feels much more meaningful, and a personal connection to the work. It also increases the chances of discovering something profound.

It is cognitive dissonance that can result in many mental problems if you assume that research is linear and clean and doesn't diverge. In this schema, the student can be seen as a means to an end (the answer to the research problem). In reality, the path is very non-linear, often diverges, and sometimes you end up solving not the original problem, but some new problem you have stumbled upon, but could solve using your existing work so far. In this schema, the journey itself becomes the primary research, and it is this process of research that is thus nurtured and developed. Research becomes a sail into the unknown; one that requires courage and open-mindedness.
