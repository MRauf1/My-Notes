# Definition
> [!info] Smart Notes Workflow (Ahrens)[^1]
> The workflow around a [[Zettelkasten|slip-box]], built on the idea that writing doesn't start at the blank page:
> 1. Make [[Fleeting Note|fleeting notes]] to capture ideas as they come.
> 2. Make [[Literature Note|literature notes]] while reading.
> 3. Make [[Permanent Note|permanent notes]] by reviewing notes from steps 1 and 2 within a day or two and thinking about how they relate to your own research, thinking, or interests.
> 4. Add the permanent notes to the slip-box, filing each behind related notes, linking it, and making it findable from the index.
> 5. Develop topics, questions, and projects bottom-up from within the system.
> 6. Decide on a topic once enough clustered notes exist, then collect the relevant notes, order them, and find what is missing or redundant.
> 7. Turn the notes into a rough draft by translating them into a coherent argument, not copying them.
> 8. Edit and proofread.

# Properties
- "The actual writing down of the argument is the smallest part of its development." The real question is what to do in the weeks and months *before* the blank page. Productive writing rests on good note-taking: it is far easier to turn written notes into a text than to assemble everything in your head.[^2]
- In reality you work on many ideas in different stages at once, which is where the system's strengths show.[^3]
- **Simple structure, complex content.** Keeping structure simple lets complexity build up where it belongs, in the content (cf. Sull and Eisenhardt). The workflow should be made of simple, repeatable tasks that become automatic and *fit together seamlessly*. Collecting many techniques that don't fit together makes work harder. Good tools reduce distraction from thinking rather than adding features: "an undistracted brain to think with and a reliable collection of notes to think in" is all you need.[^4]
- **Standardization**: like the shipping container, everything goes into one slip-box in one format, streamlined toward one goal, insight that can be published. Always handling notes the same way cuts the number of decisions per session (cf. [[Willpower]]).[^5]
- **Typical mistakes**: treating every note as permanent (no critical mass; good ideas diluted), keeping only project-specific notes (starting over after every project), or treating all notes as fleeting (nothing accumulates).[^6]
- A tool is only as good as your handling of it. The common misunderstanding is to focus on the slip-box in isolation and ignore the workflow around it.[^7]
- Based on two ideas: Luhmann's slip-box and the overarching workflow of David Allen's *Getting Things Done* (GTD).[^8]
- Related: [[Writing]], [[Circularity of Writing]], [[Virtuous Circle of Learning]].
