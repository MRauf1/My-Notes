# Definition
> [!info] Vertical and Horizontal Relationships[^1]
> A **vertical relationship** treats the other person as above or below oneself. A **horizontal relationship** treats them as an equal: "equal but not the same". [[Adlerian Psychology]] rejects all vertical relationships and proposes that all relationships be horizontal. This can be seen as its fundamental principle.

# Properties
- **No praise, no rebuke.** Praise is "the passing of judgment by a person of ability on a person of no ability". Its goal is to manipulate someone of lesser ability, not gratitude or respect. Praise and rebuke are carrot and stick, both manipulation, which is why reward-and-punishment education is rejected.[^2]
- Wanting to be praised, or deciding to praise someone, shows one sees relationships as vertical.[^3]
- **Being praised teaches incapacity.** Enjoying praise means accepting the vertical relation and one's own lack of ability. Making praise one's goal means living by another person's yardstick and limiting one's freedom.[^4]
- **Intervention vs assistance.** Intervention comes from seeing the other as beneath one and as wrong, and it is manipulation. Assistance presupposes [[Separation of Tasks]] and horizontal relations, and helps the person face their own tasks: this is [[Encouragement (Adlerian Psychology)]].[^5]
- **Inferiority belongs to vertical relations.** In horizontal relationships there is no room for an [[Inferiority Complex]].[^6]
- **All or nothing.** Building even one vertical relationship leads one to treat all relationships as vertical. Building one truly equal relationship is a major lifestyle transformation, after which all relationships gradually become horizontal.[^7]
- **Judgment is vertical.** In horizontal relationships, judgment gives way to gratitude, respect and joy.[^8]
- **Competition vs cooperation.** The competition principle (competing for strength and rank) always creates winners, losers and hierarchy, that is, vertical relationships. Horizontal relationships run on the cooperation principle: no winning or losing, and differences in ability don't matter. See [[Adlerian Education]].[^kk1]

# Evidence
- **Correction: praise is not always harmful.** In Deci, Koestner & Ryan's (1999) meta-analysis, tangible rewards undermined intrinsic motivation for interesting tasks, but verbal rewards (positive feedback) on average *enhanced* it. Praise helps when it is sincere, informational, attributes success to controllable causes and supports autonomy. It hurts when it is controlling, judges traits or compares people (Henderlong & Lepper, 2002). The supported core of Adler's view is to avoid controlling, evaluative, trait-based praise. See [[Process Praise]], [[Intrinsic Motivation]].
