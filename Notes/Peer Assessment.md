# Definition
> [!info] Peer Assessment[^1]
> Students evaluate each other's work or contributions, usually against criteria, and receive aggregated feedback. Pausch used repeated peer ratings in team courses so students could "judge for themselves how they're coming along." The goal was to "teach students how to see their minds growing in the same way they can see their muscles grow when they look in a mirror."

# Properties
- **Evidence.**
	- A meta-analysis found that peer assessment improves academic performance ($g \approx 0.31$) compared with no assessment. It was at least as good as teacher assessment (Double, McGrane and Hopfenbeck, 2020).
	- Self-assessment also improves achievement modestly, but only when taught with criteria. Unaided self-assessment is poorly calibrated: people tend to overrate their skill, especially the least skilled (Kruger and Dunning, 1999).
- **Why it works.** Pausch built "mechanical ways to get people to listen to feedback". He showed students where they ranked among peers, which overcomes denial.[^2] Feedback from several raters is harder to dismiss than one person's opinion.
- **Honesty over flattery.** "Too much stroking and too little real feedback."[^3] See [[Self-Esteem]] and [[Feedback Intervention Theory]].
