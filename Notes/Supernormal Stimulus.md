# Definition
> [!info] Supernormal Stimulus (Tinbergen)[^1]
> An exaggerated version of a natural stimulus that elicits a *stronger* response than the natural one. Animals seem preloaded with behavioral rules, and an exaggerated version of the triggering feature overdrives them. Niko Tinbergen found that herring gull chicks pecked faster at artificial beaks with bigger or more red spots than at a real parent's beak.

# Properties
- **Humans.** Food is abundant, but the brain still craves salt, sugar and fat as if they were scarce. Food science exploits this by optimizing orosensation (how food feels in the mouth) and dynamic contrast (combined sensations, such as crunchy and creamy). "We've gotten too good at pushing our own buttons." (Stephan Guyenet)[^2] In a controlled inpatient trial, people on an ultra-processed diet ate about 500 kcal/day more and gained weight compared with an unprocessed diet matched for presented nutrients (Hall et al., 2019).
- Modern society is full of engineered stimuli more attractive than the world we evolved in (shopping, social media, pornography). The trend is toward more concentrated rewards. "We have the brains of our ancestors but temptations they never had to face."[^3]
