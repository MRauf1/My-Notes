# Definition
> [!info] Smell (Mathematics)[^1]
> The informal impression that experienced mathematicians (perhaps subconsciously) form of how convincing a human-generated mathematical argument is, well before they have checked its individual steps.

# Properties
- A key component of a favourable smell (after Thurston) is that the argument provides understanding or insight: not merely that hypotheses logically entail a conclusion, but a causal narrative of how the entailment is possible, which parts do the "heavy lifting", which are novel relative to the literature, and which are routine.[^2]
- Such interpretations are not captured by the official frameworks of rigorous mathematics (first-order logic, set theory), but are essential for drawing lessons about how an argument generalises or interacts with other methods.[^2]
- Narrative structure strengthens confidence in robustness: a single misplaced sign can invalidate a long [[Proof|proof]], but if the proof has a clear strategy for isolating and addressing key difficulties, following analogies with successful prior arguments, local errors are more likely repairable in the spirit of the original. Not all errors are equally disastrous, and some may even be beneficial.[^2]
- "Good" mathematics, however defined, tends in practice to fit into broader mathematical narratives ([[Good Mathematics]]).[^2]
- Because full step-by-step checking is expensive and rarely done except for high-profile results, the community increasingly (over-)relies on smell to assess credibility, despite mathematics' foundations in principle allowing consensus by checking each step against the axioms and inference rules.[^3]
- AI-generated arguments break the correlation between surface form and underlying reasoning, making smell unreliable; this motivates [[Formal Proof Assistant|formal verification]] ([[AI-Assisted Mathematics]]).
