# Definition
> [!info] Relativistic Doppler Effect
> The shift in the observed frequency $\nu$ (and wavelength $\lambda$) of light due to relative motion between source and observer, given for motion directly along the line of sight by
> $$
> \begin{align}
> \nu' = \sqrt{\frac{1-\beta}{1+\beta}}\,\nu, \qquad \beta = v/c
> \end{align}
> $$
> where $\nu$ is the frequency in the source's frame and $\nu'$ the frequency a receding observer measures.

# Properties
- The speed of a [[Photon]] is invariant (equal to $c$ in every reference frame), so relative motion between observers cannot change how fast light travels; instead, all of the relativistic frame-dependence is carried entirely by a shift in frequency/wavelength, and correspondingly, since $E=h\nu$, in the photon's measured energy and momentum.
- A receding observer measures a lower frequency (redshift: lower energy and momentum); an approaching observer measures a higher frequency (blueshift: higher energy and momentum).
- Distinct from cosmological redshift, in which the [[Expansion of the Universe]] stretches the wavelength of light in transit; the two effects share the same qualitative signature (a wavelength shift with no violation of $c$) but arise from different mechanisms.
