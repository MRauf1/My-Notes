# Definition
> [!info] Quantum Electrodynamics[^1]
> The quantum-mechanically consistent theory of the interaction between electrically charged particles and the [[Electromagnetic Field]], equivalently between charged particles and [[Photon]]s.

# Properties
- Built from just two measured input numbers, the mass and the [[Electric Charge]] of the electron (with corresponding numbers for other charged particles); most other quantities follow as consequences.
- The strength of the electron-photon coupling is the fine-structure constant, approximately 1/137.
- All ordinary electrical, mechanical, and chemical laws follow, in principle, from quantum electrodynamics outside the atomic nucleus; no confirmed exception has been found there.
- Predicts an [[Antiparticle]] for every charged particle, such as the positron as the antiparticle of the electron.
- Among the most precisely confirmed theories in physics.

[^1]: [The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. I, Ch. 2: Basic Physics](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_02.html)
