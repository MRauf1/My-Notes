# Definition

Slowly exposing the client to the thing that triggers fear/anxiety in the client so that the client slowly gets used to them.[^1]

Can be in real life, imagination, or [[Virtual Reality]].

![[Pasted image 20251015022806.png]]

Another approach is [[Systematic Desensitization]] where a hierarchy of anxiety is created for the stimulus, and increasingly higher anxiety-provoking levels of the stimulus are paired with a relaxation technique.

Goggins' fear of heights eased as he became "conditioned to the risk" through repeated freefall jumps.[^2] Exposure-based treatments are the most effective treatments for specific phobias (Wolitzky-Taylor et al., 2008). See also [[Steeling Effect]].
# Stoic and Cognitive Perspectives (Robertson)
- Confronting fears in reality or imagination produces *habituation*, which tends to bring *desensitisation* (less distress). "FEAR: Face Everything And Recover."[^rob1]
- **Cognitive benefit (Beck).** Facing fears also lets one reality-test preconceptions and try new ways of thinking (cognitive restructuring; [[Cognitive Reappraisal]]). Hence the second acronym, "False Evidence Appearing Real": most fears are mediated by [[Cognitive Distortion|cognitive distortions]], so the situation is seldom as catastrophic as it seems.[^rob2]
- **Russell.** "Every kind of fear grows worse by not being looked at"; averting one's thoughts pays tribute to the spectre's horribleness. Think about it rationally, calmly and with great concentration until it becomes familiar and even boring.[^rob3]
- Ancient precursor: [[Premeditatio Malorum]].
