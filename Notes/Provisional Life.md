# Definition
> [!info] Provisional Life (von Franz)[^1]
> Marie-Louise von Franz's term for the feeling that one is "not yet in real life": current work and relationships are not what is really wanted, and "the real thing" will come in the future. It goes with a dread of being pinned down, of "entering time and space completely, and of being the singular human being that one is".

# Properties
- Treating the present as leading up to when real life begins means treating life as something to "get through" until it ends without the meaningful part ever arriving.[^2]
- **Taking life too seriously** is another route to it: obsessing over using time efficiently for future purposes, so the present becomes mere preparation. Looking back, one may feel pity for the past self who sacrificed for a future self.[^3]
- It offers "a spurious and delusive immortality" (Keynes): if the point of life lies later, you must still be alive to reach it.[^4]
- Being present concerns *how* you pursue plans, not abandoning them. Pursuing ambitious goals is one excellent way to be fully immersed in life. Figuring things out is the substance of an absorbing life, not preparation for one.[^5]
- (The marshmallow studies found that children who resisted temptation fared better later. Burkeman uses this to note that delayed gratification has real value. A large replication found the link much weaker than originally reported; see [[Hyperbolic Discounting]].)[^6]
- "To treat life as a pilgrimage to a future and better existence is to disown its present value."[^7]
- Related, but distinct from Frankl's [[Provisional Existence]]. Compare [[Psychological Time]], [[Means to an End]].
