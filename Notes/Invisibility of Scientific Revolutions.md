# Definition
> [!info] Invisibility of Scientific Revolutions[^1]
> The systematic disguising of the existence and significance of [[Scientific Revolution|scientific revolutions]] by science's main source of authority, textbooks (with popularisations and philosophical works modelled on them), which record only the stable outcome of past revolutions.

# Properties
- Textbooks address an already articulated body of problems, data, and theory, the current paradigms, and need not relate how those bases were first recognised and embraced.[^1]
- As pedagogic vehicles of [[Normal Science|normal science]], they are rewritten after each revolution, disguising its role and existence; unless one lived through a revolution, one's historical sense extends only to the outcome of the latest.[^2]
- By selection and distortion, past scientists are portrayed as working on the same fixed problems under the same canons, so science appears cumulative; but many current puzzles did not exist before the latest revolution, and the whole network of fact and theory has shifted.[^3]
- Scientists are especially tempted to rewrite history, since research results show no obvious dependence on historical context and their position seems secure outside crises: "A science that hesitates to forget its founders is lost."[^4]
