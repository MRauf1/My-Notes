# Definition
> [!info] Clarity in Writing[^1]
> Readers judge authors (and audiences judge speakers) as more intelligent the clearer and more to the point their expression is (Oppenheimer, 2006). "If you can't say it clearly, you don't understand it yourself." (John Searle)

# Properties
- "The principle is that you must not fool yourself, and you are the easiest person to fool." (Feynman) Writing exposes whether you have really understood.[^2]
- **Sapere aude** (Kant, 1784): "Have the courage to use your own understanding." Immaturity is self-imposed when it comes not from lack of understanding but from a lack of courage to think without another's guidance.[^3]
- Only in written form can an argument be examined from a distance (see [[Writing]]).[^4]
