# Definition
> [!info] Human-Machine Partnership[^1]
> A novel mode of problem-solving in which humans define a problem or goal for a machine; the machine, operating in a realm just beyond human reach, determines the optimal process to pursue; and once it brings a process into the human realm, humans study, understand, and ideally incorporate it into existing practice.

# Properties
- AI will lead to decisions made in three ways: by humans (familiar), by machines (becoming familiar), and by human-machine collaboration (unfamiliar and unprecedented).[^2]
- Because the current partnership requires a definable problem and a measurable goal, there is no reason to fear all-knowing, all-controlling machines.[^1]
- AI will become a permanent companion in perceiving and processing information, occupying a different "mental" plane: we may know it assists us as intended without knowing what exactly it is doing or why it works, whether we treat it as tool, partner, or rival.[^3]
- AI cannot reflect on its results or their moral significance; humans must decide their significance, and must therefore regulate and monitor the technology.[^4]
- **Opacity**: humans will often not know what an AI has learned or how, but human learning is similarly opaque (intuition in artists, athletes, mechanics); just as societies developed certification, regulation, and law for opaque human expertise, similar techniques should be applied to AI.[^5]
- An interlocutor with a different perception can greatly benefit science, creative work, and software development, but requires accepting that human reason is not the only, and perhaps not the most informative, way of knowing ([[AI as a Way of Knowing]]).[^6]
- Popular fears of self-aware machines rebelling mistakenly assume AI's culmination is to act like individual humans.[^7]
- Compare the "junior partner" view in [[Human-Centered Artificial Intelligence]] and [[AI-Assisted Scientific Research]].
