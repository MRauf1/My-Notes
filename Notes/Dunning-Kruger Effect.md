# Definition
> [!info] Dunning-Kruger Effect[^1]
> A cognitive bias in which people with low competence in a domain overestimate their ability, partly because the skills needed to perform well are the same skills needed to recognise good performance (and, conversely, highly competent people tend to slightly underestimate their relative standing).

# Properties
- People in general are poor at estimating their performance and ability; in [[Carol Dweck|Dweck]]'s studies those with a [[Fixed Mindset|fixed mindset]] accounted for almost all the inaccuracy, while those with a [[Growth Mindset|growth mindset]] were remarkably accurate.[^2]
- Explanation via mindset: learning requires accurate, even unflattering, information about current abilities, so growth-minded people are open to it; when every outcome is good or bad news about precious fixed traits, some outcomes get magnified and others explained away, until "you don't know yourself at all".[^2]
- Exceptional people have "a special talent for identifying their own strengths and weaknesses" (Howard Gardner).[^3]
