# Definition
> [!info] Hedonic Adaptation[^1]
> The tendency for the affective impact of any new source of pleasure (or pain) to fade as we grow accustomed to it, so that it moves to the backdrop of our lives and stops delivering as much joy. Repeated across gains, it is the *hedonic treadmill* (Brickman and Campbell, 1971).

# Properties
- **Evidence.**
	- Adaptation to positive changes is typically faster and more complete than adaptation to many negative ones. Adaptation is incomplete for some events, e.g. unemployment and disability leave lasting drops in life satisfaction (Lucas, 2007; Diener, Lucas and Scollon, 2006).
	- Variety, surprise and appreciation slow adaptation (Lyubomirsky, 2011, hedonic adaptation to positive and negative experiences).
- **Reversal by imagined loss.** Regularly reminding yourself that you might lose what you enjoy, and will eventually lose all of it at death, should counter adaptation (Stoic negative visualisation, see [[Stoic Exercises]]).[^2] Evidence supports this: mentally subtracting a positive event from one's life increases positive affect more than imagining its presence (Koo et al., 2008). See [[Gratitude Intervention]].
