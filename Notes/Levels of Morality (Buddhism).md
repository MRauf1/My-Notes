# Definition
> [!info] Levels of Morality (Gunaratana)[^1]
> 1. **Externally enforced rules** — adherence to rules laid down by someone else, with someone to "swing the stick". Requires no [[Meditation|meditation]].
> 2. **Internalized rules** — obeying the same rules when no enforcer is present. Requires some mental control.
> 3. **Ethics** — no hard-and-fast rules. One chooses behavior according to the needs of each situation, producing a unique, creative, appropriate response. This requires intelligence and an objective view that gives equal weight to one's own needs and others', free of greed, hatred, envy, and selfishness.

# Properties
- The third level is "a real paradigm shift" and absolutely demands meditation, since there is no other way to acquire the skill.[^2]
- Consciously sorting all the factors at this level is exhausting. A deeper level of mind does it with ease, through intuition that arises when the "logic circuits" are disengaged. Meditation teaches the art of stepping out of one's own way (cf. [[Unconscious Thought Theory]]).[^3]
- Until selfishness is seen through, a person's good works tend to be an extension of their own [[Ego|ego]]. Cleansing yourself of selfishness is not a selfish activity.[^4]
- The third level resembles [[Virtue Ethics]] more than rule-based [[Normative Ethics|normative]] systems; see also [[Ethics]] and [[Threefold Training]].
