# Definition
> [!info] Expansion of the Universe
> The increase over time in the distance between distant galaxies, caused not by galaxies moving through space but by the expansion of space itself, which carries matter along with it; galaxies are approximately at rest relative to their local surroundings while the space between them grows.

# Properties
- Quantified, for the present era, by [[Hubble's Law]].
- Because it is space itself that stretches rather than matter moving through space, this expansion is not bounded by the speed-of-light limit from special relativity, which restricts only the local motion of objects and signals through space, not the global rate at which the spacetime metric grows; for sufficiently distant galaxies, the rate of increase of proper distance exceeds $c$.
- Has decelerated and accelerated in different eras: gravity acting on matter decelerated the expansion for billions of years after [[Cosmic Inflation]] ended, until [[Dark Energy]] became dominant roughly 5 billion years ago, after which the expansion began accelerating again.
- [[Cosmic Inflation]] is a distinct, far more extreme episode of this same kind of expansion, confined to a tiny fraction of a second after the [[Big Bang]].
