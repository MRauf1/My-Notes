# Definition
> [!info] Crossing Bridges When You Come to Them (Burkeman)[^1]
> We are trapped in the present and cannot see into the future, so we suffer "total vulnerability to events" (Robert Saltzman). Worry is the mind trying to picture every bridge that might need crossing and how to cross it. Many worries concern questions with no "fact of the matter" yet: no answer exists until the moment arrives.

# Properties
- **Worry is doubly impossible**, hence compulsive and repetitive. We can't think of every future challenge, and even if we could, the reassurance we want would only come from having actually crossed the bridges.[^2]
- "What is anxiety? It is the next day." (Kierkegaard) "The future destroys the present."[^3]
- Worry made sense in the ancestral environment. Today's *delayed-return environment* can take months to show whether a problem is real, so anxiety has no useful behavior to motivate and just loops.[^4]
- Certainty about the future is impossible, but *confidence* in your ability to deal with it when it arrives is possible. "You will meet it, if you have to, with the same weapons of reason which today arm you against the present." (Marcus Aurelius)[^5]
- Spend less energy manipulating the future and have more faith in your capacity to handle challenges when, and if, they arrive.[^5]
- Your responsibility is only ever to the next moment: do "the next and most necessary thing" (Carl Jung).[^6]
