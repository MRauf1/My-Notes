# Definition
> [!info] Role-Playing (Tolle)[^1]
> Losing yourself in pre-established social roles (customer, waiter, parent, expert), which give a comforting identity but make authentic interaction impossible. Your mental image of yourself relates to your mental image of the other, and theirs does the same, so an egoic interaction between two people is really an interaction between four mind-made fictions. "There is no true relationship."

# Properties
- Role identities outlive their function, e.g., parents who can't stop being "the parent" to a forty-year-old child, or who need to be needed.[^2]
- You are most powerful when an action is done for its own sake, not to protect, enhance, or conform to a role. Without roles there is no ego in what you do, and you are fully focused on the situation. Those who don't try to appear more than they are stand out and truly make a difference.[^3]
- "Being yourself" needs no method; just stop adding unnecessary baggage to who you already are.[^4]
- Give up defining yourself, to yourself or others. When others define you, they limit themselves.[^5]
- "In form, you are and will always be inferior to some, superior to others. In essence, you are neither inferior nor superior to anyone." True self-esteem and true humility come from this (cf. [[Self-Esteem]]).[^6]
- True love between, e.g., parent and child is possible only beyond form, in Being: "To love is to recognize yourself in another."[^7]
