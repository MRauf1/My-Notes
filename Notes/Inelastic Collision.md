# Definition
> [!info] Inelastic Collision[^1]
> A collision between two bodies in which [[Kinetic Energy]] is not conserved: some of it is converted into other forms, such as heat and vibrational energy, so the bodies' rebound speeds are smaller than their speeds before the collision.

# Properties
- [[Momentum]] is still exactly conserved in an inelastic collision — only kinetic energy fails to be conserved, since some of it leaves the bodies' mechanical motion as heat or vibration (which is itself subsequently dissipated as heat).
- The amount of kinetic energy recovered after the collision depends on the material: none is recovered for a highly deformable material like putty, while stiffer materials recover more.
- The complementary case to an [[Elastic Collision]], which conserves kinetic energy as well as momentum.

[^1]: [The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. I, Ch. 10: Conservation of Momentum](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_10.html)
