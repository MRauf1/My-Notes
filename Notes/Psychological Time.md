# Definition
> [!info] Psychological Time (Tolle)[^1]
> Identification with the past and continuous, compulsive projection into the future, as opposed to *clock time*, the practical use of past and future, such as learning from the past and planning. The compulsion arises because the past seems to give you an identity and the future promises salvation or fulfillment. Tolle considers both illusions.

# Properties
- **Only the Now is real.** Nothing ever happened in the past or will happen in the future; it happens in the Now. The past is a former Now and the future an imagined Now, so their reality is "borrowed" from the present. The eternal present is the one constant space in which life unfolds.[^2]
- The Now is the main access point to [[Being (Spirituality)|Being]] (see [[The Unmanifested]]). The more you focus on time, the more you miss the Now.[^3]
- The mind cannot stay in control without time, so it experiences the timeless Now as a threat. It covers the present with past and future, or reduces the present to a means to an end ([[Means to an End]]).[^4]
- **Practice.** Make the Now your dwelling place and make brief visits to past and future only when practical matters require it. Use clock time, then immediately return to present-moment awareness. The enlightened person's attention is mainly in the Now, but they remain peripherally aware of time.[^5]
- A mistake used as a lesson is clock time. A mistake dwelt on with self-criticism, remorse, or guilt becomes "me" and "mine", which is psychological time.[^6]
- **Negativity** comes from accumulated psychological time and denial of the present. Unease, anxiety, tension, stress, worry, and all forms of fear come from too much future. Guilt, regret, resentment, grievances, sadness, bitterness, and all forms of nonforgiveness come from too much past.[^7]
- Excessive focus on a goal reduces the Now to a stepping stone, and life becomes an obsessive need to "make it". Collectively, ideologies that place the highest good in the future and justify any means show how "belief in a future heaven creates a present hell".[^8]
- The future is usually a replica of the past. Real change depends on the *quality of consciousness now*, which is determined by one's degree of [[Presence (Spirituality)|presence]].[^9]
- Things *can* get better in the future, so hope is not always an illusion. But hope keeps attention in the future and so continues the denial of the Now.[^10]
- **The unconscious past.** There is no need to dig into it except as it shows up now as a thought, emotion, desire, reaction, or event. Delving into the past is a bottomless pit. Only the present can free you of it: "The past cannot survive in your presence."[^11]
- Suffering needs time; it cannot survive in the Now. Zen is "walking along the razor's edge of Now". Life-threatening emergencies and dangerous sports force people into the Now.[^12]
