# Definition
> [!info] Random Walk Mean Squared Displacement[^1]
> For a [[Random Walk]] of $N$ steps of unit length, the [[Expectation|expected value]] of the squared net displacement $D_N^2$ is just $N$, the number of steps taken:
> $$\langle D_N^2 \rangle = N$$
> For a walk with step length $l$, this generalizes to $\langle D_N^2 \rangle = N l^2$.

# Properties
- The [[Standard Deviation]] of the displacement therefore grows as $\sqrt{N}$, so the [[Probability Density Function|probability density]] describing $D_N$ widens in proportion to $\sqrt{N}$ as $N$ increases, and its height must shrink in proportion to $1/\sqrt{N}$ to keep the total probability equal to $1$.

[^1]: [The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. I, Ch. 6: Probability](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_06.html)
