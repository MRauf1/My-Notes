# Definition
> [!info] Perceived Speed of Time[^1]
> The sense that time passes faster as we age: "the fewer of them we have left, the faster we seem to lose them." The likeliest explanation is that the brain encodes the passage of time by how much information it processes, or how many distinct memories it forms, in a given interval.

# Properties
- **Evidence.** Older people are more likely to say the past decade flew by, but age effects on the felt speed of shorter intervals (weeks, months) are weak (Wittmann and Lehnhoff, 2005; Friedman and Janssen, 2010). Looking back, a period full of novel, memorable events feels longer, even if it felt fast at the time (the "holiday paradox").
- **Remedies.** Novel experiences lengthen remembered time, but cramming life with them is impractical and worsens overwhelm. Burkeman suggests paying more attention to ordinary moments instead, finding novelty by plunging deeper into the life you already have.[^2] That suggestion hasn't been tested.
