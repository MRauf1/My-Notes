# Definition
> [!info] Zero-Point Motion[^1]
> As temperature approaches absolute zero, the vibrational motion of atoms decreases toward a minimum but nonzero amount; atoms never come completely to rest.

# Properties
- Qualifies the [[Kinetic Theory of Heat]]'s picture of atomic motion vanishing at absolute zero.
- Determines whether a substance solidifies as its temperature is lowered toward absolute zero, since [[Melting]] (and its reverse, freezing) depends on whether the residual vibration is small enough for a [[Crystalline Structure]] to form.
- Required by the [[Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle]]: an atom completely at rest would have simultaneously precise position and (zero) momentum, which the principle forbids.

[^1]: [The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. I, Ch. 1: Atoms in Motion](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_01.html)
