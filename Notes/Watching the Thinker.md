# Definition
> [!info] Watching the Thinker (Tolle)[^1]
> The practice of listening impartially to the voice in your head as a witnessing presence, without judging or analyzing it. You become aware both of the thought and of yourself as its witness. The thought then loses its power and subsides, leaving a gap in the mental stream: "no-mind", consciousness without thought.

# Properties
- **First step.** The beginning of freedom is realizing that you are not the thinker. This lets you observe it, which activates a higher level of consciousness.[^2]
- The "I am" realization ("there is the voice, and here I am watching it") is not a thought; Tolle says it arises from beyond the mind.[^1]
- Gaps of no-mind start out a few seconds long and grow with practice. In them one feels stillness, peace, and a subtle "joy of Being". It is not a trance: one is more alert than in the mind-identified state.[^3]
- **Alternative route.** Direct attention intensely into the Now. Any routine activity that is normally a means to an end can be given full attention so that it becomes an end in itself. Tolle calls this the essence of [[Meditation|meditation]].[^4]
- **Success criterion**: the degree of peace felt within.[^5]
- "Identification with the mind gives it more energy; observation of the mind withdraws energy from it." The moment you realize you are not present, you are present.[^6]
- Emotions must be watched as much as thoughts. Feel the emotion's energy in the body without analyzing it. Thought and emotion otherwise form a feedback loop (see [[Emotion]]).[^7]
- One day you may catch yourself smiling at the voice in your head as you would at a child's antics. That means your sense of self no longer depends on the mind's content.[^8]
- Strongly emotional situations call for intense presence; otherwise the reaction takes you over and you "become" it.[^9]
- Parallels the bare attention of [[Mindfulness]] and the handling of [[Distraction (Meditation)|distractions]] in [[Vipassana Meditation]].
