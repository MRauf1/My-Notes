# Definition
> [!info] Central Governor Model (Noakes)[^1]
> The brain regulates exercise intensity in anticipation, holding back a physiological reserve to protect the body from catastrophic failure (heat stroke, muscle breakdown, cardiac damage). On this view, fatigue is a protective sensation produced by the brain rather than simply muscles running out of fuel (Noakes, 2001, 2005). Goggins' "governor" is the same idea: the brain stops us before we "risk it all", and it "can't stop us unless we buy into its bullshit and agree to quit."

# Properties
- **Evidence for a reserve.**
	- Athletes speed up near the finish despite accumulated fatigue (the end spurt).
	- Not all muscle fibers are recruited even at exhaustion.
	- Performance changes when athletes are misled about distance or time, or are trained in motivational [[Self-Talk]].
- **Related model.** In the psychobiological model, exhaustion occurs when perceived effort reaches the maximum a person is willing to give. Mental fatigue alone reduces endurance (Marcora et al., 2009).
- Whether a literal "central governor" exists is still debated, and peripheral factors in the muscles also matter.
- **The 40% Rule (Goggins).** Goggins claims most people quit at about 40% of their maximum effort.[^2] No research supports the figure. A reserve exists, but its size is unknown and varies between people and situations.
- **The reserve protects.** Overriding it can cause real harm. Goggins' own story includes stress fractures, suspected kidney failure, rhabdomyolysis and a long breakdown later attributed to overtraining.[^3]
- **Gradual overload.** Push 5–10% past your usual stopping point, then raise the baseline again the following week, so body and mind can adapt and injury is avoided.[^4] This matches the well-established principle of progressive overload in training.
- **Chunking.** When you want to quit, commit only to the next visible landmark, then the next.[^5] This matches the evidence on near-term subgoals (see [[Goal-Setting Theory]]).
- Related: [[Grit]], [[Steeling Effect]].
