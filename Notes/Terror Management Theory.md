# Definition
> [!info] Terror Management Theory[^1]
> (Greenberg, Pyszczynski and Solomon, 1986, formalising Becker's *The Denial of Death*) Awareness of inevitable death creates potential terror, which humans manage by investing in cultural worldviews and self-esteem that promise literal or symbolic immortality (Becker's *immortality projects*: religions, nations, careers, art). Reminders of death (*mortality salience*) should therefore strengthen defence of one's worldview.

# Properties
- **Mortality salience effects.** People prompted to think about death cling harder to their immortality projects:[^1]
	- they endorse aggressive, "fighting talk" leaders and outgroup hostility more ("what calm, what relief" to give oneself over to strong-looking demagogues, Becker);
	- they show stronger disgust at bodily waste, read as buffering against reminders of "creatureliness", i.e. that like other animals they are physically mortal (Goldenberg et al., 2001). Becker's logic may also explain taboos around menstruation and the privacy of excretion.[^2]
- **Evidence status.** A meta-analysis of 277 experiments reported a moderate mortality-salience effect ($r \approx 0.35$) (Burke, Martens and Faucher, 2010). However, the large multi-lab Many Labs 4 replication of the classic worldview-defence effect found no effect (Klein et al., 2022), and later meta-analyses suggest publication bias inflates it. Treat the experimental effect as uncertain.
- **Depression.** For Becker, mental illness is a malfunction of the death-denial machinery: depressed people fail to shield themselves from the truth that they are not cosmically significant heroes and will soon die.[^1] This claim is untested.
