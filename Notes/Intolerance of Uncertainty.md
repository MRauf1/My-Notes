# Definition
> [!info] Intolerance of Uncertainty[^1]
> A dispositional tendency to find uncertainty aversive and threatening, regardless of the probability of a negative outcome (Dugas; Carleton, 2016). People high in it act to remove the *feeling* of not knowing in the present, even when doing so doesn't serve their long-term interests.

# Properties
- **Goal clinging.** Much investment in goals and plans is driven not by the value of preparation but by discomfort with uncertainty: we invest in a preferred vision of the future because it relieves present-moment anxiety, not because it helps achieve it. Many major life decisions are taken mainly to minimise present emotional discomfort, scrambling "to the next patch of firm ground, whatever direction it may be in".[^1] See [[Goal-Setting Theory]].
- **Evidence.**
	- It is a transdiagnostic vulnerability factor, most strongly linked to [[Generalized Anxiety Disorder]] and also to OCD, social anxiety and depression (meta-analysis: McEvoy, Hyett, Shihata, Price and Strachan, 2019).
	- Treatments that target it reduce worry and GAD symptoms (Dugas and Ladouceur, 2000; Hebert and Dugas, 2019).
- "The quest for certainty blocks the search for meaning. Uncertainty is the very condition to impel man to unfold his powers" (Fromm). Uncertainty is where opportunities wait.[^2]
- Nussbaum: the ethical life is based on "a trust in the uncertainty", "more like a plant than a jewel", fragile but beautiful because of that fragility.[^2]
- Related: [[Worry]], [[Effectuation]], [[Not Knowing (Burkeman)]].
