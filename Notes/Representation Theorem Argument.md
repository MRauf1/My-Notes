# Definition
> [!info] Representation Theorem Argument[^1]
> An argument for [[Probabilism]] that reasons backward from an agent's preferences, which are assumed to depend only on their [[Utility Function|utilities]] for outcomes and their [[Credence|degrees of belief]] that each outcome will occur, to what those beliefs must be. With [A Critical Introduction to Formal Epistemology](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/9XYCZDPF?page=$ a set of reasonable constraints on preferences (e.g., those of the [[Von Neumann-Morgenstern Utility Theorem]]):
> - **Preference Consistency.** If an agent is rational, then their preferences satisfy constraints [A Critical Introduction to Formal Epistemology](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/9XYCZDPF?page=$.
> - **Representation Theorem.** If an agent's preferences satisfy constraints [A Critical Introduction to Formal Epistemology](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/9XYCZDPF?page=$, then they can be represented as having degrees of belief $ that satisfy the rules of probability.
> - **Representation Accuracy.** If an agent can be represented as having degrees of belief $ that satisfy the rules of probability, then the agent's actual degrees of belief are $.
> - **Probabilism.** Therefore, if an agent is rational, then their degrees of belief satisfy the rules of probability.

# Properties
- The Representation Theorem itself is a mathematical result and above reproach; the other two premises can be challenged.
- **Against Preference Consistency**: reject one or more of the constraints [A Critical Introduction to Formal Epistemology](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/9XYCZDPF?page=$, most commonly Independence, which the [[Allais Paradox]] shows actual agents violate.
- **Against Representation Accuracy**: being representable as having beliefs $ does not mean one really has beliefs $; acting as if something is true differs from its being true.
	- The premise is plausible only if there is nothing more to having a belief than behaving as if one has it, as held by instrumentalists (beliefs are not real entities but useful fictions for telling a story about behavior) and behaviorists (beliefs are identical to behavior).
	- These views were influential for much of the twentieth century, but most contemporary philosophers adopt a realist theory of [[Belief|belief]], and so reject Representation Accuracy.

[^1]: [A Critical Introduction to Formal Epistemology](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/9XYCZDPF?page=49)
