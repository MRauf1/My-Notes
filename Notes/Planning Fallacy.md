# Definition
> [!info] Planning Fallacy[^1]
> The systematic underestimation of how long tasks will take. In Buehler, Griffin, and Ross (1995), only 45% of students finished their papers within the time they were 99% sure they would need, and experience didn't seem to correct this.

# Properties
- Be skeptical of planning focused on the *outcome*. Imagining yourself having finished doesn't help; a realistic picture of the *steps* needed does, both for performance and motivation (Singer et al., 2001; Pham and Taylor, 1999).[^2]
- **Small, concrete daily targets** (e.g., three notes a day, review one paragraph) give hundreds of feedback events a year, which calibrate your expectations far better than an occasional missed deadline.[^3]
- People with the strongest habits were the *least* accurate yet *most* confident in predicting their behavior: familiarity breeds an illusion of control (Dean, 2013) (see [[Habit]]).[^4]
- Plans also impose inflexibility. Insight can't be scheduled, so the workflow should let new ideas drive the work. "Real experts … don't make plans" (Flyvbjerg); they act on trained intuition (see [[Intuition]]).[^5]
- Related: [[Parkinson's Law]].
