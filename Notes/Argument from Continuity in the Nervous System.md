# Definition
> [!info] Argument from Continuity in the Nervous System[^1]
> The objection that the [[Nervous System]] is not a discrete-state machine: a small error in the size of a nervous impulse can make a large difference to the outgoing impulse, so one cannot expect to mimic its behaviour with a discrete-state machine such as a digital computer.

# Properties
- One of the [[Objections to Machine Intelligence]].
- Turing's rebuttal: a discrete-state machine is indeed different from a continuous one, but under the conditions of the imitation game ([[Turing Test]]) the interrogator cannot exploit this difference, because a digital computer can approximate the outputs of a continuous machine (e.g. by giving suitably distributed answers) closely enough to be indistinguishable.

[^1]: [Turing, Computing Machinery and Intelligence, p. 15](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/PGZER5NA?page=15&annotation=EBRYT432)
