# Definition

Open tasks remain in our [[Short-Term Memory]] until they are considered done. A useful hack though is writing the task down to be done later because our brain will treat this as the task being "done", which will free up our limited short-term memory.[^1]

The effect can also be used deliberately: keep an unanswered question in mind while doing something undemanding (walking, showering, cleaning), and the brain keeps working on it, which is why answers often come in casual moments.[^2] See also [[Parkinson's Law]].

**Evidence.** A 2025 meta-analysis (Ghibellini and Meier) found no reliable memory advantage for unfinished over finished tasks. The related tendency to *resume* interrupted tasks (the Ovsiankina effect) held up better. So treat the claims above with caution.
