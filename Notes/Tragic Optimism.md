# Definition
> [!info] Tragic Optimism[^1]
> ([[Viktor Frankl|Frankl]]) Remaining optimistic in spite of the *tragic triad* of human existence, (1) pain, (2) guilt, and (3) death, by "saying yes to life in spite of everything". It presupposes that life is potentially meaningful under any conditions and that humans can creatively turn life's negative aspects into something positive, making the best of any situation.

# Properties
- The human potential at its best allows for:[^1]
	- turning **suffering** into a human achievement and accomplishment ([[Suffering]]);
	- deriving from **guilt** the opportunity to change oneself for the better;
	- deriving from life's **transitoriness** an incentive to take responsible action ([[Permanence of the Past]]).
- Optimism cannot be commanded or forced against all odds.[^2]
- **Guilt**: a crime cannot be fully explained by biological, psychological, or sociological factors; totally explaining it would explain away guilt and treat the person as a machine to be repaired; one is responsible for overcoming guilt by growing beyond oneself. Frankl rejects collective guilt.[^3]
- **Transitoriness**: each moment is dying and will never recur, a reminder to make the best use of each; hence the maxim "Live as if you were living already for the second time and as if you had acted the first time as wrongly as you are about to act now", which confronts one with life's finiteness and the finality of one's choices.[^4]
