# Definition
> [!info] Head Fake (Pedagogy)[^1]
> Pausch's term for indirect learning: students pursue an engaging surface goal while acquiring a deeper skill. Children playing football think they are learning football, but they also learn "teamwork, perseverance, sportsmanship, the value of hard work, an ability to deal with adversity." Students using Alice think they are making movies and games. They are actually learning to program.

# Properties
- **Evidence (Alice).** In a controlled study, middle-school girls using Storytelling Alice spent 42% more time programming than girls using a version without story features. They were about three times as likely to sneak extra time on it, and more interested in future programming (Kelleher, Pausch and Kiesler, 2007). Learning outcomes were similar. The gain was in motivation.
- **Evidence (sport as life skills).** Claims that sport teaches character transfer only when coaches teach the skills deliberately. Implicit "head fakes" through sport alone show weak transfer (Gould and Carson, 2008).
- **Mechanism.** The surface goal recruits [[Intrinsic Motivation]]. This is related to game-based learning and *interest-driven* learning. It does not guarantee transfer, which usually needs explicit connection.
- **Fundamentals.** "You've got to get the fundamentals down, because otherwise the fancy stuff is not going to work."[^2] See [[Deliberate Practice]].
- **The book itself.** Pausch says the whole lecture was a head fake. It was not about achieving dreams but about how to lead your life, and it was for his kids.[^3]
- Related: [[Productive Struggle]].
