# Definition

> [!info] Definition 1 (Arithmetic Sequence)[^1]
> A [[Sequence]] $\{a_n\}_{n=k}^{\infty}$ is arithmetic if there is a number $d$, the common difference, such that
> $$
> \begin{align}
> a_{n+1} = a_n + d \quad \text{for all } n \geq k
> \end{align}
> $$

> [!abstract] Theorem 2 (Explicit Formula)[^2]
> An arithmetic sequence with first term $a$ and common difference $d$ is
> $$
> \begin{align}
> a_n = a + (n - 1)d, \quad n \geq 1
> \end{align}
> $$

# Properties
- The explicit formula follows from the recursion by [[Proof by Induction]].
- $a_n$ is the restriction to $\mathbb{N}$ of a linear function of $n$ with slope $d$ ([[Linear Function Slope-Intercept Form]]): constant first differences.
- Sum of the first $n$ terms: [[Arithmetic Series]].
- Counterpart: [[Geometric Sequence]] (constant ratio instead of constant difference).
