# Definition
> [!info] Existential Psychotherapy[^1]
> (Yalom) A dynamic approach to therapy that focuses on concerns rooted in the individual's existence. Its dynamic conflict is not between suppressed instincts (Freud) or internalised significant adults (neo-Freudians), but flows from the individual's confrontation with the givens of existence, the [[Ultimate Concerns]]: death, freedom, isolation, and meaninglessness.

# Properties
- *Dynamic* psychotherapies are based on a model of mental functioning in which conscious and unconscious forces, motives, and fears operate within the individual.[^2]
- **Contrast with Freud**: the Freudian child is instinct-driven and at war with a world that prevents gratification; the neo-Freudian (interpersonal) child is shaped by the need for security and approval, and "growth is always compromised for the sake of security". The existential view instead treats the person primarily as a fearful, suffering being (Otto Rank).[^3]
- **"Deep" is not "early"**: for Freud, deepest conflict meant earliest conflict (archaeological excavation). Existential dynamics are not developmental: to explore deeply is to think about *that* one is rather than how one came to be. The future-becoming-present is the primary tense of existential therapy.[^4]
- European existential analysts objected to Freud's energy-conservation model, reductionism (few basic drives), materialism (explaining higher by lower), and determinism; Binswanger: "there are as many spaces and times as there are subjects."[^5]
- Challenges the Cartesian subject–object split: the person is *Dasein*, both a constituted object (empirical ego) and the constitutor of its world (transcendental ego), so studying all beings with one standard instrument introduces error; empirical research on existential issues is correspondingly hard.[^6]
- It is "a paradigm, not *the* paradigm": useful for some patients and therapists, with no exclusive hegemony.[^7]
- Clinical truths most therapists recognise: apprehending finiteness can catalyse a shift of perspective, "it is the relationship that heals", patients are tormented by choice, the therapist must catalyse the patient's will, and most patients suffer from a lack of meaning.[^8]
- Each concern contributes a segment of therapy: death → understanding anxiety and the [[Boundary Situation]]; freedom → [[Responsibility (Existential Psychology)|responsibility]], [[Will (Existential Psychology)|will]], and [[Decision (Existential Psychology)|decision]]; isolation → the role of relationship ([[Existential Isolation]]); meaninglessness → [[Engagement (Existential Psychotherapy)|engagement]].[^9]
- A therapist must have personally worked through these issues to help patients with them.[^10]
- Related: [[Existentialism]], [[Humanistic Therapy]], [[Logotherapy]], [[Psychotherapy]]. Review: [[Existential Psychotherapy (Yalom)]].
