# Definition
> [!info] Frustration Tolerance[^1]
> (Ellis, [[Rational Emotive Behavior Therapy|REBT]]) The ability to accept that one's desires or actions are frustrated: failure, setbacks, inconvenience. *Low frustration tolerance* ("I can't stand it") comes from rigid demands on oneself, life and other people. *High frustration tolerance* develops when these demands are relaxed into flexible preferences.

# Properties
- Frustration is predominantly self-imposed misery, so it is open to cognitive change.[^1]
- Ellis's irrational belief: "The idea that it is awful and catastrophic when things are not the way one would very much like them to be". The rational alternative: do your best to change objectionable circumstances, and when you can't, "become philosophically resigned to our fate and accept things the way they are" ([[Dichotomy of Control]]).[^1]
- **Stoic counterpart.** The sage wants only to do his best, no more and no less, and accepts success or failure with equal serenity, because he cares only about the quality of his actions, not their results. This is the archer analogy of the [[Reserve Clause]].[^2]
- **Evidence.** Discomfort intolerance is a distinct, measurable construct (Frustration Discomfort Scale; Harrington, 2005). It is associated with procrastination, anger and emotional problems independently of self-esteem-related beliefs.
- Related: [[Amor Fati]], [[Cognitive Distortion]] (awfulizing).
