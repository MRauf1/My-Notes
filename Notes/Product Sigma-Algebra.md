# Definition
> [!info] Product $\sigma$-Algebra[^1]
> Let $\{X_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in A}$ be an indexed collection of nonempty sets, $X = \prod_{\alpha \in A} X_\alpha$ their [[Cartesian Product]], and $\pi_\alpha : X \to X_\alpha$ the coordinate maps. If $\mathcal{M}_\alpha$ is a [[Sigma-Field|$\sigma$-algebra]] on $X_\alpha$ for each $\alpha$, the product $\sigma$-algebra on $X$ is the [[Generated Sigma-Algebra|$\sigma$-algebra generated]] by
> $$
> \begin{align}
> \left\{\pi_\alpha^{-1}(E_\alpha) : E_\alpha \in \mathcal{M}_\alpha,\ \alpha \in A\right\}.
> \end{align}
> $$
> It is denoted $\bigotimes_{\alpha \in A} \mathcal{M}_\alpha$, or $\bigotimes_1^n \mathcal{M}_j = \mathcal{M}_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes \mathcal{M}_n$ when $A = \{1, \dots, n\}$.

The generators $\pi_\alpha^{-1}(E_\alpha)$ are "cylinder sets" that constrain one coordinate and leave all others free. The product $\sigma$-algebra is the smallest $\sigma$-algebra making every coordinate map measurable.

# Properties
> [!abstract] Proposition 1.3[^2]
> If $A$ is countable, then $\bigotimes_{\alpha \in A} \mathcal{M}_\alpha$ is the $\sigma$-algebra generated by the measurable boxes $\left\{\prod_{\alpha \in A} E_\alpha : E_\alpha \in \mathcal{M}_\alpha\right\}$.

> [!abstract] Proposition 1.4 (Generators)[^3]
> Suppose $\mathcal{M}_\alpha$ is generated by $\mathcal{E}_\alpha$, $\alpha \in A$. Then $\bigotimes_{\alpha \in A} \mathcal{M}_\alpha$ is generated by
> $$
> \begin{align}
> \mathcal{F}_1 = \left\{\pi_\alpha^{-1}(E_\alpha) : E_\alpha \in \mathcal{E}_\alpha,\ \alpha \in A\right\}.
> \end{align}
> $$
> If $A$ is countable and $X_\alpha \in \mathcal{E}_\alpha$ for all $\alpha$, it is generated by $\mathcal{F}_2 = \left\{\prod_{\alpha \in A} E_\alpha : E_\alpha \in \mathcal{E}_\alpha\right\}$.

> [!abstract] Proposition 1.5 (Borel Sets of a Product)[^4]
> Let $X_1, \dots, X_n$ be [[Metric Space|metric spaces]] and $X = \prod_1^n X_j$ with the product metric. Then $\bigotimes_1^n \mathcal{B}_{X_j} \subseteq \mathcal{B}_X$. If the $X_j$ are [[Separable Metric Space|separable]], then $\bigotimes_1^n \mathcal{B}_{X_j} = \mathcal{B}_X$.

> [!abstract] Corollary 1.6[^5]
> $\mathcal{B}_{\mathbb{R}^n} = \bigotimes_1^n \mathcal{B}_\mathbb{R}$.

- Corollary 1.6 lets the [[Borel Sigma-Algebra]] of $\mathbb{R}^n$ be built from that of $\mathbb{R}$, which is how $n$-dimensional [[Lebesgue Measure]] is later obtained as a product measure.
