# Definition
> [!info] Overimitation[^1]
> The tendency of children to reproduce even visibly unnecessary actions of a demonstrator, which increases with age (from one to three and three to five) and persists even when children show they know which actions are causally irrelevant.

# Properties
- Not mindless but sophisticated: children model the demonstrator as a rational agent acting efficiently; if an adult who can see an action is useless performs it anyway, there must be a reason, so the child copies it too. Better theory of mind yields more overimitation.[^2]
- Sensitive to pedagogical intent: children copy "unnecessary" steps when the adult presents as an expert teacher, but only effective steps when the adult presents as unfamiliar with the task.[^3]
- Imitation is the starting point, not the culmination, of development: infants imitate and know when they are being imitated, and recognising self-other equivalence is the foundation of social cognition and of norms, values, ethics, and empathy (Meltzoff).[^4]
- Informs [[Imitation Learning]] and [[Inverse Reinforcement Learning]] in machines.
