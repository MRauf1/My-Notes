# Definition
> [!info] $\sigma$-Algebra Generated by a Family of Sets[^1]
> Let $\mathcal{E} \subseteq \mathcal{P}(X)$. The $\sigma$-algebra generated by $\mathcal{E}$ is the unique smallest [[Sigma-Field|$\sigma$-algebra]] containing $\mathcal{E}$,
> $$
> \begin{align}
> \mathcal{M}(\mathcal{E}) = \bigcap \{\mathcal{M} : \mathcal{M} \text{ is a } \sigma\text{-algebra on } X,\ \mathcal{E} \subseteq \mathcal{M}\}.
> \end{align}
> $$

It is well defined because an arbitrary intersection of $\sigma$-algebras is a $\sigma$-algebra, and the family being intersected is nonempty since it contains $\mathcal{P}(X)$.

# Properties
> [!abstract] Lemma 1.1[^2]
> If $\mathcal{E} \subseteq \mathcal{M}(\mathcal{F})$, then $\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{E}) \subseteq \mathcal{M}(\mathcal{F})$.

- Lemma 1.1 is the standard tool for showing two generating families give the same $\sigma$-algebra: show each family lies in the $\sigma$-algebra generated by the other (e.g. the generators of the [[Borel Sigma-Algebra]] on $\mathbb{R}$).
- $\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{E})$ can be built "from below" by transfinite iteration of countable unions and complements up to the first uncountable ordinal ([[Set of Countable Ordinals]]); see [[Borel Sigma-Algebra]].
- The $\sigma$-algebra generated by an [[Algebra of Sets|algebra]] is the domain to which a [[Premeasure]] extends ([[Carathéodory Extension Theorem]]).
