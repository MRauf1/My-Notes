# Definition
> [!info] Normal Science[^1]
> Research firmly based on an accepted [[Paradigm (Philosophy of Science)|paradigm]]: the "mopping-up" work of articulating the phenomena and theories the paradigm already supplies, which engages most scientists throughout their careers. It attempts to force nature into the preformed, relatively inflexible box the paradigm provides.

# Properties
- Normal science does not aim to call forth new phenomena or invent new theories; phenomena that do not fit the box are often not seen, and scientists are often intolerant of others' new theories.[^1]
- These restrictions are essential: by focusing on a small range of esoteric problems, the paradigm forces investigation of nature in otherwise unimaginable detail and depth; and a built-in mechanism relaxes them when the paradigm ceases to function.[^2]
- **Puzzle-solving**: outcomes are usually anticipated, often in uninteresting detail, but the way to achieve them is in doubt; success means achieving the anticipated in a new way, solving instrumental, conceptual, and mathematical puzzles. The individual's motivation is the challenge of solving a puzzle no one has solved, or solved so well.[^3]
- A *puzzle* requires not only an assured solution but rules limiting acceptable solutions and the steps to reach them.[^4]
- Results matter because they extend the scope and precision of the paradigm; a result outside the anticipated range is usually a research failure reflecting on the scientist, not nature. Normal science progresses rapidly because practitioners tackle problems only their own lack of ingenuity prevents them from solving.[^5]
- Normal science is not testing: its puzzles presuppose the paradigm's validity, so failure discredits only the scientist, not the theory; every puzzle could, from another viewpoint, be seen as a counterinstance.[^6]
- The more precise and far-reaching the paradigm, the more sensitive an indicator it is of [[Anomaly (Philosophy of Science)|anomaly]]; normal science thus prepares the way for its own change.[^7]
- Contrasted with extraordinary science during [[Crisis (Philosophy of Science)|crisis]] and [[Scientific Revolution|revolution]].
