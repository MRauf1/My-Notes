# Definition
> [!info] Relationship as Spiritual Practice (Tolle)[^1]
> The view that a relationship is there "to make you conscious instead of happy". Its conflicts bring unconscious patterns (of the [[Ego|ego]] and [[Pain-Body|pain-body]]) into the light, where presence can dissolve them.

# Properties
- **Addictive love.** Without presence, intimate relationships soon become love/hate relationships. The positive and negative cycles cannot be separated. Romantic love is so intensely sought because it seems to free us from fear, need, lack, and incompleteness. The partner becomes a single external center of identity, which leads to neediness, clinging, and fear of loss.[^2]
- When the pain comes back, the partner is perceived as its cause. The ego attacks or manipulates, hoping to make the partner a cover-up for the pain again.[^3]
- Avoiding relationships to avoid pain doesn't work, because the pain is there anyway.[^4]
- **Practice.** Be the silent watcher of your own patterns and roles. Accept the partner completely, without judging or trying to change them; this is the greatest catalyst for change and the end of codependency. Don't argue from identification with a mental position. When the partner is unconscious, give up all judgment and be "the knowing" rather than "the reaction". If you react, knowing that you reacted means nothing is lost. Give space to others and to yourself.[^5]
- Every crisis is both danger and opportunity, but the opportunity appears only once all the facts are acknowledged and accepted.[^6]
- If you cannot be at ease alone, you will seek a relationship to cover the unease, and it will resurface inside the relationship. In enlightenment, "you" and "yourself" merge, and the relationship with yourself disappears.[^7]
- On the level of form one may still lack the complementary male or female polarity while being complete within.[^8]
- Tolle's further claims (that women are closer to Being through the body, that men's main obstacle is the thinking mind and women's the pain-body) rest on gender essentialism, not evidence.[^9]
- "True communication is communion": the realization of oneness, which is love (cf. [[States of Being]], [[I-Thou Relationship]], [[Love]]).[^10]
