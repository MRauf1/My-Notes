# Definition
> [!info] Commitment Device[^1]
> A choice made in the present that restricts or penalizes your future options, locking in the desired behavior, e.g., Ulysses having his sailors tie him to the mast so that he could hear the Sirens without steering toward them (a "Ulysses pact"). It makes the bad behavior more work than the good one.

# Properties
- **Rationale.** Under [[Hyperbolic Discounting]], the present self can foresee the future self's preference reversal and bind it.
- **Evidence.**
	- A commitment savings account raised savings by 81% after one year relative to controls (Ashraf, Karlan and Yin, 2006).
	- A voluntary commitment contract for smokers, forfeiting a deposit after a failed urine test, raised verified six-month abstinence by about 3 percentage points (Giné, Karlan and Zinman, 2010).
- **Habit contract.** A written agreement stating your commitment to a habit and the punishment if you don't follow through, signed by one or two accountability partners. It adds an immediate social cost. Even an accountability partner alone helps, because we care what others think of us.[^2] Supported by evidence: publicly reporting progress strengthens the effect of [[Progress Monitoring]] (Harkin et al., 2016).
- **Make it immediately unsatisfying.** The more local, concrete and immediate a consequence, the more it influences behavior. A punishment must match the strength of the behavior and be reliably enforced.[^3]
- Related: [[Choice Architecture]], [[Law of Response]], [[Law of Reward]].
