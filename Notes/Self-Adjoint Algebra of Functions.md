# Definition

> [!info] Definition 1 (Self-Adjoint [[Algebra of Functions]])[^1]
> An [[Algebra of Functions|algebra]] $\mathscr{A}$ of complex functions is self-adjoint if for every $f \in \mathscr{A}$ its [[Complex Conjugate]] $\bar{f}$ also belongs to $\mathscr{A}$, where $\bar{f}$ is defined by $\bar{f}(x) = \overline{f(x)}$.

Self-adjointness guarantees that $\operatorname{Re} f = (f + \bar f)/2$ and $\operatorname{Im} f = (f - \bar f)/2i$ lie in $\mathscr{A}$, which reduces the complex case of the [[Stone-Weierstrass Theorem]] to the real case.

# Properties
- Extra condition required for the complex [[Stone-Weierstrass Theorem]].
