# Definition
> [!info] Curriculum Learning[^1]
> A form of [[Shaping (Machine Learning)|shaping]] in which a learner is trained on simplified versions of a problem first and on progressively harder versions afterward, rather than on the full problem from the outset.

# Properties
- Elman's recurrent network could not learn a complex grammar from full "adult" language, but succeeded when simple sentences were presented first, partially resembling children's incremental language acquisition.[^1]
- *Reverse curriculum*: a robot learned to fasten a washer by starting almost at the goal (washer nearly down the bolt) and backing the start state progressively further away until it could handle any initial configuration.[^2]
- Compare Turing's [[Child Machine]] and the education of an [[Unorganized Machine|unorganized machine]].
