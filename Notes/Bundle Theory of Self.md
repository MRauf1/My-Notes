# Definition
> [!info] Bundle Theory of Self (Hume)[^1]
> There is no unitary, persisting self over and above our experiences. Introspection finds only particular perceptions, and a person is "nothing but a bundle or collection of different perceptions, which succeed each other with an inconceivable rapidity, and are in perpetual flux and movement."

# Properties
- **Critique of the cogito.** Lichtenberg: from the fact that thinking is going on, Descartes was entitled only to "thinking is occurring", not to a unitary thinking agent "I" ("I think, therefore I am").[^1] Compare [[Mind Identification]] and [[Descartes Evil Demon Thought Experiment]].
- **Neuroscientific support.** There is no "centre in the brain where things do all come together" (Broks). Split-brain patients behave as if each hemisphere were an independent self, and the verbal left hemisphere invents reasons for actions initiated by the right ([[Confabulation]]).[^2]
- **Not eliminativism.** A bundle of perceptions is still real (Baggini); the claim is that the self is not the simple *thing* we take it to be, and is best seen as a useful fiction.[^2]
- Even thinking is less voluntary than we imagine; most of the time it just seems to happen.[^3]
- **Against "true self" claims.** Dismantling the conventional self does not imply finding a deeper "true Being" behind it (as Tolle suggests). Perhaps there is no deeper meaning to who we are.[^4]
- Parallels the Buddhist doctrine of [[Anatta]]; see also [[Ego]].
