# Definition
> [!info] Permanence of the Past[^1]
> ([[Viktor Frankl|Frankl]]) Only the potentialities of life are transitory; once actualised, they are "rescued" into the past, where "nothing is irretrievably lost but everything irrevocably stored". Hence "having been is also a kind of being, and perhaps the surest kind."

# Properties
- Transitoriness does not make existence meaningless but constitutes our responsibleness: at every moment we choose which potentialities are condemned to non-being and which become an immortal "footprint in the sands of time".[^2]
- People see only the "stubble field of transitoriness" and overlook the "full granaries of the past": deeds done, loves loved, and sufferings borne with courage.[^3]
- The active person is like one who files each calendar leaf with diary notes on the back and reflects with pride on the life already lived: "Instead of possibilities, I have realities in my past."[^4]
- There is no reason to pity the old; the young should envy them, for they have realities rather than possibilities.[^5]
- In the camps: "What you have experienced, no power on earth can take from you."[^6]
- Hence logotherapy is "not pessimistic but rather activistic"; cf. Yalom's discussion in [[Cosmic and Terrestrial Meaning]] and [[Tragic Optimism]].
