# Definition
> [!info] Conservation of Angular Momentum[^1]
> If no external [[Torque]] acts on a system of particles, its total [[Angular Momentum]] remains constant:
> $$
> \begin{align}
> \tau_{\text{ext}} = 0 \implies \frac{dL}{dt} = 0.
> \end{align}
> $$

# Properties
- Special case of the [[Torque-Angular Momentum Theorem]]; holds for any collection of particles, rigid or not. Rotational counterpart of [[Conservation of Momentum]].
- For a body rotating about a fixed axis, $L = I\omega$, so if its [[Moment of Inertia]] changes from $I_1$ to $I_2$ (e.g. by drawing mass toward the axis) its [[Angular Velocity]] must change so that $I_1\omega_1 = I_2\omega_2$; reducing $I$ increases $\omega$.
- Under a central force (no torque about the force center), it is equivalent to [[Kepler's Second Law]] of equal areas in equal times.

[^1]: [The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. I, Ch. 18: Rotation in Two Dimensions](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_18.html)
