# Definition
> [!info] Kayak and Superyacht (Burkeman)[^1]
> To be human is to sit in a one-person kayak carried along the river of time toward an inevitable but unpredictable death. You are at the mercy of the current and can only stay alert and steer as wisely as possible. The "superyacht" is the fantasy of a controlled, systematized life in which you have become "the kind of person who does that sort of thing".

# Properties
- **Thrownness.** This is Heidegger's *Geworfenheit*: to exist is to be thrown into a time, place, and personality you didn't choose, with time flowing away beneath you.[^1]
- **Just doing something.** The key skill is the willingness to do something here and now as a one-off, whether or not it belongs to a system, habit, or routine. Otherwise you fall into the counterproductive project of *becoming the kind of person who does it* (e.g., trying to "become a meditator" rather than sitting down to meditate once, for five minutes).[^2]
- Doing one meaningful thing today means giving up a sense of control: not knowing whether you'll do it well (you'll do it imperfectly) or whether it will become a habit. It is an act of faith.[^3]
- Without this, you become "the kind of person who spends your life drawing up plans for how you're going to become a different kind of person later on".[^4]
- Do things because what you bring into reality is worth bringing into reality for itself, not only to become a better person.[^5]
- "Like it or not, you're always in charge of the kayak."[^6]
- Contrast the identity-based habit approach of [[Atomic Habits (Clear)]]; see [[Imperfectionism]].
