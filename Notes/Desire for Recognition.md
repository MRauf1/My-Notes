# Definition
> [!info] Desire for Recognition (Adlerian Psychology)[^1]
> The wish to be acknowledged and positively judged by others, as a way of feeling valuable and erasing the [[Feeling of Inferiority]]. [[Adlerian Psychology]] rejects it: one must not seek recognition, because seeking it means living other people's lives.

# Properties
- **Origin.** It often comes from reward-and-punishment education. This produces mistaken lifestyles: "If no one praises me, I won't act appropriately" and "If no one punishes me, I'll act inappropriately."[^2]
- **We do not live to satisfy others' expectations.** It follows that others do not live to satisfy ours.[^3]
- **Really a wish not to be disliked.** Never being disliked requires constantly gauging everyone's feelings and swearing loyalty to all of them. That is lying to oneself and to others.[^4]
- **Self-centered.** People obsessed with recognition seem to look at others but look only at themselves: how much attention and approval they get. The remedy is to switch from "attachment to self" to "concern for others" ([[Community Feeling]]).[^5]
- **Recognition is a shortcut to a feeling of contribution.** People want to like themselves and feel worth, which requires a [[Feeling of Contribution]]. Recognition is an easy but unfree way to get it. Someone with a real feeling of contribution no longer needs recognition.[^6]
- Remedy: [[Separation of Tasks]], since how others judge you is their task. See also [[Freedom (Adlerian Psychology)]].
- Related: [[Contingencies of Self-Worth]], [[Extrinsic Motivation]], [[Recognition]].
- **No end to approval.** Worth gained from approval is fleeting and granted from outside, like clockwork dolls that move only when wound up. Someone happy only when praised stays dependent and seeks praise until death. One must approve oneself: "Your worth is not decided by someone else."[^kk1]
