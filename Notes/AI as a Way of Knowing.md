# Definition
> [!info] AI as a Way of Knowing[^1]
> The thesis that, to the two traditional ways by which people have known the world, faith and reason, AI adds a third: AI accesses reality differently from humans and may access different aspects of reality, detecting relationships that humans have not perceived, or perhaps cannot perceive or describe.

# Properties
- Motivating cases:[^2]
	- AlphaZero, given only the rules of chess and trained for four hours by self-play ([[Reinforcement Learning]]) with no human strategies, became the strongest chess program, showing AI no longer constrained by established human knowledge.
	- An MIT model trained on antibacterial molecules learned attributes that had eluded human conception, and found halicin, a novel, predicted non-toxic antibiotic unlike existing ones, from 61,000 candidates, without anyone being able to articulate precisely why it works.
	- DeepMind's data-centre cooling AI reduced energy use by a further 40% beyond expert engineers; AlphaFold more than doubled protein-folding accuracy (about 40% to 85%).
- Raises the question of whether there is a form of logic humans have not or cannot achieve: when a system learns a model no human recognises or understands, are we advancing toward knowledge, or is knowledge receding from us?[^3]
- In Kantian terms, human reason knows reality only through the filter of cognition and experience, never the [[Thing-in-Itself|thing-in-itself]]; AI begins to offer an alternative means of accessing, and thus understanding, reality, portending progress toward "the essence of things".[^4]
- Humans and AI might approach the same reality from complementary standpoints, or perceive two partially overlapping realities, one elaborated by reason and one by algorithms. In the latter case, certain knowledge might require entrusting AI to acquire it and report back, and AI will increasingly appear as a fellow "being" knowing the world, "a combination of tool, pet, and mind".[^5]
- Extends a lineage of challenges to reason: the Romantics held subjective emotion to be a form of truth, the postmoderns questioned discerning objective reality through subjective experience, and Gnostic ideas of an inner reality beyond ordinary experience may become newly significant.[^6]
- **Data-driven science**: models are increasingly derived not from theoretical understanding but from AI drawing conclusions from experimental results, requiring expertise in which data, and which representation of it, will train a useful model (cf. the fourth of the [[Paradigms of Scientific Discovery]], [[Representation (Machine Learning)]]).[^7]
- Unlike classical algorithms, which consist of steps producing precise results, [[Machine Learning|machine-learning]] algorithms consist of steps for improving upon imprecise results.[^8]
- Since Turing and McCarthy, intelligence has been defined by performance, the manifestation rather than the mechanism ([[Turing Test]]).[^9]
- AI cannot reflect on what it discovers or feel moral compulsion to do so; humans must interpret its insights, integrate them into knowledge, and regulate it ([[Human-Machine Partnership]]).[^10]
- Echoes the [[Bitter Lesson]]: systems free of human-supplied knowledge discover strategies beyond it.
