# Definition
> [!info] Definition 1 (Infinite-Dimensional [[Linear Operator]])[^1]
> A linear operator $T: V \rightarrow W$ is infinite-dimensional if $V$ or $W$ is an [[Infinite-Dimensional Vector Space]], such as a [[Function Space]]. Unlike a [[Finite-Dimensional Linear Operator]], $T$ generally cannot be represented by a [[Matrix]] once a basis is chosen.

# Example
> [!example]- Derivative and Integral Operators
> The derivative operator $D = \frac{d}{dx}$ acting on the [[Vector Space]] of polynomials is a linear operator that is not a finite matrix.
> The integral operator $T(f) = \int_0^1 f(x) \, dx$ is a linear operator that cannot be represented as a matrix.

[^1]: [Why is there no matrix representation of a linear operator?](https://math.stackexchange.com/questions/2761887/why-is-there-no-matrix-representation-of-a-linear-operator)
