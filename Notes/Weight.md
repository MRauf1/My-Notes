# Definition
> [!info] Weight[^1]
> The gravitational [[Force]] exerted on an object near a massive body's surface:
> $$
> \begin{align}
> W = mg
> \end{align}
> $$
> where $m$ is the object's [[Mass]] and $g$ is the local acceleration of gravity.

# Properties
- Distinct from [[Mass]]: mass measures an object's inertia (its resistance to acceleration by a given force) and is the same everywhere, while weight is a force that depends on the local strength of gravity — an object's weight would differ on Mars even though its mass, and the force needed to overcome its inertia, would not.
- Mass and weight are proportional to one another at a given location (both scale with $m$), which is why, on Earth's surface, the two are often numerically conflated; this proportionality is itself a manifestation of the [[Equivalence Principle]].
- A special case of [[Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation]], $F = GmM/R^2$, evaluated near a body's surface where $R$ is nearly constant, giving $g = GM/R^2$.

[^1]: [The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. I, Ch. 9: Newton's Laws of Dynamics](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_09.html)
