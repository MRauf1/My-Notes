# Definition
> [!info] Three Unwholesome Roots[^1]
> Greed, hatred, and delusion: the roots of all [[Skillful and Unskillful Thoughts|unskillful]] mental states. In everyday perception they appear as three mental habits, depending on whether an experience is filed as good, bad, or neutral:
> - **Grasping** (greed) — a perception labeled "good" makes us try to freeze time and hold on to it.
> - **Rejecting** (hatred, aversion) — a perception labeled "bad" makes us try to push it away.
> - **Ignoring** (delusion) — a perception labeled neutral is robbed of its fair share of attention.

# Properties
- Together they form a "perpetual treadmill race to nowhere": endlessly chasing pleasure, fleeing pain, and ignoring 90% of our experience. The result is [[Dukkha]].[^2]
- **Scope.** Greed ranges from avarice to a subtle need to be respected as a moral person. Hatred ranges from petty peevishness to murderous rage. Delusion ranges from daydreaming to hallucination.[^3]
- The grasping/rejecting reaction is the first link of the worry chain: anxiety is a procedure, not just a state.[^4]
- A mechanism deep in the mind accepts what it perceives as pleasant and rejects what it perceives as painful. This is where the [[Five Hindrances]] come from.[^5]
- [[Mindfulness]] is the alternative: impartial watchfulness that neither grasps, rejects, nor ignores.[^6]
- Positive attachments (patriotism, parental protectiveness, love) hold you "in the mud" just as surely as negative ones.[^7]
