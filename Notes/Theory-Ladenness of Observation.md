# Definition
> [!info] Theory-Ladenness of Observation[^1]
> The thesis that observation is not fixed and neutral but shaped by prior conceptual commitments: "what a man sees depends both upon what he looks at and also upon what his previous visual-conceptual experience has taught him to see", so something like a paradigm is prerequisite to [[Perception|perception]] itself.

# Properties
- Challenges the three-century Western epistemology holding sensory experience fixed and neutral and theories mere interpretations of given data; Kuhn could not fully relinquish it but judged attempts to rescue it with a neutral observation language hopeless.[^2]
- Laboratory operations and measurements are not "the given" but "the collected with difficulty": they are selected because they promise fruitful elaboration of an accepted paradigm, so they are paradigm-determined, and scientists with different paradigms perform different manipulations.[^3]
- Theories do not evolve piecemeal to fit pre-existing facts; they emerge together with the facts they fit, transforming previously accessible information into facts that did not exist for the preceding paradigm.[^4]
- Underpins [[Incommensurability]]; related to [[Underdetermination of Theory by Evidence]].
