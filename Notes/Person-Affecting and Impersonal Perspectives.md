# Definition
> [!info] Person-Affecting and Impersonal Perspectives[^1]
> Two normative stances for evaluating a policy. The *person-affecting perspective* asks whether a change is in "our interest", i.e. of morally considerable beings who exist or will exist independently of the change. The *impersonal perspective* gives no special consideration to such beings, counting everybody equally regardless of temporal location, and sees great value in bringing into existence new people with lives worth living.

# Properties
- The two perspectives can disagree about the desirable speed of AI development: the person-affecting view weighs the benefits to currently existing people (e.g. extended lives), while the impersonal view weighs the astronomical number of future lives at stake in existential risk.
- Relevant to [[Differential Technological Development]] and to trade-offs in [[Indirect Normativity]] such as the MR proposal.
- Related to [[Utilitarianism]] in population ethics.

[^1]: [Bostrom, 2014, p. 228](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/9ECZCLTQ?page=245&annotation=VTQ4KQZ8)
