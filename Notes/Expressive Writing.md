# Definition
> [!info] Expressive Writing (Pennebaker)[^1]
> Writing about your deepest thoughts and feelings concerning emotional or traumatic events, typically for 15–20 minutes over 3–4 sessions (Pennebaker and Beall, 1986). "Translating emotional events into words leads to profound social, psychological, and neural changes." (Pennebaker, 1997)

# Properties
- **Evidence.** Meta-analyses find real but modest benefits for physical and psychological health. Smyth (1998) reported $d \approx 0.47$. A larger meta-analysis of 146 studies found a smaller effect of $r \approx 0.075$ (Frattaroli, 2006), and later studies tend to show smaller effects than early ones.
- It illustrates that writing doesn't only record thinking but also enriches it ([[Writing]]).[^1]
- Goggins' first exercise is a version of this: write out your story and your pain in full, minute detail, then use it as fuel.[^2]
- Related: [[Emotion]], [[Generation Effect]], [[Adverse Childhood Experiences]].
