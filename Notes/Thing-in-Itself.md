# Definition
> [!info] Thing-in-Itself[^1]
> In Kant's philosophy, objective reality in the strictest sense: reality as it is independent of experience and of filtration through human concepts. It is ever-present but inherently beyond direct human knowledge, since human cognition and experience filter, structure, and distort all that we know, even when reasoning "purely" by logic.

# Properties
- Kant posited a realm of *noumena*, "things as they are understood by pure thought", existing independently of experience; because the human mind relies on conceptual thinking and lived experience, it can never achieve the pure thought needed to know this inner essence.[^1]
- At best we can consider how our minds reflect such a realm; we may hold beliefs about it, but these do not constitute knowledge of it.[^1]
- Contrasted with phenomena, reality as it appears to us; a central idea of Kantian [[Epistemology|epistemology]].
- Kissinger et al. suggest AI may provide an alternative, non-human route toward the essence of things ([[AI as a Way of Knowing]]).[^2]
