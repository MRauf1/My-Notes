# Definition
> [!info] Existential Guilt[^1]
> Guilt for transgression against oneself: the failure to live the life allotted to one and to fulfil one's potential. Each person has an innate set of potentials and a primordial knowledge of them, and one who fails to live as fully as one can feels existential guilt. It issues from omission, not from a committed wrong.

# Properties
- Rank: "we feel ourselves guilty on account of the unused life, the unlived life in us"; repression brings safety but also life restriction and this guilt.[^1]
- Full acceptance of responsibility broadens guilt by removing escape hatches; for Heidegger "being guilty" means being responsible for, so guilt is fundamental to *Dasein* and proportional to failure to fulfil authentic possibility, heard as the "call of conscience".[^2]
- Tillich: man's being is also demanded of him, and he is his own judge; Rabbi Susya: "they will ask 'Why were you not Susya?'"[^3]
- May: the unconscious is the potentialities one cannot or will not actualise; guilt is "a perception of the difference between what a thing is and what it ought to be".[^4]
- Horney: under adverse conditions the child abandons its potential self for an *idealized self*, and the discrepancy breeds self-contempt; Maslow: denying one's inner core makes one sick and despise oneself (deficiency vs growth motivation, see [[Deficiency and Growth Motivation]]).[^5]
- It is a positive, constructive guide "calling oneself back to oneself", compatible with and even necessary for mental health.[^6]
- Dealt with only by atonement: "one cannot will backward"; one atones for the past only by altering the future, and must first forgive oneself for the present and future.[^7]
- Related: [[Authenticity (Existentialism)]], [[Death Anxiety]] (unlived life heightens it).
