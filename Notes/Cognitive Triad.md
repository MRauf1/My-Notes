# Definition
> [!info] Cognitive Triad[^1]
> (Beck) The pattern of negative cognition characteristic of depression, which concerns three dimensions: the **self**, the **world** and the **future**.

# Properties
- Ellis claimed an earlier, similar distinction (late 1950s): REBT's three major categories of irrational belief concern the **self**, **life** (the world) and **others** ([[Rational Emotive Behavior Therapy]]).[^1]
- **Stoic correlation (Robertson).** The three fields of Stoic philosophy ([[Divisions of Philosophy (Stoicism)]], [[Three Disciplines (Epictetus)]]) map roughly onto these dimensions:[^2]
	- *Physics* (discipline of fear and desire): the world/life, seeing external events and others' opinions as transient.
	- *Ethics* (discipline of action): others and society; compare social skills, communication and assertiveness training in CBT.
	- *Logic* (discipline of assent): the self's own judgements.
- Target of [[Cognitive Therapy]]; depressive cognitions are a cluster of [[Cognitive Distortion|cognitive distortions]].
- **Evidence.** Negative views of self, world and future reliably accompany depression. Support for their *causal* role as a pre-existing vulnerability is weaker and more mixed (Haaga, Dyck & Ernst, 1991).
