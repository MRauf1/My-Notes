# Definition
> [!info] Distraction (Meditation)[^1]
> In [[Vipassana Meditation]], any mental state or preoccupation that pulls attention off the [[Breath as Meditation Object|breath]]. From the point of view of [[Mindfulness|mindfulness]] there is really no such thing as a distraction: every distraction becomes a secondary, temporary object of meditation.

# Properties
- **Handling.** When a mental state arises strongly enough to pull you away, switch attention to it briefly. Wordlessly note what it is, how strong it is, and how long it lasts. Then return to the breath. Beginners may need words for this; with practice it becomes a wordless habit.[^2]
- **Why it works.** A mental state arises first in the [[Unconsciousness|unconscious]], and grasping happens there almost instantly. By the time we consciously notice a distraction, we are already stuck in it and *thinking the thought*. Stepping back to inspect it ends the grasping and restores detached awareness.[^3]
- **Aware of a thought vs. thinking a thought.** A thought held in bare attention feels light, like a bubble, with distance between it and the awareness. It passes without triggering the next thought. Ordinary conscious thought is heavy, compulsive, and obsessional. It takes over attention, chains straight into the next thought, and creates tension in the body.[^4]
- Fighting a distraction feeds it energy and makes it stronger. Bare attention weakens it, and repeated observation makes it fade for good. Deep-seated habitual thought patterns may take years.[^5]
- Do not look for distractions. Stay with the breath until something pulls you away, then let attention flow to it until it evaporates.[^6]
- Realizing that you have been distracted is itself an act of mindfulness. Mindfulness grows by being exercised, like a muscle, so no self-condemnation is needed. If condemnation arises, treat it as one more distraction.[^7]
- Emptying the mind matters less than being mindful of what the mind is doing. The mind's chatter is one more thing to be mindful of.[^8]
- Repressed material in the unconscious can surface as restlessness or fear for no apparent reason. Sit through it and watch it as an interested bystander. Once it has run its course in conscious attention, it won't sink back to haunt you.[^9]
- Emotional states (fear, boredom, discouragement, a sense of failure) are mental states like any other. Observe them mindfully and they pass. There is no failure in meditation unless you give up entirely.[^10]
- The recurring categories of distraction are the [[Five Hindrances]].
