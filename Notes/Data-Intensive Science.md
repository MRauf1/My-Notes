# Definition
> [!info] Data-Intensive Science[^1]
> The fourth of the [[Paradigms of Scientific Discovery]], in which scientific knowledge is obtained by exploring massive datasets, consisting of three basic activities: *capture*, *curation*, and *analysis* (with visualisation).

# Properties
- Science often lags the commercial world in inferring meaning from data and acting on it, since scientific disciplines are not easily summarised in a few numbers and most scientific data lacks enough economic value to fuel rapid tool development.[^2]
- Gray foresaw data living forever as archival media, publicly accessible in the cloud to humans and machines ([[Cloud Computing]]).[^3]
- **Data iceberg**: much data is collected but never curated or published systematically; the published literature is only its tip.[^4]
- Scientific data management and analysis tools are "truly dreadful": software cost dominates capital expenditure of projects, and progress on simulation tools has outpaced analysis tools, which computer scientists could fix with generic tools such as a [[Laboratory Information Management System]].[^5]
- As datasets grow beyond what can be FTP'd or grepped, one needs indices and parallel access, and it becomes more efficient to move queries to the data than the data to the user ([[Gray's Laws]]).[^6]
- Data-intensive computing is more than storing and moving large volumes: dataset complexity and diverse data flows make the traditional compute/data-centre model obsolete. IT has commoditised generic storage and computing and radically personalised research.[^7]
- The research enterprise will come to resemble an ecosystem: loose networks of services, rapid innovation at the edges, closer partnership between knowledge creators and users, and emergent behaviour, challenging agencies and universities.[^8]
- Enabled by [[Scientific Workflow|scientific workflows]], [[Open Scholarly Communication|open scholarly communication]], and [[Citizen Science|citizen science]]; applied in [[Applications Science]], [[Data-Intensive Healthcare]], [[Connectomics]], and [[Algorithmic Systems Biology]].
- Fundamental advances in visualisation are needed to extract meaning from petascale and exascale datasets.[^9]
