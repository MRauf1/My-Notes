# Definition
> [!info] Enlightenment (Tolle)[^1]
> Regaining awareness of [[Being (Spirituality)|Being]] and abiding in that state of "feeling-realization". It is one's natural state of felt oneness with Being: a state of wholeness, being "at one", and therefore at peace. Tolle endorses the Buddha's definition of enlightenment as "the end of suffering".

# Properties
- The Buddha used a *negative* definition so that the mind cannot turn enlightenment into something to believe in, or into a superhuman goal that seems impossible to reach.[^2]
- It is the end of suffering, of continuous inner and outer conflict, and of enslavement to incessant thinking ([[Mind Identification]]).[^3]
- It means rising *above* thought, not falling back to the pre-thought level of an animal or plant. The thinking mind is still used when needed, more effectively than before, but without the involuntary inner dialogue.[^4]
- It cannot be "achieved" in the future. The desire to become enlightened is itself just another craving for fulfillment in the future ([[Psychological Time]]). One "gets there" by realizing one is already there.[^5]
- It brings freedom from the "illusion of the self" (the Buddha's phrase): the belief that you are nothing more than your body and mind. With it goes freedom from fear and from "sin", meaning the suffering you unconsciously inflict on yourself and others.[^6]
- Test: if there is no emanation of love and joy, full presence, and openness toward all beings, it is not enlightenment.[^7]
- A temporary taste of it is *satori* (Zen): a flash of insight, a moment of no-mind and total presence, but not a lasting transformation.[^8]
- What is needed is a *permanent* shift in consciousness. Intellectual agreement is just another belief; the truth has to be lived.[^9]
- Buddhist counterpart: [[Nibbana]].
