# Definition
> [!info] Centripetal Acceleration[^1]
> An object moving at [[Speed]] $v$ along a circular path of radius $R$ has an acceleration of magnitude
> $$
> \begin{align}
> a = \frac{v^2}{R}
> \end{align}
> $$
> directed at right angles to its velocity, toward the center of the circle.

# Properties
- Changes only the direction of the velocity, not its [[Speed]]: a [[Force]] directed at right angles to an object's velocity causes it to move along a curved path without speeding it up or slowing it down.
- The radius of curvature of the path followed under a given sideways force can be found by using [[Newton's Second Law]] to get this acceleration from the force and [[Mass]], then solving for $R$.
- Underlies the derivation, via [[Newton's Cannonball]], of the orbital speed at which a projectile's circular fall matches a planet's surface curvature.

[^1]: [The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. I, Ch. 9: Newton's Laws of Dynamics](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_09.html)
