# Definition

Measure of matter within an object.

Within [[Mechanics]], can be viewed as a quantitative measure of the resistance of a body to [[Acceleration]] by a given [[Force]].

# Standard of Mass

The kilogram is the mass of a [[Platinum|platinum]]-[[Iridium|iridium]] [[Cylinder|cylinder]] kept at the International Bureau of Weights and Measures.[^1]

# Properties
- Under special relativity, mass refers exclusively to invariant (rest) mass: every observer, in every inertial frame, measures the same value for a given particle's mass, even though its energy and [[Relativistic Momentum|momentum]] are frame-dependent. The older notion of "relativistic mass" ($\gamma m$, growing without bound as $v \to c$) is no longer used, since the quantity that actually diverges as $v\to c$ is momentum and energy, not mass itself.
- A particle can be at rest only if it has nonzero mass; a massless particle (e.g. a [[Photon]]) can never be at rest and must always travel at the speed of light in every reference frame.
- Distinguished from [[Weight]]: mass measures inertia and is the same everywhere, whereas weight ($W=mg$) is the force exerted on that mass by gravity and depends on the local gravitational field.
- Combined with [[Velocity Vector|velocity]], forms [[Momentum]] ($\mathbf{p}=m\mathbf{v}$), the quantity whose rate of change [[Newton's Second Law]] relates to force.
