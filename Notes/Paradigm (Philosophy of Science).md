# Definition
> [!info] Paradigm (Philosophy of Science)[^1]
> In Kuhn's account, a scientific achievement (theory, laws, applications, and instrumentation) that is accepted by a scientific community because it seems better than its competitors at solving a few problems the community recognises as acute, and that thereby defines the legitimate problems, methods, and standards of solution for subsequent research.

# Properties
- A paradigm need not, and never does, explain all the facts it confronts; at first it is very limited in scope and precision, and "more successful" does not mean completely successful with one problem or notably successful with many.[^1]
- Like an accepted judicial decision in common law, a paradigm is an object for further articulation under new or more stringent conditions, the work of [[Normal Science]].[^2]
- It directs research: it suggests which experiments are worth performing, making fact collection and theory articulation highly directed and efficient, evidence for Bacon's dictum "Truth emerges more readily from error than from confusion."[^3]
- It supplies a criterion for choosing problems assumed to have solutions; other problems are dismissed as metaphysical, as belonging to another discipline, or as too problematic, and socially important problems not reducible to puzzle form can be excluded.[^4]
- Paradigm procedures and instruments are as necessary as paradigm laws and theories and have the same effect: they restrict the phenomena accessible at any time, since choosing an apparatus assumes only certain circumstances will arise (*instrumental* as well as theoretical expectations).[^5]
- Paradigms need not be shared as broadly as explicit rules; science as a whole is a "ramshackle structure" of many paradigms with little coherence among them.[^6]
- Once a first paradigm exists, there is no research in the absence of any paradigm: rejecting one without substituting another is rejecting science itself ("the carpenter who blames his tools").[^7]
- Emerges from [[Pre-Paradigm Science]]; replaced through [[Scientific Revolution|scientific revolutions]].
- Distinct from, though related to, the methodological [[Paradigms of Scientific Discovery]].
