# Definition
> [!info] Kinetic Theory of Pressure[^1]
> The pressure exerted by a gas arises from the aggregate force of molecular collisions against a confining surface. For a piston of area $A$ held by pressure $P$, the force is $F = PA$, so $P$ is independent of area at fixed molecular density. At fixed temperature (fixed molecular speed), pressure is proportional to the number density $n$ of molecules, $P \propto n$; at fixed density, pressure is proportional to temperature, $P \propto T$, since faster molecules strike both harder and more often.

# Properties
- Follows from the [[Atomic Hypothesis]] together with the [[Kinetic Theory of Heat]].
- The proportionalities $P \propto n$ and $P \propto T$ are close approximations that hold well at low density; intermolecular attraction slightly lowers the pressure, while the finite volume occupied by the molecules slightly raises it.
- Implies [[Adiabatic Compression and Expansion]]: slowly compressing a gas raises its temperature, and slowly expanding it lowers its temperature.
