# Definition
> [!info] Hubble's Law
> $$v = H_0 d$$
> The recession velocity $v$ of a distant galaxy due to the [[Expansion of the Universe]] is proportional to its distance $d$ from the observer, where $H_0$ is the Hubble constant.

# Properties
- Consistent with the universe having no center: every observer, in every galaxy, sees all sufficiently distant galaxies receding, with recession velocity increasing linearly with distance.
- Beyond the Hubble radius ($d > c/H_0$, roughly $14$ billion light-years), the recession velocity predicted by this law exceeds the speed of light $c$; this does not violate special relativity, since it reflects the growth rate of space itself rather than the motion of any object through space.
