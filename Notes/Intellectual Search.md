# Definition
> [!info] Intellectual Search[^1]
> A search carried out by a brain for combinations with particular properties, e.g. searching through values of a variable, or through all proofs in a formal system, for one that solves a given problem.

# Types
- Intellectual search - searches by brains for combinations with particular properties.[^1]
- Genetical (evolutionary) search - the search for a combination of genes, with survival value as the criterion; its remarkable success partly confirms that intellectual activity consists mainly of various kinds of search.[^1]
- Cultural search - the search for new techniques carried out by the human community as a whole rather than by individuals: an isolated person develops no intellectual power, but absorbs the techniques of others, may add a few discoveries, and passes these on.[^1]

# Properties
- Solving a problem by searching through proofs in a formal system (like *Principia Mathematica*) converts the original problem into another of the same form: instead of searching through values of the original variable, one searches through values of something else. In practice one applies complex "transformations" of the problem, mixing searches over variables analogous to the original with something like a search through all proofs.[^1]
- Turing predicted that research into machine intelligence would be very greatly concerned with such searches, an early statement of search as a core technique of [[Artificial Intelligence]], later echoed in the [[Bitter Lesson]].[^1]
- Evolutionary search is the inspiration behind evolutionary algorithms such as the [[Evolution Strategy]].
- Search supplies the initiative component of [[Discipline and Initiative]].
