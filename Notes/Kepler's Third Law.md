# Definition
> [!info] Kepler's Third Law (Harmonic Law)[^1]
> For any two planets, the squares of their orbital periods are proportional to the cubes of the semimajor axes of their orbits:
> $$
> \begin{align}
> T^2 \propto a^3
> \end{align}
> $$
> where $T$ is the orbital period and $a$ is the semimajor axis of the elliptical orbit; equivalently $T \propto a^{3/2}$. For a nearly circular orbit, $a$ reduces to the orbital radius.

# Properties
- Unlike [[Kepler's First Law|Kepler's first]] and [[Kepler's Second Law|second laws]], which each describe a single planet's orbit, the third law relates the orbits of different planets to one another.
- Derivable from [[Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation]] together with [[Newton's Second Law]].

[^1]: [The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. I, Ch. 7: The Theory of Gravitation](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_07.html)
