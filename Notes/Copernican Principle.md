# Definition
> [!info] Copernican Principle[^1]
> The philosophical viewpoint that the Earth is just one planet among countless others in the universe, receiving no preferential treatment whatsoever from the fundamental laws of nature.

# Properties
- There is no contradiction between the universe's disinterest in Earth and our own strong investment in it: we may justifiably prioritise Earth's issues while accepting that other planets exist and matter comparably to their own inhabitants.[^2]
- Analogous dethronings occurred elsewhere: the Darwinian revolution removed the unique status of humans among evolving species, and non-Euclidean geometry removed Euclidean geometry's privileged role as a source of synthetic [[A Priori Knowledge|a priori]] truth.[^3]
- **Copernican view of intelligence**: humanity has held an intellectual analogue of geocentrism, with human intelligence at the centre of the cognitive universe. With other "planets" of intelligence now being discovered or created, one can accept human and artificial intelligence as belonging to the same ontological category, with distinctive differences and complementarities, rather than denying their importance or arguing over which is the "centre". Our attachments remain with the human sphere, but its relationship with other intelligences can be explored both practically and to gain an external perspective on human cognition.[^4]
- Offers the middle ground among the [[Philosophical Stances on Artificial Intelligence]].
