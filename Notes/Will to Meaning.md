# Definition
> [!info] Will to Meaning[^1]
> ([[Viktor Frankl|Frankl]]) The striving to find a meaning in one's life, which [[Logotherapy]] holds to be the primary motivational force in humans, not a "secondary rationalization" of instinctual drives. It contrasts with Freud's *will to pleasure* and Adler's *will to power*.

# Properties
- The meaning is unique and specific: it must and can be fulfilled by the person alone, and only then satisfies their will to meaning.[^1]
- Humans can live and even die for their ideals and values; in one poll 89% agreed that man needs "something" to live for and 61% had something or someone they would die for; 78% of surveyed students named "finding a purpose and meaning to my life" as their first goal, against 16% "making a lot of money".[^2]
- Values are occasionally a camouflage for inner conflicts (pseudovalues to be unmasked), but unmasking must stop at what is genuine; otherwise the "unmasking psychologist" reveals only their own need to depreciate the genuinely human.[^3]
- When frustrated, it may be vicariously compensated by a will to power or a will to pleasure; its frustration is the root of [[Noogenic Neurosis]] and the [[Existential Vacuum]].[^4]
- Freud stresses frustration in sexual life; Frankl, frustration in the will to meaning.[^5]
- It is fulfilled through the [[Avenues to Meaning (Frankl)|three avenues to meaning]] and generates healthy [[Noo-Dynamics|noö-dynamic]] tension.
