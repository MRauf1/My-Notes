# Definition
> [!info] Crucial Consideration[^1]
> An idea or argument with the potential to change our views not merely about the fine structure of implementation but about the general topology of desirability; even one missed crucial consideration could render our efforts futile or actively harmful, like a soldier fighting on the wrong side.

# Properties
- The search for crucial considerations constitutes *strategic analysis*.[^1]
- **Value of discovery**: a discovery moves the arrival of information earlier in time, so its value is the value of having it *sooner*, not the value of the information itself. If a problem would soon have been solved anyway, solving it first benefits the world little, unless the solution is immediately deployed or enables important and urgent further work. This temporal transport should be compared with what a world-class mind could achieve elsewhere; "the Fields Medal might indicate a life spent solving the wrong problem".[^2]
- Philosophical progress may be maximised indirectly (e.g. via superintelligence) rather than by immediate philosophising, so one should prioritise problems that are both important and *urgent*, i.e. needed before the intelligence explosion, and *elastic*, i.e. much more solvable per unit of extra effort.[^3]
- Guides research prioritisation on the [[AI Control Problem]].
