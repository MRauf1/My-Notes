# Definition

> [!info] Definition 1 (Equilibrium Solution)[^1]
> An equilibrium solution of a [[First Order Ordinary Differential Equation]] $y' = f(t, y)$ is a constant solution $y(t) \equiv c$. For an autonomous equation $y' = f(y)$, these are exactly the zeros $f(c) = 0$.

# Properties
- In a [[Direction Field]] they appear as horizontal lines of zero slope. For autonomous equations, the sign of $f(y)$ between consecutive zeros tells whether solutions increase or decrease there.
- Example: the logistic equation $\frac{dP}{dt} = kP\left(1 - \frac{P}{M}\right)$ has equilibria $P = 0$ and $P = M$ (the carrying capacity).
- Stability: nearby solutions approach a stable equilibrium and move away from an unstable one ([[Stable Equilibrium Solution]]).

[^1]: [Calculus: Early Transcendentals](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/EEFDQ9Y5?page=619)
