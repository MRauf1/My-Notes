# Definition
> [!info] Prohairesis[^1]
> ([[Epictetus]]'s "will", or volition) The faculty by which we make use of [[Impression (Stoicism)|impressions]]: assenting, desiring, averting, and inclining. It is the only thing truly ours; nothing and nobody can compel or hinder it, so it is entirely up to us to choose one action over another.

# Properties
- Everything we consciously do, however trivial, stems from the will and so indicates moral character.[^2]
- The will is the supreme faculty: sight, hearing, and expression serve it, and only it assesses the worth of all faculties, itself included; it decides whether to look, listen, believe, or be irritated.[^3]
- Nothing that is not subject to will can obstruct will; only a deviant version of will itself can. Hence only will becomes vicious or virtuous; "your will constrained your will" when a threat seems to compel you.[^4]
- What is truly good for people is the right kind of will; rightly applied it makes a person good and happy, neglected it makes them bad and unhappy.[^5]
- The other faculties are not worthless: to value will above them is not to discount their usefulness; we care for our eyes for the sake of the will.[^6]
- "You are will": the will is the core, though not the totality, of the self.[^7]
- Free will as a birthright differs from **freedom of will**, which is hard-won: only a will consisting of a perfectly consistent set of true beliefs about value (the [[Stoic Sage|sage's]]) is truly free; see [[Freedom (Stoicism)]].[^8]
- Central term of the [[Dichotomy of Control]].
