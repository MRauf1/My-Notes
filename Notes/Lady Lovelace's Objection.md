# Definition
> [!info] Lady Lovelace's Objection[^1]
> The objection, derived from Ada Lovelace's remarks on Babbage's Analytical Engine, that a machine has no pretensions to originate anything and can only do whatever we know how to order it to perform, i.e. a machine can only do what we tell it to do.

# Properties
- One of the [[Objections to Machine Intelligence]]; a variant is that a machine can never "do anything really new" or "take us by surprise".
- Turing's replies: machines frequently surprise us, since we cannot foresee all consequences of the instructions we give; and the objection assumes that knowing the premises implies knowing all their consequences, which is false.
- Turing's strongest reply is the learning machine ([[Child Machine]]): a machine whose rules of operation change through education, so that its teacher is largely ignorant of what goes on inside it and its behaviour is not simply what it was told to do.[^1]

[^1]: [Turing, Computing Machinery and Intelligence, p. 18](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/PGZER5NA?page=18&annotation=25D6WYNB)
