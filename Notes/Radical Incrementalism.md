# Definition
> [!info] Radical Incrementalism (Boice)[^1]
> Making important work a small but daily part of your routine, and stopping when the daily time is up even when you are bursting with energy. The urge to push on "includes a big component of impatience about not being finished". Stopping strengthens the patience that lets you return day after day and sustain productivity over a career.

# Properties
- **Evidence.** Robert Boice's studies of new academics found that those writing in brief, regular daily sessions produced substantially more and reported less stress than "binge" writers. Faculty taught to write briefly and daily increased their output (Boice, 1989, 1997). The samples were small, but the finding agrees with research on spaced over massed effort.
- **Patience as power.** In a world geared for hurry, the capacity to let things take the time they take is a way to do the work that counts and to find satisfaction in the doing.[^2]
- **"Stay on the bus" (Minkkinen).** Originality lies on the far side of unoriginality. Distinctive work begins only after a patient phase of copying others, learning skills and accumulating experience.[^3]
