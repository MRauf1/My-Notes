# Definition
> [!info] Progress Monitoring (Habit Tracking)[^1]
> Recording progress toward a goal, e.g., marking each day a habit is done on a calendar. A meta-analysis of 138 studies found that interventions that increased progress monitoring improved goal attainment ($d \approx 0.40$). The effect was larger when progress was physically recorded or reported publicly (Harkin et al., 2016).

# Properties
- **Clear's three benefits.** Tracking makes a habit *obvious* (the record is a visual cue for the next action), *attractive* (visible progress motivates, and you don't want to break the streak) and *satisfying* (recording is a small immediate reward).[^2]
- **Honesty.** Most of us think we act better than we do. Measurement counters this blindness to our own behavior.[^3]
- **Practice.** Automate measurement where possible, and track manually only your most important habits. Record each measurement immediately after the habit ("After [CURRENT HABIT], I will [TRACK MY HABIT]").[^4]
- **Never miss twice.** Missing once is an accident; the danger is the spiral of repeated misses and all-or-nothing thinking.[^5] Supported by evidence: missing a single opportunity did not materially affect habit formation (Lally et al., 2010).
- **Danger.** We optimize what we measure, so the wrong measure yields the wrong behavior ([[Goodhart's Law]]). Measurement should guide you, not consume you.[^6]
