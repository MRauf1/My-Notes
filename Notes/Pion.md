# Definition
> [!info] Pion[^1]
> The particle proposed by Yukawa as the carrier of the strong nuclear force between protons and neutrons, analogous to the [[Photon]]'s role in mediating the electromagnetic force; confirmed experimentally in 1947-1948 as the π-meson.

# Properties
- Yukawa inferred its approximate mass, two to three hundred electron masses, from the known short range and strength of nuclear forces, reasoning by analogy between a force's range and the mass of its carrier particle.
- Initially confused with the muon (μ-meson), a similarly massed particle discovered earlier in cosmic rays that does not participate in the strong interaction and is unrelated to nuclear binding.
- Understood today, within quantum chromodynamics, as a composite bound state of a quark and an antiquark rather than an elementary force carrier; the fundamental carriers of the strong force are gluons.
- Historically motivated a "quantum nucleodynamics" analogous to [[Quantum Electrodynamics]], a program that stalled because the required calculations proved intractable.
- One of the mediators historically proposed for the [[Four Fundamental Interactions|strong nuclear interaction]].
