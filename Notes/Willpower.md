# Definition

It is a limited resource whose capacity cannot be significantly improved.[^1]

Newport (*Deep Work*) builds on the same "finite, depletable willpower" model (Baumeister's *ego depletion*), citing Hofmann and Baumeister's finding that people fight desires all day long.[^2]

**Update:** Ego depletion has largely failed to replicate. A 23-lab preregistered replication (Hagger et al., 2016) and a multisite study (Vohs et al., 2021) found effects near zero. Beliefs about willpower appear to moderate fatigue effects (Job, Dweck and Walton). The practical advice still holds for a different reason: routines reduce the number of effortful decisions and temptations you face.

Ahrens himself notes the ego-depletion results are "under intense scrutiny". His defensible conclusion is that a reliable, standardized working environment is less taxing on attention and willpower, and that self-control depends more on environment than on ourselves (cf. Thaler). The smart strategy is to "cheat": make the work something you feel like doing ([[Virtuous Circle of Learning]]).[^3]

Carr (*Easy Way to Quit Smoking*): "You need willpower only when you have a conflict of wills." Remove the desire and there is nothing to resist ([[Easyway Method]]).[^4] This fits the finding that people high in trait self-control report *fewer* temptations, rather than resisting more of them (Hofmann et al., 2012).
