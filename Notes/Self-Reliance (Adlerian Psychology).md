# Definition
> [!info] Self-Reliance (Adlerian Psychology)[^1]
> **Breaking away from self-centredness.** Everyone starts life almost entirely self-centred, which is necessary for survival. Self-reliance means making peace with the world and understanding that one is a *part* of it rather than its centre. It is an attitude toward life, an issue of [[Lifestyle (Adlerian Psychology)|lifestyle]], not an economic or work issue. It is the objective of education and one of the two behavioral objectives of [[Adlerian Psychology]].

# Properties
- **A fundamental desire.** All people seek freedom from their helpless, unfree condition, and self-reliance. This is the [[Pursuit of Superiority]].[^2]
- **Lifestyle for being loved.** As a survival strategy, every child chooses a lifestyle aimed at "How can I be loved?" It is self-centred: it gathers attention and tries to stand at the centre of the world. Children who cry and rage are not failing to control their emotions. They control them all too well and turn them into action to secure love.[^3]
- **Control through weakness.** Children control adults through their weakness. **"Pampered children"** (Adler) are adults who keep doing so, using misfortune, hurt and trauma as weapons to control others by making them worry. Cf. the [[Inferiority Complex]].[^4]
- **Achieved through loving.** One leaves the childhood lifestyle when one resolves to love someone. "It is through loving others that we at last become adults." Only by loving does one escape the control of the love one receives. See [[Love]].[^5]
- **Kant.** "Man's juvenile condition is not due to lack of reason… he has neither the resolution nor the courage to use his reason without direction from another. Have courage to use your own reason!" (*What Is Enlightenment?*, 1784). Living by others' direction is easier, because one need not think or take responsibility for failure.[^6]
- **Overprotection blocks it.** Adults scare children about the dangers of self-reliance and over-nurture them, producing people who cannot decide without direction. They do this to protect themselves from responsibility, not for the child's sake.[^7]
- **Practice.** Tell the child: "That is something you can decide on your own." Provide the knowledge and experience decisions require. Watch from a distance close enough to help but not too close. If a decision fails, the child learns "one can choose one's own life".[^8]
- **No dependence on the helper.** Counselling that ends with "thanks to you I'm better" has failed, because it means "I can't do anything by myself". Educators should take satisfaction in a [[Feeling of Contribution]], not in gratitude. Hoping to hear "it's thanks to you" stands in the way of the other person's self-reliance.[^9]
- Love, self-reliance and [[Community Feeling]] are all connected.[^10]

# Evidence
- Overparenting ("helicopter parenting") is associated with lower self-efficacy, poorer coping and more anxiety and depression in young adults (Segrin et al., 2012; Schiffrin et al., 2014). Autonomy support predicts better adjustment (Soenens & Vansteenkiste, 2010). See [[Self-Efficacy]].
