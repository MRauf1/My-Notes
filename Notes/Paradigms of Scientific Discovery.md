# Definition
> [!info] Paradigms of Scientific Discovery[^1]
> The successive methodological paradigms by which science produces knowledge, as framed by Jim Gray: experimentation and theory as the classic, mutually supporting pair, followed by large-scale computational simulation and, most recently, data-intensive scientific discovery.

# Types
- *First paradigm* - empirical/experimental science, describing natural phenomena.[^1]
- *Second paradigm* - theoretical science, using models and generalisations (e.g. laws of motion).[^1]
- *Third paradigm* - computational simulation of complex phenomena, emerging with von Neumann and others in the mid-20th century.[^1]
- *Fourth paradigm* - [[Data-Intensive Science|data-intensive science]] (data exploration), unifying theory, experiment, and simulation around massive datasets captured by instruments or generated by simulations.[^2]

# Properties
- The fourth paradigm acts as an integrating framework in which the first three interact and reinforce each other, echoing the classic cycle in which theory makes testable predictions and experiments reveal phenomena needing explanation.[^1]
- Simulation fell short of its initial promise (e.g. long-term weather prediction) partly because of the sensitivity of complex systems to initial conditions and chaos, so simulation, theory, and experiment must work together with massive data.[^1]
- The ready availability of diverse data shifts science from the traditional hypothesis-driven method toward exploration-based science.[^3]
- Data is not sweeping away the old reality; it places burdens on the methods and social habits by which we communicate empiricism and theory, on the robustness of simulations, and on how we expose, transmit, and integrate knowledge.[^4]
- Since the book (2009), the data-driven paradigm has been extended by machine learning and AI, which derive models from data rather than theory; see [[AI as a Way of Knowing]] and [[AI-Assisted Mathematics]] (which warns that it could crowd out the other paradigms).
- Here "paradigm" is used loosely for a methodology; compare Kuhn's technical sense in [[Paradigm (Philosophy of Science)]].
- Introduced in [[The Fourth Paradigm (Hey et al.)]].
