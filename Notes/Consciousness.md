# Definition

Consciousness describes our awareness of internal and external stimuli.

Conscious thought is expensive, which is why basic tasks are relegated to the [[Unconsciousness|unconscious]]. [[Evolution|Evolutionarilly]], we have a biological tendency to avoid effortful thinking.

A popularly cited estimate holds that the conscious mind accounts for only around 1%–5% of daily decisions, actions, and brain activity, with the remainder handled by the [[Unconsciousness|unconscious mind]]. This figure is a widely repeated approximation rather than a precisely measured constant, since the brain functions as one interconnected network rather than two cleanly separable compartments — it should be treated as a current estimate that may be revised as research improves.[^1]

# Properties
- Active Focus: engaged when concentrating hard, learning a genuinely new skill, or working through a complex, unfamiliar problem.
- Limited Capacity: processes far less information at a time than the [[Unconsciousness|unconscious mind]]'s background processing.
- [[Confabulation]]: the conscious mind often constructs a logical narrative to explain a decision after the fact, even when the [[Unconsciousness|unconscious mind]] actually made the call first.

[^1]: Popular science synthesis of psychology/neuroscience estimates on conscious vs. unconscious processing (exact primary sources were not resolvable from the source material).
