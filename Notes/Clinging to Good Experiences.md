# Definition
> [!info] Clinging to Good Experiences (Burkeman)[^1]
> Making ourselves more miserable than necessary by trying too hard to hold on to good experiences: getting them "under your belt", collecting them, or securing their future supply, instead of living them. Burkeman calls Buddhism uniquely insightful about this form of suffering (cf. grasping in the [[Three Unwholesome Roots]]).

# Properties
- Good experiences are for living, not holding on to. The way to collect vivid memories is to live experiences fully, not to try to collect them.[^1]
- "Perhaps all anxiety might derive from a fixation on moments – an inability to accept life as ongoing." (Sarah Manguso)[^2]
- Gripping experiences expresses a wish to store them up, freeze time, and resist the truth that "this is it". Since we are finite, the value of a good experience must lie at least partly in experiencing it as it happens.[^2]
- In the Japanese tea ceremony, fleetingness is the source of value, not a threat to it ([[Ichigo Ichie]], "one time, one meeting"): "The transience is the whole point."[^3]
- "The nature of finite things as such is to have the seed of passing away as their essential being." (Hegel)[^4]
- Moving through life "unclenched", without trying to make yourself appreciate or feel grateful for things, makes them more naturally enjoyable: "The less I'm trying to get something out of an experience, the more I find I can get into it."[^5]
- Related: [[Anicca]], [[Imperfectionism]].
