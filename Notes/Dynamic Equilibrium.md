# Definition
> [!info] Dynamic Equilibrium[^1]
> A macroscopically unchanging state produced not by an absence of activity but by two opposing microscopic processes proceeding at equal rates, so that their effects cancel.

# Properties
- Exemplified by [[Evaporation]] balanced by condensation at a liquid surface, and by dissolution balanced by precipitation of a solid.
- A system in dynamic equilibrium can appear static macroscopically while remaining continuously active at the atomic level, per the [[Atomic Hypothesis]].
- Disturbing one side of the balance (e.g. removing returning vapor) breaks the equilibrium and drives net change, as in [[Evaporative Cooling]].

[^1]: [The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. I, Ch. 1: Atoms in Motion](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_01.html)
