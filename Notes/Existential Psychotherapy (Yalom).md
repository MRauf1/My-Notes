# Review[^1]

Existential psychotherapy is a dynamic approach to therapy which focuses on concerns that are rooted in the individual's existence.

Psychodynamics assumes Freud's theory that there are various forces (both conscious and unconscious), motives, fears that operate within each individual.

Existential Psychodynamics claims that the conflict stems from the individual's confrontation with the givens of existence. The nature of these existential givens is discovered through deep personal reflection. In particular, four of the most important ultimate concerns are
1) Death
	1) Conflict in the tension between unavoidable death and the desire to continue to be.
2) Freedom
	1) Universe has no "inherent design". Each individual is entirely responsible of their own life, choices, actions. This creates a feeling of dread and a conflict between the groundlessness of the universe and the desire for ground and structure.
3) Isolation
	1) There is a fundamental isolation between the individual and other creatures and the world as one arrives into the world and departs from the world alone. There always exists an isolation barrier between the individual and others. Conflict arises between our awareness of absolute isolation and our desire for contact, protection, and being a part of a larger whole.
4) Meaninglessness
	1) Given the previous ultimate concerns, it follows that there is no pre-ordained meaning of life, so each individual must construct their own meaning. But is such a constructed meaning of life sturdy enough to bear one's life? Conflict emerges due to a meaning-seeking creature who is thrown into a universe that has no meaning.

Given these, the pattern that forms is Awareness of Ultimate Concern -> Anxiety -> Defense Mechanism.

Unlike other types of Psychodynamics, like Freudian and Neo-Freudian, Existential Psychodynamics largely doesn't focus on the past and focuses on the future-becoming-present to understand one's current mode of facing the ultimate concerns. The past is only relevant insofar as it relates to this current mode of facing the ultimate concerns. In a way, it seems more similar to Adlerian teleological psychology.

This existential schema is inherent to the human condition and applies to every human individual.

Psychopathalogy is not just about the presence of stress factors, but about the interactions between the stress factors present in the individual and the individual's defense mechanisms/resistance.

The existential position challenges the Cartesian view of the world where all things are objective and things can be studied objectively. The existential position claims that since the individual gives meaning to their life, they participate in the construction of their reality, meaning that each individual perceives reality differently, so it would be erroneous to study reality only from the "objective" point of view without taking into account the individual's perspective of reality.

Life and death are interdependent and exist simultaneously, not consecutively. At least psychologically, they merge into one. You live and die each day. The last day of your life is for the most part no more special than any other day, and it does not cause your death, but reveals it as Montaigne said. Furthermore, the lifelong consideration of death typically enriches one's life rather than diminish it.

According to Heidegger, the awareness of death shifts from the first mode of existence of A State of Forgetfulness of Being to a higher mode of existence of A State of Mindfulness of Being. In the former, one lives in the everyday world and marvels at the way things are, but in the latter, one marvels at the fact *that* things are. One is constantly aware of their being, and can be seen as inside the "ontological mode". And it is only when present in this state that one gains the power to change oneself. And death in particular, is the condition that allows one to move from the former mode of existence to the latter. In particular, events in which one is close to death.

Life loses a lot of its impact and meaning without the threat of death. It's like those people who claim that an immortal life would lose all meaning and value.

The first humans bunched up together to form a society in an attempt to avoid death, according to Freud's speculations. History in particular, is another way that humans try to avoid death and achieve "immortality". Humans attempt to live on in symbolic "immortality" through:
1) Their progeny
2) Through religion and ascending to a higher plane of existence
3) Through their works and creations that impact others
4) Through rejoining nature
5) Through "losing oneself" in an intense state that leads one to dissolve the concepts of life and death and to continuously live in the present

According to Jacques Choron, there are 3 types of death fear
1) What comes after death
2) The event of dying
3) The ceasing to be
The first two are fears related to death, and the third one is the primary fear of death.

Kierkegaard differentiates between the fear of *some*thing and the anxiety of *no*thing, where the nothing is a nothing with which an individual has nothing to do. To combat anxiety, one must displace it from nothing to something. However, this transformation can result in something less toxic, but still toxic to the individual. For example, death anxiety could transform into the fears of whether others like them, about their self-esteem, and more.

Strong anxiety does not remain conscious; it is repressed and "processed" whereby the affect is separated/isolated from the object.

While death makes humans feel more alive and allows them to live more meaningfully and authentically, improperly handling death anxiety can result in a vast variety of negative and abnormal symptoms, signs, and character traits.

There is even a whole subsection of the first chapter attacking Freud and showing how he had failed in some of his findings. Yalom really seems against Freud and his teachings (at least some of them, especially the ones relating to existential concepts like death).

Fear and thinking of death begins as early as in children, and it is more prevalent in them than most people think. The coping mechanism of children in regards to death is oftentimes denial-based.

Boundary Condition - event/urgent experience that propels one into confrontation with one's existential "situation" in the world. Death is a boundary condition.

Imagine daily the thoughts of death or accidents that would take things you consider precious, such as your limbs, your belongings, your loved ones, and such. Think about what would be left over and how you would live on in such a situation. This exercise will allow you to appreciate the little things in everyday life that you take for granted.

Death anxiety exists at the deepest levels of being and is heavily repressed and not "present" in the everyday life of most people. But Yalom claims that it can manifest itself in other anxieties and heavily impact the character development of a human.

It's impossible to eliminate anxiety since it is necessary for proper human functioning. The role of a therapist is to reduce anxiety to normal levels and use it to increase's patient's awareness and vitality.

Death anxiety is inversely proportional to life satisfaction.

To be responsible is to take one's own life into one's own hands (authorship).

The world/external things acquire meaning through one's perspective. World/external things inherently do not have an objective meaning.

Anxiety of groundlessness stemming from the weight of responsibility does not manifest itself in everyday life like death anxiety can. But it can lead to avoiding situations in which one must take personal responsibility and can lead one to rely on others, such as authority figures.

Modern people have more freedom compared to the past and are overwhelmed by this freedom and the corresponding responsibility that comes with it.

Responsibility avoidance is bad for mental health, while responsibility acceptance is good for mental health. Although this is an oversimplification.

Many depressed have an external locus of control, which results in hopelessness and helplessness.

Learned-helplessness by Martin Seligman - person learns early in their life that their efforts have effect (external locus of control), which leads to depression and fatalism. In existential terms, those who believe that they are not responsible for their life take a heavy penalty as a result. Thus, early childhood development is crucial for later mental health.

There is a vast amount of research supporting reciprocal determinism. That is, human behavior is shaped by an ongoing, continuous loop of mutual influence: personal factors (cognition, beliefs, and traits), the environment, and the behavior itself.

Even diseases/illnesses can be influenced by one's perspective, cognitive/psychological state.

Guilt naturally stems from the existential responsibility and adds a guilt of transgressions towards oneself too. One is guilty to the same extent as one is responsible for oneself and their world. It is a healthy emotion (in proper amounts) and helps one accomplish their potential and truly live their life. In fact, many existentialists claim that this existential guilt is precisely how one finds one's own potential and when they stray from their right path.

Each person has a unique set of potentials that yearn to be realized. One who is unable to fully live their life in such a way, ends up experiencing existential guilt.

Awareness of responsibility is the first step. It must be followed by action. Willing is composed of wishing and action.

One's inability to wish may also stem from one's inability to feel (alexithymia). If the inability to feel is taken care of, one's inability to wish may be taken care of as well automatically. Wishing requires feeling. If one cannot feel, one may emulate feeling by feeling what others feel, but this type of inauthentic feeling is insufficient for true wishing.

Decision is the bridge between wishing and action.

Best way to deal with guilt is through atonement. Not by getting stuck in the past, but by altering the future.

If a decision is difficult because one must relinquish one thing while accepting another, a decision can be made easier by making the relinquishment smaller/simpler.

In a decision, one feels more discomfort when the choices are nearly equivalent in value. Comfortable decision-making demands that one option is obviously more attractive than the other.

Every person must own and take responsibility for their decisions. They shouldn't look to others to make the decisions for them. By accepting an active approach to decision where you are the master of your decisions, you also accept the reality that you are the master of your life. This helps prevent learned-helplessness.

Responsibility is continuous: one does not create their world/life once and for all; one is continuously creating oneself. Similarly, so does change appear from oneself continuously.

Secondary anxiety stemming from anxiety often creates more havoc than the primary disease.

Different interpretations such as Jungian, Freudian, Adlerian, and so on are human constructs. They do not truly exist (as far as we know). None is better than the other objectively, but they can be more suited to a specific individual than the other. Thus, for most effectiveness, a suitable interpretation must be tailored to each patient so that this interpretation effectively improves the life of the patient. That's the primary criteria. Even if the interpretation is religious or astrological or something like that in nature, as long as it truly helps the patient, then this interpretation is valid.

Responsibility applies to every human equally in the same manner. You are not special in terms of this so do not assume more guilt or responsibility than others because you feel like you "deserve" it.
