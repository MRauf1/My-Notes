# Definition
> [!info] Drag Force[^1]
> The resistive [[Force]] on an object moving through a fluid (e.g. air), empirically found, at sufficiently high speed, to be approximately proportional to the square of its speed:
> $$
> \begin{align}
> F \approx cv^2
> \end{align}
> $$
> where $c$ is a constant depending on the object's shape, size, and the fluid.

# Properties
- An empirical, approximate law: the underlying flow (air rushing over a surface, swirling in the wake, and so on) is far too complicated to derive this simple form from first principles, yet the quadratic dependence on speed holds up remarkably well.
- Contrasts with the drag on a slowly-moving object through a viscous fluid (e.g. an object creeping through honey), where the drag force instead grows linearly with speed rather than quadratically; the classical result for this low-speed, high-viscosity regime is Stokes' drag, $F = 6\pi\eta r v$ for a sphere of radius $r$ in a fluid of viscosity $\eta$.
- Like sliding [[Friction]], an example of a force whose empirical law is simple even though its underlying microscopic mechanism is highly complex.
