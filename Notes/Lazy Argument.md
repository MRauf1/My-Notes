# Definition
> [!info] Lazy Argument[^1]
> (*Argos logos*) The fallacious inference that if everything is fated, effort is pointless. The ancient example: "if you are fated to recover, you will recover whether or not you call a doctor". The Stoics (Chrysippus, reported in Cicero's *De Fato*) answered that outcomes are *co-fated* with the actions that bring them about. Our actions are part of the causal chain, not bypassed by it.

# Properties
- The fallacy assumes events are determined *only* by external causes, i.e. that people are wholly passive. Stoic determinism instead holds that events are co-determined by internal and external causes ([[Stoic Determinism]]): "my actions are part of the causal network".[^1]
- It confuses **fatalism** (the outcome is fixed whatever one does) with **determinism** (the outcome is fixed *through* what one does).
- Determinism doesn't entail apathy: one may be causally determined to respond to it with renewed commitment and vigorous action.[^2]
- Philosophical acceptance remains appropriate for what is genuinely beyond one's control ([[Dichotomy of Control]], [[Amor Fati]]).[^1]
