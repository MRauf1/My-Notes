# Definition

> [!info] Definition 1 (Isometry)[^1]
> Given a [[Function|function]] $f: X \rightarrow X$ on a metric space $(X, d)$ and points $a, b \in X$, it is an isometry if
> $$
> \begin{align}
> d(f(a), f(b)) = d(a, b)
> \end{align}
> $$

Isometry is a distance-preserving transformation. It is defined generally for any metric space $(X, d)$, not only Euclidean space with the [[Euclidean Distance|Euclidean distance]].

Every isometry is also an [[Affine Transformation|affine transformation]], but not every affine transformation is an isometry; the symmetries of a shape under the wider group of affine transformations are called [[Affine Symmetry|affine symmetries]].
