# Review[^1]

A pretty depressing read about a very unfortunate man who could never relate to humans and never felt like a human. His experiences were always out of the norm with regular human experiences and thoughts and beliefs and this distinction ended up isolating him from the rest of humanity. However, this was done internally, as the author kept on putting on a mask to pretend to be like other humans. 

Even then, he never talked back to other when they would, for example, insult him as he believed that these people have simply seen through his mask, and that the author was the one who was failing to act as a human. He even thought that the true human nature was particularly terrifying that he would experience severe physiological and mental symptoms during such experiences. He thought that this was part of the true horrific nature that is needed to survive as a human. The thought of him having to adopt this nature to survive and be human himself was particularly dreadful for the author. So the cycle continued. The author would hid his deepest horrors and unfortunes as deep inside of himself as he could lest these break through his cage and his inhumanity becomes exposed to the society. In this desire, he would always play the role of a jester, acting specifically to the human interests and avoiding offending them so that in their eyes, the author is nothing. Nothing at all. So that the author is the same as the wind to these people. Some of his methods were actively and both physically and mentally self-deprecating. After all, these types of pains are nothing compared to the true human nature that he would have to otherwise witness. And thus, he became a fantastic clown, regularly fooling the people around him including his family. But even then, there would always be points of failure and dissappointment.

When asked for presents, he would always say nothing. Perhaps there was fear involved as with all of his external actions, but to him, he wanted nothing primarily because he thought that there was nothing that would make him happy. Even so, he could not express his feeling when gifted a present, such as "I do not like it".

A point I saw being mentioned online is how Yozo downplays the abuse he received as a child. It wasn't physical by the sounds of it, but the very fact that he felt the way he felt when talking to his father, when telling his father what present he would like, subtlely shows the abusive dynamic between them, which would only escalate in the future. It's no wonder that he keeps saying that his father is the one man that he feared the most (or among the most).

His upbringing wasn't particularly harsh or horrible nor was it luxurious and rich. Although he did live in a rural setting without leaving the place and seeing urban territories and even a train until later in his childhood. His father would also buy him and the family and relatives presents after his job travel. They had a piano in their house too, so overall, his family was relatively well-off.

In school, he ranked first and often was known as the one who had the "brains". In particularly, he became better and better at crafting stories and writing, which may have very well led to him becoming a writer and an author. Crafting stories as his responsibility as the jester who is trying to fool everyone.

Related to [[Existential Psychotherapy (Yalom)]], it seems to me that what Dazai was experiencing and suffering from is a severe case of existential isolation that prohibited him from forming genuine relationships with others and being able to relate to others.

Despite his best efforts, he veil was discovered by the one boy he expected the least - a quiet kid who was not doing well in his studies. A simpleton, by the words of the author. To prevent him from revealing his secret to others, Dazai began to actively interacting with him in hopes of befriending him and becoming his best friend. His only other option was to hope for the kid's death.

Despite most of his family being women, he had a very hard time understanding women, more so than men. This led to him making mistakes and getting "severely wounded" as a result. In particular, he was heavily and repeatedly manipulated by some of the women in his life. In particular, he describes women as a different species that is both incomprehensible and insidious. 

With his exposure to certain artists like van Gogh and others who depicted humans in unusual ways that Dazai described as though portraying monsters and devils, he thought that these artists may be people like him who say humans for what they truly are and depicted their true nature within their works. He believed that these were the type of people he could one day be friends with.

He began painting, and it was only once he made self-portraits that he was able to capture his true self despite how heart-rending the painting were. He affirmed this self as his true self and made sure there was no escape from it.

He met a good-for-nothing city boy who became his friend and introduced him to the "pleasure of life": alcohol, tobacco, prostitutes, and more. He did not view prostitutes as human beings, but he did find solace and security from his dread of humans in their hands. Not just in prostitutes, but in alcohol and other such things too. This led to his experience of interacting with women, which built a sort of a charming effect around him, which made women flock to him. His realization of this effect made him suddenly lose all interest in prostitutes.

His friend also introduced him to Communist meetings, where he took part, become charismatic, and earned a high reputation leading to him taking part in some of the most secretive missions. But despite all of this, Dazai always saw these Marxists as complete idiots who spent their lives on completely inconsequential missions and tasks. Nevertheless, he didn't leave them. He once again took the role of the jester as he took pleasure in the irrationality and stupidity of these people. Despite his inner feelings, no one was able to see through his mask and uncover his true feelings.

He ends up attempting to commit a love suicide with a married waitress. The interesting thing is that despite the significance of this event, he doesn't even remember properly the girl's name or her appearance. But he does vividly remember how bad the sushi he ate before this event tasted and what the old man making the sushi looked like. He believes that the reason for this is because of how much he hated the taste of that sushi that it is still in his memory. I do wonder the real psychological reason for this phenomenon though.

The gloominess and misery flowing from this waitress, during their first night together, combined with his own strong gloomy current temporarily freed him from fear and uneasiness and gave him fleeting happiness.

He never had a desire for possessions or anything of the like. So much so, that later in life, he would end up watching in silence as his own wife was violated.

His dread of the humans, his alienation in this world, and his newly-born poverish life has led to him accepting the love suicide with her. In the end though, she was the one who died, while he was saved. Thinking of her afterwards, he realized that she was the only one he had ever loved.

He was later taken to the police station for the investigation. Finding the scenario absurd, he didn't hesitate to hide some of the truth and make up some lies as well. Most interestingly, it was during his time in custody that he felt calm and relaxed rather than when he was out in the world.

He starts addressing himself as a parasite, trying to imagine what the  others must've thought of him.

Flatfish didn't tell him the plans for Dazai's future directly, which may have helped set Dazai a bit more straight. Instead, this vagueness led to Dazai's future path being forced astray.

The more he feared others, the more he tried to make others like him. The more others liked him, the more he began fearing others. In the end, that feeling would get so strong that he would end up running away from everybody.

His new family seemed better off without him in it, and he was afraid that he would end up ruining their lives as if he was a disaster incarnate. In the end, he ran away once more.

In the end, he found a very trusting girl, to a fault actually, and he found this trustworthiness of her beautiful and a virtue, which led to him promptly marrying her and starting a life together. Everything was going well initially until one day he saw her get violated by another man due to her trustworthy nature. This ended up ruining both of them. The girl lost her virtue of trust and no longer acted the way she used to. Meanwhile, the one thing, the one virtue that kept Dazai afloat backfired on him, and he now had nothing left to cling onto. He started suspecting his wife of other affairs, began drinking more frequently, and eventually, tried to commit suicide for the second time by overdosing on sleeping pills. Once again, he did not succeed.

His friend as well (if he can even be called that) was quite cruel in this event as well. The way he didn't bother to stop it, but instead went back up to Yozo to inform him about, and then followed along to see his reaction before leaving altogether as Yozo's life was falling apart in real time.

In an attempt to save his health, he ended up going to a pharmacy where he connected with the crippled shopkeeper lady. She gave him medicine as well as morphine to make him quit his drinking addiction. But that instead gave way to his new morphine addiction. He would frequently visit the pharmacy, begging for more, accumulating a large debt, while also having an affair with the crippled widow. In the end, he was admitted to a mental hospital. Now, he was a lunatic. He truly ceased being a human being.

According to him, his unhappiness stemmed from his inability to say no. He believes that many of his problems would not have happened had he just said no.

In the end of the story, he relocates to an isolated house in the country with an elder maid to take care of him thanks to his elder brother's help. Now, everything just passes him. He does not feel alive. He does not feel anything. Everything just passes.

I love the epilogue of the story how when asking others about Yozo, the bar woman described him as a sweet angel. Throughout the entire story, Yozo kept describing himself as a parasite that only ruins other people. And yet, some people still viewed him positively. It's a fascinating dichotomy just how different one's perspective about oneself can be from how others view you.

Apparently, the story is a semi-autobiography, and I'm not sure which parts are autobiographical and which ones are fictional.
