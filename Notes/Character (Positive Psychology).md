# Definition
> [!info] Character (Positive Psychology)[^1]
> In [[Carol Dweck|Dweck]]'s sense (also called heart, will, or the mind of a champion), the ability to dig down and find strength when things are going against you: it is what makes one practise and what lets one perform when it is most needed. Character grows out of the [[Growth Mindset|growth mindset]] and can be learned.

# Properties
- Champions win when things are not quite right, raising their level of play when the match is on the line; mental toughness is learned, not innate, and is how less talented people beat more talented opponents.[^2]
- "Ability can get you to the top, but it takes character to keep you there" (John Wooden).[^3]
- Dweck's three findings on growth-minded athletes:[^4]
	1. They find success in doing their best, learning, and improving, not just winning ("the best me").
	2. They find setbacks motivating and informative, a wake-up call; in the fixed mindset setbacks label you.
	3. They take charge of the processes that bring and maintain success (conditioning, practice, motivation), whereas fixed-minded athletes blame outside forces and act as finished products who must protect themselves.
- **Somebody–nobody syndrome**: the fixed-mindset belief "if I win I'll be somebody; if I lose I'll be nobody", where success means establishing superiority and effort casts doubt on talent. Somebodies are instead those who go for it with all they have.[^5]
- Being a "natural" can be a curse: much praise and little need to stretch lead to a fixed mindset, and naturals never learn to work hard or cope with setbacks.[^6]
- Teamwork wins championships; fixed-mindset stars act as superstars apart from the team, to its cost.[^7]
- Culture reveres naturals (Malcolm Gladwell), preferring to see champions as born superheroes rather than ordinary people who made themselves extraordinary.[^8]
- Related to [[Grit]].
