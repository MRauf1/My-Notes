# Definition

> [!info] Definition 1 (Logical Equivalence)[^1]
> Two formulas are (logically) equivalent if they have the same truth value under every assignment of truth values to their variables, i.e. their [[Truth Table|truth tables]] agree in every row.

# Properties
- Equivalent formulas can be substituted for each other inside any larger formula.
- Standard equivalences: [[DeMorgan's Laws]], [[Commutative Laws]], [[Associative Laws]], [[Idempotent Laws]], [[Distributive Laws]], [[Absorption Laws]], [[Double Negation Law]], [[Tautology Laws]], [[Contradiction Laws]], and the conditional law $P \to Q \equiv \neg P \lor Q$ ([[Conditional Statement]]).
- $P$ and $Q$ are equivalent iff $P \leftrightarrow Q$ is a [[Tautology]] ([[Biconditional Statement]]).
- Set identities are proved by translating membership into logical formulas and applying these equivalences.

[^1]: [HOW TO PROVE IT: A Structured Approach, Second Edition](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/THI2Q4PN?page=33)
