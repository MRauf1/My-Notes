# Definition
> [!info] Cynicism[^1]
> Ancient philosophical movement (Antisthenes, Diogenes of Sinope, Crates, Hipparchia) whose adherents lived as vagabonds owning only what they could carry, demonstrating that virtue and happiness require nothing externals can give. [[Epictetus]] idealised the Cynic as a messenger sent by Zeus to show people by example where good and bad lie.

# Properties
- The ideal Cynic has no house, city, possessions, or family, yet lacks nothing, is free from distress and fear, and never blames God or man.[^2]
- He must first consult God and know himself; he will be beaten and must love those who beat him, treating hardship as Zeus's training (Diogenes: "a fight between man and fever").[^3]
- Epictetus's Cynic does not marry or have children, being "the father of the entire human race"; he serves the public more than any politician by speaking about happiness and freedom.[^4]
- Requirements: a healthy physique (as testimony that the simple life is harmless), cleanliness, natural charm and wit, and above all a command center "purer than the sun", lest he censure others while vicious himself.[^5]
- Where will and impressions are concerned he is watchful as Argus; about all else he is relaxed "to the point of snoring".[^6]
- Diogenes said Antisthenes freed him by teaching what is his and what isn't; see [[Freedom (Stoicism)]].[^7]
- A precursor and influence on [[Stoicism]]; cf. [[Cosmopolitanism]].
- **Therapeutic shamelessness.** The Cynics deliberately practised shamelessness (*anaideia*) as a psychotherapeutic exercise. They rejected cleanliness, courtesy and social propriety, begged, and spoke with provocative frankness (*parrhesia*) (Hadot). Ellis's shame-attacking exercises are a milder version ([[Rational Emotive Behavior Therapy]]).[^rob1]
- Diogenes: other dogs bite their enemies, the Cynic bites his friends to save them. Antisthenes: physicians must sometimes use bitter remedies. The Cynics wrote and debated less than other schools and focused on living the philosophical life ([[Hellenistic Philosophy]]).[^rob2]
