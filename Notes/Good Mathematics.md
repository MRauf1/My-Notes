# Definition
> [!info] Good Mathematics[^1]
> (Tao) Mathematics whose quality is judged along many diverse "local" criteria, but which, in its very best examples, is also part of a greater mathematical story: it builds upon earlier achievements and unfurls to generate many further pieces of good mathematics of many different types.

# Types
Local criteria of mathematical quality (in no particular order):[^1]
- Good problem-solving, technique, theory, insight, and discovery.
- Good application, exposition, pedagogy, vision, and taste.
- Good public relations and meta-mathematics.
- Rigour, beauty, elegance, creativity, usefulness, strength, depth, and intuition.
- Good definitions.

# Properties
- This multifaceted nature is healthy: it allows many approaches to the subject and exploits many types of mathematical talent toward the common goal of progress and understanding.[^2]
- Pursuing only one or two criteria at the expense of the others is detrimental to a field; each area's existing results, folklore, intuition, and experience indicate which approaches deserve most resources and which speculative ones merit a few independent-minded mathematicians "just to cover all bases."[^3]
- Good mathematics is therefore measured not only by local qualities but by the "global" question of how it fits with other good mathematics; one should be aware of the larger context a result could be placed in, since this may bring the greatest long-term benefit to the result, the field, and mathematics as a whole.[^4]
- Fitting into a broader narrative is also a component of an argument's favourable [[Smell (Mathematics)|smell]]; compare the [[Crucial Consideration|value of discovery]] and Hamming's [[Important Problem|important problems]].
