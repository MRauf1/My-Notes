# Definition
> [!info] Intentionality[^1]
> The feature of certain mental states by which they are directed at, or about, objects and states of affairs in the world. Beliefs, desires, and intentions are intentional states; undirected forms of anxiety and depression are not.

# Properties
- Intentional states are defined by their *content*, not their form, which distinguishes them from purely formal symbol manipulation.[^2]
- Understanding (e.g. of a story in a language) is a paradigm case of intentionality.
- According to Searle, intentionality in humans and animals is a product of causal features of the brain, i.e. certain brain processes are sufficient for it ([[Biological Naturalism]]).[^3]
- Whatever intentionality computers appear to have exists solely in the minds of those who program, use, supply input to, and interpret output from them (derived rather than intrinsic intentionality).[^4]
- Central concept in the [[Chinese Room Argument]] against [[Strong Artificial Intelligence]].
