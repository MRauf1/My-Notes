# Definition
> [!info] Singleton[^1]
> A world order in which there is, at the global level, a single decision-making agency, e.g. formed by a [[Superintelligence|superintelligence]] with a [[Decisive Strategic Advantage|decisive strategic advantage]]. A singleton is by definition a highly collaborative social order.

# Properties
- **Wise-singleton threshold**: a capability set exceeds it iff a patient, existential-risk-savvy system with that capability set, facing no intelligent opposition, could colonise and re-engineer a large part of the accessible universe, e.g. via self-replicating von Neumann probes spreading through the Hubble volume and organising matter and free energy to maximise its utility function over cosmic time.[^2]
- **Cosmic endowment**: the resources at stake are astronomical, e.g. Dyson spheres capturing stellar output, O'Neill cylinder habitats (perhaps $10^{43}$ human lives), or about $10^{85}$ computational operations, compared to $10^{31}$-$10^{44}$ ops to simulate all neuronal operations in Earth's history.[^3]
- In singleton scenarios, the post-transition outcome depends almost entirely on the singleton's values, and hence on whether the [[AI Control Problem]] was solved. Predictions must rely on laws of physics, [[Instrumental Convergence Thesis|convergent instrumental values]], and guesses about its final values.[^4]
- Contrasted with [[Multipolar Scenario|multipolar scenarios]]. In a fast takeoff, greater pre-transition collaboration might paradoxically yield less post-transition collaboration by preventing a singleton; in general more post-transition collaboration appears desirable.[^5]
