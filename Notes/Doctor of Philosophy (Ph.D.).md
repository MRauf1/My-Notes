# Definition
> [!info] Doctor of Philosophy (Ph.D.)[^1]
> A long, in-depth research exploration of one topic, in which the emphasis is not on classes but on research.

# Types
Funding models:[^2]
- Research Assistantship (RAship) - tuition is waived and a living stipend is paid from the advisor's grants, with research as the only responsibility; requires an advisor with funding.
- Teaching Assistantship (TAship) - the stipend is earned by working as a teaching assistant each semester.
- Graduate Fellowship - external funding from companies or government that covers most of the degree, independent of the advisor's funding. It is worth applying to all fellowships one is eligible for, before even choosing schools.[^3]

# Properties
- Research differs fundamentally from coursework; only about half of entering students finish.[^4]
- Research is a roller-coaster of highs and lows; the biggest frustration is realising you are not as great as you thought.[^5]
- Its joys include discovering something no one knew, truly understanding, making an impact, doing it right, and being the recognised authority whose work is read and cited.[^5]
- Career paths:[^6]
	- *Research university professor* - research on anything, working with students, teaching, grant-writing, travel, and service; for new faculty, "your life becomes your job".
	- *Teaching college professor* - mostly teaching and service, with occasional undergraduate research advising.
	- *Research lab* - research split between one's own interests and the company's, plus collaboration and some travel.
	- For those who never do research again, the Ph.D. was largely a waste of time.
- For admission to top CS programs, the most important component is prior research experience and what letters and statements say about it. This does not require a publication or even a result, only evidence of knowing what research is like. The "personal statement" is really a research statement and not a commitment: about a third of students end up in a different area.[^7]
- Focus on a topic so narrow that you can understand it completely: a student "should know something about everything and everything about something" (Anatole France).[^8]
- An advisor may work in your area (offering sage guidance) or not (so you must learn from others and teach them); either can work, but know which you have.[^8]
- **Tenure and student risk**: tenure lets professors attempt bold, risky projects, but students funded by their grants often cannot refuse them; a professor survives years of failure, whereas a student's career and psychological health may not. Fellowships grant far more freedom to direct one's own work.[^9]
- **Why do it without becoming a professor**: it can be a safe environment to push oneself beyond one's mental limits and emerge stronger, the intellectual equivalent of intense athletic training (e.g. an Ironman); though many burn out, and such traits can be built elsewhere.[^9]
- Most of the work (about 10,000 hours) is solitary grinding, with the fun parts under 5% of the time; it is fulfilling rather than fun, since fulfilment comes from overcoming meaningful challenges. Some discover they prefer being a spectator rather than a continual producer of research.[^9]
