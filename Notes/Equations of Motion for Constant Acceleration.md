# Definition
> [!info] Equations of Motion for Constant Acceleration[^1]
> For a body moving with constant [[Acceleration Vector|acceleration]] $a$, starting at time $t=0$ with initial velocity $v_0$ and initial position $s_0$:
> $$
> \begin{align}
> v(t) &= v_0 + at \\
> s(t) &= s_0 + v_0 t + \tfrac{1}{2}at^2
> \end{align}
> $$
> obtained by integrating the constant acceleration once (for velocity) and twice (for position).

# Properties
- Special case, starting from rest ($v_0 = 0$) and from the origin ($s_0 = 0$): $v = at$ and $s = \tfrac{1}{2}at^2$; e.g. for a body in free fall under gravitational acceleration $g$, $v = gt$ and $s = \tfrac{1}{2}gt^2$.
- A direct instance of the general fact, following from the [[Fundamental Theorem of Calculus]], that [[Velocity Vector|velocity]] is the [[Antiderivative]] (integral) of [[Acceleration Vector|acceleration]], and [[Position Vector|position]] is the antiderivative of velocity.
