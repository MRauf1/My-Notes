# Definition
> [!abstract] Uniformization Theorem[^1][^2]
> Every simply connected Riemann surface is conformally equivalent to exactly one of: the open unit disk, the complex plane $\mathbb{C}$, or the Riemann sphere $\hat{\mathbb{C}}$. In particular, any (topological) disk equipped with a metric can be conformally mapped to a planar region.

Practical consequence for surfaces: around any point $p$ of $f(M)$ there is a small disk-like neighborhood with a [[Conformal Map|conformal parameterization]]. As with unit-speed curves, it is often enough to know such a parameterization exists without constructing it.[^1]

Strictly, the *local* existence of conformal (isothermal) coordinates on a smooth surface is a separate, earlier result (Gauss for analytic metrics; Korn–Lichtenstein and Chern in general); it is in fact what makes every oriented surface with a metric a Riemann surface, to which uniformization then applies globally.[^2]

# Properties
- Generalizes the [[Riemann Mapping Theorem]] (simply connected proper open subsets of $\mathbb{C}$ are conformal to the disk).
- Every closed surface carries a metric of constant [[Gaussian Curvature]] ($+1$, $0$ or $-1$) in its conformal class, the sign determined by the [[Euler Characteristic]].
