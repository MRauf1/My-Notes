# Definition
> [!info] Perseverative Cognition Hypothesis[^1]
> Repetitive thinking about stressors (worry and [[Rumination|rumination]]) prolongs the body's stress response beyond the stressor itself. Over time this causes physical damage (Brosschot, Gerin and Thayer, 2006). It is the modern, evidence-based form of Carnegie's claim that "business men who do not know how to fight worry die young."

# Properties
- **Evidence.**
	- A meta-analysis of 36 studies found that perseverative cognition raises blood pressure, heart rate and [[Cortisol|cortisol]], and lowers heart-rate variability (Ottaviani et al., 2016).
	- Worry and anxiety are prospectively associated with coronary heart disease (Tully, Cosh and Baune, 2013; Roest et al., 2010).
- **What Carnegie got wrong (out of date).**
	- *Stomach ulcers* are mainly caused by *Helicobacter pylori* infection and NSAIDs, not by worry (Marshall and Warren, Nobel Prize 2005). Stress can still worsen ulcers and gut symptoms.[^2]
	- His claims about arthritis, tooth decay and an overactive thyroid are uncontrolled clinical opinions from the 1940s. Stress plays some role in autoimmune flares, but these conditions are not "caused" by worry.[^3]
	- "70% of patients could cure themselves" is an unsupported figure. Psychological factors do contribute to many presentations (see [[Stress Dangers]]).[^2]
	- *Suicide* has many causes (mental disorder, especially depression, substance use, access to means). Worry alone does not explain it, and his comparison with communicable diseases reflects mid-century mortality.[^4] See [[Suicide]].
- **Nervous system.** "The Lord may forgive us our sins, but the nervous system never does" (William James).[^4]
