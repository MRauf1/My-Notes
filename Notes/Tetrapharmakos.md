# Definition
> [!info] Tetrapharmakos[^1]
> ("Fourfold remedy") The single general-purpose therapeutic formula that followers of [[Epicureanism|Epicurus]] recited and memorised (preserved by Philodemus):
> - There is no need to fear God,
> - Nor worry about death.
> - What is good is readily attainable,
> - And what is bad is easily endured.

# Properties
- Contrast: the Stoics used many formulaic precepts, while the Epicureans concentrated on one ([[Hypomnemata]]).[^1]
- For the Epicureans good and bad are pleasure and pain. For the Stoics they are acting rationally or irrationally: Stoic happiness ([[Eudaimonia]]) comes not from pleasure (*hedone*) but from the fulfilment of acting in accord with our rational nature.[^2]
- The two schools nonetheless have much in common from a modern therapeutic standpoint, e.g. anticipating Fortune ([[Premeditatio Malorum]]) and the cosmic perspective ([[View from Above]]).[^2]
- The second line is argued in the [[Epicurean Argument Against Fearing Death]].
