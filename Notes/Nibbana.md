# Definition
> [!info] Nibbana[^1]
> (Sanskrit *Nirvana*; liberation, enlightenment) The goal of all Buddhist systems of practice. It is an unconditioned, uncreated, and permanent state in which craving is extinguished and the illusion of a self evaporates. What remains is an effortless flow of interrelated, impersonal, ever-changing phenomena, without a trace of resistance or tension.

# Properties
- It is realized by experiencing the [[Three Marks of Existence]] so vividly that the futility of craving, grasping, and resistance becomes obvious.[^1]
- Being unconditioned, it cannot be conditioned or produced by thoughts, even [[Skillful and Unskillful Thoughts|skillful]] ones. A liberated person's good qualities arise purely from their own nature, no longer held back by [[Ego|ego]].[^2]
- [[Mindfulness]], and only mindfulness, produces it. Fully developed mindfulness is total non-attachment.[^3]
- It may occur at any moment, triggered by the most ordinary perception, if the mind is kept in a state of open meditative readiness.[^4]
- Liberation does not follow instantly from a first moment of mindfulness. Integrating it into life and prolonging it are further processes.[^5]
- The temporary calm of deep concentration (freedom from greed, envy, hatred, fear) is not liberation, only a stepping stone toward it.[^6]
- Tolle's counterpart: [[Enlightenment (Spirituality)]].
- The Buddha's goal has five elements: purification of mind, overcoming sorrow and lamentation, overcoming pain and grief, treading the right path to lasting peace, and attaining happiness by following that path.[^7]
