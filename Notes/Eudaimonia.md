# Definition

Flourishing of life.
# Stoic Psychology
- *Eudaimonia* covers rational fulfilment, happiness and well-being. If *daimon* is read as mind it could be translated "mental health", though the Greek also suggests being on good terms with one's inner *daemon*. It contrasts with passive sensory pleasure (*hedone*): true happiness is constant and self-generated, coming from rational activity in accord with the virtues. Its cardinal contributing virtue is wisdom (*sophia*), so philosophy, the practical pursuit of wisdom, is the art of living.[^rob1]
- The common-sense preconception that we should seek happiness is correct; the error lies in applying it, e.g. sacrificing long-term well-being for pleasant sensations ([[Preconception (Stoicism)]]).[^rob2]
- Ancient ethics was based on what is therapeutic; "ethics" (from *ethos*, character) meant the development of character rather than morality.[^rob3]
- Related: [[Stoic Virtue]], [[Tetrapharmakos]].
