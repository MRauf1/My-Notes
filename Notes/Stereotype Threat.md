# Definition
> [!info] Stereotype Threat[^1]
> The drop in performance that occurs when a negative stereotype about one's group is evoked before a task in the domain it concerns (Claude Steele and Joshua Aronson): even checking a box for race or sex can lower test scores, because the stereotype fills the mind with distracting worries about confirming it.

# Properties
- In many studies blacks equal whites and females equal males in performance when no stereotype is evoked; merely adding more males to the room lowers a female's math score.[^1]
- It mainly afflicts people in a [[Fixed Mindset|fixed mindset]]; in a [[Growth Mindset|growth mindset]] the stereotype does not disrupt performance, since it is seen as someone else's view rather than a verdict.[^2]
- Women with a growth mindset about math kept a strong, stable sense of belonging despite perceived stereotyping; fixed-mindset women's belonging shrank over the semester, the more so the more stereotyping they perceived.[^3]
- Many able, high-achieving females trust others' opinions of them too much: as young girls they are praised as perfect and learn to trust such estimates, whereas boys (who got eight times more conduct criticism in grade school) learn that evaluations lose their power. Fixed mindset, stereotyping, and this trust together contribute to the gender gap in math and science.[^4]
- Fixed-mindset boys got a self-esteem boost from endorsing stereotypes about girls; growth-mindset boys did not.[^5]
