# Definition
> [!info] Superintelligence[^1]
> Any intellect that greatly exceeds the cognitive performance of humans in virtually all domains of interest.

# Types
- *Speed superintelligence* - a system that can do all that a human intellect can do, but much faster. To it, the external world appears to unfold in slow motion, so it would prefer digital objects, virtual reality, and the information economy, or act physically via fast nanoscale manipulators.[^2]
- *Collective superintelligence* - a system composed of a large number of smaller intellects whose overall performance across many very general domains vastly outstrips any current cognitive system. It excels at problems decomposable into sub-problems that can be pursued in parallel and verified independently, and is enhanced by more or better constituent intellects or better organisation.[^3]
- *Quality superintelligence* - a system at least as fast as a human mind and vastly qualitatively smarter, i.e. as superior to human intelligence as human intelligence is to that of elephants, dolphins, or chimpanzees.[^4]

# Properties
- The three forms are practically equivalent: any one could, over time, develop the technology to create the others, so their *indirect reach* is equal.[^5]
- The potential for intelligence in a machine substrate is vastly greater than in a biological one; humans are probably best thought of as the stupidest possible biological species capable of starting a technological civilisation, having filled that niche by getting there first rather than by being optimally adapted to it.[^6]
- Human biological limits include limited long-term memory, although it is unclear whether a lifetime's slow accumulation ever exhausts it.[^7]
	- *Creator's note*: some argue human long-term memory is effectively unlimited.
- Can arise via several [[Paths to Superintelligence]], and plausibly through an [[Intelligence Explosion]].
- Stakes: just as the fate of gorillas depends more on humans than on gorillas, humanity's fate would depend on a machine superintelligence. Our one advantage is that we build it; but we may get only one chance, since an unfriendly superintelligence would prevent us from replacing it or changing its preferences, making this probably the last challenge humanity faces ([[AI Control Problem]]).[^8]
- An AI need not resemble a human mind and will likely be extremely alien, with goal systems that could diverge radically from human ones ([[Orthogonality Thesis]]); anthropomorphic framings encourage unfounded expectations about its growth trajectory, psychology, motivations, and capabilities.[^9]
- Bostrom's analysis (2014) predates modern large language models; see [[AI-Assisted Scientific Research]] for the capabilities of later systems.
