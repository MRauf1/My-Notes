# Definition
> [!info] Tidal Force[^1]
> The net force resulting from the differential (non-uniform) gravitational pull of one body across the diameter of another: the near side is pulled more strongly than the average pull at the center, and the far side less strongly, than the pull that is exactly balanced by orbital motion about the [[Barycenter]]. Both the near and far sides are left with a net force pointing away from the center, producing two bulges rather than one.

# Properties
- On Earth, the Moon's tidal force raises two bulges in the oceans — one facing the Moon, one on the opposite side — producing two high tides per day rather than one.
- A consequence of the $1/r^2$ dependence in [[Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation]]: the force is stronger at $r - \Delta r$ than at $r$, and weaker at $r + \Delta r$, so a finite-sized body experiences a stretching effect along the line joining it to the attracting body.
