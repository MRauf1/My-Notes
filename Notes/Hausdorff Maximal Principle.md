# Definition
> [!info] Hausdorff Maximal Principle[^1]
> Every [[Partial Order|partially ordered set]] has a maximal [[Total Order|linearly ordered]] [[Subset]].

Here "maximal" is with respect to inclusion: a linearly ordered subset $E$ that is not properly contained in any other linearly ordered subset.

> [!info] Maximum Principle (Strict Partial Order Form)[^2]
> Let $A$ be a set and $\prec$ a [[Strict Partial Order]] on $A$. Then there exists a maximal simply ([[Strict Total Order|totally]]) ordered subset $B$ of $A$.

# Properties
- The Hausdorff maximal principle, [[Zorn's Lemma]], the [[Well Ordering Principle (Set Theory)|well ordering principle]], and the [[Axiom of Choice]] are equivalent (given the other axioms of ZF set theory).[^1]
- Applied to a maximal linearly ordered subset, it immediately yields [[Zorn's Lemma]]: an upper bound of that subset must be a maximal element.
