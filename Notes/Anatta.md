# Definition
> [!info] Anatta[^1]
> (Selflessness, no-self) The third of the [[Three Marks of Existence]]: there is no permanent, unchanging entity that we call soul or self. There are only impersonal processes, caused and conditioned by previous processes — "thoughts but no thinker, emotions and desires, but nobody doing them".

# Properties
- The "I" concept is a process, not a thing. We solidify the flow of experience into a "me" just as we solidify other perceptions into discrete mental objects.[^2]
- Searching for "me" finds only a body, sensations, emotions, thought patterns, and opinions that we identify with and become defensive about. No static self is ever found: "The house itself is empty. There is nobody home."[^3]
- The sense of [[Ego|ego]] is essentially a feeling of separation between "me" and "other". It is held in place only by constant exercise, and the [[Five Hindrances|hindrances]] are that exercise.[^4]
- Every evil deed and every example of heartlessness stems from this false sense of "me" as distinct from everything else.[^5]
- Once the illusion is seen through, one is left free to use the "I" concept or not, as the situation requires.[^6]
- In pure [[Mindfulness|mindfulness]] there is no watcher, only watching.[^7]
- Western counterpart: [[Bundle Theory of Self]].
