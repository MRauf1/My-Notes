# Definition
> [!info] Intuition (Expertise)[^1]
> Gut feeling understood "not [as] a mysterious force, but an incorporated history of experience": the practical, embodied side of intellectual work on which explicit knowledge builds.

# Properties
- Experts act from this intuition rather than explicit plans: "Real experts… don't make plans" (Flyvbjerg, 2001, following the Dreyfus model of skill acquisition).[^2]
- Becoming an expert requires the freedom to make your own decisions and the mistakes that teach you.[^2]
- "Civilization advances by extending the number of important operations which we can perform without thinking about them." (Whitehead) (see [[Habit]])[^3]
- **Somatic markers.** "Emotions organize — rather than disrupt — rational thinking." In the Iowa Gambling Task, participants' skin-conductance responses began to anticipate the risky card decks before they could explain why they avoided them (Bechara et al., 1997): "Our intuitive mind learns, and responds, even without our conscious awareness."[^4] The *unconscious* part is contested: when probed more carefully, participants often already had conscious knowledge of which decks were bad (Maia and McClelland, 2004).
