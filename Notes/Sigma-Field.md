# Definition
> [!info] Sigma-Field ($\sigma$-Algebra)[^1]
> A collection $\mathcal{B}$ of [[Subset|subsets]] of a [[Sample Space]] $\mathcal{C}$ is a $\sigma$-field if
> 1. $\mathcal{C} \in \mathcal{B}$ (equivalently, $\mathcal{B} \neq \emptyset$),
> 2. $A \in \mathcal{B} \implies A^c \in \mathcal{B}$ (closed under [[Set Complement|complements]]),
> 3. $A_1, A_2, \dots \in \mathcal{B} \implies \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty A_n \in \mathcal{B}$ (closed under [[Countable Set|countable]] [[Set Union|unions]]).
>
> Its members are the [[Event|events]].

The $\sigma$-field answers "what should the collection of events be?". It must be rich enough to contain every event of interest, but for uncountable $\mathcal{C}$ (e.g. $\mathbb{R}$) it cannot in general be the whole [[Power Set]], since no reasonable probability can be assigned to every subset; the standard choice on $\mathbb{R}$ is the Borel $\sigma$-field generated by the intervals.

# Properties
- If $\mathcal{C}$ is finite, the [[Power Set]] $\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{C})$ can be taken as $\mathcal{B}$.
- Closed under countable [[Set Intersection|intersections]], by [[DeMorgan's Laws]]: $\bigcap_n A_n = \left(\bigcup_n A_n^c\right)^c$.
- $\emptyset = \mathcal{C}^c \in \mathcal{B}$.
- The domain of a [[Probability|probability set function]]; the triple $(\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{B}, P)$ is a [[Probability Space]].
