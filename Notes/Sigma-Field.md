# Definition
> [!info] Sigma-Field ($\sigma$-Algebra)[^1]
> A collection $\mathcal{B}$ of [[Subset|subsets]] of a [[Sample Space]] $\mathcal{C}$ is a $\sigma$-field if
> 1. $\mathcal{C} \in \mathcal{B}$ (equivalently, $\mathcal{B} \neq \emptyset$),
> 2. $A \in \mathcal{B} \implies A^c \in \mathcal{B}$ (closed under [[Set Complement|complements]]),
> 3. $A_1, A_2, \dots \in \mathcal{B} \implies \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty A_n \in \mathcal{B}$ (closed under [[Countable Set|countable]] [[Set Union|unions]]).
>
> Its members are the [[Event|events]].

The $\sigma$-field answers "what should the collection of events be?". It must be rich enough to contain every event of interest, but for uncountable $\mathcal{C}$ (e.g. $\mathbb{R}$) it cannot in general be the whole [[Power Set]], since no reasonable probability can be assigned to every subset; the standard choice on $\mathbb{R}$ is the Borel $\sigma$-field generated by the intervals.

> [!info] $\sigma$-Algebra (Measure-Theoretic Definition)[^2]
> Let $X$ be a nonempty [[Set]]. A $\sigma$-algebra on $X$ is an [[Algebra of Sets|algebra]] $\mathcal{M} \subseteq \mathcal{P}(X)$ that is closed under [[Countable Set|countable]] [[Set Union|unions]]. Equivalently, $\mathcal{M}$ is nonempty and
> $$
> \begin{align}
> E \in \mathcal{M} \implies E^c \in \mathcal{M}, \qquad \{E_j\}_{j=1}^\infty \subseteq \mathcal{M} \implies \bigcup_{j=1}^\infty E_j \in \mathcal{M}.
> \end{align}
> $$

Closure under complements is **required**: it is inherited from the definition of an algebra, so a $\sigma$-algebra is closed under complements, countable unions, and (by De Morgan) countable intersections. The underlying set $X$ may be of any cardinality.

A $\sigma$-algebra is an algebraic structure on a family of sets, in the same spirit as a [[Ring]] or a [[Field]]: the operations are complement (negation), countable union (OR), and countable intersection (AND). Formally it is a $\sigma$-complete Boolean algebra. A set $X$ generally carries many $\sigma$-algebras, ordered by inclusion from the coarsest $\{\emptyset, X\}$ to the finest $\mathcal{P}(X)$. The power set is not always used because, for uncountable $X$ such as $\mathbb{R}$, it contains sets (e.g. the [[Vitali Set]]) to which no geometrically reasonable measure can be assigned ([[Banach-Tarski Paradox]]). For countable $X$, $\mathcal{P}(X)$ is always safe.

The "countable" restriction is about the **operations**, not the size of $X$. Closure under uncountable unions would be too strong: if a measure had to be additive over uncountable disjoint unions, then $[0,1] = \bigcup_{x \in [0,1]} \{x\}$ would force length $0$.

# Properties
- If $\mathcal{C}$ is finite, the [[Power Set]] $\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{C})$ can be taken as $\mathcal{B}$.
- Closed under countable [[Set Intersection|intersections]], by [[DeMorgan's Laws]]: $\bigcap_n A_n = \left(\bigcup_n A_n^c\right)^c$.
- $\emptyset = \mathcal{C}^c \in \mathcal{B}$.
- $\mathcal{P}(X)$ and $\{\emptyset, X\}$ are $\sigma$-algebras on any $X$.[^3]
- If $X$ is uncountable, the [[Countable-Cocountable Sigma-Algebra]] is a $\sigma$-algebra.[^3]
- The intersection of any family of $\sigma$-algebras on $X$ is a $\sigma$-algebra; hence every $\mathcal{E} \subseteq \mathcal{P}(X)$ has a smallest $\sigma$-algebra containing it, the [[Generated Sigma-Algebra]].[^4]
- Important instances: [[Borel Sigma-Algebra]], [[Product Sigma-Algebra]], the Lebesgue $\sigma$-algebra of [[Lebesgue Measure]].
- An [[Algebra of Sets|algebra]] closed under countable disjoint unions is already a $\sigma$-algebra.
- A pair $(X, \mathcal{M})$ is a [[Measurable Space]]; the domain of a [[Measure (Measure Theory)|measure]].
- The domain of a [[Probability|probability set function]]; the triple $(\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{B}, P)$ is a [[Probability Space]].
