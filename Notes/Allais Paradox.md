# Definition
> [!info] Allais Paradox (Allais, 1953)[^1]
> Actual agents systematically violate the Independence axiom of the [[Von Neumann-Morgenstern Utility Theorem]]. Offered a choice between
> - 1A: $1M for certain, and 1B: $1M with probability /bin/zsh.89$, $5M with probability /bin/zsh.10$, $0 with probability /bin/zsh.01$,
>
> and a choice between
> - 2A: $1M with probability /bin/zsh.11$, $0 otherwise, and 2B: $5M with probability /bin/zsh.10$, $0 otherwise,
>
> most people prefer 1A to 1B and 2B to 2A. But each pair differs only by replacing a common /bin/zsh.89$ chance of $1M with a common /bin/zsh.89$ chance of $0, so Independence requires the same preference in both.

# Properties
- No [[Utility Function|utility function]] rationalizes the typical choices as [[Expected Utility|expected utility]] maximization: A \succ 1B$ requires /bin/zsh.11\,u(1\text{M}) > 0.10\,u(5\text{M}) + 0.01\,u(0)$, while B \succ 2A$ requires the reverse inequality.
- Used to reject Preference Consistency in the [[Representation Theorem Argument]].

[^1]: [A Critical Introduction to Formal Epistemology](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/9XYCZDPF?page=50)
