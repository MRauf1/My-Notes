# Definition
> [!info] Pre-Paradigm Science[^1]
> The early developmental stage of a science, characterised by continual competition among several distinct schools with incommensurable views of nature, each roughly compatible with observation and method, before any single [[Paradigm (Philosophy of Science)|paradigm]] achieves consensus.

# Properties
- The schools differ not by failures of method (all are "scientific") but by their ways of seeing the world; observation drastically restricts admissible belief but cannot alone determine it, so an arbitrary element of personal and historical accident always shapes a community's beliefs ([[Underdetermination of Theory by Evidence]]).[^1]
- Historically typical (e.g. motion before Aristotle, heat before Black, chemistry before Boyle), except in fields like mathematics and astronomy with prehistoric paradigms; the road to consensus is extraordinarily arduous.[^2]
- Without a paradigm all facts seem equally relevant, so fact-gathering is nearly random and limited to data ready to hand, including esoteric data from crafts (medicine, calendar-making, metallurgy), which is why technology often plays a vital role in new sciences; natural histories often omit the very details later found illuminating.[^3]
- Marked by frequent deep debates over legitimate methods, problems, and standards, which define schools rather than produce agreement; such debates recur just before and during revolutions.[^4]
- Divergences disappear, apparently once and for all, through the triumph of one school that emphasised only part of the inchoate pool of information; when a synthesis attracts most of the next generation, older schools vanish by conversion, and holdouts are read out of the profession.[^5]
- Books are the vehicle of research in this stage; in mature sciences, books become textbooks or retrospectives and research moves to articles, so laymen can no longer follow progress from original reports.[^6]
- *Creator's note*: the competition between LLM-based and world-model-based approaches can be read as a pre-paradigm era within current deep learning.
