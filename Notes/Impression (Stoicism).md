# Definition
> [!info] Impression (Stoicism)[^1]
> (*Phantasia*) An appearance that strikes the mind, as soon as consciousness has an image of something, together with an implicit claim about what it is and its value. The occurrence of impressions is not up to us; our response to them ([[Assent (Stoicism)|assent]]) is.

# Properties
- "Making use of impressions" means not being taken in by appearances but testing them with reason to see whether they deserve assent. The right use of impressions is the one thing that is up to us, the master faculty given by the gods, and the only thing to take pride in.[^2]
- Every action is determined by an impression, which may be right or wrong.[^3]
- Reason itself is "a systematic arrangement of impressions of various kinds", and so can examine itself; a philosopher's most crucial work is to assess and distinguish impressions and accept only assessed ones.[^4]
- "Just as Socrates used to say that we shouldn't live an unexamined life, so we shouldn't accept an unexamined impression."[^5]
- *Cognitive impressions* (*kataleptic*) are those that grasp their object clearly and warrant assent.[^6]
- Involuntary reactions to impressions are [[Propatheia|first movements]], not yet passions.
