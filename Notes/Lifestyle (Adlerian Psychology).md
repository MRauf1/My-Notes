# Definition
> [!info] Lifestyle (Adlerian Psychology)[^1]
> A person's tendencies of thought and action in life: how one sees the world and how one sees oneself. It is roughly what is usually called personality or character, plus the meaning one gives to oneself and the world. In Adlerian psychology a lifestyle is **chosen**, not innate, so it can be chosen again.

# Properties
- **Change is always possible.** People can change at any time, whatever their environment. They fail to change because they keep deciding not to, since the familiar lifestyle is easier and safer despite its complaints.[^2]
- **Change takes courage.** Changing brings anxiety, while not changing brings disappointment. Unhappiness is a lack of the "courage to be happy", not a lack of ability.[^3]
- **Living in possibility.** Never acting keeps alive the illusion that "I could do it if only I had the time". Changing one's lifestyle means acting, for example submitting the manuscript and accepting rejection, which allows growth or redirection. Cf. [[Self-Handicapping]].[^4]
- First step: decide to stop the current lifestyle. When the way of giving meaning to the world and oneself changes, interactions and behavior have to change too.[^5]
- One accepts that one alone chose one's lifestyle, so blaming others is a [[Life-Lie]].[^6]
- A lifestyle concerns the here and now. A past that looks like a straight line only looks that way because of constant resolutions not to change. The future is a blank page. See [[Energeial Life]].[^7]
- Follows from [[Teleology (Adlerian Psychology)]].
- **Lifestyle for being loved.** The original childhood lifestyle aims at "How can I be loved?" Leaving it is [[Self-Reliance (Adlerian Psychology)|self-reliance]].[^kk1]
- **Time limit.** Asked whether there is a time limit for change, Adler said yes: "Until the day before you meet your maker." The future cannot be seen because it holds infinite possibilities, and that is why we can be the masters of our destinies.[^kk2]

# Evidence
- **Correction: lifestyle is not freely chosen.** Twin studies estimate personality heritability at about 40% (Vukasović & Bratko, 2015), and early temperament predicts adult traits ([[Big Five Personality Traits]]).
- **Supported kernel: personality changes.** Traits change substantially across adulthood (Roberts, Walton & Viechtbauer, 2006). A meta-analysis found that clinical interventions produce lasting trait change, especially in emotional stability (Roberts et al., 2017). So "can change" is supported, while "chose it entirely" is not.
