# Definition
> [!info] Crisis (Philosophy of Science)[^1]
> A period of pronounced professional insecurity in a science, generated by the persistent failure of the puzzles of [[Normal Science|normal science]] to come out as they should; the failure of existing rules is the prelude to a search for new ones. Crises are a necessary precondition for the emergence of novel theories.

# Properties
- Symptoms: complexity increasing faster than accuracy, with discrepancies corrected in one place reappearing elsewhere (Ptolemaic astronomy); and proliferation of versions of a theory (as many versions of phlogiston theory as pneumatic chemists).[^2]
- Alternative theories can always be constructed for the same data ([[Underdetermination of Theory by Evidence]]), but scientists invent them only in pre-paradigm periods and crises: "retooling is an extravagance to be reserved for the occasion that demands it", and crisis signals that the occasion has arrived.[^3]
- Scientists in crisis do not renounce the paradigm or treat anomalies as counterinstances; a paradigm is declared invalid only when an alternative candidate is available, and rejecting one paradigm is always simultaneously accepting another, judged by comparing both with nature and with each other. Defenders devise ad hoc articulations to eliminate conflict.[^4]
- Crisis blurs the paradigm and loosens the rules of normal research through divergent, partially successful articulations, until even standard solutions are questioned; research then resembles the [[Pre-Paradigm Science|pre-paradigm]] period, but with a smaller, better-defined locus of difference.[^5]
- All crises close in one of three ways: normal science eventually handles the problem; the problem is labelled and set aside for a future generation; or a new candidate paradigm emerges and a battle over its acceptance ensues ([[Scientific Revolution]]).[^6]
- A new paradigm may emerge in embryo before crisis is recognised, or long after breakdown; in crisis, scientists turn to philosophical analysis, and by concentrating attention on a narrow area, crisis proliferates discoveries.[^7]
