# Definition
> [!info] Definition (Snell's Law)[^1]
> At a boundary between two materials, an incident light ray bends, an effect called [[Refraction]]. The angles of the incident and refracted rays, measured from the surface normal, are related to the indices of refraction of the two materials by
> $$
> \begin{align}
> n_1 \sin(\theta_1) = n_2 \sin(\theta_2)
> \end{align}
> $$
> where $\theta_1, \theta_2$ are the angles in region 1 and region 2, and $n_1, n_2$ are the [[Index of Refraction|indices of refraction]] of the materials in region 1 and region 2, respectively.

# Properties
- The amount of bending depends on the change in the speed of light between the two materials and the orientation of the incoming ray relative to the interface surface.
- Can be derived by matching the wavelength of light projected along the interface boundary on each side of the boundary.
- Underlies how a [[Lens]] bends light rays to focus them onto a sensor.
