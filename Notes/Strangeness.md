# Definition
> [!info] Strangeness[^1]
> A quantum number assigned to strongly interacting particles (hadrons), introduced by Gell-Mann and Nishijima to classify the many particles discovered in cosmic-ray and accelerator experiments.

# Properties
- Conserved, like [[Electric Charge]], in reactions proceeding via the strong (nuclear) interaction, but not necessarily conserved in weak-interaction processes such as beta decay.
- Superseded in its microscopic explanation by the quark model: strangeness is now understood to count the net number of strange quarks in a hadron.
- One of several conserved quantum numbers, alongside [[Electric Charge]] and baryon number, that classify and constrain allowed reactions within the [[Four Fundamental Interactions|strong interaction]].

[^1]: [The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. I, Ch. 2: Basic Physics](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_02.html)
