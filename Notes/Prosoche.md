# Definition
> [!info] Prosoche[^1]
> ("Attention to oneself") Continuous vigilance over one's own impressions, judgements and acts of will in the present moment. Hadot calls it the fundamental Stoic spiritual attitude: "self-consciousness which never sleeps", through which the philosopher is fully aware of, and fully wills, what he does at each instant. It is the Stoic counterpart of [[Mindfulness]].

# Properties
- "Attend to your impressions, watch over them without sleeping, for it is no small thing that you are guarding" (Epictetus, *Discourses* 4.3.7). The progressing Stoic watches himself "as he would his own deadliest enemy, lying in wait" (*Enchiridion* 48).[^1]
- Its object is the "ruling faculty" (*hegemonikon*), the helmsman of the soul. Because the Stoic identifies the self with this faculty of judgement, mindful meditation and cognitive psychotherapy are already one practice for him.[^2]
- Postponing attention fosters a habit of complacency and inattention.[^3] It can be practised in any activity, e.g. by periodically asking *Quo vadis?*: where is this judgement leading, toward happiness or ruin?[^3]
- **Here and now.** The sphere of control is centred on the present moment, since the past and the future are not up to us. Passions are always caused by the past or the future, while the tiny present moment is always bearable and controllable (Hadot). The present is where lessons from the past are learned and preparation for the future is made.[^4]
	- The ability to project thought across time becomes a curse when planning turns into anxious [[Worry]] and reflection turns into depressive [[Rumination]].[^5]
	- Immersion in the present, without comparing it to the past, recovers its novelty and the sense of wonder with which philosophy begins (*Theaetetus* 155d).[^5]
	- Linked to mortality and urgency: remembering death raises the value of each present moment ([[Memento Mori]]).[^6]
- **Pause.** No important decision should be made in the grip of rage or despair; first pause, observe and recover composure. Giving in to an impulse strengthens it, while questioning it builds a habit of resistance ([[Habit]]).[^7]
- Its natural product is *cognitive katharsis*, the separation of one's own judgements from perception of the external world ([[Objective Representation (Stoicism)]], [[Cognitive Distancing]]). It keeps the fundamental rule, the [[Dichotomy of Control]], always "at hand" (*procheiron*).[^8]
- Hadot lists the here and now as one of the four features of the Stoic attitude ([[Stoicism]]).[^9]
- **Neglected by early CBT.** It is probably the most notable aspect of Stoicism that early [[Cognitive Behavioral Therapy]] neglected.[^10] Later "third-wave" therapies (mindfulness-based cognitive therapy, acceptance and commitment therapy) put it back.
- **Evidence.** Mindfulness-based cognitive therapy reduces the risk of depressive relapse compared with usual care (hazard ratio about 0.69 in an individual-patient-data meta-analysis; Kuyken et al., 2016).
