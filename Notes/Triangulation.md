# Definition
> [!info] Triangulation[^1]
> Triangulation is a method of measuring [[Distance|distance]] indirectly by observing the angles to a distant object from two known points, rather than directly comparing it against a reference length such as a meter stick.

# Properties
- Agrees with meter-stick measurements of distance wherever both can be used, since physical space closely follows Euclidean geometry at terrestrial and solar-system scales.
- Extends to astronomical distances by using two separated observation points (e.g., two telescopes at different locations on earth) to obtain the two required angles, as used to measure the height of a satellite or the distance to the moon.
- Fixing the absolute scale of the solar system requires only one absolute triangulation measurement, such as the distance to a nearby planetoid; combined with the relative distances of the planets from astronomical observation, this yields all absolute distances, such as the earth-to-sun distance.

[^1]: [The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. I, Ch. 5: Time and Distance](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_05.html)
