# Definition
> [!info] Gravitational Potential[^1]
> $$\Psi(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_i -\frac{Gm_i}{r_i}$$
> The scalar field $\Psi$ generated by masses $m_i$, defined so that the [[Gravitational Field]] is recovered as $\mathbf{C} = -\nabla\Psi$, and the [[Gravitational Potential Energy|potential energy]] of a mass $m$ placed at a point is $U = m\Psi$.

# Properties
- Related to the [[Gravitational Field]] exactly as [[Potential Energy]] is related to [[Force]]: the potential is the potential energy per unit mass, just as the field is the force per unit mass.
- Being a scalar, the potential due to several masses is found by ordinary addition, unlike the field, which must be summed as a vector; the field can always be recovered afterward by differentiation.
- For a spherical shell of mass $m$ and radius $a$ (see [[Shell Theorem]]), $\Psi = -Gm/r$ outside the shell ($r>a$) and the constant $\Psi = -Gm/a$ everywhere inside; the field, and hence the force, vanishes inside precisely because the potential is constant there.
- Defined only up to an additive constant, since only differences (and hence gradients) of potential are physically meaningful.

[^1]: [The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. I, Ch. 14: Work and Potential Energy (B)](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_14.html)
