# Review[^1]

Simulation is usually based on the real; simulacrum is usually based on either the real or unreal.

Hyperreality is when the simulacra supersedes the physical/real world. It dictates how the real world should be experienced and constructed. Physical reality feels dull by comparison to the hyperreality.

Simulation is the process of simulating something real that could in turn take this away from the real into unreal.

Simulacrum is the copy/representation itself that comes about because of the simulation. In particular, it is a copy of something that never had an original or whose original has been rendered irrelevant. In other words, it is a representation of other images/signs (simulacra).

Successive phases of the image:
1) It is the reflection of profound reality.
2) It masks and denatures the profound reality.
3) It masks the absence of profound reality.
4) It has no relation to any reality whatsoever. It is its own pure simulacrum.
In the first 2 stages, there is still a dichotomy between the real and the unreal. In the latter two, you enter simulation, the objects produced are simulacra, and the resulting cultural space is the hyperreal.

He uses the various examples like Disneyland, Watergate, and so on to show how deluded our society is and how we are already living in the 4th stage.
