# Definition
> [!info] Dukkha[^1]
> (Unsatisfactoriness, suffering) The second of the [[Three Marks of Existence]]. It is not just bodily agony but the deep, subtle, all-pervasive sense of unsatisfactoriness that is part of every mental treadmill. Under every joy runs an undercurrent of tension that the moment, however great, is going to end.

# Properties
- **Source.** It comes from our own mental habits, not from the world: the endless treadmill of [[Three Unwholesome Roots|grasping]] pleasure, rejecting pain, and ignoring the neutral 90% of experience.[^2]
- The cause of suffering is [[Desire|desire]] (craving), which humans mistake for the source of happiness. Suffering inevitably follows clinging.[^3]
- What we really seek is not surface goals but the feeling of relief when a drive is satisfied. That relief fades "like smoke in the wind", leaving the "if only" syndrome.[^4]
- Total control would mean "perfect happiness", but it is impossible; historical holders of ultimate power were not happy. Learning to control one's own mind is difficult, but "the difficult is preferable to the impossible".[^5]
- **"Pain is inevitable, suffering is not."** Pain has two components: the bare sensation and our physical and mental *resistance* to it. Relax the physical tension, then the wordless mental "go away" rejection. The barrier between "me" and "the pain" dissolves. Only sensation remains, and suffering is gone.[^6]
- Identifying with sensation ("*I* have a pain in *my* leg") adds the concept of "I" and a gap between reality and awareness, which amplifies the pain. Without "I", pain is just a flowing pattern of energy (see [[Anatta]]).[^7]
- Some pain is unavoidable, so time invested in learning to deal with discomfort is well spent. The technique generalizes to anxiety and depression. Buddhists call this pragmatic optimism, not pessimism.[^8]
- It sounds bleak only from the ordinary level of mind at which the treadmill runs. Beneath that level lies a learnable way of perceiving beyond pleasure and pain.[^9]
- See also [[Suffering]].
