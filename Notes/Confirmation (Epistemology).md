# Definition
> [!info] Degree-of-Belief-Raising Theory of Confirmation[^1]
> For agent $S$, [[Proposition|proposition]] $H$, evidence $E$, and time $t$:
> $$
> \begin{align}
> E \text{ confirms } H &\iff P_{S,t}(H \mid E) > P_{S,t}(H) \\
> E \text{ disconfirms } H &\iff P_{S,t}(H \mid E) < P_{S,t}(H) \\
> E \text{ is independent of } H &\iff P_{S,t}(H \mid E) = P_{S,t}(H)
> \end{align}
> $$
> where $P(H \mid E)$ is the [[Conditional Belief|conditional belief]] in $H$ given $E$.

# Properties
- $E$ can confirm $H$ without making $H$ likely; confirmation only requires that $H$ becomes more likely than it would otherwise be, and the increase in [[Credence|credence]] can be arbitrarily tiny.
- The same evidence can confirm two mutually incompatible hypotheses, since confirmation is a comparative, not absolute, relation.
- Confirmation requires only correlation between $E$ and $H$, not causation.
- Confirmation does not distribute over logical entailment: $E$ confirming $H$ does not imply $E$ confirms every proposition that $H$ entails.

[^1]: [A Critical Introduction to Formal Epistemology](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/9XYCZDPF?page=10)
