# Definition
> [!info] Appropriate Action (Stoicism)[^1]
> (*Kathēkon*) An act that is in accord with nature and admits of rational justification, and is therefore the material for virtue. For [[Epictetus]], appropriate actions are generally given by our relationships and roles.

# Properties
- Preferred [[Indifferent (Stoicism)|indifferents]] and the appropriate actions concerning them are in accord with our nature.[^1]
- **Roles**: each person has natural and acquired relationships (son, father, brother, citizen, neighbour, ruler, subject). Determining what is reasonable requires considering both the value of externals and one's own role; one should "act the role you've been assigned well, but the role was chosen by someone else".[^2]
- Do not take on a role beyond your capabilities: you disgrace yourself in it and forgo the role you could have performed well.[^3]
- Each person knows the price at which he sells himself; "don't sell [your will] cheap".[^4]
- Behave toward others as you ought regardless of how they treat you: "My brother shouldn't have treated me as he did." "No, … but that's his concern."[^5]
- Social duties should be performed without compromising trustworthiness and self-respect, which are what one contributes to one's city.[^6]
- A citizen should act like a hand or foot that understood the whole, having inclinations only by reference to the whole; lacking foreknowledge, we select what naturally presents itself for selection.[^7]
- A good person acts only for the sake of doing right, not appearances; the reward of acting rightly is acting rightly, as the reward of writing "Dion" correctly is the writing.[^8]
- Trained by the second of the [[Three Disciplines (Epictetus)]].
