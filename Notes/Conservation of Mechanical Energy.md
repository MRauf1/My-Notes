# Definition
> [!info] Conservation of Mechanical Energy[^1]
> $$T + U = \text{constant}$$
> If only [[Conservative Force|conservative forces]] act on an object, the sum of its [[Kinetic Energy]] $T$ and [[Potential Energy]] $U$ remains constant throughout its motion.

# Properties
- Follows directly from combining the [[Work-Energy Theorem]] (that $\Delta T$ equals the work done) with the definition of a conservative force (that same work equals $-\Delta U$): the two changes cancel.
- A special case of the fully general [[Conservation of Energy]], restricted to situations where no energy leaves the mechanical (kinetic + potential) form, e.g. no friction, heat, or other dissipation.
- Motivates the modern view that all fundamental forces are conservative: apparently nonconservative forces such as friction are macroscopic averages over microscopic interactions that are themselves conservative, with the "lost" mechanical energy converted into heat.

[^1]: [The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. I, Ch. 14: Work and Potential Energy (B)](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_14.html)
