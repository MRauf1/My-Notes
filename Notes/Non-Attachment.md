# Definition
> [!info] Non-Attachment[^1]
> Approaching the whole of life (thoughts, emotions, events and circumstances) without *clinging* (grasping at what we like and trying to keep it forever) or *aversion* (pushing away what we dislike at all costs). Both are forms of attachment. To live non-attachedly is to feel impulses, think thoughts and experience life without getting hooked by mental narratives of how things should or should never be, remaining present and non-judgmentally aware.

# Properties
- **Root of suffering.** By the second noble truth, attachment causes suffering ([[Dukkha]], [[Craving]]). Pain is inevitable, but suffering is an optional extra produced by attachments, which try to deny that everything is impermanent ([[Anicca]]).[^1] See [[Suffering]].
- **Meditation has no target state.** Meditation is not about reaching bliss or calm. In [[Vipassana Meditation|vipassana]] one lets thoughts and feelings rise and fall without forcing them in or out; trying to drive out agitation "is just more agitation". One views passing mental events as a spectator, returning to the breath whenever carried off by a narrative ([[Breath as Meditation Object]]).[^2] The mind is treated as a sixth sense whose objects are thoughts.[^3]
- **Versus Stoicism.** Stoics can seem attached to tranquility: the perfect Stoic adapts thinking to stay undisturbed; the perfect Buddhist treats thinking itself as another circumstance to observe non-judgmentally (compare [[Apatheia]]).[^3]
- **Pain evidence.** After about 80 minutes of mindfulness training, participants rated experimentally induced pain as less intense and unpleasant, with reductions of 11–93% across participants, and more than distraction did; the training helped even when participants weren't actively meditating (Zeidan et al., 2010; 2011). Attachment to thoughts about pain (anticipation and struggle) is much of the distress.[^4]
- **Non-attachment scale.** Self-reported non-attachment correlates positively with well-being and mindfulness and negatively with anxiety and depression (Sahdra, Shaver and Brown, 2010).
- **Objection: attachment motivates.** Non-attachment need not mean resignation, just as Stoic acceptance doesn't. Most motivational techniques try to change how you *feel*, which is an attachment to an emotional state. One can note reluctance as "passing weather" and act anyway (see [[Procrastination]]; this is the committed-action principle of Acceptance and Commitment Therapy, which has meta-analytic support, A-Tjak et al., 2015).[^5]
- **Vipassana vendetta.** On retreat, the mind, conditioned to attach to storylines, inflates tiny irritations into hate campaigns.[^6]
- Related: [[Clinging to Good Experiences]], [[Mindfulness]], [[Meditation]], [[Backwards Law]], [[Rational Emotive Behavior Therapy]].
