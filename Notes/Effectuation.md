# Definition
> [!info] Effectuation (Sarasvathy)[^1]
> A mode of decision-making under uncertainty used by expert entrepreneurs. *Causal* reasoning starts from a given goal and selects among available means to reach it. *Effectual* reasoning starts from the means at hand (who you are, what you know, whom you know) and imagines which ends, or provisional next directions, those means make possible, allowing the destination itself to change.

# Types
Principles of effectuation:[^2]
- **Bird in hand.** Start with your means; don't wait for the perfect opportunity.
- **Affordable loss.** Decide on a next step by how much you could lose if it failed, not by how large the payoff would be if it succeeded; if the loss is tolerable, take the step and see what happens. This echoes the Stoic focus on the worst case ([[Stoic Exercises]]).
- **Crazy quilt.** Form partnerships with self-selected stakeholders who commit resources and thereby shape the goals.
- **Lemonade.** Treat surprises as resources rather than deviations from a plan.
- **Pilot in the plane.** Control what you can rather than predict an unpredictable future.

# Properties
- **Flexibility over vision.** Kayes argues that the key entrepreneurial skill is improvisational flexibility, not only about the route but also a willingness to change the destination, which rigid focus on one goal can stifle.[^1]
- **Evidence.** A meta-analysis of 48 samples found that effectual principles (means-based action, partnerships, leveraging contingencies) correlate positively with new-venture performance (Read, Song and Smit, 2009). Expert entrepreneurs used effectual reasoning far more than MBA students in think-aloud protocols (Dew et al., 2009).
- A counterweight to rigid [[Goal-Setting Theory|goal setting]]; see also [[Intolerance of Uncertainty]].
