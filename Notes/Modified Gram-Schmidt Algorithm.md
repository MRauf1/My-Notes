# Definition
> [!info] Definition 1 (Modified Gram-Schmidt Orthogonalization)[^1]
> A rearrangement of the classical [[Gram-Schmidt Algorithm]] that computes the reduced [[QR Decomposition]] $A = Q_1 R$: as soon as each new $q_k$ is computed, every remaining column is immediately orthogonalized against it, so $R$ is generated by rows rather than by columns.

![[Modified Gram-Schmidt Algorithm.png]]

It is mathematically equivalent to, but numerically superior to, classical Gram-Schmidt.[^1]

> [!abstract] Theorem 2 (Rank Deficiency)[^1]
> In either version of Gram-Schmidt, $r_{kk} = 0$ at step $k$ iff column $k$ of $A$ is linearly dependent on the first $k-1$ columns, so $A$ is not of full column rank, and the algorithm as stated cannot continue.

Unlike the column-oriented Gram-Schmidt procedures (classical or modified), the row-oriented modified Gram-Schmidt procedure allows [[QR Factorization with Column Pivoting|column pivoting]], which identifies a maximal linearly independent set of columns and handles tiny-but-nonzero $r_{kk}$ (near rank deficiency) gracefully.[^1]

> [!abstract] Theorem 3 (Least Squares via Augmented Matrix)[^2]
> Even modified Gram-Schmidt loses orthogonality among the columns of $Q_1$ through cancellation when $A$ is ill-conditioned (though much less than classical Gram-Schmidt), so computing $c_1 = Q_1^T b$ explicitly is inadvisable. Instead, treat $b$ as an $(n+1)$-st column and factor
> $$
> \begin{align}
> \begin{bmatrix} A & b \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} Q_1 & q_{n+1} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} R & c_1 \\ 0^T & \rho \end{bmatrix}
> \end{align}
> $$
> then solve the triangular system $Rx = c_1$ for the [[Linear Least Squares Problem|least squares]] solution.

> [!info] Definition 4 (Reorthogonalization)[^2]
> The orthogonality of $Q_1$ from either version of Gram-Schmidt can be significantly enhanced by repeating the procedure on $Q_1$. It could be repeated as a form of [[Iterative Refinement|iterative refinement]], but a single reorthogonalization is typically enough.

# Properties
- Storage: $a_k$ and $q_k$ can share storage, but $Q_1$ and $R$ need separate storage, a disadvantage relative to [[Householder QR Factorization|Householder]], where $R$ and the implicit $Q$ overwrite $A$. In exchange, Gram-Schmidt gives $Q_1$ explicitly.[^3]
