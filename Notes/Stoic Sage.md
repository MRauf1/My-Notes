# Definition
> [!info] Stoic Sage[^1]
> The ideal Stoic: a person whose will consists of a perfectly consistent set of true beliefs about the value of things, and who is therefore the only one truly free, happy, and capable of love. Epictetus doubted any living example existed: "By the gods, I'd love to see a Stoic."

# Properties
- Is sick, in danger, dying, in exile, or out of favour, and happy.[^2]
- Desires are never disappointed, aversions never fail, and there is no grief, fear, or turmoil, while natural and acquired relationships are preserved.[^3]
- Is invincible, since he enters only contests about what is subject to will.[^4]
- Is self-sufficient: able to live with himself and converse with himself, as Zeus does alone; **loneliness** is not solitude but helplessness, the lack of anyone trustworthy to help.[^5]
- Only the universe is entirely self-sufficient.[^6]
- Still experiences [[Propatheia|first movements]] and [[Eupatheia|good feelings]].
- The goal of [[Moral Progress (Stoicism)]].
