# Definition
> [!info] Feeling of Inferiority[^1]
> A subjective value judgment that one is lacking, felt when one cannot reach one's ideals. It is a subjective interpretation, not an objective inferiority. It is the counterpart of the [[Pursuit of Superiority]], and in itself it is healthy, because it can drive striving and growth.

# Properties
- **Subjective, therefore changeable.** Objective facts cannot be changed, but subjective interpretations can. The same trait, such as being short, can be read as a disadvantage or an advantage. Subjectivity leaves the choice open.[^2]
- **Interpersonal in origin.** It arises from comparison with others, since value is social. See [[All Problems Are Interpersonal Relationship Problems]].[^3]
- **Healthy form.** A healthy feeling of inferiority comes from comparing oneself with one's *ideal self*, not with others.[^4]
- **Compensation.** No one can bear the feeling for long. The healthiest compensation is striving and growth. People without the courage for that slip into an [[Inferiority Complex]] or a [[Superiority Complex]].[^5]
- It arises within vertical relationships. In horizontal relationships ("equal but not the same") there is no room for inferiority complexes. See [[Vertical and Horizontal Relationships]].[^6]
- Being recognized by others seems to wipe it away, which drives the [[Desire for Recognition]].[^7]
- Compare [[Social Comparison Theory]] and [[Self-Esteem]].
- **Universal in childhood.** All humans live with feelings of inferiority in childhood. This is a major premise of Adlerian psychology, and the feeling has always driven effort and growth. At the species level, humans compensated for physical inferiority with the [[Division of Labor (Adlerian Psychology)|division of labor]].[^kk1]
