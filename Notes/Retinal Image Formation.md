# Definition
> [!info] Definition (Retinal Image Formation)[^1]
> The optical imaging of the light stimulus incident at the [[Cornea]] into an image at the light-sensitive [[Photoreceptor Cell|photoreceptors]] of the [[Retina]]. It is the first in a series of visual transformations — retinal image $\to$ photoreceptor neural response $\to$ optic nerve representation $\to$ cortical representation — by which the visual pathways encode light into visual experience.

# Properties
- Because the optics of the [[Cornea]] and [[Eye Lens]] are not perfect, the retinal image is a blurred copy of the object being imaged rather than an exact copy.
- Can be modeled as an instance of a [[Camera as Linear System|linear imaging system]] mapping a vector of scene light intensities to a vector of retinal image intensities; the eye's optics are approximately [[Shift-Invariant System|shift-invariant]] near the [[Fovea]], where image quality is fairly uniform across nearby positions.
- The blur introduced by this transformation is characterized by the eye's [[Point Spread Function]] (or, for one-dimensional stimuli, its [[Line Spread Function]]).
- The [[Optic Nerve]] carries the resulting neural signal onward, exiting the eye through the optic disk, a hole in the retina that contains no photoreceptors and is therefore the origin of the blind spot.
