# Definition
> [!info] Behavioral Activation (BA)[^1]
> A treatment for depression that schedules meaningful, rewarding or mastery-building activities. It counteracts the withdrawal and avoidance that maintain low mood: action comes first and mood follows (Jacobson et al., 1996; Martell et al.). Carnegie's Rule 1, "Keep busy", is the folk version: "The worried person must lose himself in action, lest he wither in despair."

# Properties
- **Evidence.**
	- In a component study, BA alone was as effective as full cognitive therapy for depression (Jacobson et al., 1996).
	- For severe depression it matched antidepressant medication (Dimidjian et al., 2006).
	- Meta-analyses confirm large effects (Cuijpers, van Straten and Warmerdam, 2007; Ekers et al., 2014).
	- Psychiatrists have long used "moral treatment" with occupational activity (the Quaker asylums Carnegie mentions), though without controlled tests.[^2]
- **The activity must engage.** Carnegie's widow found that housework done "mechanically, without using my mind" left room for worry. Absorbing work that is both mental and physical did not.[^3] This fits limited attentional capacity (see [[Attention]]). Carnegie's "law" that the mind can think of only one thing at a time is an overstatement, but demanding tasks do crowd out worry. Worry peaks in idle moments (see [[Boredom]], [[Distraction]]).[^4]
- **Temporary remedy or cure?**
	- *As distraction*, keeping busy reduces [[Rumination]] and negative mood in the short term (Nolen-Hoeksema, 1991). If it becomes a way of avoiding the feared content, it can maintain anxiety (see [[Distraction]], [[Exposure Therapy]]).
	- *As activation*, it changes the conditions that keep depression going: lost reinforcement, avoidance and inactivity. That makes it a mechanism of recovery, not just a symptom patch.
	- The difference is whether the activity approaches valued goals or escapes discomfort.
- Related: [[Major Depressive Disorder]], [[Flow State]], [[Worry]].
