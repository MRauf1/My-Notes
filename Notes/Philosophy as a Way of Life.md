# Definition
> [!info] Philosophy as a Way of Life[^1]
> The conception, dominant in ancient (especially post-classical) philosophy, of philosophy as something to live by and practise daily, rather than a purely theoretical discipline. For [[Epictetus]], knowledge comes not from reading and understanding theory but from putting it into practice, which alone turns information into knowledge.

# Properties
- Theory is to be digested, not "spewed up": show the changes to your command center, as athletes show their shoulders and a builder builds a house rather than lecturing on building.[^2]
- Interpreting Chrysippus makes one "a literary critic rather than a philosopher"; only putting the precepts into practice is worth pride.[^3]
- "We're resolute and fluent in the classroom … but if we're forced to turn to their practical application, you'll see us flounder like shipwrecked sailors."[^4]
- Read and write for the purpose of using impressions in accord with nature: measure a day not by "so many lines read" but by how one used inclination, desire, and aversion.[^5]
- One is "hardworking" only if one's efforts aim at keeping one's command center in accord with nature, not merely reading and writing.[^6]
- **Philosophy as therapy**: "a philosopher's school is a doctor's office"; you should leave in pain, not pleased. Philosophy promises nothing external: each life is the material of the art of living.[^7]
- Don't call yourself a philosopher or flaunt theories; act on them, and let actions rather than guise mark you (Euphrates, Socrates).[^8]
- Judgments are formed only by saying and hearing the same ideas daily while putting them to work.[^9]
- Beginners should avoid non-philosophical company, since beliefs "melt away … like wax in the sun" until they become solid; associating with others makes you like them or them like you.[^10]
- Related to [[Divisions of Philosophy (Stoicism)]], [[Stoic Exercises]], and modern [[Cognitive Behavioral Therapy]].
