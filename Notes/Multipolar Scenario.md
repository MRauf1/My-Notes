# Definition
> [!info] Multipolar Scenario[^1]
> A post-transition outcome in which no single agent obtains a [[Decisive Strategic Advantage|decisive strategic advantage]], so that multiple agents coexist and additional constraints arise from how they interact; contrasted with a [[Singleton]].

# Properties
- **Labour**: to the extent cheap machine labour substitutes for human labour, human jobs may disappear, with humans competitive only where customers prefer human-made work (as some groups shun non-halal or non-kosher food, some might shun products of unsanctioned machine intelligence). Like horses, which survive mainly through peculiar human preferences, humans face a downside of not just wage cuts but starvation.[^2]
- **Capital**: labour's income share would fall to nearly nil and capital's to nearly 100% of world product; given astronomical growth, even small pre-transition wealth would balloon, and the very rich could pay premiums for "fair-trade" human labour. Governments could redistribute via taxation or asset sales to pension off citizens.[^3]
- **Malthusian return**: historically most people lived at subsistence (with a thin elite above it) until growth outpaced population, larger populations themselves accelerating growth via collective intelligence; the poorest countries have not yet completed the demographic transition. Post-transition, most biological humans might be idle rentiers eking out a living on savings, possibly preserved in a niche if emulations were ineligible for pensions.[^4]
- **Digital workers**: whether machine minds are owned as capital (slaves) or hired as free labourers, the worker gets the necessities and nothing more. Copies can be spawned and terminated to fit demand, fatigued emulations erased and replaced by rested saved states, the best trainees used as templates for millions of daily copies, and sensitive workers frequently reset for security; each might wake optimised for loyalty and unbothered by its imminent end-of-day deletion, since emulations with death neuroses would not be selected. The inference that happiness levels adaptive for ancestral humans will remain adaptive for modified emulations is weak.[^5]
	- *Creator's note*: this picture resembles Huxley's *Brave New World*.
- **Military and social control**: deterrence weakens if the dead can be restored from backups; superorganisms of agents fully committed to a common goal (e.g. branched copies of one ur-template) require a partial solution to the control problem; billions of copies of a verified loyal emulation could staff a uniform police and bureaucracy, enforcing anything from arms control to permanent totalitarianism.[^6]
- **Coordination**: international collaboration could avoid arms races, coordinate AI development, and guarantee living standards, and agents with resource-satiable preferences would prefer sharing to winner-takes-all. Obstacles are monitoring and enforcement (new inspection versus new concealment, especially in cyberspace), which may require a trusted enforcement agency amounting to a superintelligent Leviathan singleton, and bargaining failures, where commitment to intransigence can backfire into war.[^7]
- Multipolarity does not guarantee a good outcome: the original principal-agent problem remains, buried under new coordination failures.[^8]
- Evidence from Earth's long history of life says little about the probability of intelligent life, owing to an [[Observation Selection Effect]].[^9]
