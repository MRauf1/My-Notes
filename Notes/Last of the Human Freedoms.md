# Definition
> [!info] Last of the Human Freedoms[^1]
> ([[Viktor Frankl|Frankl]]) "Everything can be taken from a man but one thing: the last of the human freedoms — to choose one's attitude in any given set of circumstances, to choose one's own way." It is not freedom *from* conditions but freedom to take a stand *toward* them.

# Properties
- Frankl's camp experience answered the question of whether man is merely a product of biological, psychological, and sociological conditions: some prisoners overcame apathy, suppressed irritability, comforted others, and gave away their last bread, proving a vestige of spiritual freedom remains.[^2]
- Every day offered decisions whether to submit to the powers that threatened to rob one of inner freedom; the sort of person a prisoner became "was the result of an inner decision, and not the result of camp influences alone".[^3]
- This spiritual freedom, which cannot be taken away, "makes life meaningful and purposeful"; Dostoevsky: "There is only one thing that I dread: not to be worthy of my sufferings."[^4]
- **Self-determination**: man is not fully conditioned; he decides whether to give in to conditions or stand up to them, has the freedom to change at any instant, and is predictable only statistically, never individually. "Things determine each other, but man is ultimately self-determining"; which potential (good or evil) is actualised depends on decisions, not conditions.[^5]
- Freedom is the negative aspect of a whole whose positive aspect is **responsibleness**; without it, freedom degenerates into arbitrariness.[^6]
- Recognised both by the ancient Stoics (cf. [[Dichotomy of Control]], [[Prohairesis]]) and by modern existentialists (cf. [[Responsibility (Existential Psychology)]]).[^7]
