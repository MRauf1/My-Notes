# Definition
> [!info] Forgiveness (Tolle)[^1]
> Relinquishing a grievance, and so letting go of grief. It happens naturally once you see that the grievance serves no purpose except to strengthen a false sense of self. In its widest sense it means offering no resistance to life.

# Properties
- Nonforgiveness comes from "too much past" and implies a heavy burden of [[Psychological Time|psychological time]].[^2]
- To check for it, feel the lingering emotion and see whether the mind is holding a grievance pattern (blame, self-pity, resentment) that feeds it.[^3]
- True forgiveness of yourself or others is impossible while you derive your sense of self from the past. It comes only through presence.[^4]
- Forgiving reclaims your power from the mind.[^1]
- What looks like evil from a limited perspective becomes part of a "higher good" only through forgiveness; until then it remains evil.[^5]
- Related: [[Pain-Body]], [[Surrender (Spirituality)]].

# Evidence (Carnegie)
Carnegie's case is self-interest: "When we hate our enemies, we are giving them power over us." Revenge hurts the avenger more than the target.[^6]
- **Revenge.** People who punished a free rider ruminated more about them and felt *worse* than those who could not punish, though they predicted the opposite (Carlsmith, Wilson and Gilbert, 2008).
- **Forgiveness interventions.** These increase forgiveness and reduce depression and anxiety compared with no treatment (meta-analysis, Wade et al., 2014).
- **Anger and health.** Anger and hostility are associated with incidence of coronary heart disease (meta-analysis, Chida and Steptoe, 2009). See [[Perseverative Cognition Hypothesis]].[^7]
- **Understanding.** "To know all is to understand all": seeing others as products of circumstance and heredity leaves less room for condemnation.[^8] Compare [[Stoic Determinism]].

- **Admitting fault (Carnegie, 1936).** "If you are wrong, admit it quickly and emphatically."[^9] Apologies reliably increase forgiveness (meta-analysis, Fehr, Gelfand and Nag, 2010).
