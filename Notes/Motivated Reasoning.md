# Definition
> [!info] Motivated Reasoning[^1]
> Reasoning shaped by a desired conclusion rather than by accuracy (Kunda, 1990). People evaluate the same evidence more critically when it threatens their beliefs or self-image. James Harvey Robinson, quoted by Carnegie: "most of our so-called reasoning consists in finding arguments for going on believing as we already do," because "it is not the ideas themselves that are dear to us, but our self-esteem which is threatened."

# Properties
- **Evidence.**
	- People rate studies that support their position as more convincing than identical studies that oppose it (Lord, Ross and Lepper, 1979).
	- Threats to identity increase biased processing. Self-affirmation, which reduces that threat, makes people more open to unwelcome evidence (Cohen and Sherman, 2014).
- **The backfire effect is mostly a myth.** Carnegie claims that "nine times out of ten, an argument ends with each of the contestants more firmly convinced than ever."[^2] The early "backfire effect", where corrections strengthen the original belief (Nyhan and Reifler, 2010), largely failed to replicate. Across 52 issues and more than 10,000 participants, factual corrections moved beliefs in the correct direction (Wood and Porter, 2019). What persists is *small* belief change, especially when identity is at stake. Arguments rarely produce dramatic conversions, but they do not usually make things worse.
- **Belief perseverance.** Beliefs persist even after the evidence for them is discredited (Ross, Lepper and Hubbard, 1975).
- **Ego involvement.** Carnegie defended overpriced draperies to a critic, then admitted the mistake to an admirer.[^3] Admitting error is easier when self-esteem is not under attack. Compare [[Confirmation Bias]] and [[Cognitive Dissonance]].
- **Understanding before judging.** Carl Rogers held that our first reaction to others' statements is "an evaluation or judgment, rather than an understanding of it."[^4] See [[Active Listening]].
- Related: [[Psychological Reactance]], [[Cognitive Bias]].
