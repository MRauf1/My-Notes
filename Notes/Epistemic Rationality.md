# Definition
> [!info] Epistemic Rationality[^1]
> An agent is epistemically rational when they correctly fit their [[Belief|beliefs]] to the evidence, i.e., when they believe as they ought to. The rules that an agent should satisfy in order to be rational are called **rationality constraints**.

# Properties
- A normative but not moral standard: calling an agent irrational holds them to the standard of a rational agent and says they fall short of it, without judging them to be a bad person.
- Determining which rationality constraints hold is a central question of [[Epistemology]] and [[Formal Epistemology]].
- The most basic and widely accepted rationality constraint is [[Probabilism]].
- Contrasts with pragmatic (practical) rationality, which concerns acting so as to achieve one's goals, as for a [[Rational Agent|rational agent]]; this contrast underlies the main objection to the [[Dutch Book Argument]].

[^1]: [A Critical Introduction to Formal Epistemology](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/9XYCZDPF?page=36)
