# Definition
> [!info] Cosmic and Terrestrial Meaning[^1]
> (Yalom) Two senses of meaning in life. **Cosmic meaning** is a design existing outside of and superior to the person, invariably a magical or spiritual ordering of the universe. **Terrestrial meaning** ("the meaning of *my* life") is purpose: experiencing life as having some function or overriding goals to fulfil. Terrestrial meaning can be entirely secular.

# Types
Secular sources of terrestrial meaning:[^2]
- **Altruism** - leaving the world better, serving others.
- **Dedication to a cause** - "What man is, he has become through that cause he has made his own" (Jaspers).
- **Creativity** - the creation justifies itself; includes scientific discovery and self-discovery; overlaps with altruism and love.
- **Hedonism** - living fully and savouring life; elastic enough to absorb the others.
- **Self-actualisation** - realising inbuilt potential (Maslow).
- **Self-transcendence** - see [[Self-Transcendence]].

Frankl's three categories: what one gives (creations), what one takes (encounters and experiences), and one's stand toward unchangeable suffering.[^3]

# Properties
- **The dilemma**: humans seem to require meaning, yet existential freedom holds the world is contingent with no grand design; how can a self-created meaning be sturdy enough to bear one's life?[^4]
- Those with cosmic meaning usually derive terrestrial meaning from it; those who cannot find their place in it suffer from guilt rather than meaninglessness ("the branch cannot hope to know the tree's meaning", Pascal).[^5]
- **Camus**: the *absurd* is the tension between a meaning-seeking being and an indifferent world; from nihilism he generated values of courage, prideful rebellion, solidarity, love, and secular saintliness. Sartre too valued the search; both require inventing one's meaning and committing wholeheartedly ("half-sure and whole-hearted").[^6]
- Frankl holds meaning is *discovered*, not invented; Yalom notes this is basically religious, and "it is man, not God, who is obsessed with purpose". Invented meaning comforts less if one remembers inventing it.[^7]
- **Why we need meaning**: it is an anxiety emollient against a world without structure, and it begets values, "conceptions of the desirable" that rank modes of action.[^8]
- The question is *contaminated*: death anxiety (Tolstoy failed to find "a meaning that would destroy death"), groundlessness, and isolation masquerade as meaninglessness. Yet death and meaning are not fused: the past is real (Frankl's calendar leaves), and some ends are desirable on their own account (Hume), avoiding infinite regress.[^9]
- The demand for goal-directed striving and "progress" is a relatively recent Western cultural artifact; the East frames life as a mystery to be lived, "doing" versus "being".[^10]
- **Findings**: lack of meaning correlates roughly linearly with psychopathology; meaning correlates with religious belief, self-transcendent values, group membership, causes, and clear goals, and changes developmentally ([[Generativity]]).[^11]
- Meaningful patients live more fully and face death with less despair.[^12]
- Pathology: [[Existential Vacuum]]; therapy: [[Engagement (Existential Psychotherapy)]], [[Dereflection]]. See also [[Meaning of Life]].
