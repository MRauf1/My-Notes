# Definition
> [!info] Cosmic Inflation
> A period of extremely rapid, exponential expansion of space in the very early universe, lasting from roughly $10^{-36}$ to $10^{-32}$ seconds after the [[Big Bang]], during which a causally-connected patch of space expanded by a factor of at least $10^{26}$.

# Properties
- Hypothesized to be driven by the inflaton field, a temporary high-energy quantum field whose repulsive form of gravitational energy (similar in character to [[Dark Energy]]) drove the exponential stretching of space; inflation ended when the inflaton field decayed, converting its energy into the hot, dense soup of elementary particles associated with the [[Big Bang]].
- Explains why the universe is so uniform (homogeneous and isotropic) over such vast distances: regions now far apart were in causal contact before being rapidly stretched apart.
- Seeds the large-scale structure of the universe: quantum fluctuations (via the [[Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle]]) in the energy density of the inflaton field were stretched apart so quickly that they were "frozen in" once separated by more than the distance light could travel in the time available (crossing the causal horizon), fixing them as small, permanent density variations; after inflation ended, gravity amplified these slightly denser regions into the seeds of galaxies and cosmic structure.
- A distinct episode of expansion from the [[Expansion of the Universe|universe's present-day expansion]], though speculatively related to it — e.g. "quintessential inflation" models propose a single scalar field that drove inflation early on and now, at a far lower energy, plays the role of [[Dark Energy]].
