# Definition

Thinking that attaches value to things that you've invested resources in that is greater than the value of the things today.[^1]

# Properties
- **Evidence.** Theater-goers who paid full price for season tickets attended more plays than those given discounts (Arkes and Blumer, 1985). The bias is robust in human decision experiments, though its size varies across domains.
- **Emotional sunk costs (Carnegie).** Set a "stop-loss order" on worries and resentments: decide how much a thing matters, at what point to stop investing in it, and whether you have already "paid more than it is worth."[^2] Ruminating over unchangeable past losses is the emotional counterpart (see [[Rumination]]).
