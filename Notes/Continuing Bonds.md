# Definition
> [!info] Continuing Bonds[^1]
> The view that healthy grieving can involve keeping an ongoing inner relationship with the deceased rather than "letting go" (Klass, Silverman and Nickman, 1996). It replaced the older idea that grief work requires detachment. Pausch prepared such bonds for his children with videos, letters and "the specific idiosyncratic ways in which I related to them." "Kids… need to know their parents love them. Their parents don't have to be alive for that to happen."

# Properties
- **Evidence (mixed).**
	- Continuing bonds are common and often comforting.
	- Whether they help depends on their form. Bonds through memories and legacy tend to be adaptive. Bonds that deny the death, such as illusions of the person's presence, can predict worse adjustment (Field, 2006; Stroebe and Schut, 2005).
- **Bereaved children.** Adults who lost a parent young told Pausch they valued knowing how much the parent loved them, and that the parent died with good memories of them.[^2]
	- A structured program strengthening the surviving parent's warmth and the child's coping reduced mental-health problems in bereaved children for years afterwards (the Family Bereavement Program; Sandler et al., 2003).
	- Reassuring children that normal life events still lie ahead of them addresses a common fear.[^3]
- **Caregivers.** Take the help offered and join others with similar losses. Social support is one of the strongest protective factors in bereavement.[^3]
