# Definition
> [!abstract] Cox's Theorem[^1]
> If numerical values are used to represent degrees of belief, then a simple set of axioms encoding common-sense properties of such beliefs leads uniquely to a set of rules for manipulating degrees of belief that is equivalent to the sum and product rules of probability.

It gave the first rigorous argument that probability theory can be regarded as an extension of Boolean logic to situations involving uncertainty (Jaynes). Hence using probability to represent uncertainty is not an ad-hoc choice but is forced on us by the requirement of rational, coherent inference, and the resulting degrees of belief are called (Bayesian) probabilities.

# Properties
- Justifies the [[Probability Bayesian Framework|Bayesian interpretation of probability]], in which probability quantifies uncertainty about anything, including model parameters and the choice of model itself.
- Other axiom systems for uncertainty (Ramsey, Good, Savage, de Finetti, Lindley) lead to the same conclusion: the resulting quantities obey the rules of probability.
- Uncertainty is then revised in light of evidence with [[Bayes' Theorem]], and optimal actions follow from [[Decision Theory]].
