# Definition
> [!info] Vipassana Meditation[^1]
> (*Vipassana Bhavana*, Insight meditation) The cultivation of the mind aimed at seeing "in a special way" that leads to insight and full understanding. *Passana* means seeing; the prefix *vi* means "in a special way", "into", and "through". Together they mean looking into something with clarity and precision, seeing each component as distinct, and piercing through to its most fundamental reality. By definition it is the cultivation of [[Mindfulness|mindfulness]], a clear awareness of exactly what is happening as it happens.

# Properties
- The oldest Buddhist meditation practice. It comes directly from the *Satipatthana Sutta* (in the [[Tipitaka]]), the Buddha's discourse on mindfulness. That text says to begin by focusing on the breath and then go on to note all other physical and mental phenomena as they arise.[^2]
- Practiced in [[Theravada Buddhism]]. It addresses insight, the counterpart of [[Samatha Meditation|Samatha]]. It is gentle, gradual, and very thorough.[^3]
- Its purpose is the radical and permanent transformation of one's whole sensory and cognitive experience, leading to [[Nibbana|liberation]]. Relaxation and concentration ([[Samadhi]]) are necessary companions to awareness, not the goal.[^4]
- It is not trance, unconsciousness, escapism, bliss-seeking, or self-centered. If you are becoming unconscious, you aren't meditating. Bliss is a by-product that comes from relaxation, so chasing it creates tension and blocks it.[^5]
- **Method.** The breath is the primary object (see [[Breath as Meditation Object]]). Anything that pulls attention away from it is a [[Distraction (Meditation)|distraction]] and becomes a secondary, temporary object. Seated practice instills mental habits that must carry over into daily life; meditation not applied to daily living is "sterile and limited".[^6]
- **Momentary concentration.** The mind can be concentrated only on the present moment. Uniting the mind with each passing moment is called momentary concentration. It is all the concentration Vipassana needs, because everything in experience lives only for one moment.[^7]
- **Two states to avoid**: *thinking*, also called monkey mind, where you are caught in the chain of thought; and *sinking*, any dimming of awareness, a void like dreamless sleep with neither concentration nor mindfulness. When sinking is noticed, note it and return to the breath.[^8]
- Learning to look at each second as if it were the first and only second in the universe is essential. A feeling of predictability or sameness signals that you are off track.[^9]
- Applied to every experience, it reveals the [[Three Marks of Existence]]: [[Anicca|impermanence]], [[Dukkha|unsatisfactoriness]], and [[Anatta|selflessness]].[^10]
- Seeing comes before change. Vipassana is learning to look at yourself exactly as you are and accept it fully, because only then can you change it.[^11]
- Deep concentration slows the thought process down and speeds up the awareness watching it. This lets you see mental states arise out of the [[Unconsciousness|unconscious]] before they reach conscious awareness.[^12]
- Psychic phenomena (e.g., intuitions, apparent past-life memories) can occur, but they are an ego trap for beginners and should not be emphasized.[^13]
- See [[Meditation Practice Guidelines]] and [[Meditation Posture]].
