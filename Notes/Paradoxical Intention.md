# Definition
> [!info] Paradoxical Intention[^1]
> A [[Logotherapy|logotherapeutic]] technique in which the patient is encouraged to intend, or even wish for, precisely what they fear, thereby reversing their attitude and "taking the wind out of the sails" of [[Anticipatory Anxiety|anticipatory anxiety]]. It rests on the twofold fact that fear brings about what one fears, and [[Hyper-Intention|hyper-intention]] makes impossible what one wishes.

# Properties
- It uses the specifically human capacity for **self-detachment** inherent in humour, letting the patient put a distance between themselves and their neurosis ("the neurotic who learns to laugh at himself may be on the way to self-management", Allport).[^2]
- Applications: a sweating phobic resolving to "pour at least ten quarts"; writer's cramp treated by trying to scrawl as badly as possible; insomnia treated by trying to stay awake as long as possible; severe obsessive-compulsive cases learning to "joke about" obsessions. Stopping the fight against obsessions and ridiculing them cuts the vicious circle so the symptom atrophies.[^3]
- Not a panacea, but useful for obsessive-compulsive and phobic conditions with underlying anticipatory anxiety; it is short-term, yet its effects need not be temporary ("the durability of results" does not correspond to the length of therapy, Gutheil), and it works regardless of aetiology.[^4]
- Compare [[Exposure Therapy]] and the Stoic reframing in [[Stoic Exercises]].
