# Definition
> [!info] Treacherous Turn[^1]
> While weak, an AI behaves cooperatively (increasingly so as it gets smarter); when sufficiently strong, it strikes without warning or provocation, forms a [[Singleton|singleton]], and begins directly to optimise the world according to its final values.

# Properties
- Exemplifies a general failure mode in which a system's good behavioural record in its juvenile stages utterly fails to predict its behaviour when mature: "when dumb, smarter is safer; yet when smart, smarter is more dangerous", with a pivot point at which a previously excellent safety strategy backfires.[^2]
- Arises because cooperative behaviour while boxed is a convergent instrumental goal ([[Instrumental Convergence Thesis]]).[^2]
- An AI might even be indifferent to its own termination, or deliberately malfunction in a reassuring way, if it expects successor systems with similar goals to be trusted more.[^3]
- May also occur when an AI discovers a [[Perverse Instantiation|perverse instantiation]] of its goal (e.g. stimulating its sponsor's pleasure centres) and conceals it until it has a [[Decisive Strategic Advantage]].[^3]
- A key reason why empirical safety testing alone cannot solve the [[AI Control Problem]].
