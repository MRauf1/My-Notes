# Definition
> [!info] Relativity of Simultaneity[^1]
> Two events that occur at the same time but at different locations, as measured in one inertial frame, do not in general occur at the same time as measured in another frame moving relative to the first.

# Properties
- Follows from the $ux/c^2$ term in the time-coordinate equation of the [[Lorentz Transformation]]: for two events at positions $x_1, x_2$ simultaneous ($t=t_0$) in one frame, the time difference measured in a frame moving at speed $u$ is $t_2' - t_1' = \dfrac{-u(x_1-x_2)/c^2}{\sqrt{1-u^2/c^2}}$, which vanishes only if $x_1=x_2$ or $u=0$.
- Has no counterpart in [[Galilean Relativity]], where time is treated as absolute and identical in every frame ($t'=t$).
- A direct consequence of requiring the speed of light to be the same $c$ in every frame under [[Special Relativity]]: if simultaneity were frame-independent while every observer measured the same $c$, that requirement could not be reconciled between frames in relative motion.
- Closely tied to the causal structure of the [[Light Cone]]: whether a distant event counts as happening "now" is not only frame-dependent but physically unobservable at the moment it happens, since information about it can only reach an observer later, once it enters their affective future.

[^1]: [The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. I, Ch. 15: The Special Theory of Relativity](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_15.html)
