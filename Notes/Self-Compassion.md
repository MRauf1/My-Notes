# Definition
> [!info] Self-Compassion (Neff)
> Treating yourself in failure with the kindness you would show a friend. It has three components (Neff, 2003):
> 1. *Self-kindness* rather than self-judgment.
> 2. *Common humanity* rather than isolation.
> 3. *Mindfulness*: honest awareness of the painful facts, rather than over-identifying with them or avoiding them.

# Properties
- **Goggins' approach.** The Accountability Mirror is harsh, honest self-confrontation: "it's okay to be cruel to yourself as long as you realize you're doing it to become better."[^1] He also praised himself for small victories.[^2] At his lowest point he found peace by understanding his younger self rather than chastising him, and by letting go of judgment and bitterness.[^3]
- **Evidence on kind vs. harsh.**
	- After a failure, people induced to be self-compassionate were *more* motivated to improve. They studied longer for a retest than people given a self-esteem boost (Breines and Chen, 2012).
	- Self-criticism predicts less goal progress (Powers, Koestner and Zuroff, 2007).
	- Self-compassion is strongly associated with lower depression and anxiety (meta-analysis, MacBeth and Gumley, 2012).
- **High standards.** Self-compassion doesn't lower standards. It keeps the honest acknowledgment of shortcomings (its mindfulness component) while dropping the contempt, and the evidence favors this combination over self-criticism.
