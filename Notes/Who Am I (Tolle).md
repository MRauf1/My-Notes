# Definition
> [!info] Who Am I (Tolle)[^1]
> "The most fundamental question of your life." For Tolle, knowing yourself is not adopting ideas or beliefs about yourself but *being* yourself: being rooted in Being instead of lost in the mind, which means ceasing to identify with content. "Nothing you can know about you is you."

# Properties
- **Confusion** is "I don't know, but I should (or need to) know." Letting go of the belief that you need to know who you are, i.e., ceasing to look to thought for an identity, dissolves confusion. Being fully comfortable with not knowing leaves what you are: "a field of pure potentiality". Defining yourself through thought limits you.[^2]
- "The consciousness that says 'I am' is not the consciousness that thinks." Awareness of thinking is not itself thinking; if there were only thought, you wouldn't know you were thinking.[^3]
- Nobody can tell you who you are, since that would be just another concept. Who you are needs no belief; every belief is an obstacle.[^4]
- What matters to you is revealed by your actions and reactions, not your words or beliefs. How you react to people and challenges is the best indicator of how deeply you know yourself. What you react to in others is also in you.[^5]
- Who you think you are shapes how you see yourself treated, e.g., "a needy 'little me' whose needs are not being met".[^6]
- **"My life"** refers to content (age, health, relationships, finances, mental state, events) rather than the life you *are*: "I don't have a life. I am life." (cf. [[Life Situation]])[^7]
