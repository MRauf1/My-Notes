# Definition
> [!info] Eupatheia[^1]
> The three "good feelings" that the Stoic sage does experience, being rational counterparts of the [[Passion (Stoicism)|passions]]: *volition* (rational pursuit), *caution* (rational avoidance), and *joy* (rational elation).

# Types
- **Volition** (counterpart of longing) - subspecies: kindness/benevolence (constantly stressed by Epictetus) and friendliness.[^1]
- **Caution** (counterpart of fear) - subspecies: modesty and reverence.[^1]
- **Joy** (counterpart of pleasure) - subspecies: a sense of humour and cheerfulness.[^1]
- Distress has no rational counterpart, since the sage has nothing bad present.

# Properties
- Show that [[Apatheia]] is not emotionlessness.
- **Diogenes Laertius's subspecies (Robertson's version).** Under preference (*boulesis*): kindness, generosity, warmth, affection. Under caution: respect and modesty. Under joy: delight, sociability, good spirits. In modern terms: "enjoyment" (rational pleasure), "mindfulness" (rational aversion), "preference" (rational desire).[^rob1]
- The Stoics, like REBT, cultivate rational and adaptive emotions. "Stoic indifference" is the transformation of irrational passions into rational ones, not the absence of feeling.[^rob2]
- Valuing things at their true worth replaces excessive craving and fear with love of wisdom and an inclination toward happiness. Desire and aversion are moved from externals onto one's own judgements and intentions.[^rob3]
