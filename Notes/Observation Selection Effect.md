# Definition
> [!info] Observation Selection Effect[^1]
> A bias in evidence arising because observations are conditioned on the existence of an observer: e.g. all observers necessarily find themselves on a planet where intelligent life arose, however likely or unlikely that was for any given planet.

# Properties
- It is therefore unsound to infer from the evolution of intelligence on Earth that evolutionary processes had a reasonably high prior probability of producing intelligence.[^1]
- The hypothesis that intelligent life is improbable does not predict we should find ourselves where life died out early; it may predict we find ourselves on a planet where intelligence arose even if such planets are a tiny fraction, so life's long track record offers scant support for its likelihood.[^2]
- Consequently, evolutionary arguments cannot meaningfully constrain the difficulty or timescale of building human-level AI ([[Paths to Superintelligence]]).
- A form of anthropic reasoning, related to the [[Copernican Principle]].
