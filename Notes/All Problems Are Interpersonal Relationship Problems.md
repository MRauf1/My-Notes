# Definition
> [!info] All Problems Are Interpersonal Relationship Problems[^1]
> The basic premise of [[Adlerian Psychology]]: every worry has other people in it. If one were alone in the universe, all problems would disappear. Since that is impossible, problems are unavoidable. Conversely, happiness is also found in interpersonal relationships ([[Feeling of Contribution]]).

# Properties
- **Loneliness needs others.** Being alone does not make one lonely. Loneliness is a deep sense of being excluded from the people and community around one. A person becomes an "individual" only in a social context.[^2]
- **Self-dislike as avoidance.** Fear of being hurt by others leads people to focus on their shortcomings and dislike themselves. Disliking oneself is then a means ([[Teleology (Adlerian Psychology)|teleology]]) of avoiding relationships.[^3]
- **Value is social.** The meaning and value of a trait, such as height, only arises from comparison with others, so the [[Feeling of Inferiority]] is also interpersonal.[^4]
- Interpersonal problems come in three [[Life Tasks]] (work, friendship, love). Their main source is intruding on others' tasks ([[Separation of Tasks]]).
- Its positive counterpart is [[Community Feeling]].[^5]
- **All joy is interpersonal relationship joy.** Alone in the universe there would be no language, logic, competition, envy or loneliness, and no joy either: a flat life. Loneliness needs someone who shuns you.[^kk1]

# Evidence
- **Correction: not all problems are interpersonal.** Illness, pain and material hardship cause distress on their own, so taken literally the claim is too strong.
- **Supported kernel.** Social relationships strongly predict well-being and health. Their effect on mortality is comparable to smoking (Holt-Lunstad, Smith & Layton, 2010). Research also defines loneliness as *perceived* social isolation, which is distinct from being objectively alone (Hawkley & Cacioppo, 2010). See also [[Existential Isolation]].
