# Definition
> [!info] Special Relativity[^1]
> Einstein's postulate that the laws of physics — not only mechanics, but electrodynamics as well — take exactly the same form in every inertial [[Frame of Reference]], together with the requirement that the speed of light $c$ is the same for every observer regardless of the motion of its source; consistency of these two demands that the Galilean transformation between frames be replaced by the [[Lorentz Transformation]].

# Properties
- Extends [[Galilean Relativity]] — which only guaranteed that *mechanical* experiments could not detect uniform motion — to all of physics, including Maxwell's equations of electromagnetism, which are not left unchanged by a Galilean transformation but are left unchanged by a [[Lorentz Transformation]].
- Forces a revision of Newtonian mechanics: momentum must be redefined as [[Relativistic Momentum]] so that [[Conservation of Momentum]] still holds under a Lorentz transformation, and kinetic energy is unified with rest energy via [[Mass-Energy Equivalence]].
- Implies that no signal or material object can travel faster than $c$, since [[Relativistic Momentum|momentum, and total energy,]] diverge as an object's speed approaches $c$.
- Consequences include the [[Relativity of Simultaneity]] and [[Length Contraction]], both of which follow from the form of the [[Lorentz Transformation]].
- The invariant combination of space and time coordinates under a Lorentz transformation, the [[Spacetime Interval]], plays the same role that ordinary Euclidean distance plays under a rotation of spatial axes — motivating the treatment of space and time as a single four-dimensional [[Spacetime]].
