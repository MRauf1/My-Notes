# Definition
> [!info] Conservation of Baryon Number[^1]
> The total baryon number of an isolated system — the number of baryons (such as protons and neutrons) minus the number of antibaryons — is conserved to extremely high precision in essentially every observed interaction.

# Properties
- Baryons are composed, in the Standard Model, of three quarks each; an antibaryon carries baryon number $-1$.
- Unlike [[Conservation of Charge]], baryon number is now understood as only an accidental symmetry of the Standard Model rather than a fundamental, gauged conservation law: it is violated in principle by non-perturbative electroweak "sphaleron" processes (which conserve the combination baryon number minus lepton number, $B-L$, rather than $B$ alone) and, if Grand Unified Theories are correct, by proton decay. Proton decay has been searched for extensively but never observed, with experimental lower bounds on the proton's lifetime exceeding $10^{34}$ years.
- At every energy and timescale accessible to experiment to date, baryon number remains exactly conserved in practice, consistent with its original formulation.
