# Definition
> [!info] Five Aggregates[^1]
> (*Khandhas*) The five components into which Buddhism analyzes a person (the "body/mind complex"):
> 1. Form (*rupa*) — the physical body.
> 2. Feeling (*vedana*) — pleasant, unpleasant, or neutral tone.
> 3. Perception (*sanna*) — recognition and labeling.
> 4. Mental formations (*sankhara*) — volitions, habits, and reactions.
> 5. Consciousness (*vinnana*) — the knowing of an object.

# Properties
- **Insight method.** First see mind and body, and each aggregate, separately. If all of them are bundled together and watched as "feeling", we get confused and cannot see where feeling comes from. Once they have been understood separately, see their interconnection: the aggregates work together, and none can exist without the others.[^2]
- What we take to be the self is only these aggregates, with which we identify. On inspection no self is found among them ([[Anatta]]).[^3]
- In [[Breath as Meditation Object|breath meditation]], the breath is the physical part (form). The feeling of the breath, consciousness of that feeling, and consciousness of the sign are the mental parts.[^4]
- Related: [[Universal Mental Factors]].
