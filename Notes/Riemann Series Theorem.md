# Definition

> [!abstract] Theorem 1 (Riemann Series Theorem)[^1]
> Let $\sum a_n$ be a series of real numbers that converges, but not [[Absolutely Convergent Infinite Series|absolutely]]. Let $-\infty \leq \alpha \leq \beta \leq \infty$ be given. Then there exists a [[Rearrangement (Series)|rearrangement]] $\sum a_n'$ of $\sum a_n$, with partial sums $s_n'$, such that
> $$
> \begin{align}
> \liminf_{n \to \infty} s_n' = \alpha, \qquad \limsup_{n \to \infty} s_n' = \beta
> \end{align}
> $$

In particular, a conditionally convergent real series can be rearranged to converge to any prescribed real number, to diverge to $+\infty$ or $-\infty$, or to oscillate between any prescribed liminf/limsup — in sharp contrast to the rearrangement invariance of absolutely convergent series (see [[Rearrangement (Series)]]).

[^1]: [Principles of Mathematical Analysis](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/3BD27IHF?page=86)
