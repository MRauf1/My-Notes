# Definition
> [!info] Separation of Tasks[^1]
> Asking "Whose task is this?" and drawing the line between one's own tasks and other people's. One does not intervene in others' tasks and does not let anyone intervene in one's own. **Criterion:** the task belongs to whoever ultimately receives the result of the choice. Almost all interpersonal troubles come from intruding on others' tasks or having one's own intruded on.

# Properties
- **Others' judgments are their task.** All one can do is choose the best path one believes in. What others think of that choice, or of one's appearance, is their task and cannot be controlled. "You're the only one who's worried how you look."[^2]
- **Not noninterference.** In parenting, noninterference (not knowing or caring what the child does) is rejected. One knows what the child is doing, tells the child that studying is their task, and offers help whenever asked, without meddling when there is no request.[^3]
- **"For your own good"** usually serves the parent's goals (appearance, control). Children sense this and rebel. Forcing change against someone's intentions provokes strong reactions ([[Psychological Reactance]]). You can only change yourself.[^4]
- **Discarding others' tasks** is the first step to a lighter, simpler life. It cuts through tangled relationships like the Gordian knot.[^5]
- **Distance.** Good relationships need some distance. Relationships based on reward ("I gave this much, so you owe me") intrude on others' tasks.[^6]
- **You hold the cards.** Whether to repair a relationship is one's own task, whatever the other person thinks or does. Many believe the other person holds the cards and so live to satisfy them.[^7]
- Adler: "Children who have not been taught to confront challenges will try to avoid all challenges."[^8]
- Not self-righteousness or defiance: just separation. It is the first step, followed by horizontal relationships and [[Encouragement (Adlerian Psychology)]].[^9]
- Remedy for the [[Desire for Recognition]]. Underlies [[Freedom (Adlerian Psychology)]] and [[Confidence in Others]]. Compare the Stoic [[Dichotomy of Control]].
- **Applied to education and love.** Whoever does not bear the final consequences must not intervene. Respect children's decisions, offer help, and watch from a helpful but not too close distance. In love, loving is one's task and the response is the other person's ([[Love]]).[^kk1]

# Evidence
- Autonomy-supportive parenting predicts better motivation and adjustment than psychologically controlling parenting (Soenens & Vansteenkiste, 2010). Neglect (true noninterference) is also harmful, which agrees with the book's middle path.
