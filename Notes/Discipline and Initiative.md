# Definition
> [!info] Discipline and Initiative[^1]
> The two things an untrained infant mind must acquire to become intelligent. *Discipline* is the capacity to reliably obey orders, whose most extreme form is converting a brain or machine into a universal machine. *Initiative* is whatever is required for intelligence in addition to discipline.

# Properties
- Discipline is necessary, since without it one cannot set up proper communication with the machine, but it is not sufficient to produce intelligence.[^1]
- Initiative is defined only negatively, as the residue beyond discipline; the task is to discover the nature of this residue in humans and copy it in machines.[^1]
- Training an [[Unorganized Machine|unorganized machine]] via a [[Pleasure-Pain System]] instils discipline; Turing suggests initiative is closely connected to search ([[Intellectual Search]]).
- A disciplined but initiative-less adult would obey the most ridiculous orders unflinchingly, have no common sense, and fall comatose once orders run out.

[^1]: [Turing, Intelligent Machinery](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/7B3JT2D6)
