# Definition

Focuses on how a person's thoughts lead to feelings of distress.[^1] Helps clients to change their dysfunctional thoughts.

# Properties
- Its core premise was anticipated by [[Epictetus]]: "People are troubled not by things but by their judgments about things"; see [[Assent (Stoicism)]] and [[Stoic Exercises]].
- Ellis's [[Rational Emotive Behavior Therapy]] preceded Beck's approach and targets absolutist "musts".
- **Beck's procedure.** The therapist helps the client to: (1) monitor negative automatic thoughts; (2) evaluate how thoughts, feelings and actions relate; (3) weigh the evidence for and against distorted or maladaptive cognitions; (4) generate alternative cognitions and substitute them; (5) identify and modify the underlying dysfunctional assumptions that predispose to automatic thoughts.[^rob1] This central method of monitoring and challenging thoughts and beliefs parallels the Stoic habit of disputing impressions ([[Impression (Stoicism)]]).[^rob2]
- Key concepts: [[Cognitive Triad]], [[Cognitive Distortion]], [[Cognitive Distancing]], [[Cognitive Mediation]]. Precursor: [[Persuasion Therapy (Dubois)]].
