# Definition
> [!info] Encouragement (Adlerian Psychology)[^1]
> Assistance based on [[Vertical and Horizontal Relationships|horizontal relationships]] that helps a person recover the courage to face their own tasks, without praising or rebuking. When someone does not face their tasks, the problem is not lack of ability but that "one has lost the courage to face one's tasks". **Order:** first [[Separation of Tasks]], then equal horizontal relationships that accept differences, then encouragement.

# Properties
- **Gratitude instead of praise.** Say "thank you", which expresses gratitude rather than judgment. Hearing gratitude tells a person they have contributed to someone, which gives a [[Feeling of Contribution]].[^2]
- **Do not judge.** "Judgment" comes from vertical relationships.[^3]
- **Courage comes from worth.** Adler: "It is only when a person is able to feel that he has worth that he can possess courage." One feels worth by feeling "I am beneficial to the community", subjectively and not through others' judgment. Then one can accept oneself and face the [[Life Tasks]].[^4]
- **Level of being, not level of acts.** Rejoice in others' very existence and thank them for it, without judging what they did. Parents should not subtract points from an idealized child but start from zero and be grateful the child is there.[^5]
- In therapy: first help the client accept "myself now", then build the courage to step forward regardless of outcome.[^6]
- Related: [[Self-Efficacy]], [[Process Praise]], [[Gratitude Intervention]].
- **Starts with respect.** Accepting someone unconditionally as they are gives them courage ([[Respect (Adlerian Psychology)]]).[^kk1]
