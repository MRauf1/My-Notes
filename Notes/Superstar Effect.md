# Definition
> [!info] Superstar Effect (Rosen)[^1]
> Economist Sherwin Rosen's observation that in a market where consumers can access all performers and everyone's quality is clear, consumers choose the very best. Even if the best is only slightly more talented than the next rung down, the superstars win the bulk of the market.

# Properties
- Communication and collaboration technologies have turned many formerly local markets into a "universal bazaar", so far more workers now compete with the stars of their field. Companies outsource key roles to stars, leaving local talent underemployed.[^2]
- Along with those who work well with intelligent machines and those with capital, superstars are one of the groups favored by the "Great Restructuring" (Brynjolfsson and McAfee). This restructuring divides jobs rather than eliminating them all (see [[Thriving Groups in Modern Age]]).[^3]
- Becoming a superstar needs both mastery and output: "If you don't produce, you won't thrive—no matter how skilled or talented you are." Both depend on [[Deep Work]] ([[Abilities to Thrive in Modern Age]]).[^4]
- The digital network revolution cuts both ways: if you can create something useful, its reachable audience is essentially limitless.[^5]
