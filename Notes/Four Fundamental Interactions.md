# Definition
> [!info] Four Fundamental Interactions[^1]
> All known particle interactions reduce to four fundamental interactions, in decreasing order of intrinsic strength: the strong (nuclear) interaction, the electromagnetic interaction, the weak interaction, and [[Gravitation]].

# Properties
- The electromagnetic interaction is mediated by the [[Photon]], couples to all electrically charged particles with strength set by the fine-structure constant (≈1/137), and is described exactly by [[Quantum Electrodynamics]].
- [[Gravitation]] couples to all mass-energy but is by far the weakest of the four.
- The [[Weak Nuclear Force|weak interaction]] governs processes such as beta decay (historically described as a neutron disintegrating into a proton, electron, and antineutrino); it is now understood, together with electromagnetism, as part of a unified electroweak interaction mediated by the W and Z bosons.
- The [[Strong Nuclear Force|strong interaction]] binds protons and neutrons together within the atomic nucleus; early theories modeled it as mediated by mesons such as the [[Pion]], but it is now understood at a deeper level through quantum chromodynamics, in which quarks are bound by gluons.
- Distinguished by characteristic conserved quantities: for example, [[Strangeness]] is conserved specifically in strong-interaction processes.

[^1]: [The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. I, Ch. 2: Basic Physics](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_02.html)
