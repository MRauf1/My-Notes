# Definition
> [!info] Salvation (Tolle)[^1]
> Fulfillment, peace, and life in its fullness, felt as an abiding presence rather than a passing experience: the "good that has no opposite". It is freedom from all wanting, needing, grasping, and clinging, and from past and future as psychological needs. It is available only here and now.

# Properties
- The unconscious mind-set "When I obtain this or am free of that, then I will be okay" creates the illusion of salvation in the future. Satisfaction from pleasures and gratification is short-lived, so it is projected forward again and again.[^2]
- Time is the greatest obstacle to salvation, not the means to it. "There is no salvation in time. You cannot be free in the future."[^3]
- The ego's sense of lack is a "bottomless hole": possessions, achievements, and relationships pursued to fill it leave it unfilled.[^4]
- Realizing that nothing external can save you can lead to world-weariness and depression unless it is paired with nonresistance. In that state, things come and are enjoyed while they last, without fear of loss.[^5]
- *A New Earth*: at the core of every utopian vision lies the old consciousness's structural dysfunction of looking to the future for salvation: "only the present moment can make us free."[^6]
- See [[Enlightenment (Spirituality)]], [[Psychological Time]].
