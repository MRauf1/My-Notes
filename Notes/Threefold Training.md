# Definition
> [!info] Threefold Training[^1]
> The three integral factors of Buddhist [[Meditation|meditation]]: morality (*sila*), concentration ([[Samadhi]]), and wisdom (*panna*). Each influences the others, so they are cultivated together, not one at a time.

# Properties
- Demanding complete morality *before* meditating is unworkable, because morality itself needs a degree of mental control.[^2]
- Wisdom leads to morality. Truly understanding a situation makes compassion toward everyone involved automatic, and compassion automatically restrains thoughts, words, and deeds that would harm oneself or others. Problems arise only from failing to understand deeply.[^3]
- Only [[Mindfulness|mindfulness]] brings wisdom; concentration alone does not understand what it sees.[^4]
- Morality comes in levels; see [[Levels of Morality (Buddhism)]].
