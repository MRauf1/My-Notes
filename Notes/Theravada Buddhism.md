# Definition
> [!info] Theravada Buddhism[^1]
> One of the two broad streams of [[Buddhism]], prevailing in South and Southeast Asia (Sri Lanka, Thailand, Burma, Laos, Cambodia). Its original literature is written in Pali.

# Properties
- Counterpart of [[Mahayana Buddhism]].[^1]
- Presents an effective system for exploring the deeper levels of the mind, down to the root of [[Consciousness|consciousness]] itself.[^2]
- Its traditional literature describes two types of [[Meditation|meditation]]: [[Samatha Meditation|Samatha]] (concentration, tranquility) and [[Vipassana Meditation|Vipassana]] (insight, clear awareness).[^3]
- Its canon is the [[Tipitaka]]. Later commentarial summaries of meditation: Upatissa's *Vimuttimagga* (*The Path of Freedom*, 1st c. CE) and Buddhaghosa's *Visuddhimagga* (*The Path of Purification*, 5th c. CE), still the standard text on meditation.[^4]
- Calls the restless, wandering mind "monkey mind" (the Tibetan tradition likens it to a waterfall of thought).[^5]
- It is traditional to begin each meditation session by reciting formulas (e.g., [[Loving-Kindness Meditation|loving-kindness]] wishes); these are psychological cleansing devices that work only with active mental participation, not as ritual performed for its own sake.[^6]
