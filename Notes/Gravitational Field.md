# Definition
> [!info] Gravitational Field[^1]
> The vector field $\mathbf{C}$ produced by a mass $m$ at displacement $\mathbf{r}$ from it,
> $$
> \begin{align}
> \mathbf{C} = -Gm\frac{\mathbf{r}}{r^3}
> \end{align}
> $$
> such that a second mass $m'$ placed at that location experiences a [[Force]] $\mathbf{F} = m'\mathbf{C}$, per [[Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation]].

# Properties
- The gravitational analogue of the [[Electric Field]], constructed by the same source/response separation.
- The total field due to multiple masses obeys the [[Superposition Principle]] only approximately: unlike the electric field's exact vector superposition, this fails once gravity is strong, since [[Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation]] itself is only the weak-field limit of general relativity.
- Recoverable as the negative gradient of the scalar [[Gravitational Potential]] $\Psi$: $\mathbf{C}=-\nabla\Psi$, mirroring $\mathbf{F}=-\nabla U$ for a general [[Conservative Force]].

[^1]: [The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. I, Ch. 12: Characteristics of Force](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_12.html)
