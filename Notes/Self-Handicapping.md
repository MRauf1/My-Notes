# Definition
> [!info] Self-Handicapping[^1]
> Withholding effort (or creating other obstacles) so that a possible failure can be attributed to lack of trying rather than lack of ability. In [[Carol Dweck|Dweck]]'s account it is a defence of the [[Fixed Mindset|fixed mindset]]: effort robs one of excuses, so trying and still failing ("I gave it my all and it wasn't good enough") is its worst fear.

# Properties
- In the fixed mindset, effort is only for the deficient and great geniuses are not supposed to need it; for those considered naturals, effort "can reduce you".[^2]
- **Low-effort syndrome**: adolescents facing hard school transitions mobilise their resources to protect their egos rather than to learn, chiefly by not trying; to adults saying "now we will measure you", they answer "No you won't."[^3]
- After failure, fixed-mindset students planned to study *less* and considered cheating, while growth-mindset students planned to study harder.[^4]
- In the growth mindset, wanting something and doing nothing about it is almost inconceivable, and "I could have been" is heartbreaking rather than comforting.[^5]
- Cf. [[Procrastination]].
