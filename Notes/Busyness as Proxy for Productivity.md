# Definition
> [!info] Busyness as Proxy for Productivity (Newport)[^1]
> Without clear indicators of what it means to be productive and valuable in their jobs, many knowledge workers fall back on an industrial-era indicator of productivity: doing lots of stuff in a visible manner.

# Properties
- Knowledge work is not an assembly line. Extracting value from information is often at odds with busyness rather than supported by it.[^2]
- It is self-preserving when job goals are unclear, and is hidden by the [[Metric Black Hole]].[^3]
- Examples driven by the [[Principle of Least Resistance]]: running your day out of your inbox, which feels satisfyingly productive; standing meetings used as a blunt form of personal organization and a "visible simulacrum of progress"; quick, vague emails that clear the sender's inbox at the receiver's expense.[^4]
- Counter-example: Feynman avoided administrative duties to protect "real good physics work".[^5]
- Newport later named this [[Pseudo-Productivity]].
- Related: [[Shallow Work]], [[Productivity Debt]], [[Deep Work]].
