# Definition
> [!info] Security Theater (Schneier)[^1]
> Countermeasures that increase the *feeling* of security without increasing, or while even decreasing, actual security. "Security is both a feeling and a reality, and they're not the same." Much human activity is driven by the desire for the feeling of safety, which is at best indirectly related to being safer.

# Properties
- **Threat-model failure.** Banning specific objects doesn't change the fact that an attacker willing to die keeps the upper hand over defenders unwilling to; such measures address the feeling, not the threat.[^1]
- **Cause.** Our risk intuitions (e.g. overweighting spectacular, novel or uncontrollable risks) evolved for ancestral environments and misfire because modern society changed faster than evolution can track.[^2] See [[Cognitive Bias]] and [[Availability Heuristic]].
- **Evidence.** In 2015 covert tests, US Department of Homeland Security red teams got prohibited items or mock explosives past airport screening in 67 of 70 attempts (about 95%), despite the visible screening apparatus (DHS Office of Inspector General, 2015).
