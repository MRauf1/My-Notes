# Definition
> [!info] Skillful and Unskillful Thoughts[^1]
> Buddhism classifies thoughts as skillful or unskillful rather than good or bad.
> - **Unskillful** thoughts are connected with greed, hatred, or delusion (the [[Three Unwholesome Roots]]). The mind most easily builds them into obsessions, and they lead away from [[Nibbana|liberation]].
> - **Skillful** thoughts are connected with generosity, compassion, and wisdom. They can be used as specific remedies for unskillful thoughts.

# Properties
- **Antidote method.** An obsession can be cancelled by generating its opposite (e.g., generosity against greed, [[Loving-Kindness Meditation|loving-kindness]] against hatred). You can find a specific antidote for any troubling thought.[^2]
- If the obsession is too strong to balance, first weaken it. Contemplate thoroughly what it does to your life, health, relationships, and progress, until you feel real loathing (the Pali texts compare it to wearing a rotting carcass around your neck). Then generate the opposite.[^3]
- Skillful thoughts are only temporary antidotes. They "kick it under the rug" long enough for [[Mindfulness|mindfulness]] to reach the root of the ego process. Liberation is unconditioned and cannot be built out of thoughts, so true generosity arises only when greed evaporates through mindfulness.[^4]
- Simply recalling why you are sitting (to focus on the breath, common to all living beings) can also settle an obsessed mind.[^5]
