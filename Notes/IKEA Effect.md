# Definition
> [!info] IKEA Effect[^1]
> People value things more when they have had a hand in creating them (Norton, Mochon and Ariely, 2012). For ideas, Carnegie's Principle 7 applies: "Let the other person feel that the idea is his or hers." People "have much more faith in ideas that [they] discover for [themselves] than in ideas that are handed to [them] on a silver platter."

# Properties
- **Evidence.**
	- Participants who assembled IKEA boxes or folded origami were willing to pay far more for their own creations than for identical pre-assembled ones. The effect vanished when they did not finish the task (Norton, Mochon and Ariely, 2012).
	- Self-generated arguments are more persuasive than arguments received from others (Aronson, 1999). See [[Motivational Interviewing]].
- **Participation in decisions.** Workers who participated in designing changes to their jobs adapted faster and resisted less than those simply told about the changes (Coch and French, 1948). Later meta-analyses find modest effects of participation on satisfaction and smaller ones on productivity (Wagner, 1994). "People are more likely to accept an order if they have had a part in the decision that caused the order to be issued."[^2]
- **Credit.** Colonel House let Woodrow Wilson believe House's ideas were Wilson's own, and even credited him publicly. Wesson sold sketches only after asking the buyer for ideas.[^3] Giving up credit is a real cost. Use it when the result matters more than recognition.
