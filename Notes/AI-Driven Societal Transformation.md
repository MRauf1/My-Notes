# Definition
> [!info] AI-Driven Societal Transformation[^1]
> The thesis that AI is not an industry, product, or strategic "domain" but an enabler of nearly all industries and facets of life, whose capacities to learn, evolve, and surprise will transform them all, altering human identity and the human experience of reality at levels unseen since the dawn of the modern age.

# Properties
- Most technologies are absorbed by existing social frameworks within recognisable categories; AI instead transforms all realms of experience, ultimately at the philosophical level of how humans understand reality and their role in it. Digitisation and AI have produced phenomena truly new rather than more efficient versions of the past.[^2]
- The partial end of the postulated superiority of human reason promises changes more profound than the Enlightenment, and a shift more significant than any since the movable-type printing press; four centuries after Descartes, if AI "thinks", who are we?[^3]
- The individual reasoning human has held pride of place as the being most capable of understanding and shaping the world; AI is a powerful new player in that quest, and the definitions of human role, aspiration, and fulfilment will change (cf. [[Copernican Principle]]).[^4]
- AI's advancement may be inevitable, but its destination is not; halting it would merely cede the future to those willing to face the implications of their own inventiveness.[^5]
- Social effects:[^6]
	- New divides within and between societies, between adopters and those who opt out or lack means.
	- Divergence into mutually unintelligible realities ([[Algorithmic Echo Chamber]]).
	- People may prefer digital assistants to humans, who are less attuned to their preferences and more "disagreeable", reducing dependence on human relationships.
	- Transformation of work may jeopardise identity, fulfilment, and financial security.
- Closely related to [[AI as a Way of Knowing]] and [[Human-Centered Artificial Intelligence]].
