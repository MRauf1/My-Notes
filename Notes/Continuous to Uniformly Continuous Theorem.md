# Definition

> [!abstract] Theorem 1 ([[Continuous Function]] to [[Uniformly Continuous Function]] Theorem)[^1]
> If $f$ is continuous on a closed interval $[a, b]$, then it is [[Uniformly Continuous Function]] on $[a, b]$.
> Alternatively, $f$ is continuous on a [[Closed Set]] and [[Bounded Set]] $S$, then it is uniformly continuous on $S$.

> [!abstract] Theorem 2 (Heine–Cantor Theorem on [[Compact Metric Space]])[^2]
> Let $f$ be a continuous mapping of a [[Compact Metric Space]] $X$ into a [[Metric Space]] $Y$. Then $f$ is [[Uniformly Continuous Function|uniformly continuous]] on $X$.

Uniform continuity and continuity are therefore equivalent on compact sets. The finiteness of the covering in the definition of compactness is essential here: the minimum of a finite set of positive numbers ($\delta$'s) is positive, whereas the infimum of an infinite set of positive numbers may be $0$.[^2]

# Properties
- Theorem 1 is the special case $X = [a, b]$ ([[Heine-Borel Theorem]]).
- Fails without compactness: [[Noncompact Subset of Real Line Continuous Function Theorem]]
