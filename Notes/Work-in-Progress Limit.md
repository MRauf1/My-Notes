# Definition
> [!info] Work-in-Progress Limit[^1]
> A hard upper limit on the number of things you allow yourself to work on at once, as in Kanban. Burkeman suggests one big project at a time, or at most one work and one non-work project, seen to completion before moving on. Starting many projects lets you feel in control, but you can bounce to another whenever one becomes difficult, frightening or boring, so you make progress on none.

# Properties
- **Little's Law** (Little, 1961). For any stable queueing system, $L = \lambda W$, where $L$ is the average number of items in progress, $\lambda$ the average throughput and $W$ the average time each item spends in the system. At fixed throughput, $W = L / \lambda$: doubling work in progress doubles the average time to finish each project, without finishing more.
- **Switching costs.** Task switching costs time (Rubinstein, Meyer and Evans, 2001; Monsell, 2003), and [[Attention Residue]] lowers performance on the next task. Throughput itself therefore falls as work in progress rises.
- **Tolerating the anxiety.** Consciously postpone everything you can except one thing. Finishing important projects makes the anxiety worthwhile and leaves less to be anxious about.[^2]
