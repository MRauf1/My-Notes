# Definition
> [!info] Uncanniness[^1]
> (Heidegger, *unheimlich*) The experience of "not being at home in the world", in which familiar meanings are wrenched from objects, symbols disintegrate, and one is torn from one's moorings of "at-homeness", glimpsing that the familiar world is one's own constitution.

# Properties
- A typical consequence of death awareness: after a near-fatal accident the world felt precarious, with danger everywhere.[^1]
- We constitute a world that conceals that we have constituted it; the cosy, interconnected world of familiar objects buries a primordial emptiness that speaks in nightmares and mythic visions, and in moments when "the curtain of reality flutters open".[^2]
- Defamiliarisation extends to roles, values, rules, and ethics; social catastrophes (the Holocaust, mob violence, war) stun us because they show that contingency reigns and there is no solid ground.[^3]
- It is found by an earnest inward search, not externally; anxiety uses it to lead one back from the everyday, fallen mode to awareness of isolation and nothingness; to escape it we absorb ourselves in diversions ("Of what is man afraid? Of nothing!").[^4]
- Leads to [[Existential Isolation]] and [[Authenticity (Existentialism)|authenticity]].
