# Definition
> [!info] Teleology (Adlerian Psychology)[^1]
> Explaining behavior and emotion by the **present goal** they serve rather than by **past causes**. It is the opposite of **etiology** (the study of causation). In the etiological reading, "he is anxious, so he cannot go out". In the teleological reading, "he does not want to go out, so he creates anxiety" as a means to that goal. "People are not driven by past causes but move toward goals that they themselves set."

# Properties
- **Etiology leads to determinism.** If past causes control present effects, then the present and the future are already fixed and cannot change. Everyone abused as a child would then turn out the same way, which they do not.[^2]
- **Trauma is denied.** Freud's trauma argument is the typical etiological view, and it is attractive because it turns a life into a narrative with clear causes. Adler: experiences influence us, but "nothing is actually determined by those influences. We determine our own lives according to the meaning we give to those past experiences."[^3]
- **Meaning, not events.** "It is the meaning that is attributed to [the past] that determines the way someone's present will be." The question is not "What happened?" but "How was it resolved?" Giving meaning to the past is the task of "you now".[^4]
- **Emotions as tools.** Anger is fabricated to serve a goal (for example, to make someone submit by shouting). It can be put away when the phone rings and taken out again after. People are not controlled by emotion. See [[Power Struggle (Adlerian Psychology)]].[^5]
- **Against nihilism.** Determinism leads to pessimism and giving up on life. If people can change, etiology cannot hold, and teleology follows. Freudian etiology "denies our free will and treats humans like machines".[^6]
- **Unhappiness is chosen.** One is unhappy because one judged "being unhappy" to be good for oneself, as a means to some goal.[^7]
- **Apparent cause and effect.** Reasons like "I have that boss, so I can't work" are created after the fact to serve the goal of not working. Changing the goal changes everything. See [[Inferiority Complex]].[^8]
- "No matter what has occurred in your life up to this point, it should have no bearing at all on how you live from now on." The past does not exist. See [[Energeial Life]].[^9]
- Basis of [[Lifestyle (Adlerian Psychology)]] and the [[Life-Lie]]. Compare [[Cognitive Reappraisal]] and [[Cognitive Therapy]], where appraisal rather than event shapes emotion.
- **The present decides the past.** Everyone is the compiler of a story of "me", rewriting the past to justify "me now", just as history is rewritten by the victor. From countless events one selects those that fit present goals, gives them meaning and turns them into memories, and erases the rest. "The past does not decide 'now'. It is your 'now' that decides the past." People who affirm their present also affirm their unhappy past ("I've been through a lot, but I'm fine with it").[^kk1]
- **The cheap wine of tragedy.** Clinging to past tragedies as "inviolable traumas" is not being shackled to the past. One *needs* that unhappy past, to numb the bitterness of an unfortunate "now".[^kk2]
- **"What should I do from now on?"** Neither "that bad person" nor "poor me" is needed. Seeking solace for past hurt breeds dependence. Look for the goals hidden in one's own and others' speech and conduct.[^kk3]
- **Living in possibility.** "If I'd had the ideal school and teacher, I wouldn't have ended up this way." For someone who does not want to change, change feels like "death itself".[^kk4]
- Adler's teleology rests on a belief in human dignity and in the ability to determine oneself at any time.[^kk5]

# Evidence
- **Correction: trauma does have causal effects.** Trauma exposure is a necessary condition for PTSD. [[Adverse Childhood Experiences]] show a dose-response relation with adult psychopathology and disease (Felitti et al., 1998). The flat denial of trauma is not supported.
- **Supported kernel.** Outcomes are not determined, and meaning matters. After potentially traumatic events, resilience is the most common trajectory (Bonanno, 2004). Negative appraisals of the trauma and its aftermath predict chronic PTSD, and therapies that change these appraisals work (Ehlers & Clark, 2000).
- **Emotions and goals.** People do regulate emotions instrumentally, for example by seeking anger before a confrontation (Tamir, 2009). Still, emotions also arise automatically from appraisals, and the claim that anger is wholly manufactured at will goes too far.
- **Memory serves present goals (supported).** Autobiographical memory is reconstructive (Bartlett, 1932). In the self-memory system, current goals of the "working self" shape which memories are retrieved and how they are built (Conway & Pleydell-Pearce, 2000). People also revise their past to fit present beliefs (Ross, 1989). See [[False Memory]]. The claim that "now decides the past" overstates this, because actual events still constrain memory.
- **Objection: goals have causes.** Even a goal like "avoid people" must have arisen from something. Modern accounts combine both: goals and appraisals are shaped by temperament and history, and in turn guide behavior.[^kk6]
