# Definition

Personal perseverance towards completing a goal/task. Tendency to keep trying and not giving up until success.

Was originally defined by [[Angela Duckworth]].

Early studies suggested grit predicts success beyond talent or IQ. **Update:** A meta-analysis of 88 samples (about 67,000 people) found that grit correlates only modestly with performance ($r \approx 0.18$). It overlaps heavily with conscientiousness ($\rho \approx 0.84$), and most of its predictive power comes from the perseverance facet. The claim that grit outpredicts talent or IQ is not supported (Credé, Tynan and Harms, 2017).

# How to Get Grit
- Pursue what interests you
- Practice until you can do it, and then keep practicing
- Find a purpose in what you do
- Have hope in what you're doing
- Surround yourself with gritty people

# Applying Grit
Adopt an attitude that looks at the end goal as the only acceptable outcome. This grants acceptance that you may not succeed on the first attempt. Failure then is seen as part of the process that grants knowledge that moves you to success.

# Properties
- In learning mathematics, grit and persistence matter more than talent; see [[Productive Struggle]].
- Goggins' *Can't Hurt Me* is an extreme case study of perseverance. Compare [[Central Governor Model]] and [[Steeling Effect]].

- **Brick walls (Pausch).** "The brick walls are there to stop the people who don't want it badly enough." Obstacles filter for commitment.[^1] Pausch's own route into a PhD needed both tenacity and "a boost from a mentor", so persistence works alongside help, not instead of it.[^2] See [[Learning from Failure]].
