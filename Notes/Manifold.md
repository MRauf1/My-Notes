# Definition
> [!info] Manifold[^1]
> A shape is (an $n$-dimensional) manifold if, "under a microscope", it looks the same as ordinary Euclidean space: every point has a neighborhood that is topologically equivalent (homeomorphic) to an open subset of [[Euclidean n-Space|$\mathbb{R}^n$]].

The manifold assumption is the basic simplifying assumption of differential geometry. It is powerful because it lets us transport what we know how to do in flat Euclidean space (work with vectors, differentiate, integrate, etc.) to curved spaces.[^2]

# Types
- Surfaces ($n = 2$): every point has a neighborhood that is a [[Topological Disk]].[^2]
- [[Simplicial Manifold]] — the combinatorial analogue; a [[Simplicial Surface]] is a simplicial 2-manifold.
- [[Smooth Surface]]

# Properties
- In machine learning, high-dimensional data is hypothesized to lie near a low-dimensional manifold ([[Manifold Hypothesis]], [[Intrinsic Dimension]]).
- There are many distinct ways a shape can "look like Euclidean space" (as a topological space, smoothly, conformally, metrically), giving rise to distinct sub-areas of differential geometry: differential topology, conformal geometry, Riemannian geometry, ….[^2]
