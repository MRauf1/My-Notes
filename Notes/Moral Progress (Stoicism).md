# Definition
> [!info] Moral Progress (Stoicism)[^1]
> (*Prokopē*) Advancement toward [[Stoic Virtue|virtue]] and thus toward happiness, equanimity, and contentment, since to progress is to draw nearer to the end that perfection produces. It is found not in reading books but in turning away from externals and working on one's own [[Prohairesis|will]], applying one's principles to whatever material comes one's way.

# Properties
- **Starting point**: awareness of one's own weakness and impotence in matters of supreme importance; recognising the condition of one's command center.[^1]
- **First task**: eliminate *presumption* (thinking one already knows), since no one can learn what he thinks he knows; also eliminate *diffidence* (believing contentment is impossible). Presumption is removed by Socratic cross-examination.[^2]
- **Signs of progress**: no criticism, praise, blame, or reproach of others; no talk of oneself as having standing; finding fault with oneself when obstructed; laughing inwardly at praise; not defending oneself from criticism; treating oneself "like an invalid", "as though he were an enemy intriguing against himself".[^3]
- The uneducated blame others, the partially educated blame themselves, the fully educated blame no one; "Oh, how I'm made to suffer … by myself" marks progress.[^4]
- Put up with being thought foolish about externals; one cannot keep the will in accord with nature and also secure externals.[^5]
- Do not procrastinate: "here, now, are the Olympic Games"; a single surrender can ruin progress. Yet failure is not final: one can compete again at once, "and if one day you're victorious, it will be as though you'd never failed".[^6]
- Do not lose heart for lack of natural ability: "we don't let the recognition that we won't reach the peak make us stop trying."[^7]
- Start by condemning what you have been doing without seeing yourself as hopeless; "there's nothing more malleable than the human mind"; both ruin and help come from within.[^8]
- Changing one's life costs something (old friends may like you less); "no one can make progress if he tries to do two things at once".[^9]
- Methods: [[Stoic Exercises]] and the [[Three Disciplines (Epictetus)]]; see also [[Growth Mindset]] and [[Procrastination]].
