# Definition
> [!info] Walking Meditation[^1]
> A transitional [[Vipassana Meditation|Vipassana]] exercise for staying calm and aware while in motion. It bridges static seated practice and everyday life. It floods consciousness with simple sensations from the feet and legs so thoroughly that everything else is pushed aside.

# Properties
- Walk very slowly back and forth along a path. Coordinate the movement with the breath (e.g., lift the heel while breathing in, rest the foot on its toes while breathing out). Stand for a minute at each end, then turn very slowly.[^2]
- Register every nuance of the movement: each muscle, every change in tactile sensation. Eventually words drop away and "the feet become your whole universe".[^3]
- The aim is to escape *into* reality rather than *from* it.[^4]
- Related daily-life practices: periodically sweep attention through the body to feel your posture, non-judgmentally. Do simple activities at very low speed so you can look deeply into them.[^5]
- Compare [[Productive Meditation]], which uses walking to think about a problem rather than for bare awareness.
