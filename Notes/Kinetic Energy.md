# Definition
> [!info] Kinetic Energy[^1]
> $$KE = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$$
> The energy an object of mass $m$ possesses by virtue of moving at velocity $v$, equal to the work required to accelerate it from rest to $v$; independent of what force produced the motion.

# Properties
- Its rate of change equals the [[Power]] delivered by the forces acting on the object, and its total change over a path equals the [[Work]] done along that path, per the [[Work-Energy Theorem]].
- For a system of several objects, the total kinetic energy is simply the sum of the kinetic energies of the individual objects.
- A non-relativistic approximation. The exact formula is $KE = (\gamma - 1)mc^2$, where $\gamma = 1/\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}$, which reduces to $\tfrac{1}{2}mv^2$ when $v \ll c$ (see [[Mass-Energy Equivalence]]).
- Interconverts with [[Potential Energy]] (e.g. [[Gravitational Potential Energy]]) under [[Conservation of Energy]].
- At the microscopic level, the disordered kinetic energy of a large number of jiggling atoms is experienced macroscopically as heat, per the [[Kinetic Theory of Heat]].
- For motion in space, $v^2$ is the [[Dot Product]] of the [[Velocity Vector]] with itself: $KE = \tfrac{1}{2}m(\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{v})$; unlike [[Momentum]], kinetic energy itself has no direction.

[^1]: [The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. I, Ch. 4: Conservation of Energy](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_04.html)
