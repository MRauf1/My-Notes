# Definition

> [!info] Definition 1 (Parametric Equations)[^1]
> A system of parametric equations, or parametrization, of a plane curve $C$ is
> $$
> \begin{align}
> x = f(t), \quad y = g(t), \qquad t \in I
> \end{align}
> $$
> where $f, g$ are [[Function|functions]] of the parameter $t$ on an interval $I$ ([[Interval Notation]]), and $C = \{(f(t), g(t)) \mid t \in I\}$.

The curve $C$ is a set of points and has no orientation. The parametrization gives it an orientation, the direction of increasing $t$, and different parametrizations of the same curve can give different orientations.

# Properties
- A curve need not be the [[Graph of Function|graph of a function]] of $x$ or of $y$ to have a parametrization.
- Eliminating the parameter gives a rectangular equation satisfied by $C$, but the range of $t$ can restrict $C$ to only part of that graph (e.g. $x = \cos t$, $y = \sin t$ on $[0, \pi]$ is the upper half of the [[Unit Circle]]).
- Directed line segment from $P_0$ to $P_1$: $(x, y) = P_0 + t(P_1 - P_0)$, $0 \leq t \leq 1$.
- Generalized to $\mathbb{R}^n$ as a [[Curve]].
