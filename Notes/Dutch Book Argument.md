# Definition
> [!info] Dutch Book Argument[^1]
> The historically most influential argument for [[Probabilism]], which says that anyone who violates probabilism is susceptible to a sure monetary loss:
> - **P1.** Your [[Credence|degrees of belief]] should be matched by your betting prices.
> - **[[Dutch Book Theorem]].** If your betting prices violate the rules of probability, then there is a Dutch Book consisting of bets at your betting prices.
> - **P2.** If there is a Dutch Book consisting of bets at your betting prices, then you are susceptible to a guaranteed loss.
> - **P3.** If you are susceptible to a guaranteed loss, then you are irrational.
> - **C.** Therefore, if your degrees of belief violate the rules of probability, then you are irrational.

# Properties
- **Objection to P1**: the bets an agent makes depend on the interaction of their degrees of belief with their [[Utility Function|utilities]] (a psychological property of the agent measuring happiness), and utility is not proportional to money, so betting prices need not match degrees of belief.
	- One response reformulates the argument in terms of utility rather than money, but utility is criticized as a mysterious concept and it complicates the argument.
	- A simpler response restricts the argument to [[Simple Agent|simple agents]], for whom monetary payoffs function as utilities, which ensures P1.
- **Objection to P2**: there are ways to block the inference from the existence of a Dutch Book to actually being susceptible to a guaranteed loss.
- **Objection to P3**: one can deny that susceptibility to a sure loss is irrational by arguing either that all agents are susceptible to a sure loss, or that there is an upside to being susceptible. The first is refuted by the [[Converse Dutch Book Theorem]].
- **General worry**: the argument is pragmatic (usefulness-based), whereas showing [[Epistemic Rationality|epistemic irrationality]] requires an epistemic (belief/truth/evidence-based) argument. All the objections to P1–P3 stem from losing money being only indirectly related to beliefs, which motivates the [[Depragmatized Dutch Book Argument]].
