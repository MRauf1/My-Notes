# Definition
> [!info] Cosmic Insignificance Therapy (Burkeman)[^1]
> The relief of remembering how little you matter in the scheme of things, through what Marcus Aurelius recommended in his *Meditations* and we might call "zooming out". When anxious, overwhelmed, or too full of yourself, widen your awareness from your small patch of ground to the whole world, and your moment in time to the backdrop of the eons. Reality doesn't need you to help run it.

# Properties
- Cosmic insignificance does *not* imply your actions don't matter. Believing things count only if they count on the vastest scale is another way of avoiding finitude. To escape it we adopt a grandiose standard of what matters and get demoralized when we fall short (Iddo Landau).[^2]
- Only a few can be extraordinary in any domain: "If we could all stand out from the crowd, there'd be no crowd." A quiet career helping a few people, an absorbing conversation, an act of kindness, or a hike can all count as meaningful.[^2]
- Sometimes you just know you are doing something that matters, and we may have an obligation to that felt sense over utilitarian calculation. If a value system implies that caring for a 95-year-old is a waste of time, the problem lies with the value system.[^3]
- We are irrevocably *of* the world. Our responsibility may be not to get our arms around it or justify ourselves before it, but to embody as fully as possible the momentary expression of it that we are.[^4]
- "The good thing about everything being so fucked up is that no matter where you look, there is great work to be done." (Derrick Jensen)[^4]
- "You don't much matter – yet you matter as much as anyone ever did."[^5]
