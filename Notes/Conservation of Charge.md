# Definition
> [!info] Conservation of Charge[^1]
> The net [[Electric Charge]] of an isolated system — the number of positive elementary charges minus the number of negative elementary charges — never changes; charge can be created or destroyed only in positive-negative pairs of equal and opposite sign.

# Properties
- One of a family of "counting" conservation laws, alongside [[Conservation of Baryon Number]] and [[Conservation of Lepton Number]], each exact in every observed interaction — unlike [[Conservation of Energy]], which sums abstract form-specific formulas rather than counting discrete units.
- Unlike baryon number and lepton number, charge conservation is not merely accidental: it is tied to the gauged local symmetry underlying [[Quantum Electrodynamics]], and remains exact in the Standard Model with no known violating process.

[^1]: [The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. I, Ch. 4: Conservation of Energy](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_04.html)
