# Definition
> [!info] Feature-Positive Effect[^1]
> The tendency to overstate the importance of information that is (mentally) easily available, which tilts thinking toward the most recently acquired facts rather than the most relevant ones (Allison and Messick, 1988; Newman, Wolff, and Hearst, 1980; Sainsbury, 1971).

# Properties
- The brain more easily remembers information that is recent, emotional, lively, concrete, or specific (ideally rhyming). Abstract, vague, or emotionally neutral information ranks low, which is a poor criterion for intellectual work.[^2]
- A slip-box counters it by confronting you with older and disconfirming notes, not just what comes to mind.[^1]
- **Darwin's golden rule.** Whenever a fact or thought contradicted his general results, Darwin wrote it down at once, because unfavorable facts "were far more apt to escape from the memory than favorable ones". As a result, few objections were raised that he had not already noticed and tried to answer.[^3]
- Related: confirmation bias, [[Zettelkasten]].
