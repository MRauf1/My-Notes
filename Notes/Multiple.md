# Definition

A [[Number|number]] is a multiple of $n$ if it is the [[Multiplication|product]] of a [[Natural Number|natural number]] and $n$.[^1] More generally, over the integers, $m$ is a multiple of $n$ if $m = kn$ for some $k \in \mathbb{Z}$; under this definition $0$ is a multiple of every integer, and every integer has negative multiples.

> [!example]- Examples 1 (Multiple)
> > [!example]- Example 1.1 (Basic Example)
> > $$
> > \begin{align}
> > 6 = 2 \cdot 3
> > \end{align}
> > $$
> > $6$ is a multiple of both $2$ and $3$.

# Properties

- A number is a multiple of $2$ iff its last digit is even.
- A number is a multiple of $3$ iff the sum of its digits is a multiple of $3$.
- A number is a multiple of $5$ iff its last digit is $0$ or $5$.
- A number is a multiple of $10$ iff its last digit is $0$.
