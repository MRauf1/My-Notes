# Definition
> [!info] Lorentz Force Law[^1]
> The total electric and magnetic [[Force]] on a charge $q$ moving with velocity $\mathbf{v}$ through an [[Electric Field]] $\mathbf{E}$ and magnetic field $\mathbf{B}$:
> $$
> \begin{align}
> \mathbf{F} = q(\mathbf{E} + \mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{B})
> \end{align}
> $$

# Properties
- The magnetic contribution is always perpendicular to both the charge's velocity and to $\mathbf{B}$, since it is given by a cross product; it therefore changes the direction of a charge's motion but never its speed, doing no [[Work]] on the charge.
- Reduces to $\mathbf{F} = q\mathbf{E}$ — [[Coulomb's Law]] via the field — for a charge at rest ($\mathbf{v}=0$).
- A charge's velocity is frame-dependent, so what one observer analyzes as a magnetic force may be analyzed by an observer in a different [[Frame of Reference]] as (at least partly) an electric force; $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ mix into one another under a change of reference frame, much as energy and momentum mix in the [[Four-Momentum]].

[^1]: [The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. I, Ch. 12: Characteristics of Force](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_12.html)
