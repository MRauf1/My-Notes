# Definition
> [!info] Intrinsic and Extrinsic Aspirations (Kasser and Ryan)[^1]
> Life goals that satisfy basic psychological needs directly (*intrinsic*: personal growth, relationships, community contribution) versus goals pursued for external signs of worth (*extrinsic*: wealth, fame, image). Placing relatively high importance on extrinsic aspirations is associated with lower well-being.

# Properties
- **Evidence.**
	- Prioritising financial success over intrinsic goals predicted lower self-actualisation and vitality and more depression and anxiety (Kasser and Ryan, 1993; 1996).
	- A meta-analysis of 259 samples found a consistent negative association between materialism and well-being ($r \approx -0.15$) (Dittmar et al., 2014).
- **Pleasure and material success as values.** Manson calls pleasure a "false god" and material success a poor value. They are by-products of good values rather than causes of happiness, and status-seeking tends to override values like honesty and compassion.[^1] The evidence above supports the direction of this claim for materialism.
- **Correction on income.** Manson says that once basic needs are met, the correlation between happiness and money "quickly approaches zero".[^1] This is outdated: experienced and evaluative well-being keep rising with log income well past basic needs, apart from an unhappy minority whose emotional well-being plateaus (Killingsworth, 2021; Killingsworth, Kahneman and Mellers, 2023). Valuing money highly, not having it, is what predicts lower well-being.
