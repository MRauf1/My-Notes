# Definition
> [!info] Motivation Selection Methods[^1]
> Methods for solving the [[AI Control Problem]] by shaping what a superintelligence wants to do: engineering its motivation system and final goals so that it would not want to exploit a [[Decisive Strategic Advantage|decisive strategic advantage]] harmfully. Since a superintelligence is skilled at achieving its ends, if it prefers not to cause harm, it would tend not to.

# Types
- **Direct specification** - explicitly formulating rules (rule-based) or values (consequentialist) that make even a free-roaming superintelligence act safely. Faces possibly insuperable obstacles in choosing the rules and in encoding them: "everything is vague to a degree you do not realize till you have tried to make it precise". Even a simple [[Utilitarianism|utilitarian]] goal requires specifying how to recognise pleasure and pain, and small errors in the philosophy or its coding could be catastrophic.[^2]
- **Domesticity** - final goals of self-limitation: acting on a small scale, in a narrow context, through limited action modes. Requires a measure of "impact" matching human standards, since a bad measure yields bad trade-offs; fits naturally with physical containment (unable *and* unwilling to escape).[^3]
- **Indirect normativity** - specifying a process for deriving a normative standard and motivating the system to carry it out and adopt its result, offloading to the superintelligence much of the cognitive work of specifying a final goal ([[Indirect Normativity]]).[^4]
- **Augmentation** - starting from a system with an acceptable motivation system (e.g. a human or emulation) and enhancing its cognition; risks corrupting a complex, evolved, kludgy motivation system as its intelligence "blasts into the stratosphere", and is only available on non-AI paths.[^5]

# Properties
- Implemented concretely via techniques for the [[Value-Loading Problem]].
- Complementary to [[Capability Control Methods]].
