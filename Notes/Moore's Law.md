# Definition
> [!info] Moore's Law[^1]
> Integrated circuit resources (chiefly the number of transistors per chip) double at a roughly constant rate over time. Gordon Moore originally predicted a doubling every 18–24 months, though in recent years the rate has slowed to closer to every two to three years.

# Properties
- One of the [[Great Ideas in Computer Architecture]]: computer architects design for the exponential growth in resources that Moore's Law predicts, rather than for the resources available today.
- Governs the growth of transistor density on an [[Integrated Circuit Manufacturing|integrated circuit]], not the performance of a single [[Central Processing Unit|processor]] directly.
- Its slowdown, together with the practical power limit for cooling commodity microprocessors, is a key reason clock rates stopped growing and the industry shifted toward [[Multicore Microprocessor|multicore microprocessors]].
