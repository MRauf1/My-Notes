In an experiment by [[Daniel Gilbert]], after choosing a poster, one group was told they could exchange it for another within a month, while the other group was told their decision was final. The group whose choice was final appreciated their poster far more.[^1] Gilbert and Ebert (2002) also found that people prefer to keep options open while failing to predict that reversibility lowers satisfaction.

**Settling (Burkeman).** Every real choice entails losing countless alternatives, so there's no point procrastinating to avoid those losses. "We find more charm in hope than in possession, in dreams than in reality" (Bergson), because a fantasized future can contain contradictory possibilities at once.[^2] Living fully requires settling on something to strive at (Goodin), ideally in a way that makes it harder to back out. When you can no longer turn back, anxiety falls away.[^3] See [[Commitment Device]] and [[Paradox of Choice]].
