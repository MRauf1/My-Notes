# Definition
> [!info] Big Bang
> The event marking the start of the [[Expansion of the Universe]] from an extremely hot, dense state that uniformly filled all of space; not a localized explosion into pre-existing empty space, but the beginning of space itself expanding everywhere at once.

# Properties
- Has no center or point of origin: since the initial dense state filled all of space uniformly, every location is equally "the center," and an observer anywhere sees all sufficiently distant galaxies receding from them.
- Matter does not fly outward through space like debris from an explosion; rather, space itself stretches and carries matter along with it, so new space is continuously created between galaxies instead of galaxies moving into a pre-existing void.
- Despite the early universe's density vastly exceeding that of any black hole, it did not immediately collapse under its own gravity: because the mass-energy was distributed perfectly uniformly, the gravitational pull on any point from every direction canceled out, leaving no net force and no center to collapse toward; in addition, the initial expansion rate was fast enough to outrun the deceleration caused by gravity, analogous to a projectile exceeding escape velocity.
- Immediately preceded (or, in some usages, marked by the end of) [[Cosmic Inflation]], a much faster and briefer episode of expansion.
