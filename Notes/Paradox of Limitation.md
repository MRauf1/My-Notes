# Definition

The more you try to manage your time with the goal of achieving total control and freedom of human limitations, the more stressful, empty, and frustrating life gets.[^1]

See also [[Imperfectionism]] and [[Efficiency Trap]].

In practice, embracing limits means accepting that you'll never have time for everything, making hard choices consciously rather than by default, and standing firm against FOMO. Missing out is guaranteed, and it is what makes choices meaningful.[^2]

**Giving up hope (Jensen, Chödrön).** To hope for an outcome is to place your faith in something outside yourself and outside the present moment. Giving up that hope, including the master hope that "this isn't really it", frees you to do the work and to focus on a few things that count.[^3] This "hope" is closer to wishful thinking than to hope as psychologists measure it. Snyder's hope theory (agency plus pathways to goals) predicts *better* outcomes, while pure positive fantasy predicts less effort (see [[Mental Contrasting]]). Related: [[Being-towards-Death]], [[Work-in-Progress Limit]].
