# Definition

> [!info] Definition 1 (Normal Space)[^1]
> A topological space $X$ is normal if every pair of disjoint [[Closed Set|closed sets]] $A, B \subseteq X$ can be covered by disjoint [[Open Set|open sets]], i.e., there exist open $U \supseteq A$ and $V \supseteq B$ with $U \cap V = \emptyset$.

> [!abstract] Theorem 2 ([[Metric Space|Metric Spaces]] are Normal)[^2]
> If $A$ and $B$ are disjoint closed sets in a [[Metric Space]] $X$, there exist disjoint open sets $U \supseteq A$ and $V \supseteq B$. Hence every metric space is normal.

Normality is a property defined for general topological spaces; some topological spaces are normal and some are not, but metric spaces always are.

# Properties
- Normal spaces are exactly the spaces where the [[Tietze Extension Theorem]] (and Urysohn's lemma) holds.
