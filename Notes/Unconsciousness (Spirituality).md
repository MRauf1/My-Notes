# Definition
> [!info] Unconsciousness (Tolle)[^1]
> Being identified with some mental or emotional pattern; the complete absence of the watcher (cf. [[Watching the Thinker]]). It comes in two degrees, which differ in degree, not in kind:
> - **Ordinary unconsciousness**: identification with one's thoughts, emotions, reactions, desires, and aversions. It is felt as a nearly continuous low-level unease, discontent, boredom, or nervousness, created by habitual resistance to or denial of what is.
> - **Deep unconsciousness**: an intensified version, triggered when the ego is challenged or threatened. It brings acute suffering and intense negativity (anger, acute fear, aggression, depression).

# Properties
- Ordinary unconsciousness is always linked to denial of the Now, which includes the *here*.[^2]
- In ordinary unconsciousness, activities that would be enjoyable in moderation become compulsive or addictive and give only short-lived symptom relief.[^3]
- Once you start witnessing your thoughts and emotions, you may notice the background "static" of ordinary unconsciousness and how rarely you are truly at ease.[^4]
- The remedy is to make it conscious: habitually monitor your mental-emotional state through self-observation. Ask whether there is any trace of resentment or unwillingness. Whether it is justified makes no difference; it is resistance to what is.[^5]
- How you meet challenges is the best indicator of your level of consciousness: challenges make the unconscious more deeply unconscious and the conscious more intensely conscious. So practice presence when things are going smoothly.[^6]
- Many ordinary patterns can simply be dropped once you realize you have a choice and are "not just a bundle of conditioned reflexes". Deep pain needs transmutation ([[Pain-Body]]). If a pattern has already arisen, accept it: you were not conscious enough to have a choice.[^7]
- As long as the conditioned mind runs your life, the apparent freedom of choice is an illusion.[^8]
- Tolle sees humanity's collective violence (over 100 million people killed by other humans in the twentieth century) as collective insanity, and claims "all evils are the effect of unconsciousness". He adds that sanity lies underneath the madness.[^9]
- Unrelated to the cognitive-psychology notion of the [[Unconsciousness|unconscious mind]].
