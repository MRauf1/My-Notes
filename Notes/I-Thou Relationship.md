# Definition
> [!info] I-Thou Relationship[^1]
> (Buber) A wholly mutual relationship involving full experiencing of the other with one's whole being, in contrast to the **I-It** relationship: a functional, subject–object relation to equipment (or to a person made into a thing) wholly lacking mutuality. "Man is a creature of the between."

# Properties
- The "I" itself differs in the two: the "I" is betweenness, created anew with each Thou; in I-It one holds back and inspects, categorises, and judges, while in I-Thou nothing is withheld.[^2]
- It differs from empathy: there is no "I" as such, only the basic word I-Thou.[^1]
- Its mode is **dialogue**, turning toward the other with one's whole being; turning away to oneself is *reflexion* and yields monologue. It requires *genuine* rather than *pseudo* listening, relinquishing stereotypes and letting oneself be shaped by the other's response.[^3]
- Frankl: much "encounter" in encounter groups is self-expression, "monologues disguised as dialogue".[^4]
- An ideal existing only in rare moments; one lives mostly in the I-It world, lest one burn up "in the white flame of the Thou".[^5]
- Patients moving from I-It to I-Thou ask "What are people for anyway?"; the sexually compulsive never exist "between" but always observe themselves.[^6]
- Healthy relating in [[Existential Isolation]]; compare mature [[Love]].
