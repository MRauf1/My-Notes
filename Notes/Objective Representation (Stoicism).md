# Definition
> [!info] Objective Representation (Stoicism)[^1]
> The Stoic technique of re-describing an event in objective, concrete, value-free language, sometimes in writing, so that only what the comprehensive (*kataleptic*) [[Impression (Stoicism)|impression]] contains remains. Emotive figures of speech and the value judgements added to the bare facts are deliberately stripped away, which moderates the emotional response.

# Properties
- **Rationale (Hadot).** Passions result from errors of judgement. So we must monitor our inner discourse to see whether erroneous value judgements have crept in, "for this would add something foreign to the comprehensive representation".[^1]
- **Counter-rhetoric.** Rhetoric condenses emotive power into aphorisms; objective description inverts it by removing the emotive charge, describing things more concretely and less abstractly.[^2]
- **Link to physics.** It describes things as if from an impersonal perspective (the [[View from Above]]): placing them in the context of the whole universe, noting their lifespan from origin to demise, and seeing them as part of universal causation ([[Stoic Determinism]]).[^3]
- Marcus Aurelius reminds himself to pause over initial impressions before adding any further value judgements or unfounded assumptions ([[Assent (Stoicism)]]).[^4]
- By suspending judgement about externals, the Stoic "purifies what was previously mingled", distinguishing subjective opinion from objective reality ([[Cognitive Distancing]]).[^5]
- Practical rule: "Separate your thoughts from the real facts. Stick to the facts and avoid using rhetoric to distort your own emotions."[^6]
- **Evidence.** Watkins's work on rumination finds that concrete, specific processing of upsetting events is more adaptive than abstract, evaluative processing. A randomised trial of *concreteness training* reduced depressive symptoms (Watkins et al., 2012).
