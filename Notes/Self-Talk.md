# Definition
> [!info] Self-Talk[^1]
> The inner dialogue a person directs at themselves. It can be *motivational* ("keep going") or *instructional* ("elbows in"), and positive or negative. "The most important conversations you'll ever have are the ones you'll have with yourself."

# Properties
- **Evidence.**
	- A meta-analysis of 32 studies found that self-talk improves sport performance ($d \approx 0.48$) (Hatzigeorgiadis et al., 2011).
	- Two weeks of motivational self-talk training increased cycling time to exhaustion by about 18% and lowered perceived exertion (Blanchfield et al., 2014).
- **Distanced self-talk.** Referring to yourself by name or as "you", as Goggins does ("You are a fucking machine!"), improved emotion regulation and performance under social stress compared with "I" (Kross et al., 2014).[^2]
- **Negative self-talk.** Goggins: doubt can ride as a backseat driver but must never pilot. Indulging in negative self-talk keeps you from pushing on.[^3] Reviews find that negative self-talk generally does not help performance (Tod, Hardy and Oliver, 2011).
- **Affirmations can backfire.** Repeating "I'm a lovable person" lowered mood in people with low self-esteem (Wood, Perunovic and Lee, 2009); see [[Ironic Process Theory]].
- Related: [[Self-Compassion]], [[Cognitive Reappraisal]], [[Central Governor Model]].
