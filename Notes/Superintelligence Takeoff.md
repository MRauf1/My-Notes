# Definition
> [!info] Superintelligence Takeoff[^1]
> The transition from roughly human-level machine intelligence to [[Superintelligence|superintelligence]], classified by its duration.

# Types
- *Slow takeoff* - over decades or centuries; offers excellent opportunities for human political processes to adapt and respond.[^1]
- *Moderate takeoff* - over months or years; gives humans some chance to respond but little time to analyse, test approaches, or solve complicated coordination problems.[^1]
- *Fast takeoff* - over minutes, hours, or days; offers scant opportunity to deliberate, so humanity's fate depends essentially on preparations already in place.[^1]

# Properties
- Bostrom argues a slow takeoff is improbable; if and when a takeoff occurs, it will likely be explosive ([[Intelligence Explosion]]).[^2]
- **Anthropomorphic illusion of a sharp jump**: humans treat "village idiot" and "Einstein" as the extremes of the intelligence scale, whereas on the scale of minds-in-general they are nearly indistinguishable. An AI may thus seem "dumb" while passing mice and chimpanzees, and then appear to cross from infra-idiot to ultra-Einstein in a very short period.[^3]
- **Takeoff speed determines the outcome**: with a fast takeoff, the first project almost certainly completes its takeoff before any other starts, likely gaining a [[Decisive Strategic Advantage]] and possibly forming a [[Singleton]]; with a slow takeoff, several projects may take off concurrently with none gaining an overwhelming lead ([[Multipolar Scenario]]); a moderate takeoff could go either way.[^4]
- Even a lead of a few days could, given a breakthrough, catapult the frontrunner into a decisive strategic advantage.[^5]
