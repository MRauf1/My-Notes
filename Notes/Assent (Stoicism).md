# Definition
> [!info] Assent (Stoicism)[^1]
> (*Synkatathesis*) The act of the mind accepting an [[Impression (Stoicism)|impression]] as true. Assent is up to us, whereas impressions are not; assenting to an impression that something is worth pursuing produces an inclination/desire, while withholding assent produces aversion (or, if uncertain, suspension of judgment).

$$\begin{align}
\text{impression} \xrightarrow{\text{assent / withholding / suspension}} \text{judgment} \longrightarrow \text{inclination (desire/aversion)} \longrightarrow \text{action}
\end{align}$$

# Properties
- Assent is prompted only by the feeling that something is the case, withholding by the feeling that it isn't, and suspension by the feeling that it is uncertain; likewise inclination follows judgment of what is expedient, so it is impossible to judge one thing best and desire another.[^2]
- Adult humans, unlike infants and animals, can choose whether and how to react to stimuli, by developing a disposition not to assent to immediate impressions.[^3]
- Assent mirrors our moral being: appropriate behaviour results from assenting to impressions that correctly judge the value of their source.[^4]
- "Our whole life is entirely a matter of what we assent to and what not"; beliefs and desires are forms of assent (M. Frede).[^5]
- No one can compel assent to a falsehood or prevent assent to a truth, so in the domain of assent the will is unimpeded.[^6]
- Epictetus called for an "art of assent"; its training is the third of the [[Three Disciplines (Epictetus)]].[^7]
- Hence "people are troubled not by things but by their judgments about things" (Handbook 5), a precursor of [[Cognitive Therapy]].[^8]
