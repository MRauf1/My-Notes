# Definition
> [!info] Implementation Intention[^1]
> (Peter Gollwitzer) A vivid, concrete plan specifying *when*, *where*, and *how* one will act toward a goal, typically of the form "if situation $Y$ arises, then I will do $Z$". Unlike mere goal intentions or vows, which are often useless ("the next day comes and the next day goes"), such plans greatly raise follow-through.

# Properties
- Procedure: think of something to do, learn, or confront; make a concrete plan (when, where, how); when obstacles and setbacks arise, form a new plan; act regardless of how one feels.[^2]
- Used in [[Mindset Change]] as the growth-oriented step after a setback.
- Cf. [[Procrastination]] and [[Habit]].
- **Evidence.** A meta-analysis of 94 studies found a medium-to-large effect on goal attainment ($d \approx 0.65$) (Gollwitzer and Sheeran, 2006). In an exercise study, 91% of people who planned when and where they would exercise did so at least weekly, versus 35–38% in the control and motivation-only groups (Milne, Orbell and Sheeran, 2002).[^3]
- **Clear's formula.** "I will [BEHAVIOR] at [TIME] in [LOCATION]." Time and location are the two most common cues. The plan removes the decision at the moment of action: "Many people think they lack motivation when what they really lack is clarity."[^4]
- **Habit stacking (BJ Fogg).** A special form that pairs the new habit with a current one: "After [CURRENT HABIT], I will [NEW HABIT]." The anchor should be specific, immediately actionable and of the same frequency as the new habit.[^5] Supported by evidence: flossing became more automatic when anchored *after* toothbrushing (Judah, Gardner and Aunger, 2013).
- Related: [[Fresh Start Effect]], [[Law of Cue]], [[Goal-Setting Theory]], [[Mental Contrasting]].
