# Definition
> [!info] Electron Cloud[^1]
> The electron cloud (or probability cloud) is the quantum-mechanical picture of an atom in which an electron does not follow a definite orbit around the nucleus, but instead has a position governed by a probability density $p(r)$, so that the atom is pictured as a nucleus surrounded by a cloud whose density is proportional to $p(r)$.

# Properties
- Follows from the [[Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle]]: in the hydrogen atom, the uncertainty in the electron's position is as large as the atom itself, so a definite classical orbit is meaningless.
- For an undisturbed hydrogen atom, the position probability density is $p(r) = Ae^{-2r/a}$, where $a$, the atomic radius ($\approx 10^{-10}$ m), is the characteristic distance over which $p(r)$ decays rapidly.
- Replaces the classical planetary model of the atom, in which the electron was imagined to orbit the nucleus at a definite radius.

[^1]: [The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. I, Ch. 6: Probability](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_06.html)
