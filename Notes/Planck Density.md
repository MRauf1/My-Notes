# Definition
> [!info] Planck Density
> $$\rho_P = \frac{c^5}{\hbar G^2} \approx 5.16 \times 10^{96}\ \text{kg/m}^3$$
> The natural unit of mass density formed from the fundamental constants $c$, $\hbar$, and $G$; the theoretical maximum energy density at which quantum mechanics and general relativity both become significant, so that a complete theory of quantum gravity would be required to describe physics beyond this density.

# Properties
- The mass-energy of the entire presently observable universe, if compressed to Planck density, would occupy a volume smaller than a proton — illustrating that no density law is being broken, since mass is not a rigid, volume-occupying substance but a form of energy, which can in principle be compressed without limit up to this quantum-gravitational threshold.
- Marks the boundary of validity for the classical [[Big Bang]] model; conditions in the universe at or before the [[Planck Time]] are described only speculatively, pending a working theory of quantum gravity.
- Part of the system of [[Planck Units]], alongside the [[Planck Length]] and [[Planck Time]].
