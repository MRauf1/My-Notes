# Definition
> [!info] Five Stages of Problem Behavior[^1]
> Contemporary Adlerian psychology's classification of misbehavior (in children and adults) by its hidden **goal**. Every stage is rooted in the need for belonging, pursued as a *special* position in the community. Intervene as early as possible, before the behavior escalates.
> 1. **Demand for admiration.** Trying to be the "good child" in order to be praised and privileged. Without praise, motivation collapses, and cheating may begin.
> 2. **Attention drawing.** "It's okay not to be praised, so I'll just stand out." The principle is standing out, not being bad. Rebuke is preferred to being ignored.
> 3. **Power struggle.** Provocation and defiance to show one's strength and gain a privileged position.
> 4. **Revenge.** When love is not forthcoming, the person seeks hatred instead: "love's revenge" on those who would not recognize the irreplaceable "me".
> 5. **Proof of incompetence.** Despairing of oneself and announcing "I'm incompetent, so don't expect anything of me", to avoid further hurt.

# Properties
- **Stages 1–2.** Respond with [[Respect (Adlerian Psychology)|respect]]: show that they have worth without being special. Attend to everyday details rather than praising "good" acts.[^2]
- **Stage 3.** Taking up the racquet of anger and returning a reprimand means joining their court, and the rally begins. Leave the court as soon as you detect a power struggle. Even looking about to get angry puts you on the court. Illegal acts are handled legally. See [[Power Struggle (Adlerian Psychology)]].[^3]
- Stages 1–3 express the love-starved wish "I want you to have greater regard for me". When the person concludes that love will not come, they turn to hatred.[^4]
- **Stages 4–5.** Help from the person targeted only escalates the behavior. Turn to an uninvolved outside party (another teacher or a specialist). Stage 5 can resemble mental illness and is hard even for specialists.[^5]
- Most problem behavior stops at stage 3, so educators play a large preventive role.[^6]
- An application of [[Teleology (Adlerian Psychology)]]: look for the goal hidden in each act, and remember that every act is directed at someone.[^7]
- Related: [[Adlerian Education]], [[Desire for Recognition]], [[Community Feeling]].

# Evidence
- The framework extends Rudolf Dreikurs's four "mistaken goals" of misbehavior (attention, power, revenge, display of inadequacy; Dreikurs & Soltz, 1964). It is a clinical heuristic with little rigorous empirical validation. Its practical advice (not reinforcing attention-seeking, avoiding coercive escalation) agrees with evidence-based parent training, for example coercion theory (Patterson, 1982).
