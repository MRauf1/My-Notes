# Definition
> [!info] Shaping (Machine Learning)[^1]
> Skinner's technique for instilling complex behaviour through simple rewards by reinforcing a series of successive approximations of the target behaviour, adopted in reinforcement learning to overcome *reward sparsity* (agents learn only by first stumbling onto success, which may never happen).

# Types
- [[Curriculum Learning]] - start with simplified versions of the task.[^2]
- [[Reward Shaping]] - keep the full task but add bonus "pseudorewards" as incentives.[^2]

# Properties
- Skinner found the most persistent behaviour arose from *variable ratio schedules*, where reward follows a fluctuating number of responses ([[Operant Conditioning]]).[^3]
- Applies to humans as well as animals and machines: we are almost always reinforcing others' behaviour whether we mean to or not (e.g. respond promptly to polite bids for attention and less to loud ones). What separates modern humans from cave-dwellers is not raw intellect but a good curriculum.[^4]
- "The subjects were always right": if a learner fails, the fault lies with the experimenter's shaping.[^5]
- Games are compelling because they are "Skinnerian masterpieces": levels are a perfect curriculum, points perfect pseudorewards, each mission at the edge of the player's ability; the real world is much harder to shape. Environments where myopic decision-making works train people to rely on it.[^6]
