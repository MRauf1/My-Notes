# Definition
> [!info] Mass-Energy Equivalence[^1]
> $$E = mc^2$$
> An object possesses energy $E$ by virtue of its rest [[Mass|mass]] $m$ alone, independent of any motion or configuration; first derived by Einstein from special relativity, with $c$ the speed of light in vacuum acting as the conversion factor between mass and energy units. This is the special case, for an object at rest, of the more general [[Relativistic Energy-Momentum Relation]].

# Properties
- When a particle and its [[Antiparticle]] annihilate, their combined rest-mass energy is liberated, e.g. as radiant [[Photon]] energy.
- Unifies mass and energy as contributions to a single conserved quantity under [[Conservation of Energy]]: a system's total energy includes its rest-mass energy alongside its [[Kinetic Energy]] and other forms.
- Extends [[Kinetic Energy]] to the exact relativistic formula $KE = (\gamma-1)mc^2$, in which the rest-mass energy $mc^2$ is the part remaining even at zero velocity.
- Special case ($p=0$) of the [[Relativistic Energy-Momentum Relation]].
- Makes rest mass non-additive for a composite system: when two moving objects collide and stick together, the rest mass of the resulting object exceeds the sum of the colliding objects' individual rest masses, since the kinetic energy they carried does not disappear but is absorbed into the composite's rest energy — required for [[Conservation of Momentum|momentum]] and [[Conservation of Energy|energy]] to both hold across the collision. Consequently, a particle's total energy need not be decomposed into the rest, kinetic, and potential energies of its internal parts; it is simpler, and often necessary, to speak only of its total energy $mc^2$.[^2]
