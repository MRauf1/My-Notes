# Definition
> [!info] Organizational Mindset[^1]
> The [[Mindset|mindset]] woven into the fabric of an organisation, on which its members show strong consensus: a fixed-mindset **culture of genius**, which prizes natural talent, or a growth-mindset **culture of development**, which prizes the development of ability.

# Properties
- **Employees**: in growth-mindset organisations people have far more trust in the company and more empowerment, ownership, and commitment, and report support for reasonable risk-taking, innovation, and creativity; in fixed-mindset organisations they want to leave more, and report cutthroat or unethical behaviour, since everyone wants to be the superstar and may cheat or cut corners, and teamwork nosedives.[^2]
- **Supervisors** in growth-mindset companies see far more management potential in their team members.[^3]
- **Fixed-mindset leaders** use the company as a platform to affirm their superiority: concern with a "reputation for personal greatness" (Jim Collins), a "gargantuan personal ego" in over two-thirds of comparison-company leaders, a "genius with a thousand helpers" model, no great teams, refusal to face deficiencies, feeling invulnerable while leading toward ruin, and finally blame, excuses, and stifling critics and rivals. "CEO disease" is reigning from a pedestal.[^4]
- **Brutal bosses** enhance their own sense of power at subordinates' expense, often targeting the most competent (the greatest threat); everything then revolves around pleasing the boss and the whole company falls into a fixed mindset of fear of being judged, killing courage and innovation.[^5]
- **Growth-mindset leaders** (e.g. Jack Welch, Lou Gerstner, Anne Mulcahy) believe in human potential, use the company as an engine of growth, emphasise teamwork over the "royal I", listen to front-line workers, select for mindset rather than pedigree, shut down elitism and hierarchy, replace terror with mentoring, reward teamwork and shared credit, face brutal facts while keeping faith in success, and end full of gratitude rather than bitterness. True self-confidence is "the courage to be open".[^6]
- **Managers**: fixed-mindset managers judge employees once, coach little, fail to notice improvement, and reject critical feedback; growth-mindset managers coach more, notice improvement, and welcome critiques. The growth mindset can be taught to managers in a workshop, with effects persisting over six weeks of follow-up.[^7]
- **Negotiation** (Kray and Haselhuhn): 47% of those taught a fixed mindset about negotiation chose a show-off task versus 12% of those taught a growth mindset; the growth group did almost twice as well, found creative deals serving both parties' interests, and earned higher course grades.[^8]
- **Creating a growth-mindset environment**: present skills as learnable; value learning and perseverance over ready-made genius; give feedback that promotes learning; present managers as resources for learning. Leaders are made, not born (Warren Bennis).[^9]
- Fixed-mindset cultures breed [[Groupthink]].
