# Definition
> [!info] Argument from Various Disabilities[^1]
> The objection to machine intelligence of the form "a machine can never do X", where X ranges over traits such as being kind, having a sense of humour, telling right from wrong, making mistakes, falling in love, enjoying strawberries, learning from experience, being the subject of its own thought, or doing something really new.

# Properties
- One of the [[Objections to Machine Intelligence]].
- Turing's diagnosis: such claims are usually unsupported and arise from an unconscious application of scientific [[Principle of Induction (Epistemology)|induction]] to the limited, special-purpose machines people have seen, concluding that all machines must share those limitations.[^1]
- The works and customs of mankind are poor material for scientific induction: reliable conclusions require investigating a very large part of space-time, otherwise one draws hasty generalisations from a narrow sample (a [[Fallacy of Weak Induction]]).[^1]
- Many of the listed disabilities are connected to limited storage capacity of existing machines, or are disguised forms of the [[Argument from Consciousness]].

[^1]: [Turing, Computing Machinery and Intelligence, p. 13](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/PGZER5NA?page=13&annotation=UBSYRXFU)
