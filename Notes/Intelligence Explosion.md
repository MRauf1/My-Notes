# Definition
> [!info] Intelligence Explosion[^1]
> I. J. Good's hypothesis that, since designing machines is itself an intellectual activity, an ultraintelligent machine could design even better machines, leading to a recursive runaway increase in intelligence that leaves human intelligence far behind. Thus the first ultraintelligent machine would be the last invention humans need ever make, provided it is docile enough to tell us how to keep it under control.

# Properties
- The speed of the resulting transition is characterised by its [[Superintelligence Takeoff|takeoff]].
- The rate of change of intelligence can be modelled as optimisation power divided by *recalcitrance* (the inverse of responsiveness to optimisation effort):[^2]
$$\begin{align}
\frac{d\,\text{Intelligence}}{dt} = \frac{\text{Optimization power}}{\text{Recalcitrance}}
\end{align}$$
- For individuals and governments most resources have diminishing returns, but the same need not hold for AIs.[^3]
- A seed AI ([[Seed AI]]) improving its own architecture is the canonical mechanism.
- Achieving a "controlled detonation" requires solving the [[AI Control Problem]] in advance.
