# Definition
> [!info] Facing the Consequences (Burkeman)[^1]
> "You are free to do whatever you like. You need only face the consequences." Every choice comes with costs you can choose to shoulder. "There are no solutions, only trade-offs": at any moment of choice, the only questions are what the price is and whether it is worth paying.

# Properties
- Acknowledging that an option would be terrible, yet that you are free to take it and bear the cost, gives psychological room to maneuver.[^2]
- The consequences of a given choice can be far more severe for some people than for others. That unfairness doesn't change the fact that every choice is a weighing of trade-offs.[^3]
- **Bad faith (Sartre).** There is a secret comfort in telling yourself you have no options. It is easier to feel trapped than to face the dizzying responsibility of freedom (see [[Authenticity (Existentialism)]], [[Responsibility (Existential Psychology)]]).[^4]
- In practice you will decline things you never cared about but didn't dare refuse. When you do the undesired thing anyway, you do it knowing the cost, which feels very different from a resentful "I have no choice".[^5]
- The result is *freedom in limitation*, not freedom from it: the freedom to examine the trade-offs and pick the one you like.[^6]
