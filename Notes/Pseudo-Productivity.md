# Definition
> [!info] Pseudo-Productivity (Newport)[^1]
> The use of visible activity as the primary means of approximating actual productive effort. It is not a formal system but "more like a mood—a generic atmosphere of meaningful activity maintained through frenetic motion".

# Properties
- **Origin.** Industrial productivity notions didn't fit [[Knowledge Work]], where effort varies, workloads are complex and shifting, and organizing work is left to individuals. "The knowledge worker cannot be supervised closely or in detail… He can only be helped. But he must direct himself." (Peter Drucker, 1967) Lacking metrics and processes, managers fell back on visible activity as a crude proxy (cf. [[Busyness as Proxy for Productivity]]).[^2]
- Long sessions without obvious "contrails of effort" become a source of anxiety, so it feels safer to chime in on email threads and jump on calls than to create a bold new strategy.[^3]
- **Breakdown.** From the mid-1990s, email, portable computing, and low-friction communication made the proxy unsustainable. It became an ever-amplifying cycle of simply working a lot, cramming effort into every corner of life. Burnout and stress rose alongside measures of employee effort (Gallup).[^4]
- Burnout hurts most "when you want to care about something but you're removed from the capacity to do the thing … properly", because you are expected to do too many other things.[^5]
- Industrial-style metrics will never fit knowledge work, and shouldn't, since that quantitative approach brings its own inhumanities.[^6]
- The alternative is [[Slow Productivity]].
