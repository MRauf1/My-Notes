# Definition
> [!info] Stoic Determinism[^1]
> The Stoic view that everything has antecedent causes and the world's events form an endless chain of necessary causation (fate), combined with the claim that things are not always *necessitated* by their antecedent causes: an outcome depends both on an **external cause** and on the **internal cause**, the nature of the thing acted upon. It is an early form of compatibilism.

# Properties
- Chrysippus's cylinder (roller): the person pushing it is the external cause, but that it *rolls* is due to its own shape, its internal cause.[^1]
- So the things that happen to us are entirely predetermined, but our responses to them are not: impressions are not up to us, [[Assent (Stoicism)|assent]] is.[^2]
- This leaves only inner life as the sphere of choice and responsibility, grounding the [[Dichotomy of Control]].[^2]
- Fated events thus become opportunities to practise virtue rather than constraints.[^3]
- Cleanthes's hymn, quoted by Epictetus: "Lead me, Zeus, both you and Destiny, … and I shall gladly follow. And if I am unwilling out of wickedness, still I shall follow."[^4]
- **Two senses of freedom (Robertson).** "Free will" is ambiguous between metaphysical freedom (independence from antecedent causes) and ordinary freedom (performing one's function without external obstruction). The Stoics affirm only the latter, which is compatible with determinism: "the cylinder rolls freely, its movement determined by antecedent events". The mind's character is the internal cause that directs its response to external impingements.[^rob1]
- The inner feeling of free will reflects ignorance of causes (Spinoza; Dubois).[^rob2]
- **Therapeutic use.** Existential therapists stress free will (Frankl). The Socratic tradition, from the Stoics to Spinoza and Dubois, instead held that contemplating determinism calms the emotions: it supports acceptance ([[Amor Fati]]) and empathy ([[Philosophical Empathy]]). It doesn't license passivity ([[Lazy Argument]]).[^rob3]
