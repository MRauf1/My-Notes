# Definition
> [!info] Hooke's Law[^1]
> The restoring [[Force]] exerted by an ideal spring is proportional to its displacement $x$ from equilibrium, and directed opposite to that displacement:
> $$
> \begin{align}
> F = -kx
> \end{align}
> $$
> where $k$ is the spring constant.

# Properties
- Combined with [[Newton's Second Law]], yields the governing equation of [[Simple Harmonic Motion]]: $-kx = m\dfrac{d^2x}{dt^2}$.
- The restoring nature of the force — always directed back toward equilibrium, and growing with displacement — is what produces sustained oscillation, rather than the object simply flying off or coming to rest at the point of maximum displacement.
- Emerges as the small-displacement, linear approximation to the [[Van der Waals Force|interatomic force]] curve near its equilibrium separation, for any material; how large a displacement this approximation tolerates depends on the material (very small for soft materials like putty, much larger for steel).

[^1]: [The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. I, Ch. 9: Newton's Laws of Dynamics](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_09.html)
