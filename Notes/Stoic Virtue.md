# Definition
> [!info] Stoic Virtue[^1]
> The proper functioning of the soul in accord with its nature under the guidance of reason, i.e. perfected rationality. It is the only good, and is both necessary and sufficient for happiness ([[Eudaimonia]]). Following [[Socrates]], the Stoics held that virtue is knowledge.

# Types
The four primary (cardinal) virtues, each a kind of knowledge:[^2]
- *Prudential wisdom* - knowledge of what is good and bad.
- *Courage* - knowledge of what to fear and what not to fear.
- *Moderation* - knowledge of what to pursue and what to avoid.
- *Justice* - knowledge of what to give or not give others.

# Properties
- Virtue always benefits both oneself and those around one.[^1]
- Rationality is the only fundamentally human quality, shared only with the gods; a rational, virtuous person has fulfilled his potential as a human being. Progress is not acquiring new faculties but learning to trust one's rational faculty.[^2]
- All virtues arise from natural human tendencies; the source of virtue is internal.[^3]
- Since virtue is knowledge, vice is ignorance ([[Socratic Intellectualism]]).[^4]
- Virtue is craft-knowledge of the "art of living", practised on [[Indifferent (Stoicism)|indifferents]] as raw material.[^5]
- Each species is better or worse by its own peculiar virtue (a horse by its speed, not its bridle); human excellence is self-respect, trustworthiness, and justice, not wealth or ancestry. Beauty in a human is excellence of the will: "you aren't flesh or hair: you are will."[^6]
- A form of [[Virtue Ethics]].
- **Psychological virtue (Robertson).** For the Stoic, *arete* is closer to psychological than moral virtue: the strengths of character by which one achieves well-being, i.e. the positive traits behind mental health (cf. [[Character (Positive Psychology)]]). The cardinal virtues are wisdom, self-mastery (*sophrosyne*), integrity ("justice") and courage. All are unified in practical wisdom: "All virtues are one".[^rob1]
- "Virtue is a weapon of which man cannot be deprived" (Diogenes Laertius).[^rob2]
- For a rational animal, self-preservation means above all preserving the mind and the ability to think clearly, since identity is bound up with self-consciousness. So "What should I do?" depends on "Who am I?".[^rob3]
