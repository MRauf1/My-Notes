# Definition
> [!info] Coulomb's Law[^1]
> The electric [[Force]] between two point charges $q_1, q_2$ separated by the vector $\mathbf{r}$ (pointing from one charge to the other) is
> $$
> \begin{align}
> \mathbf{F} = \frac{q_1 q_2}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{\mathbf{r}}{r^3}
> \end{align}
> $$
> where $\epsilon_0$ is the permittivity of free space and $r = |\mathbf{r}|$. The force acts along the line joining the charges: attractive for unlike signs, repulsive for like signs.

# Properties
- Formally identical in structure to [[Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation]] — both are inverse-square, central forces along the line joining two point sources — except that [[Electric Charge]] can be of either sign, so the electric force can be attractive or repulsive, whereas [[Mass]] is always positive and gravity is always attractive.
- Valid, in this simple form, only for charges at rest relative to one another; a moving charge additionally experiences the velocity-dependent part of the [[Lorentz Force Law]].
- The [[Electric Field]] is defined so that this law factors into a source part and a response part: $\mathbf{F} = q_2\mathbf{E}$, where $\mathbf{E}$ is the field produced by $q_1$ alone.

[^1]: [The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. I, Ch. 12: Characteristics of Force](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_12.html)
