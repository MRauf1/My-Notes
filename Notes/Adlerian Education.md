# Definition
> [!info] Adlerian Education[^1]
> Education whose objective is [[Self-Reliance (Adlerian Psychology)|self-reliance]] and whose method is **assistance, not intervention**. It rests on the educator's [[Respect (Adlerian Psychology)|respect]] for the learner, and its core content is "human knowledge": how to live with others, which can only be learned in relationships. Educators are counsellors, and counselling is re-education.

# Properties
- **Why education exists.** If one were the only person on Earth, no knowledge or education would be needed. Education serves the two behavioral objectives (be self-reliant, live in harmony with society) and the two psychological ones ("I have the ability", "people are my comrades").[^2]
- **Respect first.** The teacher respects the learner. Without respect there is no good relationship, and without a good relationship one's words do not reach anyone.[^3]
- **The democratic classroom.** The classroom is a democratic nation whose sovereigns are the students, not a dictatorship run by the teacher. Rules are set by student consensus. Adler does not ignore rules, provided they are made democratically.[^4]
- **Renounce the standpoint of the judge.** Maintaining law and order is not the educator's job. Protecting the child in front of you is.[^5]
- **Teach, don't reprimand.** Children often do not know that something is wrong, so teach them with words of reason, without emotion or raised voices. Reprimand is a form of violence: low-cost, easy communication that shows one's immaturity, and children sense this. Adler: "Anger is an emotion that pulls people apart." Communication aims at consensus, not just conveying.[^6]
- **Ask about goals, not causes.** Asking why a student misbehaved produces only excuses. Focus on goals and think together about "What should I do from now on?" ([[Teleology (Adlerian Psychology)]]).[^7]
- **Against reward and punishment.** Praise and punishment make people obey to be praised or avoid rebuke. They turn the group into a competition for reward and for the leader's favour, which teaches the lifestyle "all other people are my enemies". Competition breeds gamesmanship and unfairness, and it always produces [[Vertical and Horizontal Relationships|vertical relationships]] (winners and losers).[^8]
- **The cooperation principle.** Run the classroom on cooperation rather than competition, so students adopt the lifestyle "people are my comrades". Horizontal relationships are cooperative: no one competes, and differences in ability do not matter. Treat the community, not only the individual.[^9]
- Handling misbehavior: [[Five Stages of Problem Behavior]].
- Related: [[Separation of Tasks]] (studying is the child's task), [[Encouragement (Adlerian Psychology)]], [[Empathy (Adlerian Psychology)]].

# Evidence
- Meta-analyses find that cooperative goal structures produce higher achievement and more positive peer relationships than competitive or individualistic ones (Roseth, Johnson & Johnson, 2008).
- Physical punishment predicts worse outcomes (aggression, mental health problems) and does not improve compliance over time (Gershoff & Grogan-Kaylor, 2016).
- The praise prohibition goes too far. Informational, process-focused feedback helps ([[Process Praise]]).
