# Definition
> [!info] Paradox of Choice (Schwartz)[^1]
> Barry Schwartz's thesis that less choice can increase productivity, freedom, and the ability to be present and enjoy the moment. Not having to make choices frees potential otherwise wasted on choosing. Ahrens adds academic writing to the list of domains where less choice is better.

# Properties
- **Constraints enable creativity.** Literature exists not despite the restriction to 26 letters but because of it. The canvas's limits let Lucio Fontana cut into it. Binary, more restricted than the alphabet, opened unprecedented possibilities.[^2]
- The biggest threat to creativity and progress is a *lack* of structure and restrictions. Without structure we can't compare or experiment with ideas; without restrictions we are never forced to decide what is worth pursuing. "Indifference is the worst environment for insight."[^2]
- A standardized workflow reduces decisions (see [[Smart Notes Workflow]]). Compare [[Choice]].
- **Avoiding commitment (Manson).** Too many options can lead people to keep options open and avoid committing. Breadth of experience has diminishing returns and denies the rewards of depth; committing to one person, place or activity is itself liberating.[^manson]
- **Evidence check.** The famous jam study found that 30% of shoppers bought from 6 jams versus 3% from 24 (Iyengar and Lepper, 2000). But a meta-analysis of 50 experiments found a mean choice-overload effect near zero with large heterogeneity (Scheibehenne, Greifeneder and Todd, 2010). Overload appears mainly with complex choice sets, unclear preferences or difficult trade-offs (Chernev, Böckenholt and Goodman, 2015). *Maximizers*, who seek the best option, report lower satisfaction and happiness than *satisficers* (Schwartz et al., 2002).
