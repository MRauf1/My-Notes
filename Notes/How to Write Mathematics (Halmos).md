# Review[^1]

If you have something to say, organize it, write then rewrite it until satisfied, think hard about it and work hard on the details.

Have only one thing to say at a time. Not less than one and not more than one.

Write for someone. Specifically, think about your audience and who you are writing for. This doesn't have to be general either. You could have a specific person in mind, which might give a more personal sense to what you're writing.

Make sure your notation is well thought out and consistent.

Write in spirals. As you finish chapter 1, go to chapter 2, but then you may go back to 1, then go to 3, but come back to 2, and so on. Future chapters may give you ideas on how to improve earlier chapters. Adopt this same plan for organization too.

Make the first draft messy. And then rewrite better versions.

Consistency matters the most. Write every day even if you write a little bit at a time.

Bad English can distract the reader, so write properly.

Don't repeat proofs. If some steps are common in multiple theorems, then take out those steps and establish them as a separate lemma.

Use "we", not "I". Use factual and imperative tone.
