# Definition
> [!info] Prospective and Retrospective Meditation[^1]
> Two complementary daily contemplations that the Stoics took over from the Pythagoreans:
> - **Prospective** (morning): calm the mind, then mentally rehearse the day ahead, its likely challenges, and the precepts needed to meet them ([[Premeditatio Malorum]]).
> - **Retrospective** (evening): before sleep, review the whole preceding day three times from beginning to end, as self-analysis and memory training.

# Properties
- According to Iamblichus, morning meditation goes back to Pythagoras.[^1]
- **Evening questions.** The Pythagorean *Golden Verses* ask "How slip? What deeds? What duty left undone?":[^2]
	1. Review the errors made during the day, disputing one's own actions.
	2. Recount what was done well and praise oneself for it, reinforcing constructive behaviour.
	3. Plan alternative behaviour, i.e. what should have been done instead.
	
	This resembles CBT problem solving: reviewing the pros and cons of one's behaviour and generating alternatives for the next day ([[Problem-Solving Therapy]]).
- **Criterion.** "The highest duty is honour to self": the review asks whether one's actions served one's peace of mind, well-being ([[Eudaimonia]]) and wisdom, not whether they obeyed a moral code.[^3]
- **Feedback loop.** Lessons from the evening review are taken into the next morning's rehearsal.[^4]
- Hadot: "First thing in the morning, we should go over in advance what we have to do… In the evening, we should examine ourselves again, so as to be aware of the faults we have committed or the progress we have made."[^5]
- Of the two, morning premeditation interested the ancients most and is most relevant to CBT.[^6]
- **Robertson's daily regimen.**[^7]
	- *Morning*: quiet meditation, the [[View from Above]], rehearsal of generic precepts ("follow nature"; "make good use of impressions"), rehearsal of the day's challenges, periodic contemplation of catastrophe and death, and [[Contemplation of the Sage]].
	- *Throughout the day*: [[Prosoche|self-awareness]] organised by the three disciplines: logic (separate thoughts from facts), physics (accept the moment, "will your fate"; [[Amor Fati]]) and ethics (take responsibility, question each thought's truth and helpfulness). Also oneness: [[Philosophical Empathy]] and cosmic consciousness.
	- *Evening*: retrospection three times. Condemn the badly done actions, not oneself, moderately; praise genuine strengths; plan improvements. Then relax with contentment, "as if you could die pleased with your life so far".
- One of the [[Stoic Exercises]]; the evening review is also a form of self-monitoring ([[Hypomnemata]]).
