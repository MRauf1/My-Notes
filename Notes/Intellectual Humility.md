# Definition
> [!info] Intellectual Humility[^1]
> Recognising that one's beliefs might be wrong, and staying open to evidence that they are (Leary et al., 2017). Learning is approximation: "we don't go from wrong to right, but from wrong to slightly less wrong", treating values as hypotheses, actions as experiments and the resulting emotions and thoughts as data.

# Properties
- **Certainty breeds dogmatism.** Certainty is the enemy of growth; people cling to painful certainties (e.g. "nobody would find me attractive") rather than test them. Feeling certain of one's own righteousness is what licenses harming others.[^2]
- **Self-questioning prompts.** (1) What if I'm wrong? (2) What would it mean if I were wrong? (3) Would being wrong create a better or worse problem, for me and others? Being able to entertain a thought without accepting it is central to changing one's values.[^3]
- **Evidence.**
	- Intellectual humility is associated with more accurate evaluation of evidence quality, more knowledge-seeking, and less dogmatism and political animosity (Leary et al., 2017; Porter and Schumann, 2018).
	- A growth mindset about intelligence predicts it, and prompting a growth mindset can raise it (Porter and Schumann, 2018).
- Grounded in the unreliability of memory and belief ([[False Memory]], [[Superstitious Behavior]], [[Confirmation Bias]]).
- Related: [[Not Knowing (Burkeman)]], [[Intolerance of Uncertainty]], [[Growth Mindset]].
