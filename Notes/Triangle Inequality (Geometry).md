# Definition
> [!info] Theorem 1 (Triangle Inequality Theorem)
> For a [[Triangle]] with side lengths $a$, $b$, and $c$, all three of the following inequalities must hold:
> $$
> a + b > c, \quad a + c > b, \quad b + c > a
> $$

The sum of the lengths of any two sides of a triangle must be **strictly** greater than the length of the remaining third side. If this condition is not met, the three side lengths cannot form a closed triangle.

This is the strict, geometric analog of the algebraic [[Triangle Inequality]] $\lVert v + w \rVert \leq \lVert v \rVert + \lVert w \rVert$: taking $v, w$ as two sides of the triangle (as vectors), the third side is $v + w$, and the triangle is degenerate (has zero area) exactly when equality holds in the vector triangle inequality, i.e. when $v$ and $w$ point in the same direction.
