# Definition

Mindfulness is non-judgmental observation. It accepts everything as equal. All thoughts, feelings, emotions, experiences. Nothing is suppressed or repressed. It is independent of the mental/emotional state.

When you first become aware of something, there is a fleeting instant of pure awareness before the mind conceptualize that thing. That pure awareness without the medium of thought is mindfulness.

Fully developed mindfulness is a state of total non-attachment and utter absence of clinging to anything in the world. The result is an invulnerable mind that is unaffected by the ups and downs of life.

Mindfulness requires energy and effort, but it requires gentle effort.

# [[Self]]
There is no "me" in pure awareness/mindfulness.

# [[Vipassana Meditation]]
Within [[Vipassana Meditation]], it is both an end and a means to an end.

# Growth
Mindfulness, like muscle, grows through exercising it.

If mindfulness is to grow, one must accept what they find through mindfulness.

# Properties (Gunaratana)
- English translation of the Pali *Sati*, also rendered "bare attention". It also carries the sense of remembering, meaning a wordless knowing of what we are doing and how to do it, not memory of the past. Another Pali term translated as mindfulness is *Appamada*: non-negligence, absence of madness.[^g1]
- It is pre-symbolic and present-time. Remembering your second-grade teacher is memory; becoming aware that you are remembering is mindfulness; saying "Oh, I am remembering" is thinking.[^g2]
- Characterizations: mirror-thought, non-judgmental observation, impartial watchfulness, nonconceptual awareness, present-time awareness, non-egoistic alertness, goal-less awareness, awareness of change, and participatory observation. Its field of study is the universe *within*.[^g3]
- **Three functions** (a functional definition, since it is "too simple and open" to define in words): (a) it reminds us what we are supposed to be doing; (b) it sees things as they really are; (c) it sees the true nature of all phenomena, i.e., the [[Three Marks of Existence]].[^g4]
- It re-establishes itself: noticing that you have not been noticing is already noticing. It has a light, clear, energetic flavor; conscious thought is heavy and ponderous by comparison.[^g5]
- Objective observation of a mental state is psychologically impossible without first accepting it, especially for unpleasant states.[^g6]
- It is the specific antidote and preventive measure for the [[Five Hindrances]], and it makes the growth of wisdom and compassion possible.[^g7]
- It is inclusive and cannot be forced, whereas concentration ([[Samadhi]]) is exclusive and can be forced. Mindfulness manages; concentration supplies the power.[^g8]
- Tolle's [[Watching the Thinker]] is a closely parallel practice.
- It is the center of [[Vipassana Meditation]] and, alone, leads to [[Nibbana]].[^g9]
