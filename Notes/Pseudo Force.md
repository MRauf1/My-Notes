# Definition
> [!info] Pseudo Force[^1]
> An apparent [[Force]] that appears in [[Newton's Second Law]] only because the coordinate system used is not an inertial one (e.g. it is rotating or accelerating), rather than because of any real interaction with another body.

# Properties
- Always proportional to the mass of the object it appears to act on — the same distinguishing feature shared by [[Gravitation|gravity]]. A classic example is centrifugal force: an observer in a rotating frame (e.g. a spinning box) finds objects apparently pushed outward toward the walls, an effect entirely attributable to using a non-inertial, rotating [[Frame of Reference]] rather than to any real outward-pushing force.
- Because it shares gravity's defining feature — proportionality to mass — it raises the possibility that gravity itself might be understood as a pseudo force arising from using the "wrong" (accelerating) coordinate system, which is the physical content of the [[Equivalence Principle]] that Einstein used as the starting point for general relativity.
- Unlike an ordinary pseudo force, gravity cannot be eliminated everywhere by a single global change of reference frame: choosing an accelerating frame can cancel gravity's effect at one location, but not simultaneously at another location on the far side of a gravitating body. This obstruction to a single global cancellation is what led Einstein to propose that gravity instead reflects the curvature of [[Spacetime]] itself — illustrated qualitatively by the fact that initially parallel straight-line paths on a curved surface (e.g. great circles on a sphere) can converge as if attracted, with no real attractive force involved.
- This general-relativistic account of gravity as spacetime curvature, rather than a force (real or pseudo), remains the accepted and extensively experimentally confirmed description of gravity today.
