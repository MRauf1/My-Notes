# Definition
> [!info] Groupthink[^1]
> (Irving Janis, early 1970s) The state in which everyone in a group starts thinking alike: no one disagrees or takes a critical stance, which can lead to catastrophic decisions.

# Properties
- It often comes out of a [[Fixed Mindset|fixed mindset]]:[^2]
	- unlimited faith in a talented leader, a genius, seen as a god who never errs;
	- a group carried away by its own brilliance and superiority;
	- a fixed-mindset leader punishing dissent, so people stop speaking up even if they still think critically;
	- workers seeking the leader's validation falling into line.
- In Robert Wood's management simulation, growth-mindset groups stated honest opinions and disagreements openly, while fixed-mindset groups, anxious about who was smart and about disapproval, fell into something like groupthink; the growth mindset leads to full, open discussion and better decisions.[^3]
- Counter-practices: postponing a unanimous decision "to give ourselves time to develop disagreement" (Alfred P. Sloan), or rewarding dissent (David Packard gave an employee a medal for defying him).[^4]
- See [[Organizational Mindset]].
