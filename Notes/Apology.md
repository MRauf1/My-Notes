# Definition
> [!info] Apology[^1]
> An acknowledgment of wrongdoing aimed at repairing a relationship. Pausch's three parts of a proper apology:
> 1. "What I did was wrong."
> 2. "I feel badly that I hurt you."
> 3. "How do I make this better?"
>
> "Apologies are not pass/fail… any performance lower than an A really doesn't cut it."

# Properties
- **Evidence.**
	- Lewicki, Polin and Lount (2016) tested six apology components. The most effective was *acknowledgment of responsibility*, followed by an *offer of repair*. These are Pausch's parts 1 and 3. Expressing regret and explaining what went wrong helped moderately. A request for forgiveness helped least.
	- Apologies reliably increase forgiveness (meta-analysis, Fehr, Gelfand and Nag, 2010). See [[Forgiveness]].
- **Insincere apologies.** "Halfhearted or insincere apologies are often worse than not apologizing at all."[^2] Apologies perceived as insincere, or that deflect blame ("I'm sorry if you were offended"), are less effective and can backfire.
- **Offer of repair.** Asking how to make it better invites the other person to set a modest, achievable repair. In Pausch's experience, people rarely exploit it.[^3]
- **Don't demand reciprocity.** Whether the other person apologizes back is not up to you ([[Dichotomy of Control]]). People reach the emotional readiness to apologize at different times, so patience is needed.[^4]
