# Definition
> [!info] Strong Nuclear Force
> One of the [[Four Fundamental Interactions]]: binds quarks together into protons and neutrons, and binds protons and neutrons together into atomic nuclei, overcoming the electromagnetic repulsion between positively charged protons.

# Properties
- The strongest of the four fundamental interactions, roughly 100 times stronger than the electromagnetic interaction, but acts only over an extremely short range (about $10^{-15}$ m, the scale of a nucleon).
- Mediated by gluons at the fundamental (quark) level, described by quantum chromodynamics; historically modeled at the nuclear level as an effective, longer-range residual force carried by [[Pion|pions]] and other mesons between protons and neutrons.
- This residual, nucleon-level force dies off exponentially beyond roughly $10^{-13}$ cm (the nuclear scale), rather than falling as an inverse square; no simple, precise law for it was known before the development of quantum chromodynamics, and the pion-exchange picture is itself only a crude approximation.
