# Definition
> [!info] Taste (Creativity)[^1]
> The visceral intuition of what works and what doesn't, against which the spontaneous possibilities of the creative act are filtered. It is easier to learn to recognize what's good than to master the skills to produce it. But no amount of grinding leads to brilliance without first understanding what brilliance could mean.

# Properties
- **Cross-domain taste.** Know your own field, but also study what is great in other domains. It is a more flexible source of inspiration and a reminder of what makes creation exciting (e.g., a writer studying film criticism such as Ebert's *The Great Movies* and Tarantino's *Cinema Speculation*).[^2]
- **Groups.** The collective taste of people with similar ambitions can exceed any individual's. Performing for a crowd also pushes your mind into a higher gear than solo introspection, giving an instant upgrade to your quality standard.[^3]
- Part of Newport's principle *obsess over quality* ([[Slow Productivity]]). Related: [[Deliberate Practice Requirements]] (feedback).
