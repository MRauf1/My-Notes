# Definition
> [!info] Momentum[^1]
> The product of an object's [[Mass]] and its [[Velocity Vector|velocity]]:
> $$
> \begin{align}
> \mathbf{p} = m\mathbf{v}
> \end{align}
> $$

# Properties
- Distinct from velocity: pushing equally hard on a more massive object produces a smaller change in velocity than pushing on a less massive one, since it is the rate of change of momentum, not of velocity alone, that is set by the applied [[Force]] (see [[Newton's Second Law]]).
- Under [[Newton's First Law]], momentum is constant unless acted on by a net external [[Force]].
- Newton's original formulation of the [[Newton's Second Law|second law of motion]] is stated in terms of the time-rate-of-change of momentum, $F = \frac{d}{dt}(m\mathbf{v})$, which reduces to $F=m\mathbf{a}$ only under the (Newtonian) approximation that mass is constant.

[^1]: [The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. I, Ch. 9: Newton's Laws of Dynamics](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_09.html)
