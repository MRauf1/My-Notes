# Definition
> [!info] Overhead Tax (Newport)[^1]
> Each commitment you take on brings administrative overhead (emails, meetings, check-ins, "swing by" conversations) on top of the work itself. As workload grows, this overhead eventually passes a tipping point, beyond which logistics devour so much of the schedule that you can't finish old tasks fast enough to keep up with new ones.

# Properties
- This is why overload *reduces* output: it fragments attention and leaves little room for the actual work, which is the justification for doing fewer things ([[Slow Productivity]]).[^2]
