# Definition
> [!info] Three Marks of Existence[^1]
> The three prime characteristics that [[Buddhism]] teaches are the deepest truths of existence:
> 1. [[Anicca]] (impermanence) — all conditioned things are inherently transitory.
> 2. [[Dukkha]] (unsatisfactoriness) — every worldly thing is, in the end, unsatisfying.
> 3. [[Anatta]] (selflessness) — there are no unchanging, permanent entities (soul or self), only processes.

# Properties
- Only [[Mindfulness|mindfulness]] can perceive them, and seeing them is the third function of mindfulness.[^1]
- Seeing them shows that there is no sense in grabbing onto any passing phenomenon; peace and happiness cannot be found that way.[^2]
- As mindfulness develops, resentment of change, dislike of unpleasant experience, greed for pleasant experience, and the notion of selfhood are replaced by insight into the three marks. This fosters a calmer, more mature attitude toward life.[^3]
- Experiencing all three vividly at once leads to [[Nibbana]].[^4]
