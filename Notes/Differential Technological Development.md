# Definition
> [!info] Principle of Differential Technological Development[^1]
> Retard the development of dangerous and harmful technologies, especially ones that raise the level of existential risk; and accelerate the development of beneficial technologies, especially those that reduce the existential risks posed by nature or by other technologies.

# Properties
- **Technological completion conjecture**: if scientific and technological development efforts do not effectively cease, then all important basic capabilities obtainable through some possible technology will be obtained. This motivates the claim that blocking research is futile, and the reasoning by which even an altruist develops a harmful technology, since the harm seems inevitable anyway.[^2]
- Nevertheless, what matters is not only *whether* a technology is developed but *when*, *by whom*, and *in what context*.[^1]
- Some technologies, including [[Superintelligence|superintelligence]], have ambivalent effects on existential risk; superintelligence should preferably precede e.g. nanotechnology, since it would reduce nanotechnology's risks but not vice versa. The riskiness of an intelligence explosion likely declines over a multidecadal timeframe.[^3]
- Timing is governed by the distinction between [[State Risk and Step Risk|state and step risks]].
- **Technology coupling**: two technologies may have a predictable timing relationship, such that developing one robustly leads to the other, as precursor or irresistible application, complicating attempts to accelerate one selectively.[^4]
- **Second-guessing arguments** (e.g. start a risky research effort early so preparation starts early; welcome small catastrophes to spur precaution) rely on treating others as irrational and playing to their biases rather than presenting the case honestly.[^5]
- Computing developments also shape the intelligence explosion indirectly through diffuse social effects.[^6]
- Collaboration reduces haste, allows more safety investment, avoids conflict, shares ideas on the control problem, and distributes benefits more equitably, cf. the [[Common Good Principle]].[^7]
