# Definition
> [!info] Statistical Inference[^1]
> Drawing conclusions, from numerical data, about quantities that are not observed. The unobserved quantities about which inferences are made are the [[Estimand|estimands]].

# Types
- [[Bayesian Inference]]: uncertainty about the estimands is expressed by a probability distribution conditional on the data.
- Frequentist inference: procedures are evaluated over hypothetical repeated datasets ([[Frequentist vs Bayesian Inference]]).
- [[Causal Inference]]: estimands are outcomes under treatments not received.
- Predictive inference: estimands are future observables ([[Prior Predictive Distribution]], [[Predictive Distribution]]).

# Properties
- The usual starting point is the (often tacit) assumption that the observations are [[Exchangeability|exchangeable]].[^2]
- In [[Statistical Learning]], the narrower sense of [[Inference]] is understanding the input-output relationship, as opposed to [[Prediction]].
