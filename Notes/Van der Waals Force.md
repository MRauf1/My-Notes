# Definition
> [!info] Van der Waals Force[^1]
> An attractive [[Force]] between electrically neutral atoms or molecules, arising from the mutual influence of their electric charge distributions, distinct from and generally much weaker than direct electrostatic forces between net charges.

# Properties
- A molecule is polar if the centers of its positive and negative charge do not coincide (giving it a dipole moment: charge times the separation of these centers), and nonpolar if the centers coincide, as in the symmetric oxygen molecule.
- Between polar molecules, the dominant contribution is a dipole-dipole force, generally stronger than the force between nonpolar molecules.
- Even between nonpolar molecules, whose net electrostatic force vanishes, a residual attractive force remains at large separation, falling off much faster than an inverse-square force; a full explanation of its precise form (a quantum-mechanical effect, the London dispersion force) requires quantum mechanics.
- At short range, overlapping atoms or molecules repel strongly, which is what prevents ordinary matter from interpenetrating.
- Not fundamental in the way [[Gravitation]] is: it arises from the complex, aggregate electromagnetic interaction of every electron and nucleus in one molecule with every electron and nucleus in the other, even though the net result can be fit by simple empirical formulas.
- Near the equilibrium separation where the net force is zero, the force-versus-distance curve is well approximated by a straight line, so a small displacement from equilibrium is met by a restoring force proportional to the displacement — the molecular origin of [[Hooke's Law]].
