# Definition
> [!info] Five Hindrances[^1]
> The mental states that block the development of both [[Samadhi|concentration]] and [[Mindfulness|mindfulness]]:
> 1. **Desire** for sensual pleasure (greed).
> 2. **Aversion** (hatred, ill will).
> 3. **Mental lethargy** (sloth and torpor).
> 4. **Restlessness** (agitation).
> 5. **Doubt** (mental vacillation).

# Properties
- **Handling** (the same for all five): do not repress, avoid, or condemn them. Observe the hindrance with bare attention as it arises, gauge its extent or degree, note how long it lasts and when it fades, then return to the [[Breath as Meditation Object|breath]]. Firsthand observation shows "at a gut level" that each is an unskillful way to run your life.[^2]
- **Lethargy** is closely related to aversion: a way of avoiding unpleasant issues by dulling the mind, "an enforced stupidity pretending to be sleep". It must be caught right at its start, or it outpaces mindfulness and ends in sinking mind or sleep. Inquisitive attention to drowsiness itself dissolves it; if it doesn't, suspect a physical cause.[^3]
- **Restlessness** often covers repressed unconscious material. Sitting through it shows that agitation is superficial and ephemeral.[^4]
- **Doubt** is like a man at an unmarked crossroads in the desert who just stands there vacillating. It is a smoke screen against becoming aware of what is happening.[^5]
- They are avoided not because they are evil but because they are compulsive: they take over attention, circle in tight loops, and seal us off from living reality.[^6]
- They are the primary manifestations of the [[Ego|ego]] process: the exercise that keeps the sense of separation alive (see [[Anatta]]). Clear mindfulness inhibits their growth; continuous mindfulness extinguishes them.[^7]
- They arise from the [[Three Unwholesome Roots]].
