# Definition
> [!info] Communitarianism[^1]
> A view in [[Political Philosophy]] that individuals are formed by their communities. On this view, rights must be balanced by responsibilities to the community that secures them. Pausch: "It makes no sense to talk about rights without also talking about responsibilities. Rights have to come from somewhere, and they come from the community."

# Properties
- **Origins.** Academic communitarianism was a critique of Rawlsian liberalism (Sandel, MacIntyre, Taylor, Walzer). It rejected the idea of an "unencumbered self" choosing values independently of any community. The *responsive communitarian* movement (Etzioni, *The Spirit of Community*, 1993) took it into public life with the slogan "rights and responsibilities."
- **Criticism.** Liberals worry that emphasizing community values can justify pressure on minorities and dissenters. Communitarians reply that rights themselves rest on shared commitments.
- **Civic participation.** Pausch's father made teenage baseball players umpire younger leagues. They argued less with umpires and modelled volunteering for the younger kids: "When we're connected to others, we become better people."[^2] Volunteering is associated with higher well-being (see [[Prosocial Behavior]]).
