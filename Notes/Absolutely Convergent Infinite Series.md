# Definition

> [!info] Definition 1 (Absolutely Convergent [[Infinite Series]])[^1]
> [[Infinite Series]] $\sum a_n$ is absolutely convergent if $\sum |a_n|$ is convergent.

# Properties
- [[Absolutely Convergent Infinite Series Convergence Theorem]]
- For a series of nonnegative terms, absolute convergence and convergence coincide.[^2]
- If $\sum a_n$ converges but $\sum |a_n|$ diverges, $\sum a_n$ is said to converge nonabsolutely (or conditionally).[^2]
- The [[Infinite Series Comparison Test|comparison]], [[Infinite Series Root Test|root]], and [[Infinite Series Ratio Test|ratio]] tests are really tests for absolute convergence, and give no information about nonabsolutely convergent series; [[Summation by Parts|summation by parts]] (e.g. the [[Dirichlet Test]]) can sometimes handle those instead.[^2]
- A [[Power Series]] converges absolutely at every point in the interior of its circle (or interval) of convergence.[^2]
- Every [[Rearrangement (Series)|rearrangement]] of an absolutely convergent series converges to the same sum; contrast [[Riemann Series Theorem]] for nonabsolutely convergent series.
