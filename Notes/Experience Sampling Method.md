# Definition
> [!info] Experience Sampling Method (ESM)[^1]
> A research method (Csikszentmihalyi and Larson) in which subjects are prompted at random intervals, originally by pagers and now by smartphone apps, to record what they are doing and how they feel at that moment. Because responses are recorded during the activity, they are more accurate than retrospective reports.

# Properties
- ESM studies underlie the theory of [[Flow State|flow]]: "The best moments usually occur when a person's body or mind is stretched to its limits in a voluntary effort to accomplish something difficult and worthwhile."[^2]
- People were happier at work and less happy relaxing than they expected. Jobs, like flow activities, have built-in goals, feedback, and challenges, while free time is unstructured and harder to enjoy.[^3]
- The more flow experiences in a week, the higher life satisfaction. It is even more important to learn to seek out opportunities for flow; [[Deep Work]] is well suited to producing them.[^4]
- Unlike Gallagher's attention-based view ([[Attention]]), flow theory is mostly agnostic about the *content* of attention: going deep is rewarding in itself.[^5]
