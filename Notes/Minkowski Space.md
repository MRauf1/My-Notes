# Definition
> [!info] Minkowski Space[^1]
> The geometry of [[Spacetime]] in [[Special Relativity]]: a four-dimensional space of [[Event (Physics)|events]] $(x,y,z,t)$ equipped, in place of the ordinary Euclidean distance, with the indefinite (pseudo-Euclidean) [[Spacetime Interval]] $c^2t^2-x^2-y^2-z^2$ as its invariant "distance" — a flat geometry of signature $(+,-,-,-)$, whose distance-preserving transformations are exactly the [[Lorentz Transformation|Lorentz transformations]] together with ordinary spatial rotations and translations.

# Properties
- Distinguished from ordinary Euclidean geometry solely by this signature: the time term enters the invariant interval with the opposite sign from the three space terms, so the squared interval need not be positive, unlike a squared Euclidean distance.
- The Lorentz transformation is, in this geometry, exactly analogous to a rotation of coordinate axes in Euclidean geometry: it mixes coordinates (here, space and time) while leaving the invariant interval unchanged, just as a rotation mixes $x$ and $y$ while leaving $x^2+y^2$ unchanged.
- Flat (uncurved) everywhere, unlike the curved [[Spacetime]] of general relativity, which reduces to Minkowski space locally wherever [[Gravitation]] can be neglected.
- Named for Hermann Minkowski, who first recognized that Einstein's special relativity could be reformulated as the geometry of a single four-dimensional entity unifying space and time, remarking that "space of itself, and time of itself, will sink into mere shadows, and only a kind of union between them shall survive."

[^1]: [The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. I, Ch. 17: Space-Time](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_17.html)
