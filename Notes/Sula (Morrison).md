# Review[^1]

I wonder what the "monstrosity" of Shadrack's hands supposed to represent other than the trauma he received? And why did seeing his face help him out and stabilize him so much? I suppose one reason is because he still didn't feel like a real entity, a real human.

He instituted the National Suicide Day as a way to devote oneself to death on this one day fully, so that for the rest of the year, one is free from the fears and unexpectedness of death.

Also curious why that smile by Helene.

After the trip, seems like Nel has matured and is now starting to act more independently. She also doesn't seem to love her mother all too much either.

Very unfortunate how Eva ended up caressing her son Plum before setting him on fire to put him out of his misery as a result of World War I.
