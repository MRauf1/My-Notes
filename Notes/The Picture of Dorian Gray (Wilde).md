# Review[^1]

What an interesting and philosophical preface.

Henry believes that intelligence and beauty cannot coexist and are distinctly separate.

Basil says that it's better not to be different from others. That the fools are the ones who have it the best in this world.

To Basil, hiding common things in life like the name of the subject in the picture or where Basil is going to are the little things that add a bit of romance and mystery to his life that make his life more interesting to live.

Basil believes that when painting a subject, it is the painter themselves who is expressed and revealed within the painting, not the subject.

Dorian Gray has unlocked a brand new perspective of sorts within Basil, a brand new art style that brings infinite inspiration to the artist. His paintings with him are his best works, and he puts all of himself into his art, exposing even his inner soul within. Thus, he also becomes afraid of letting the paintings see the light of day in fear that his inner self will be exposed through these paintings.

Henry tells Dorian that people nowadays do not focus enough on themselves. All of their thoughts, ideas, sins, and virtues come as a result of other people and other people's influences on them. He advocates for self-improvement, self-expression, self-actualization, and such. That is the goal of each individual and the peak of humanity that will bring back the Hellenic ideal and rid their world of many of the maladies. These precise words struck a cord with Dorian Gray and seem to have been the first step towards his future change.

I am curious what makes Dorian so afraid of Henry's words. I suppose we would need to learn more about his character to understand.

Previously, Henry told Basil something different - that the Genius outlasts beauty. That they are different from each other. Now, he's telling Dorian that Beauty is a higher form of Genius. Is this hypocrisy/manipulation or am I incorrectly interpreting his words? I do wonder what his motive in all this is. He did say that he loves seeing the emotions of other people. Maybe his manipulating Dorian to produce the most vivid emotions as a result? Although Henry might say that it is not manipulating; it's just revealing the real Dorian.

He tells Dorian not to waste his youth for though he has been blessed for now, his youth is not immortal and will soon leave him. If Dorian does not appreciate this youth while it lasts, he will only be able to recall it as a wasted opportunity/sad memory once he is older and no longer youthful and beautiful the way he is right now. By saying this, Henry advocates for new Hedonism as the thing that their current century needs.

After this talk, the new finished painting by Basil of Dorian struck Dorian very differently than before. Compared to before, he was now aware of his beauty and fleeting youth. He was able to see the vividness and admire it like never before. At the same time, a pang of pain passed through him as he let out tears. This made him realize that while this painting of him would remain eternally youthful, he, on the other hand, would grow old and decrepit one day. He wished for anything to be able to stay youthful forever like the painting.

Dorian tells Basil that Dorian is no more valuable to him than any of his other painting subjects like a bronze statue or the likes. Once he loses his youthfulness, he too will lose his value to Basil and will be abandoned by him. He is a painting subject to Basil, not a friend. He believes that youth is the one thing that one must obtain and that without it, Dorian would rather kill himself. Basil denies this and emphasizes that Dorian is his friend and will remain his friend.

Despite his jealousy of the painting, he does not allow Basil to tear it apart saying that this is a part of him now.

Henry found both Dorian and Basil's new change very fascinating. It seemed to him like Basil was trying to dominate Dorian and make himself to be Dorian, make Dorian's spirit his own.

The whole of chapter 5 seems like a foreshadowing of a tragedy. The naive liveliness and giddyness of Sibyl. The lack of concert from her mother. And the sense of danger of her brother that Dorian will end up ruining his sister, and him not being able to do much at the moment as he has to leave for Australia. Especially after the reveal of their father never married their mother and just left her to live and raise the children in poverty, and now the characteristics of their father being present in Dorian too.

This love made Sibyl realize just how shallow and artificial all of the acting, the stage, the audience and all of it was compared to her love for Dorian. That tanked her acting ability as she could no longer pretend to be with rose-colored glasses like she was before. But to Dorian, this was a massive blow as her acting representing something beautiful to him and without it, he felt like his love was killed. To him, she spoiled the romance of life. To him, without the art, Sibyl is nothing. He hoped to make her famous, to have her fame to be tied to his name. In other words, it seems like the reason he loved her was because she seemed like an opportunity to make his youthfulness eternal even beyond his death through her fame. But now, that was no longer possible and that dream, and that love died completely.

After this event, even when looking at the older painting of himself, it seemed to look a little different. A little crueler. That picture to him became an emblem of conscience. To not ruin the painting, to not alter it, he decided that he would not sin. That he would go back to Sibyl and make amends and stop listening to Henry.

But before he could do that, he finds out that Sibyl has already committed suicide. In a state of panic, Dorian is trying to rationalize his lack of fault in this, and how he did make the attempt to make amends even if it was late. He is trying to dissolve himself of any guilt and sin.

Rather a true tragedy that breaks him apart, Dorian is seeing this as some fascinating play of a Greek tragedy, which has no effect of him. Part of him even worries whether he is heartless, but that cannot be the case he believes.

Once again, what follows is a series of manipulation by Henry that Dorian is vulnerable towards. Thus, Dorian sees this tragedy as nothing more than a marvellous experience that he hopes to experience more of in his life. In the end, he decides to live for the experience of life, for the pleasures, sins, and more. And the portrait will bear the burden of his shame. Instead, he would make sure that his youth remains eternal, and as for the painting, what does it matter to him then?

And now, Dorian was able to extract Basil's secret, which fascinated him and explained Basil's character. This is the romance of life, isn't it?

I think I'm just now starting to understand. Does Dorian believe that his wish earlier of switching himself with the painting came true? And that's why he thinks that his youth is now eternal, while the painting will keep changing?
