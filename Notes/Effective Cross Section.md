# Definition
> [!info] Effective Cross Section[^1]
> The effective cross section $\sigma$ is the apparent area presented by an object such as an atomic nucleus, used as a convenient way to characterize its size once scales become too small to probe with visible light. For a nearly spherical nucleus of radius $r$,
> $$\sigma = \pi r^2$$

# Properties
- Becomes the natural way to measure size once objects are smaller than the wavelength of visible light (about $5\times10^{-7}$ m), since such objects cannot be directly "seen."
- Used particularly at nuclear scales, where the radius $r$ can be recovered from a measured $\sigma$.

[^1]: [The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. I, Ch. 5: Time and Distance](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_05.html)
