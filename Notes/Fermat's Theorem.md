# Definition

> [!abstract] Theorem 1 ([[Pierre Fermat]] Theorem)[^1]
> If $f$ has [[Local Minimum]] or [[Local Maximum]] at $\mathbf{c}$ and $\nabla f(\mathbf{c})$ exists (all [[Partial Derivative]] exist), then $\nabla f(\mathbf{c}) = 0$.
> Alternatively, if the above antecedent is true, then $\mathbf{c}$ is a [[Critical Number]].

> [!abstract] Theorem 2 (One-Variable Version)[^2]
> Let $f$ be defined on $[a, b]$. If $f$ has a [[Local Maximum]] at a point $x \in (a, b)$, and if $f'(x)$ exists, then $f'(x) = 0$. The same holds for a [[Local Minimum]].

This is the basis of many applications of differentiation, including [[Mean Value Theorem]] and [[Darboux's Theorem]].
