# Definition

...

Boredom is greatest threat to success/(danger in life), not failure. As habits become routine, we get bored of them, which reduces our motivation to keep doing them.
Can make use of variable rewards to stave off boredom, but ultimately, it'll only delay the boredom, not get rid of it completely. At the end of the day, one must learn to fall in love with the boredom.

Burkeman argues that boredom is not mere lack of interest but an intense reaction to confronting your limited control over a valued task.[^1] Evidence shows how aversive empty time can be: across 11 studies, people disliked spending 6–15 minutes alone with their thoughts, and 67% of men and 25% of women chose to give themselves an electric shock rather than sit idle (Wilson et al., 2014). See [[Distraction]].

**Boredom and fatigue (Carnegie).** Carnegie cites Barmack (1937): students given uninteresting tasks felt tired, sleepy and irritable. He calls boredom "one of the chief causes of fatigue".[^2] Modern accounts agree that boredom raises *perceived* effort and fatigue (see [[Mental Fatigue]]). People can make dull tasks more interesting by setting themselves challenges or adding variety. Doing so increases persistence (Sansone et al., 1992), as with Carnegie's clerk who held a daily contest with herself.[^3]
