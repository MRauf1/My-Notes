# Definition
> [!info] Capability Control Methods[^1]
> Methods for solving the [[AI Control Problem]] by limiting what a superintelligence can do.

# Types
- **Boxing** - placing the system in an environment where it cannot cause harm.[^2]
	- *Physical containment* - confining the system so it interacts with the world only via restricted output channels. Easy, broadly applicable, combinable with other methods, and unlikely to backfire; but it reduces functionality and may give a false sense of security, so it should be "icing on the cake".
	- *Informational containment* - restricting what information leaves the box. Information also leaks via any observable causal consequence of the AI's workings, and an AI anticipating observation could strategically influence its observers.
- **Incentive methods** - an environment in which the agent has instrumental reasons to promote the principal's interests, e.g. social integration and balance of power, or rewards. Rewards are hard to calibrate without knowledge of the AI's motivations, so it is better to combine with motivation selection, e.g. an AI placing final value on *cryptographic reward tokens* it cannot obtain by wireheading, with defection always carrying a higher risk of forfeiting the next token; the AI may, however, not trust the operator to deliver.[^3]
- **Stunting** - limiting the system's intellectual faculties or information access. A radically stunted AI is safe but does not prevent some other system from triggering the intelligence explosion; and information deprivation is unreliable, since any information can be relevant given background knowledge, and a superintelligence could infer much from scraps of evidence, from introspection on its own code and hardware, or even a priori via a simplicity prior. Limited hardware or restricted faculties may still serve as auxiliary measures in development.[^4]
- **Tripwires** - diagnostic tests (possibly unknown to the system) that shut it down on signs of dangerous activity, allowing inspection, modification, or abandonment. Unlike incentive methods, they do not rely on the system knowing the consequences of forbidden actions. Doubtful against a full superintelligence, but important as an adjunct in development.[^5]

# Properties
- Complementary to [[Motivation Selection Methods]]; e.g. boxing pairs naturally with domesticity.[^6]
