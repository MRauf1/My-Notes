# Definition

> [!info] Definition 1 (Implicit Differentiation)[^1]
> When $y$ is defined implicitly as a [[Differentiable Function]] of $x$ by an equation $F(x, y) = 0$, $y'$ is found by differentiating both sides of the equation with respect to $x$, treating $y$ as $y(x)$ and applying the [[Derivative Chain Rule]] to every term involving $y$ (e.g. $\frac{d}{dx} y^2 = 2y\,y'$), then solving the resulting equation for $y'$.

# Properties
- The result usually involves both $x$ and $y$; evaluating it at a point on the curve gives the slope of the [[Tangent Line]] there.
- It assumes $y$ is differentiable; whether such a local function exists is the content of the Implicit Function Theorem.
- It is used to derive the derivatives of inverse functions: [[Derivative of Inverse Function]], the inverse trigonometric derivatives (e.g. [[Derivative of Inverse Sine Function]]) and the [[Derivative of Logarithmic Function]].
- The roles of $x$ and $y$ can be swapped to find $dx/dy$.
- Basis of [[Logarithmic Differentiation]].
