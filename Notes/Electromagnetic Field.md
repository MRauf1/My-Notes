# Definition
> [!info] Electromagnetic Field[^1]
> The single unified field responsible for electric, magnetic, and optical phenomena: electricity, magnetism, and light were discovered to be different aspects of one underlying entity.

# Properties
- A historic amalgamation: separate theories of electricity, magnetism, and light were unified into one theory of the electromagnetic field.
- Sourced by [[Electric Charge|electric charges]]; a charge oscillated back and forth radiates electromagnetic waves.
- Carries oscillatory waves that differ from one another only in a single parameter, the frequency of oscillation; radio waves, visible light, and gamma rays are all electromagnetic waves distinguished solely by frequency.
- At high frequencies the field's behavior becomes markedly particle-like, appearing as discrete [[Photon]]s rather than a continuous wave, an instance of [[Wave-Particle Duality]].
- Its quantum-mechanically correct description is [[Quantum Electrodynamics]].
- One of the [[Four Fundamental Interactions]] of nature.

[^1]: [The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. I, Ch. 2: Basic Physics](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_02.html)
