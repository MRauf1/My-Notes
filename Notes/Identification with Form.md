# Definition
> [!info] Identification with Form (Tolle)[^1]
> Tolle's definition of the [[Ego|ego]]: seeking yourself, and so losing yourself, in some form, mainly thought forms but also physical and emotional forms. The ego is "a conglomeration of recurring thought forms and conditioned mental-emotional patterns that are invested with a sense of I". It arises when the formless sense of "I Am" gets mixed up with form. Tolle also defines evil, which has only relative reality, as *complete* identification with form, i.e., total unawareness of one's oneness with others and the Source.

# Properties
- **Things and consumerism.** Advertising sells "identity enhancers": things that seem to add to how you see yourself or are seen. Ego satisfaction is short-lived, which keeps consumption going. The unchecked striving for more and endless growth is a "dysfunction and a disease".[^2]
- Ownership is a fiction through which the ego hopes the solidity and permanence of objects will lend it solidity and permanence. The ego equates having with Being. Near death, ownership is revealed as meaningless.[^3]
- **Content vs. structure.** Renouncing possessions doesn't free you of the ego; it finds something else to identify with. The ego's content may change, but the mental structure stays.[^4]
- **Wanting over having.** Beneath the ego lies a sense of "not enough", really "I am not enough yet". The ego wants to want more than it wants to have, an addictive need. Unease, restlessness, boredom, and anxiety come from unfulfilled wanting, which is structural, so no content can bring lasting fulfillment.[^5]
- **Comparison.** The ego lives through comparison: how others see you becomes how you see yourself.[^6]
- **The body.** Identifying with looks, strength, or abilities brings suffering as they fade. Not identifying with the body doesn't mean neglecting it. Shift attention from its appearance to the feeling of aliveness inside it ([[Inner Body]]).[^7]
- **Collapse of forms.** When forms you identified with are taken away, or death approaches, the ego may collapse. People then sometimes find sudden peace and presence: consciousness realizes itself rather than what it had identified with.[^8]
- Accepting the impermanence of all forms brings peace and awakens the formless dimension within ("This, too, will pass"). You can then enjoy things while they last without fear of loss (cf. [[Anicca]]).[^9]
- Being aware that you are identified with something means the identification is no longer total: "I am the awareness that is aware that there is attachment." Attachment drops away by itself when you no longer seek yourself in things.[^10]
- Yielding is inner acceptance and openness; resistance is a contraction that hardens the ego's shell.[^11]
