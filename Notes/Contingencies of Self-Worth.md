# Definition
> [!info] Contingencies of Self-Worth (Crocker and Wolfe, 2001)[^1]
> The domains on which a person stakes their self-esteem (e.g. others' approval, appearance, academic competence, virtue, God's love). Self-esteem rises and falls with outcomes in the contingent domains. *External* contingencies depend on others' judgments or outcomes outside one's control; *internal* ones depend on one's own conduct.

# Properties
- **Values and metrics (Manson).** A value is what you care about; a metric is the standard by which you judge progress toward it. The same facts can mean success or failure depending on the metric (e.g. measuring one's career by "more popular than a rival band" versus by gold records). To change how you see a problem, change the value or the metric.[^2]
- **Good versus bad values.** Manson calls good values reality-based, socially constructive, and immediate and controllable (honesty, curiosity, humility, creativity); bad values are superstitious, socially destructive, and depend on external events.[^3] An externally contingent metric such as "make everyone like me" leaves self-worth at the mercy of others; "improve my social life" depends on one's own behaviour.[^4]
- **Process versus endpoint.** Good metrics are ongoing processes that are never finished ("honest expression"); an achieved goal has nothing left to give.[^4] See [[Goal-Setting Theory]].
- **Evidence.**
	- Basing self-worth on external contingencies (appearance, approval, academics) predicts more stress, anger, interpersonal conflict, drug and alcohol use, and disordered eating; internal contingencies (virtue, religious faith) predict fewer such problems (Crocker, 2002; Crocker and Park, 2004).
	- Students whose self-worth was contingent on academics felt worse after rejection letters and bad grades, though their grades were no better (Crocker et al., 2003).
- Choosing a value means rejecting alternatives, and rejecting nothing leaves no identity.[^5] Compare [[Dichotomy of Control]] and [[Intrinsic and Extrinsic Aspirations]].
