# Definition
> [!info] Indifferent (Stoicism)[^1]
> Anything that is neither virtue nor vice, and therefore neither good nor bad: the fundamental Stoic ethical tenet is that only moral virtue is good and only moral vice is bad. Wealth, health, life, death, pleasure, pain, and everything else not subject to [[Prohairesis|will]] are indifferents, and calling them good or bad is careless talk.

# Types
- **Preferred indifferents** - indifferents in accord with nature that aid progress toward virtue, e.g. health (our natural condition), moderate wealth, and some social status; these coincide considerably with society's ordinary values.[^2]
- **Dispreferred indifferents** - their opposites, e.g. illness and poverty.[^2]

# Properties
- We cannot be completely indifferent to externals: they are the *raw material* of virtue, as leather is a cobbler's or wool a tailor's; virtue is craft-knowledge of the "art of living", the skilful use of experience.[^3]
- Indifferents have *value* (a scale of worth) but no goodness; one should do all one can to acquire preferred indifferents for oneself and others, while treating success or failure as indifferent: "it is playing the game well that counts, not winning or losing", since the action is up to us but its consequences are not.[^4]
- One may *select* an indifferent but should not *want* it, since wanting brings pleasure on obtaining it, an inappropriate response that deepens attachment; the focus should be on the virtuous act, not its concomitant pleasure.[^5]
- "Material things are indifferent, but the use one makes of them isn't"; likewise life is indifferent, but what we make of it is not. Indifference is no licence for carelessness.[^6]
- Even a loved one's death is an indifferent: one loves one's child, but neither the child nor the love is the ultimate goal.[^7]
- Consequence of the [[Dichotomy of Control]]; the objects of [[Appropriate Action (Stoicism)|appropriate action]]. Compare the Bhagavad Gita's karma yoga (acting without attachment to the fruits of action).
