# Definition
> [!info] Superposition Principle (of Fields)[^1]
> The total field produced by several sources is the vector sum of the fields each source would produce individually:
> $$
> \begin{align}
> \mathbf{E} = \mathbf{E}_1 + \mathbf{E}_2 + \mathbf{E}_3 + \cdots
> \end{align}
> $$
> and likewise for the [[Gravitational Field]].

# Properties
- Holds exactly for the [[Electric Field]] — an absolutely reliable law so far as is known today, even when the individual source fields are complicated by the sources' own motion; apparent violations have always traced back to an overlooked moving charge.
- Holds for the [[Gravitational Field]] only as an approximation, valid in the weak-field (Newtonian) limit; it fails once the field is strong, since general relativity's field equations are not linear in the way [[Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation]] is.

[^1]: [The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. I, Ch. 12: Characteristics of Force](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_12.html)
