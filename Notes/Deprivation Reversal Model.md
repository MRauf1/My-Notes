# Definition
> [!info] Deprivation Reversal Model (Parrott)[^1]
> Nicotine dependence itself causes stress. Between cigarettes, nicotine depletion produces rising tension, irritability, and poor concentration, and smoking merely reverses this, returning mood to roughly where non-smokers are all the time (Parrott, 1999). The apparent relaxing effect is relief of withdrawal, not a genuine benefit.

# Properties
- **Carr's version.** "The only pleasure from smoking or vaping is the partial relief of the discomfort caused by smoking or vaping." You can never get back to a non-smoker's calm and confidence by smoking. Smokers believe cigarettes relieve stress and help them concentrate, without realizing that smoking keeps them stressed and unable to concentrate in the first place.[^2]
- **Evidence on stress and mood.** Smokers are more anxious and depressed than non-smokers.[^3] Meta-analyses find that quitting is associated with *reduced* depression, anxiety, and stress and improved positive mood compared with continuing to smoke (Taylor et al., 2014; Cochrane review, 2021).
- **Depression.** Carr says smoking causes depression. Mendelian randomization evidence suggests a two-way causal link: smoking raises the risk of depression, and depression raises smoking (Wootton et al., 2020).[^4]
- **Where Carr overstates.** Nicotine is not completely inert. A meta-analysis found small genuine improvements in attention and motor performance even in non-deprived users (Heishman et al., 2010). Nicotine also raises metabolic rate and suppresses appetite, contrary to Carr's claim.[^5] Quitters gain about 4–5 kg on average after 12 months (Aubin et al., 2012).
