# Definition
> [!info] Quantum Indeterminism[^1]
> Quantum mechanics does not permit an exact prediction of the outcome of an individual measurement, even in principle; given complete knowledge of a system's state, the theory determines only the probabilities of its possible outcomes.

# Properties
- Contrasts with classical mechanics, under which identical initial conditions were assumed to always yield identical outcomes.
- Understood as a fundamental feature of nature rather than a result of unobserved "hidden" internal variables; modern no-hidden-variable results (e.g. [[Bell's Theorem]]) rule out local hidden-variable explanations of this randomness.
- Still compatible with science being grounded in reproducible experiment: physical law is inferred from the statistical regularities experiments do exhibit, not from a requirement that identical setups produce identical individual results.
- Closely tied to the [[Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle]], which limits how precisely a system's state can even be specified to begin with.

[^1]: [The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. I, Ch. 2: Basic Physics](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_02.html)
