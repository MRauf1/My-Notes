# Definition

> [!info] Definition 1 (Distributive Property for Numbers)
> $\forall x, y, z \in \mathbb{R}$
> $$
> \begin{align}
> x(y + z) = xy + xz
> \end{align}
> $$

Distributive property states that [[Multiplication|multiplication]] can be distributed over [[Addition|addition]].[^1]

> [!info] Definition 2 (Distributive Property for Logic)
> For [[Proposition|statements]] $P, Q, R$
> $$
> \begin{align}
> P \land (Q \lor R) = (P \land Q) \lor (P \land R) \\
> P \lor (Q \land R) = (P \lor Q) \land (P \lor Q)
> \end{align}
> $$

> [!info] Definition 3 (Distributive Property for Sets)
> For [[Set|sets]] $A, B, C$
> $$
> \begin{align}
> A \cup (B \cap C) = (A \cup B) \cap (A \cup C) \\
> A \cap (B \cup C) = (A \cap B) \cup (A \cap C)
> \end{align}
> $$

More generally, for any two [[Binary Operation|binary operations]] $\circ, \bullet$ on a set, $\bullet$ distributes over $\circ$ if $x \bullet (y \circ z) = (x \bullet y) \circ (x \bullet z)$ for all $x, y, z$; multiplication over addition, AND over OR, and union over intersection are all instances of this.
