# Definition
> [!info] Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation[^1]
> Every object in the universe attracts every other object with a [[Force]] proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them:
> $$
> \begin{align}
> F = G\frac{mm'}{r^2}
> \end{align}
> $$
> where $G$ is the gravitational constant, $m, m'$ are the [[Mass|masses]] of the two bodies, and $r$ is the distance between them.

The quantitative, Newtonian formulation of [[Gravitation]].

# Properties
- Together with [[Newton's Second Law]] ($F = ma$), this law is sufficient to derive all of [[Kepler's First Law|Kepler's]] [[Kepler's Second Law|three]] [[Kepler's Third Law|laws]] of planetary motion.
- Always attractive, unlike the analogous inverse-square law for [[Electric Charge|electric charges]] (Coulomb's law), which can be attractive or repulsive; the two laws nonetheless share the same $1/r^2$ dependence on distance.
- Exactly proportional to [[Mass|mass]] — see the [[Equivalence Principle]].
- Can be recast, exactly as [[Coulomb's Law]] is for electric charge, as a source producing a [[Gravitational Field]] and a second mass responding to that field.
- By the [[Shell Theorem]], a spherically symmetric body (e.g. a planet) attracts external masses exactly as though all its mass were concentrated at its center, which is what justifies treating $m$ and $m'$ as point masses at a separation $r$.
- Superseded by general relativity: Newtonian gravity implies instantaneous action at a distance, which is inconsistent with the finite speed-of-light limit on signal propagation from special relativity. General relativity corrects this and predicts that anything carrying energy — including massless light — is deflected by gravity; this was confirmed by observing the apparent displacement of stars near the sun's disk during a solar eclipse.
- $G$ appears experimentally constant in time: modern tests from lunar laser ranging bound any secular drift to $|\dot G/G| \lesssim 10^{-13}\ \text{yr}^{-1}$[^2], ruling out significant time variation of $G$, such as that once proposed by hypotheses relating $G$ to the age of the universe (e.g. Dirac's large numbers hypothesis).

[^2]: [Lunar Laser Ranging Science](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0411095)
