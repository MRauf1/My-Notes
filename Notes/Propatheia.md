# Definition
> [!info] Propatheia[^1]
> (First movements; from Epictetus's lost Book 5, via Aulus Gellius) The rapid, involuntary reactions (a start, a pallor, a contraction of the mind) that even a sage experiences when struck by a sudden terrifying [[Impression (Stoicism)|impression]], which forestall reason before any judgment is made. They are not [[Passion (Stoicism)|passions]], since no [[Assent (Stoicism)|assent]] has yet been given.

# Properties
- The sage then withholds assent and does not "add a further opinion" (*prosepidoxazein*) to the impression, retaining his conviction that it is not truly frightening.[^1]
- The fool, by contrast, takes the impression to be as terrible as it seems and assents to it, turning the first movement into a full passion.[^1]
- Shows that Stoic [[Apatheia]] is not freedom from all feeling.
