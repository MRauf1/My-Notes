# Definition
> [!info] View from Above[^1]
> (Hadot's term) A contemplative exercise common to many ancient schools: visualising the world as if seen from very high above, and ultimately placing every event within the totality of space and time. It is a kind of dissociation through mental imagery, surveying life as a whole from a bird's-eye view.

# Properties
- The Pythagoreans most explicitly treated cosmology as meditation; the Stoics took this practice over.[^1]
- **Aim.** The aim is not contempt for human affairs but rational love of the whole cosmos and a feeling of belonging, both to the human community and to the cosmic whole. Seneca: *toti se inserens mundo*, "plunging oneself into the totality of the world". This ties it to [[Cosmopolitanism]] and to the Stoic view of personal identity as part of a single organic whole ([[Oikeiosis]]).[^2]
- **True context.** "Continual awareness of all time and space, of the size and life span of things around us. A grape seed in infinite space. A half twist of a corkscrew against eternity" (Marcus, *Meditations* 10.17). The totality is reality, so the cosmic perspective just puts things in their real context.[^3]
- No one can visualise everything at once, but the abstract concept of "all" is always available, and that is enough.[^4]
- The body is minute compared with the universe, but the mind is not: its ability to conceive vastness is what reveals the smallness and transience of physical existence.[^5]
- **Benefits.** It encourages tranquillity (*apatheia*, *ataraxia*; [[Apatheia]]). Trivial things look trivial and one's own attitude to life looks more important. It highlights the causal network of [[Stoic Determinism|determinism]] and supports [[Objective Representation (Stoicism)|objective description]] of the bare facts.[^6]
- Seeing things in constant metamorphosis leads naturally to the meditation on death ([[Memento Mori]]).[^7]
- **CBT parallel.** *Selective abstraction* ("tunnel vision") takes things out of context. Beck's *enlarging perspective* technique moves the anxious patient from a "worm's-eye view" to a "bird's-eye view". The Stoics push the same principle to its extreme context.[^4] See [[Cognitive Distortion]].
- Robertson gives a guided script for it (rising above one's body, region, planet, then cosmic time, then returning to the here and now).[^8]
- **Evidence.** Experimental work on *self-distancing* (taking a distanced, third-person vantage point on a distressing experience) shows less emotional reactivity and rumination (Kross & Ayduk, 2011). *Temporal distancing*, imagining how one will feel about a stressor in the distant future, also reduces distress (Bruehlman-Senecal & Ayduk, 2015).
- Related: [[Cosmic Insignificance Therapy]], [[Stoic Exercises]].
