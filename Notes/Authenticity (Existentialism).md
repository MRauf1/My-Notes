# Definition
> [!info] Authenticity (Existentialism)[^1]
> (Heidegger) The mode of existence of *mindfulness of being*, in which one marvels not at how things are but *that* they are, remains aware of the fragility of being and of one's responsibility for it, and so grasps the power to change oneself. It contrasts with the everyday, *inauthentic* mode of *forgetfulness of being*.

# Types
- **Forgetfulness of being (inauthentic)** - living in the world of things and everyday diversions, unaware of one's authorship of one's life, "fleeing", "falling", tranquilised, avoiding choices by being "carried along by the nobody".[^2]
- **Mindfulness of being (authentic, ontological mode)** - fully self-aware as both transcendental (constituting) and empirical (constituted) ego, embracing one's possibilities and limits, and facing absolute freedom and nothingness, with anxiety.[^2]

# Properties
- Awareness of one's personal death spurs the shift from the first mode to the second; death "is the condition that makes it possible for us to live life in an authentic fashion" ([[Boundary Situation]]).[^3]
- To be taken in by devices that let us flee our freedom is to live inauthentically (Heidegger) or in *bad faith* (Sartre); see [[Responsibility (Existential Psychology)]].[^4]
- The "call of conscience" calls one back to authentic being, experienced as [[Existential Guilt]].[^5]
- Anxiety, via [[Uncanniness]], guides one back from the fallen mode.[^6]
