# Definition
> [!info] Noogenic Neurosis[^1]
> ([[Viktor Frankl|Frankl]]) A neurosis originating not in the psychological dimension (as *psychogenic* neuroses do) but in the *noological* (from Greek *noos*, "mind") dimension of human existence: from existential problems, above all frustration of the [[Will to Meaning|will to meaning]], rather than from conflicts between drives and instincts. Its appropriate treatment is [[Logotherapy]] rather than psychotherapy in general.

# Properties
- "Existential" can refer to (1) existence itself, the specifically human mode of being; (2) the meaning of existence; (3) the will to meaning.[^2]
- **Existential frustration** is itself neither pathological nor pathogenic: concern or even despair over the worthwhileness of life is an existential distress, "the proof of one's humanness", not a mental disease; some conflict is normal and suffering may be a human achievement. It is, however, *potentially* pathogenic.[^3]
- Its mass form is the [[Existential Vacuum]]: an inner emptiness manifesting mainly as boredom (Schopenhauer: mankind vacillates between distress and boredom), since no instinct tells man what he must do and no tradition what he ought to do, leading to conformism or totalitarianism.[^4]
- The "mass neurotic triad" of **depression, aggression, and addiction** is linked to the vacuum: e.g. 90% of studied alcoholics reported abysmal meaninglessness, and 100% of studied drug addicts felt "things seemed meaningless"; aggression in youngsters subsided when they dedicated themselves to a collective purpose. Not every depression or suicide stems from it, though.[^5]
- Symptoms invade the vacuum and flourish there; filling it prevents relapses; "every therapy must in some way … also be logotherapy" (Magda Arnold).[^6]
- **Unemployment neurosis**: a twofold erroneous identification of being jobless with being useless, and useless with meaningless; depression lifted once the unemployed found unpaid but meaningful activity. "People have enough to live by but nothing to live for."[^7]
- The vacuum is a private form of *nihilism* ("being has no meaning"); psychotherapy that treats man as a pawn of conditions reinforces the neurotic's belief that he is a victim.[^8]
