# Definition
> [!info] Orthogonality Thesis[^1]
> Intelligence and final goals are orthogonal: more or less any level of intelligence could in principle be combined with more or less any final goal. Here "intelligence" means skill at prediction, planning, and means-ends reasoning in general, not rationality or reason.

# Properties
- Intelligent search for instrumentally optimal plans can serve any goal; intelligence and motivation can be pictured as two axes spanning the space of logically possible agents.[^1]
- An AI can be far less human-like in motivation than a biological alien, since the alien's evolved origin implies evolved motivations; there is nothing paradoxical about an AI whose sole final goal is counting sand grains, computing digits of $\pi$, or maximising paperclips, and such simple goals are easier to build than human-like values.[^2]
- Artificial agents may thus have utterly nonanthropomorphic goals, yet their behaviour remains partially predictable via design, inheritance, and the [[Instrumental Convergence Thesis]].[^3]
- Undercuts the assumption that a sufficiently intelligent AI would automatically share human values ([[Value Alignment Problem]]).
