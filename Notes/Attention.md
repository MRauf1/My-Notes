# Definition

...

According to psychologist [[Timothy Wilson]], we're capable of attending to about $0.0004\%$ of the information entering our 
brains at any given moment.[^1]

Since life is just the experience of the present moment, life is then the experience of attention. Whatever we pay attention to ends up being/making up our life. According to research, our brains construct our worldview based on what we pay attention to.[^2]

After negative events, what you choose to focus on gives significant leverage over your attitude (Fredrickson). Rapt attention on important work also crowds out the small negatives of life: "when you lose focus, your mind tends to fix on what could be wrong with your life instead of what's right" (Gallagher). See [[Amygdala in Young vs. Old]].[^3]

Attention, not information, is the scarce resource: a wealth of information creates a [[Poverty of Attention]].

**Caveat on the 0.0004% figure.** It comes from rough information-theoretic estimates of about $1.1 \times 10^7$ bits/s of sensory input against about 40 bits/s of conscious processing: $40 / (1.1 \times 10^7) \approx 3.6 \times 10^{-6} \approx 0.0004\%$. Only the order of magnitude is meaningful.

**Bottom-up vs. top-down.** *Bottom-up* (involuntary) attention lets the environment seize attention: oncoming buses, a crying baby, a sunset. *Top-down* (voluntary) attention can be directed at will, and Frankl kept a portion of it for his inner life even in Auschwitz.[^4]

**Attention is life.** Attention is not just a resource that facilitates life: your experience consists of everything you pay attention to. A whole job can be a distraction from something more meaningful.[^5] "Attention is the beginning of devotion." (Mary Oliver)[^6] See [[Attention Economy]].
