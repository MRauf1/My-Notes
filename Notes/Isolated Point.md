# Definition

> [!info] Definition 1 (Isolated Point)[^1]
> Let $(X, d)$ be a [[Metric Space]] and $E \subseteq X$. A point $p \in E$ is an isolated point of $E$ if $p$ is not a [[Limit Point]] of $E$, i.e., there exists a [[Epsilon Neighborhood|neighborhood]] of $p$ that contains no point of $E$ other than $p$ itself.

Unlike a [[Limit Point]], which may or may not belong to $E$, an isolated point must by definition belong to $E$ — it is a point of $E$ that is completely separated from the rest of $E$: you can draw a neighborhood around it small enough to exclude every other member of $E$, so that inside that neighborhood the isolated point is totally alone.

# Properties
- [[Perfect Subset|Perfect set]]: a [[Closed Set|closed]] set with no isolated points, i.e. every point of the set is a [[Limit Point]] of the set.
