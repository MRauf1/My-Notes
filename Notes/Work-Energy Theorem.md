# Definition
> [!info] Work-Energy Theorem[^1]
> $$dT = \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{s}, \qquad \Delta T = \int_1^2 \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{s}$$
> The change in an object's [[Kinetic Energy]] between two points along its path equals the [[Work]] done on it by the net force over that path.

# Properties
- Follows from [[Newton's Second Law]]: differentiating $T = \tfrac12 mv^2$ with respect to time and using $\mathbf{F}=m\,d\mathbf{v}/dt$ gives $dT/dt = \mathbf{F}\cdot\mathbf{v}$, the instantaneous [[Power]] delivered by the force.
- Holds along any path, however curved, since only the component of force along the direction of instantaneous motion contributes to $\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{s}$; components perpendicular to the motion do no work.
- Establishes work as the mechanism by which energy is transferred into or out of an object's kinetic energy, independent of what kind of force produced it.

[^1]: [The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. I, Ch. 13: Work and Potential Energy (A)](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_13.html)
