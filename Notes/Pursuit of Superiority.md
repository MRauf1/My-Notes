# Definition
> [!info] Pursuit of Superiority[^1]
> ([[Alfred Adler|Adler]]) The universal desire to escape the helpless state in which humans are born and to improve. Its counterpart is the [[Feeling of Inferiority]]. Neither is a disease. Both are normal stimulants to healthy striving and growth.

# Properties
- **Not competition.** Picture everyone walking on the same level ground, some ahead and some behind, at different speeds and distances. The pursuit of superiority means taking a step forward on one's own feet, not trying to be greater than others.[^2]
- Value lies in progressing past who one is now, not in beating others. There is no need to compare oneself with others.[^3]
- **Competition poisons relationships.** When one thinks in terms of victory and defeat, inferiority feelings are inevitable, others become enemies, and even a constant winner never has a moment's peace. The [[Inferiority Complex]] and [[Superiority Complex]] grow out of this.[^4]
- **Comrades.** Without competition, the need to triumph and the fear of losing disappear. One can celebrate others' happiness and contribute to it, others become comrades, and the world looks safe. See [[Community Feeling]].[^5]
- "Human beings are all equal, but not the same." See [[Vertical and Horizontal Relationships]].[^6]
- Compare [[Social Comparison Theory]] (comparison with others) and [[Deficiency and Growth Motivation]].
- What everyone ultimately seeks is freedom from helplessness, and [[Self-Reliance (Adlerian Psychology)|self-reliance]].[^kk1]
