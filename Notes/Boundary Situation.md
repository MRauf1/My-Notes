# Definition
> [!info] Boundary Situation[^1]
> (Jaspers) An event or urgent experience that propels one into confrontation with one's existential situation in the world. Confrontation with one's own death is the nonpareil boundary situation, able to cause a massive shift in the way one lives: "although the physicality of death destroys man, the idea of death saves him."

# Properties
- Death awareness moves one from wondering *how* things are to wonderment *that* they are (Heidegger's shift to [[Authenticity (Existentialism)|authentic]] being), away from trivial preoccupations.[^2]
- Without death life loses intensity: "limitation in the possibility of an enjoyment raises the value of the enjoyment"; denying death restricts awareness and experience.[^3]
- "Learning to live well is to learn to die well"; "Contemplate death if you would learn how to live"; Montaigne kept death "continually present … in my mouth".[^4]
- Many terminally ill cancer patients report personal growth: rearranged priorities, trivia dropped, living in the present, appreciation of elemental facts of life, deeper communication; those reporting change almost invariably changed toward growth, and on 19 of 29 fear items more patients reported *less* fear after cancer.[^5]
- "Existence cannot be postponed": life is the "possibility of possibility" (Kierkegaard), death the "impossibility of further possibility" (Heidegger), so one can alter one's life until the last moment. Ivan Ilyich realised he was dying badly because he had lived badly.[^6]
- Keeping death in mind yields gratitude for the givens of existence ("count your blessings").[^7]
- **Disidentification exercise**: list eight answers to "Who am I?", order them by centrality, and meditate on giving up each in turn from the periphery inward, then reverse. It counters the neurotic extension of one's defensive perimeter to career, prestige, or body ("you are not your career … draw a line around [your core]").[^8]
- Other methods: exposing everyday patients to groups of the terminally ill, and dreams of finiteness.[^9]
- Compare Stoic negative visualisation ([[Stoic Exercises]]) and [[Stoic View of Death]].
