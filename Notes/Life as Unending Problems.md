# Definition
> [!info] Life as Unending Problems (Burkeman)[^1]
> "Life is an unending series of complications, so it doesn't make any sense to be surprised by the arrival of the next one." (Sam Harris) Problems follow unavoidably from finitude. At the most abstract level, a "problem" is any situation where we meet the limits of our capacity to control how things unfold.

# Properties
- Every problem carries a second burden: its mere existence undermines our wish to feel secure and in control. So we lean into the future, judging the present, and perhaps ourselves, as fundamentally flawed.[^1]
- A life with no problems would leave nothing worth doing. Meeting your limits and working out how to respond is what makes life meaningful.[^2]
- Giving up the unwinnable fight against having problems at all lets you dive, even with relish, into the ones you actually have. The aspiration becomes a life of ever more interesting and absorbing problems.[^3]
- Jewish humor shows this sardonic acceptance of life's trials, which is not resignation but an understanding that grappling with them is what life is about. "Life completely unhindered by anything manifests as pure activity."[^4]
- Contrast Tolle's "no problems, only situations" ([[Life Situation]]); see [[Imperfectionism]].
