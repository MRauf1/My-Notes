# Definition
> [!info] Liquid Crystal Display[^1]
> A display technology using a thin layer of liquid polymers that can be used to transmit or block light according to whether a charge is applied. Rod-shaped liquid crystal molecules form a twisting helix that bends incoming light; applying a current straightens the rods so they no longer bend light, and since the liquid crystal layer sits between two screens polarized at 90 degrees, light passes through only when bent.

# Properties
- An active matrix display uses a transistor at each pixel to precisely control current and produce sharper images; a color active matrix LCD uses a red-green-blue mask and three transistor switches per pixel.
- Many touch screens use capacitive sensing: since people are electrical conductors, touching a transparent conductor over an insulator like glass distorts the screen's electrostatic field, producing a measurable change in capacitance.
