# Definition
> [!info] Connectomics[^1]
> The mapping of the detailed wiring diagrams (connectomes) of neural circuits, i.e. every axon, dendrite, and synapse, typically by serial-section electron microscopy and computational reconstruction.

# Properties
- Motivation: no detailed circuit diagrams exist for any mammalian brain, and physiology records only a tiny fraction of the neurons in a circuit; each [[Neuron|neuron]] has enormous fan-in and fan-out through many dendrites and a long, branched axon.[^2]
- Scale: the [[Cerebral Cortex|cortex]] contains about $3 \times 10^{8}$ synapses per mm$^3$, whereas fMRI resolves about 1 mm$^3$ per voxel, and synapses lie below the diffraction limit of optical microscopy.[^3]
- Electron microscopy has ample nanometre resolution but images sections tens of nanometres thick, requiring serial reconstruction of contiguous slices into a volume; separating and tracking individual neurons in the volume remains hard. Gigapixel images are stored in hierarchical pyramids for multi-scale web viewing.[^4]
- **Computational microscopes**: fast optical imaging (calcium- and voltage-sensitive dyes) records populations of tens to thousands of neurons; machine learning, information-theoretic analysis, and visualisation can help formulate and test hypotheses from high-dimensional spatiotemporal data, overlay inferences on live views, and compute the expected value of information to choose the next experiment.[^5]
- Computational methods may yield effective representations and languages for understanding sensing, learning, and decision-making in [[Nervous System|nervous systems]].[^6]
- An application of [[Data-Intensive Science]]; relevant to [[Whole Brain Emulation]].
