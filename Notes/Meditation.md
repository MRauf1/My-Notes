# Definition
> [!info] Meditation[^1]
> A living, inherently experiential mental activity of cultivating the mind (Pali *bhavana*, "mental cultivation", from the root *bhu*, "to grow, to become"). There are many styles of meditation. They differ in whether they aim only at concentration and calm, or use concentration as a tool to develop awareness.

# Types
- **Concentrative meditation** aims at one-pointedness, calm, and relaxation. Examples: Judeo-Christian prayer and contemplation, Hindu Yogic meditation, and [[Samatha Meditation]]. Typical results: deep calm, a physiological slowing of the metabolism, and a sense of peace and well-being.[^2]
- **Awareness (insight) meditation** adds awareness as the main element, with concentration as a tool. All Buddhist meditation is of this kind, e.g. [[Vipassana Meditation]], Zen, and Tantra (see [[Mahayana Buddhism]]).[^2]
- **Unstructured meditation** simply sits and is aware of whatever happens to be in the mind, with no fixed object. It is quite difficult, because without a fixed reference point one gets trapped in the thought chain.[^3]
- [[Walking Meditation]] and [[Loving-Kindness Meditation]].

# Properties
- Must be understood by doing it. It deals with levels of consciousness deeper than symbolic thought, so some of it cannot be put into words.[^4]
- Many meditation books state the procedures of one particular tradition as if they were general laws of meditation.[^5]
- Its purpose is purification of the mind and personal transformation. Civilization polishes a person superficially; meditation "softens him within, through and through".[^6]
- Meditation is a psychological activity, very sensitive to the attitude with which each session is approached. See [[Meditation Practice Guidelines]].[^7]
- It is not a duty, an obligation, a competition, or a grim endurance contest, and there is no timetable.[^8]
- Meditation is participatory observation. The mind is a set of events, and the observer takes part in those events every time they look inward. What you see depends on how you look, so the result depends on the meditator's state of mind (cf. the observer effect in physics).[^9]
- It uses a [[Meditation Posture|stable posture]] as a practice aid.
- Related: [[Productive Meditation]], [[Mindfulness]].
