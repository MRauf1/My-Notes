# Definition
> [!info] Provisional Existence[^1]
> ([[Viktor Frankl|Frankl]]) The concentration-camp prisoner's "provisional existence of unknown limit": not knowing how long imprisonment would last, one could not aim at an ultimate goal and ceased living for the future, so the whole structure of inner life decayed.

# Properties
- Time was distorted: a day filled with torture seemed endless, while a week passed quickly (cf. Mann's *The Magic Mountain*).[^2]
- Prisoners who saw no future goal lost themselves in retrospective thoughts and regarded life as unreal and pointless, forgetting that exceptionally difficult situations offer the chance to grow spiritually beyond oneself; one could make an inner triumph of the experience or simply vegetate.[^3]
- "It is a peculiarity of man that he can only live by looking to the future" (*sub specie aeternitatis*); therapy in the camp had to point to a future goal. Nietzsche: "He who has a *why* to live for can bear with almost any *how*."[^4]
- The prisoner who lost faith in the future was doomed, often in a sudden crisis (e.g. a man who died the day after his dreamed date of liberation passed); loss of hope lowers bodily immunity, and the death rate rose sharply between Christmas 1944 and New Year 1945.[^5]
- Frankl rose above suffering by imagining himself lecturing on the psychology of the camp, observing his troubles "as if they were already of the past"; Spinoza: emotion that is suffering ceases to be suffering once we form a clear and precise picture of it.[^6]
- Part of the [[Psychology of Concentration Camp Prisoners]]; cf. [[Death Anxiety]].
