# Definition
> [!info] Death Anxiety[^1]
> The ubiquitous terror of one's own death, a primary source of anxiety that permeates inner experience and consumes much of one's life energy in its denial; dealt with maladaptively it produces the signs, symptoms, and traits called psychopathology. Its core is the fear of personal extinction, *ceasing to be*.

# Types
Three types of death fear (Choron):[^2]
- What comes after death.
- The "event" of dying.
- Ceasing to be (obliteration, annihilation), the most central; the first two are fears *related* to death.

# Properties
- **Death transcendence** is a major motif from dreams and defenses to monuments, theologies, the myth of progress, and the yearning for fame; groups were formed, and history-taking pursued, as a quest for mediated immortality ([[Symbolic Immortality]]).[^3]
- Main defenses: [[Specialness (Existential Psychology)|specialness]] and the [[Ultimate Rescuer]]; used together, as they are interdependent.[^4]
- **Levels** (Feifel): consciously, over 70% deny fear of death; in fantasy, 27% deny it, 62% are ambivalent, and 11% show considerable anxiety; below awareness (reaction times to death words), most show considerable aversion.[^5]
- Correlates: lower with authoritarianism and devout religiosity; higher after losing a parent, in adolescents, in females (consciously), and in the psychologically immature or disturbed aged; lower in the aged who are engaged in life activities. Both very high and very low conscious death anxiety predict death dreams.[^6]
- **Children** are preoccupied with death far more than adults assume (about 50–60% of story completions referred to death); they perceive adults' anxiety and learn to suppress death concerns, while concrete information helps them; latency is a relatively benign period.[^7]
- Childhood denials: belief that children do not die, personification of death (an external bogeyman who might be influenced), and taunting death; "too much, too soon" confrontation is harmful.[^8]
- Psychodynamic theory excluded death by translating it into "separation"; mourning includes the survivor's own encounter with mortality ("if your mother dies, then you will die too").[^9]
- **Death anxiety is inversely proportional to life satisfaction**: a feeling of a life well lived mitigates the terror; "it is the person who will not live who is most terrified of dying"; "the horror of death is the horror of dying with unlived lives in our bodies". The equation is circular, since excess death anxiety causes a constricted life.[^10]
- Neurosis is "the way of avoiding non-being by avoiding being" (Tillich): the defense against death, by limiting life, is itself a partial death, and generates [[Existential Guilt]].[^11]
- Death anxiety is both normal and neurotic; the therapist meets a failure of its homeostatic regulation. Long-term therapy is incomplete without working it through; it may be desensitised by gradual, supported exposure (cf. [[Exposure Therapy]]).[^12]
- Mid-life crises often reflect a "race against time" (manic defenses, compulsive youthfulness).[^13]
- Its positive face is death as a [[Boundary Situation]]. See also [[Existential Anxiety]], [[Stoic View of Death]].
