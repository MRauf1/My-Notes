# Definition
> [!info] Energy-Time Uncertainty Principle[^1]
> Time measurements have a minimum error, bounded below by the reduced Planck constant $\hbar$ and the error $\Delta E$ in knowledge of the energy of the process whose time period is being measured:
> $$\Delta t \geq \frac{\hbar}{2\Delta E}$$

# Properties
- Knowing more precisely when something happened requires knowing less about what happened, since the corresponding uncertainty in energy must grow.
- Arises from the relativity of space and time, analogous to how the [[Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle]] bounds the joint knowledge of position and momentum.
- Reflects the wave nature of matter, as does the position-momentum form of the uncertainty principle.

[^1]: [The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. I, Ch. 5: Time and Distance](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_05.html)
