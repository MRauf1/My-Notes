# Definition
> [!info] Amplitude (Wave)[^1]
> For a [[Sinusoidal Wave]] $u = A\cos(kx - \omega t + \varphi_0)$, the amplitude is $A \ge 0$: the maximum displacement from equilibrium, i.e. half the crest-to-trough height. For a complex wave $\psi = |\psi| e^{i\varphi}$, the amplitude is the modulus $|\psi|$.

# Properties
- The intensity, i.e. the energy carried per unit time and area, is proportional to $A^2$; in quantum mechanics, the detection probability is $|\psi|^2$.
- Amplitude and [[Phase (Waves)|phase]] together specify a complex number $A e^{i\varphi}$, which is the polar form used for Fourier coefficients ([[Fourier Amplitude and Phase]]).
- Detectors that record intensity lose the phase; recovering it is the *phase problem* ([[X-Ray Crystallography]]).
