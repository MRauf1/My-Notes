# Definition
> [!info] Wavenumber[^1]
> The spatial analogue of angular frequency: $k = 2\pi/\lambda$ radians per unit length ([[Wavelength]] $\lambda$). It is the rate at which the [[Phase (Waves)|phase]] of a [[Sinusoidal Wave]] advances with distance: $\partial_x(kx - \omega t) = k$.

# Properties
- Conventions differ: physics uses $k = 2\pi/\lambda$ (angular), while spectroscopy and imaging often use $1/\lambda$ (cycles per unit length), i.e. the [[Spatial Frequency|spatial frequency]]. Cryo-EM writes $|k|$ in Å$^{-1}$ in the cycles convention.
- In several dimensions it becomes the [[Wave Vector|wave vector]] $\mathbf{k}$, with $|\mathbf{k}| = 2\pi/\lambda$.
- For a matter wave, $k = p/\hbar$. In a medium where it varies with position, the accumulated phase is $\int k\,dx$ ([[Phase Shift]]).
