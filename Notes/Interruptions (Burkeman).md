# Definition
> [!info] Interruptions (Burkeman)[^1]
> "Interruption" and "distraction" are a mental overlay that sorts events into those that ought and ought not to happen. Some interruptions are among the things it is wisest to let happen. As C. S. Lewis put it, what one calls the interruptions "are precisely one's real life".

# Properties
- Defending against interruptions has two perverse effects: interruptions feel worse when they happen, and more things get defined as unwelcome interruptions.[^2]
- At scale, you may see your whole life as interruptions of what you're "meant" to be doing. Treating the world as full of things to hold at bay becomes a self-fulfilling prophecy.[^3]
- Don't cling to preferences so tightly that life becomes a struggle against events that "shouldn't" happen, or that you miss welcome developments in disguise.[^4]
- Modern digital distraction is pernicious not because it disrupts attention but because it *holds* it, leaving less room for serendipitous, fruitful distraction.[^5]
- You can give someone your undivided attention while telling them you don't have time right now. That works better than half-listening, which leaves you tense and them unheard.[^6]
