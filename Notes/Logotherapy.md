# Definition

An [[Existentialism|existential]] type of therapy created by [[Viktor Frankl]]. Frankl argues that the primary motivational force in humans is the will to meaning, so this therapy treats the people by trying to give meaning their lives.

# Properties[^1]
- "The Third Viennese School of Psychotherapy" (after Freud's psychoanalysis and Adler's individual psychology); *logos* denotes "meaning".
- Less retrospective and introspective than psychoanalysis: it focuses on the future meanings to be fulfilled, defocuses the vicious circles and feedback mechanisms of neurosis, and breaks up the neurotic's self-centredness; the patient "must hear things which sometimes are very disagreeable to hear".
- It is analytical in making the patient aware of the hidden *logos* of their existence, but attends to existential realities (potential meaning, the [[Will to Meaning]]) rather than only instinctual ones.
- It leaves to the patient what or to whom they are responsible (society, conscience, or a taskmaster); it is neither teaching nor preaching and is not imposed.
- Key concepts and techniques: [[Noogenic Neurosis]], [[Noo-Dynamics]], [[Avenues to Meaning (Frankl)]], [[Tragic Optimism]], [[Paradoxical Intention]], [[Hyper-Intention]], [[Dereflection]], [[Self-Transcendence]].
