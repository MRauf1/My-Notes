# Definition
> [!info] Planck Units[^1]
> Planck units are a system of natural units, introduced by Max Planck in 1899, built from fundamental physical constants rather than human-made artifacts. In these units, four universal constants each take the numerical value $1$ by definition:
> $$c = G = \hbar = k_B = 1$$
> where $c$ is the speed of light in vacuum, $G$ is the gravitational constant, $\hbar$ is the reduced Planck constant, and $k_B$ is the Boltzmann constant.

# Properties
- Built from fundamental constants of nature rather than arbitrary human-made references, so they represent a scale intrinsic to the universe itself, independent of any particular civilization's choice of measurement standards.
- Include the [[Planck Length]] and [[Planck Time]] as base length and time units, and the [[Planck Density]] as the corresponding unit of mass density.
- Mark the scale at which gravity becomes comparable in strength to the other fundamental forces, so a complete theory of quantum gravity is needed to describe physics at or below the Planck scale.
- The narrower convention of setting only $c=1$, common throughout special relativity to simplify the [[Lorentz Transformation]] and the [[Spacetime Interval]], is the same idea restricted to a single constant.
