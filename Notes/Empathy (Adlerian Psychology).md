# Definition
> [!info] Empathy (Adlerian Psychology)[^1]
> The skill of "seeing with the eyes of another, listening with the ears of another and feeling with the heart of another" (Adler). One asks "What if I had the same heart and the same life as this person?" From there one sees that one would face the same task and probably deal with it the same way. It differs from **sympathy**, which means agreeing with someone's opinions and sharing their feelings. Empathy is an attitude of walking side by side with another person.

# Properties
- **First step: concern for other people's concerns.** Do not recommend things from where you stand. Take an interest in what interests the other person (children's games, for example), try it yourself and enjoy it. Then they feel respected as individuals rather than treated as children.[^2]
- It is needed because everyone lives in a subjective world they have given meaning to. One cannot escape one's own subjectivity, but one can try to see another person's world.[^3]
- Empathy is how [[Community Feeling]] (social feeling) is practised. It is the most difficult key concept of [[Adlerian Psychology]].[^4]
- Attend to small everyday words and actions rather than only "good" deeds, and be concerned with the person's concerns.[^5]
- Related: [[Respect (Adlerian Psychology)]], [[Vertical and Horizontal Relationships]].

# Evidence
- Research separates **cognitive empathy** (perspective taking), **affective empathy** (sharing feelings) and **empathic concern** (Davis, 1983). Adler's "empathy" corresponds to perspective taking, and the book's "sympathy" to affective sharing. Affective empathy is biased toward people who are similar and nearby and can cause distress and burnout, while compassion and perspective taking avoid these costs (Bloom, 2016; Singer & Klimecki, 2014). This partly supports Adler's preference. See [[Self-Compassion]].
