# Definition
> [!info] People-Pleasing (Burkeman)[^1]
> Overinvestment in other people's emotions: saying yes to avoid disappointing others and making your own sense of being OK depend on everyone around you being OK. Burkeman calls this a fool's errand and a denial of your finite power over reality.

# Properties
- Other people's negative emotions are ultimately their problem, and you have to allow other people their problems.[^2]
- The people you fear are angry, bored, or disappointed with you almost never are. They have their own troubles.[^2]
- Even when someone is genuinely upset that you didn't act as they wished, that news is "a report on the state of their emotional weather".[^2]
- It doesn't even please people. They sense they are being handled with kid gloves for your own comfort, feel patronized or manipulated, or must spend effort on your issues. "People-pleasing is a form of assholery," says Whitney Cummings, because it is disingenuous and denies others "the dignity of their own experience".[^3]
