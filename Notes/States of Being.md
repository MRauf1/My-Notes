# Definition
> [!info] States of Being (Tolle)[^1]
> Love, joy, and peace are deep states of [[Being (Spirituality)|Being]], three aspects of inner connectedness with it. Unlike emotions, which belong to the dualistic mind and obey the "law of opposites" (no good without bad), these states have no opposite.

# Properties
- **Pleasure vs. joy.** Pleasure always comes from something outside you; joy arises from within, uncaused, as the "joy of Being".[^2]
- **Happiness vs. peace.** Happiness depends on conditions being perceived as positive; inner peace does not. The Buddha taught that even happiness is [[Dukkha|dukkha]], inseparable from its opposite, and only the illusion of time separates them.[^3]
- Every pleasure or emotional high carries the seed of pain. Both polarities belong to the underlying pain of the mind-identified state.[^4]
- Glimpses of them occur whenever there is a gap in the stream of thought, but they stay short-lived until one is free of mind dominance. Since they lie beyond emotions, you must first become fully conscious of your emotions and able to feel them before you can feel what lies beyond.[^5]
- They can be obscured but never destroyed by the mind; they are part of one's natural state.[^6]
- **True love** has no opposite. Experiencing both "love" and attack in a relationship means confusing ego attachment and addictive clinging with love. Love is not selective or exclusive, "just as the light of the sun is not selective" (see [[Relationship as Spiritual Practice]]).[^7]
- **True compassion** goes beyond empathy: it happens only when sadness merges with the joy of Being.[^8]
- Contrast [[Emotion]], [[Happiness]], and Stoic [[Eupatheia]].
- *A New Earth*: the joy of Being cannot come to you through any form, possession, achievement, person, or event. It emanates from consciousness itself: "The joy of Being is the joy of being conscious." "To love is to recognize yourself in another."[^9]
