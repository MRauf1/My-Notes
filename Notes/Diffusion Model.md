# Definition
> [!info] Diffusion Model[^1][^2]
> A [[Probabilistic Generative Model|probabilistic generative model]] with [[Latent Variables|latent variables]] $\mathbf{z}_1, \dots, \mathbf{z}_T$ of the same dimension as the data $\mathbf{x}$, consisting of
> - **Encoder** ([[Diffusion Forward Process|forward process]]): a fixed, parameter-free stochastic map that gradually blends $\mathbf{x}$ with white noise, $q(\mathbf{z}_t|\mathbf{z}_{t-1}) = \mathrm{Norm}_{\mathbf{z}_t}\left[\sqrt{1-\beta_t}\,\mathbf{z}_{t-1}, \beta_t\mathbf{I}\right]$, until $q(\mathbf{z}_T|\mathbf{x}) \approx q(\mathbf{z}_T) \approx \mathrm{Norm}[\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I}]$.
> - **Decoder** (reverse process): a learned [[Markov Chain|Markov chain]] running backward from $\mathbf{z}_T$ to $\mathbf{x}$, with every step approximated by a normal distribution whose mean is a network output,
> $$
> \begin{align}
> Pr(\mathbf{z}_T) &= \mathrm{Norm}_{\mathbf{z}_T}[\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I}] \\
> Pr(\mathbf{z}_{t-1}|\mathbf{z}_t, \boldsymbol{\phi}_t) &= \mathrm{Norm}_{\mathbf{z}_{t-1}}\left[\mathbf{f}_t[\mathbf{z}_t, \boldsymbol{\phi}_t], \sigma_t^2\mathbf{I}\right] \\
> Pr(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{z}_1, \boldsymbol{\phi}_1) &= \mathrm{Norm}_{\mathbf{x}}\left[\mathbf{f}_1[\mathbf{z}_1, \boldsymbol{\phi}_1], \sigma_1^2\mathbf{I}\right]
> \end{align}
> $$
> with predetermined variances $\{\sigma_t^2\}$, giving the joint
> $$
> \begin{align}
> Pr(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{z}_{1\dots T}|\boldsymbol{\phi}_{1\dots T}) = Pr(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{z}_1, \boldsymbol{\phi}_1)\prod_{t=2}^T Pr(\mathbf{z}_{t-1}|\mathbf{z}_t, \boldsymbol{\phi}_t) \cdot Pr(\mathbf{z}_T)
> \end{align}
> $$
>
> All learned parameters live in the decoder, which is trained so that each step inverts the corresponding encoder step: noise is gradually removed until a realistic example remains. New examples are generated by [[Ancestral Sampling|ancestral sampling]]: $\mathbf{z}_T \sim Pr(\mathbf{z}_T)$, then $\mathbf{z}_{T-1} \sim Pr(\mathbf{z}_{T-1}|\mathbf{z}_T, \boldsymbol{\phi}_T)$, and so on down to $\mathbf{x}$.

**Does a diffusion model assume Gaussian data?** No. $Pr(\mathbf{x})$ is arbitrary. Only the distributions the encoder *defines* are normal: the steps $q(\mathbf{z}_t|\mathbf{z}_{t-1})$ by construction, and hence the [[Diffusion Kernel|diffusion kernel]] $q(\mathbf{z}_t|\mathbf{x})$ and the [[Conditional Diffusion Distribution|conditional diffusion distribution]] $q(\mathbf{z}_{t-1}|\mathbf{z}_t, \mathbf{x})$, since a [[Multivariate Normal Distribution Affine Transformation|linear-Gaussian chain]] started at a fixed point stays Gaussian. The marginals $q(\mathbf{z}_t) = \int q(\mathbf{z}_t|\mathbf{x})Pr(\mathbf{x})\,d\mathbf{x}$ are a data-dependent [[Mixture of Gaussians|mixture of Gaussians]], and the true reverse conditionals $q(\mathbf{z}_{t-1}|\mathbf{z}_t) = q(\mathbf{z}_t|\mathbf{z}_{t-1})q(\mathbf{z}_{t-1})/q(\mathbf{z}_t)$ are in general complex and multimodal.[^3]

**Why the Gaussian reverse step is still justified.** For small $\beta_t$, the factor $q(\mathbf{z}_t|\mathbf{z}_{t-1})$, viewed as a function of $\mathbf{z}_{t-1}$, is a sharp Gaussian $\mathrm{Norm}_{\mathbf{z}_{t-1}}\left[\mathbf{z}_t/\sqrt{1-\beta_t}, \frac{\beta_t}{1-\beta_t}\mathbf{I}\right]$ of width $O(\sqrt{\beta_t})$ ([[Multivariate Normal Density Product|Gaussian change of variables]]). Over such a small region the smooth prior $q(\mathbf{z}_{t-1})$ is locally log-linear, $\log q(\mathbf{z}_{t-1}) \approx \mathrm{const} + \nabla\log q(\boldsymbol{\mu}_0)^T(\mathbf{z}_{t-1} - \boldsymbol{\mu}_0)$ with $\boldsymbol{\mu}_0 = \mathbf{z}_t/\sqrt{1-\beta_t}$, and a Gaussian times an exponential-linear function is again Gaussian:
$$
\begin{align}
q(\mathbf{z}_{t-1}|\mathbf{z}_t) \approx \mathrm{Norm}_{\mathbf{z}_{t-1}}\left[\frac{\mathbf{z}_t}{\sqrt{1-\beta_t}} + \frac{\beta_t}{1-\beta_t}\nabla\log q(\boldsymbol{\mu}_0),\; \frac{\beta_t}{1-\beta_t}\mathbf{I}\right]
\end{align}
$$
The neglected curvature of $\log q$ is a higher-order correction in $\beta_t$, so with many small steps ($\beta_t \to 0$, $T$ large) each reverse step is close to Gaussian even though the data distribution is not; the non-Gaussianity is spread across the composition of $T$ steps (Feller, 1949; Sohl-Dickstein et al., 2015). The mean is a shift along the *score* $\nabla\log q(\mathbf{z})$ of the noisy marginal, which is exactly what the noise-predicting network estimates ([[Diffusion Model Loss Function]]). Large steps break the approximation, which is why sampling needs many steps.[^3][^4]

# Types
- [[Denoising Diffusion Implicit Model]]: non-Markovian forward process, deterministic sampling, fewer steps.
- [[Latent Diffusion Model]]: diffusion in the latent space of an autoencoder (Stable Diffusion).
- [[Cascaded Diffusion Model]]: a chain of models of increasing resolution.
- Conditional models via [[Classifier Guidance]] or [[Classifier-Free Guidance]].

# Properties
- **Relation to other generative models**: like a [[Normalizing Flow|normalizing flow]], a nonlinear map between latents and data of the same dimension; like a [[Variational Autoencoder|VAE]], trained with a lower bound on the likelihood using an encoder. Unlike the VAE the encoder is predetermined, so it can be viewed as a VAE with a fixed hierarchical posterior. Easy to train and produces samples more realistic than those of [[Generative Adversarial Network|GANs]].[^1]
- **Training**: maximizes the [[Evidence Lower Bound]] with the fixed posterior $q(\mathbf{z}_{1\dots T}|\mathbf{x})$, since the marginal likelihood $\int Pr(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{z}_{1\dots T}|\boldsymbol{\phi})\,d\mathbf{z}_{1\dots T}$ is intractable. The decoder does all the work: it makes the bound tighter by changing its parameters so that the static encoder approximates the posterior $Pr(\mathbf{z}_{1\dots T}|\mathbf{x}, \boldsymbol{\phi})$, and it maximizes its own parameters with respect to that bound. In practice this reduces to regressing the added noise ([[Diffusion Model Loss Function]]).[^5]
- **Architecture**: a single [[U-Net]] shared across all time steps, with the time step $t$ added as an embedding, implements $\mathbf{g}_t[\mathbf{z}_t, \boldsymbol{\phi}_t]$ for images.[^6]
- **No exact likelihood**: only the ELBO is available ([[Test Likelihood]]), and the latent space is not compact or well-behaved ([[Generative Model Desiderata]]).
- **Improvements**:[^7]
	- Also learning the reverse variances $\sigma_t^2$, which particularly helps when sampling with fewer steps (Nichol & Dhariwal, 2021).
	- Tuning the noise schedule $\beta_t$ across steps (e.g. a cosine schedule).
- **Slow sampling**: many small steps are needed so that $q(\mathbf{z}_{t-1}|\mathbf{z}_t)$ is near-Gaussian. Remedies:[^8]
	- Deterministic, step-skipping samplers ([[Denoising Diffusion Implicit Model|DDIM]]) and numerical ODE solvers on the [[Probability Flow ODE]], with tuned time discretizations (Karras et al., 2022).
	- Richer per-step models such as [[Conditional GAN|conditional GANs]] (denoising diffusion GANs) or conditional [[Energy-Based Model|energy-based models]], which cannot describe the data distribution but suffice for the much simpler reverse step, allowing fewer steps.
	- Distilling pairs of adjacent denoising steps into one (progressive distillation); adding momentum to the diffusion (critically-damped Langevin diffusion) for smoother, coarser-sampleable trajectories.
	- Since 2023, consistency models and flow matching / rectified flows reach good samples in one to a few network evaluations, largely closing the speed gap to GANs.
- **Alternatives to noise**: the corruption need not be Gaussian noise or even stochastic. Progressive blurring (inverse heat dissipation) and a large family of deterministic degradations (masking, morphing, blurring, pixelating; "cold diffusion") can be learned and inverted in the same way.[^9]
- **Spatial control**: a pre-trained model can be fine-tuned to condition on edge maps, joint positions, segmentations, depth maps, etc. by a *control network* (ControlNet): a trainable copy of the U-Net encoder attached through zero-initialized convolutions.[^10]
