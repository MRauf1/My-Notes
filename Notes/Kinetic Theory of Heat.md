# Definition
> [!info] Kinetic Theory of Heat[^1]
> Heat and temperature correspond to the disordered kinetic motion ("jiggling") of atoms and molecules: increasing the temperature of a substance increases the amplitude and speed of this atomic motion, and decreasing the temperature decreases it, down to a residual minimum at absolute zero ([[Zero-Point Motion]]).

# Properties
- Grounded in the [[Atomic Hypothesis]].
- Underlies the [[Kinetic Theory of Pressure]], since gas pressure depends on the speed of the colliding molecules.
- Explains [[Melting]] as the point at which increasing vibrational amplitude within a [[Crystalline Structure]] shakes atoms out of their fixed positions.
- Explains [[Evaporation]] and [[Evaporative Cooling]] as consequences of the distribution of molecular kinetic energies at a liquid's surface.
- Within [[Conservation of Energy]], identifies heat as a form of [[Kinetic Energy]] rather than an independently new form of energy: energy that appears to be lost from ordered macroscopic motion (e.g. to friction) is not destroyed but converted into this disordered atomic motion.

[^1]: [The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. I, Ch. 1: Atoms in Motion](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_01.html)
