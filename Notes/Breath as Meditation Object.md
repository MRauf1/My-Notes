# Definition
> [!info] Breath as Meditation Object[^1]
> In [[Vipassana Meditation]], the breath is the primary object of attention. Specifically, this means the physical, tactile sensation of air passing in and out at the rims of the nostrils. It serves as a fixed reference point ("home base"). Distraction can only be seen as distraction against such a central focus.

# Properties
- **Why breath.** It is always present. It is non-conceptual and can be experienced directly without thought. It is a living, constantly changing process with a birth, growth, and death in every inhalation and exhalation, "a miniature model of life itself". It is universal to all living things, showing one's connectedness with all life. It is a present-time process.[^2]
- The balance between involuntary breathing and deliberately controlling the breath teaches lessons about will, desire, and the compulsive need to control. The nostril tip is a "window" between inner and outer worlds, which teaches how the self-concept is formed.[^3]
- **Elephant analogy.** The wild elephant is the active mind, the rope is [[Mindfulness|mindfulness]], and the post is the breath. The tamed elephant is a concentrated mind ([[Samadhi]]).[^4]
- **Procedure.**
	1. Take three deep breaths, then breathe naturally without controlling the breath.[^5]
	2. Locate your breath point and do not deviate from it.[^5]
	3. Do not verbalize or conceptualize. Counting breaths, or labeling "in… out", is an auxiliary device for building concentration. Drop it once the breath becomes so refined that inhalation and exhalation can't be told apart.[^6]
	4. Join inhalation and exhalation into one continuous breath.[^6]
	5. As concentration deepens, a pleasant *sign* (*nimitta*) appears at the nostrils and becomes the third object of meditation. Focusing on it gives enough concentration for insight. The sign itself changes every moment.[^7]
- If the breath becomes too subtle to notice, it has not disappeared. Take a few quick breaths and continue.[^7]
- If the breath seems boring, you have stopped observing it mindfully and started conceptualizing it. Mindfulness is never boring.[^8]
- Wild, agitated mind: a few quick deep breaths and a strong act of will can re-establish attention, since concentration can be forced.[^9]
- Very sleepy: take deep breaths and hold each as long as you can, until the body warms up.[^10]
