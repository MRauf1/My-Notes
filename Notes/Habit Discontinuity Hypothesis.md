# Definition
> [!info] Habit Discontinuity Hypothesis (Verplanken and Wood)[^1]
> Habits are cued by context, so a change of context (moving house, a new job or school) removes the cues that trigger old habits and opens a window in which behavior is easier to change. "It is easier to associate a new habit with a new context than to build a new habit in the face of competing cues."

# Properties
- **Context becomes the cue.** Over time a habit becomes associated not with a single trigger but with the entire context surrounding the behavior. We relate to the objects and places around us, and different people have different habits tied to the same place.[^2]
- **Evidence.**
	- When students transferred universities, their exercise, newspaper-reading and TV habits persisted only if the new context resembled the old one (Wood, Tam and Witt, 2005).
	- A sustainability intervention changed behavior more in people who had recently moved (Verplanken and Roy, 2016).
- **Vietnam veterans (Robins, 1974).** Of soldiers addicted to heroin in Vietnam, only about 5% relapsed within a year of returning home and 12% within three years. Addiction can dissolve with a radical change of environment.[^3] (The soldiers also went through urine screening and detoxification before returning, and heroin was harder to get at home.)
- **Stimulus control (Bootzin, 1972).** Insomniacs go to bed only when sleepy and leave the bedroom if they can't sleep, so the bed comes to cue sleep alone.[^4] This is a recommended component of cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia.
- **"One space, one use"** generalizes stimulus control. When you mix contexts you mix habits, and the easier ones usually win. Where space is limited, divide it into activity zones, including digital ones.[^5] The generalization itself hasn't been tested.
- Related: [[Cue]], [[Situational Self-Control]], [[Addiction]], [[Active Inhibition]] (context-dependent memory).
