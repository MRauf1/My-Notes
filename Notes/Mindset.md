# Definition

Mindset refers to the beliefs that guide your thinking, behavior, and actions. Mindset is not an either-or, but is a combination, but the ratios of the mindset are malleable.

The two types were presented by [[Carol Dweck]], initially in 1988.

# Types

- [[Fixed Mindset|Fixed Mindset]]
- [[Growth Mindset|Growth Mindset]]

# Properties
- A simple belief about oneself that guides much of life: much of what is thought of as personality grows out of it, and mindsets are part of personality yet can be changed ("Mindsets are just beliefs").[^1]
- It rests on two meanings of ability: a fixed ability that needs to be proven versus a changeable ability developed through learning. The fixed mindset makes one concerned with how one will be judged; the growth mindset, with improving.[^2]
- People can hold different mindsets in different areas.[^3]
- Ideas about risk and effort follow from the mindset: teaching the growth mindset produces valuing of challenge and effort, while temporarily inducing a fixed mindset quickly produces fear of challenge and devaluing of effort.[^4]
