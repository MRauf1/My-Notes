# Definition
> [!info] Borel $\sigma$-Algebra[^1]
> If $X$ is a [[Metric Space]] (or more generally a topological space), the Borel $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal{B}_X$ is the [[Generated Sigma-Algebra|$\sigma$-algebra generated]] by the family of [[Open Set|open sets]] in $X$ (equivalently, by the family of [[Closed Set|closed sets]]). Its members are called **Borel sets**.

$\mathcal{B}_X$ contains open sets, closed sets, countable intersections of open sets ([[G-Delta Set|$G_\delta$ sets]]), countable unions of closed sets ([[F-Sigma Set|$F_\sigma$ sets]]), and so on up a transfinite hierarchy ($G_{\delta\sigma}$, $F_{\sigma\delta}$, ...).[^2] It is the default domain for measures on $\mathbb{R}$ and $\mathbb{R}^n$ ([[Borel Measure]]).

# Properties
> [!abstract] Proposition 1.2 (Generators of $\mathcal{B}_\mathbb{R}$)[^3]
> $\mathcal{B}_\mathbb{R}$ is generated by each of the following:
> 1. the open intervals $\{(a, b) : a < b\}$,
> 2. the closed intervals $\{[a, b] : a < b\}$,
> 3. the half-open intervals $\{(a, b] : a < b\}$ or $\{[a, b) : a < b\}$,
> 4. the open rays $\{(a, \infty) : a \in \mathbb{R}\}$ or $\{(-\infty, a) : a \in \mathbb{R}\}$,
> 5. the closed rays $\{[a, \infty) : a \in \mathbb{R}\}$ or $\{(-\infty, a] : a \in \mathbb{R}\}$.

> [!abstract] Proposition 1.23 (Transfinite Construction)[^4]
> Let $\mathcal{E} \subseteq \mathcal{P}(X)$ with $\emptyset \in \mathcal{E}$. Put $\mathcal{E}_1 = \mathcal{E}$ and, for each countable ordinal $\alpha > 1$, let $\mathcal{E}_\alpha$ be the family of sets that are countable unions of members of $\bigcup_{\beta < \alpha} \mathcal{E}_\beta$, or complements of such members. Then
> $$
> \begin{align}
> \mathcal{M}(\mathcal{E}) = \bigcup_{\alpha \in \Omega} \mathcal{E}_\alpha,
> \end{align}
> $$
> where $\Omega$ is the [[Set of Countable Ordinals]]. (Stated here in the standard form; the indexing details may differ slightly from Folland.)

- Proposition 1.23 shows that Borel sets cannot in general be reached in finitely many (or countably many fixed) steps from open sets; the iteration must run through all countable ordinals.
- $\operatorname{card}(\mathcal{B}_\mathbb{R}) = \mathfrak{c}$ ([[Cardinality of the Continuum]]), a consequence of Proposition 1.23.[^5] Hence not every Lebesgue measurable set is Borel ([[Lebesgue Measure]]).
- For separable metric spaces, the Borel $\sigma$-algebra of a finite product is the [[Product Sigma-Algebra|product]] of the Borel $\sigma$-algebras; in particular $\mathcal{B}_{\mathbb{R}^n} = \bigotimes_1^n \mathcal{B}_\mathbb{R}$.
- The finite disjoint unions of h-intervals $(a, b]$ form an [[Algebra of Sets|algebra]] generating $\mathcal{B}_\mathbb{R}$, the starting point of the [[Lebesgue-Stieltjes Measure]] construction.
- Modulo null sets, Borel sets are simple: every Borel set differs from a $G_\delta$ and from an $F_\sigma$ set by a null set ([[Lebesgue-Stieltjes Measure Regularity]]).
