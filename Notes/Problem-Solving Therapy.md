# Definition
> [!info] Problem-Solving Therapy (PST)[^1]
> A structured cognitive-behavioral intervention (D'Zurilla and Goldfried, 1971; D'Zurilla and Nezu) that trains people to:
> 1. Define the problem concretely.
> 2. Generate many alternative solutions.
> 3. Evaluate the consequences of each and decide.
> 4. Implement the solution and verify the outcome.
>
> Carnegie's versions anticipate it. One is "get the facts, analyse the facts, arrive at a decision and act on it". Another is four written questions: what is the problem, what is its *cause*, what are all possible solutions, and what solution do you suggest.

# Properties
- **Evidence.** Meta-analyses find PST effective for depression, comparable to other psychotherapies (Bell and D'Zurilla, 2009; Cuijpers et al., 2018). It also reduces anxiety and distress in medical populations (Malouff, Thorsteinsson and Schutte, 2007).
- **Problem orientation.** PST distinguishes the *skills* of problem solving from one's *orientation* to problems. A negative orientation (seeing problems as threats, doubting one's ability to solve them) predicts worry and depression more than skill deficits do.
- **Worry versus problem solving.** Carnegie: "Concern means realising what the problems are and calmly taking steps to meet them. Worrying means going around in maddening, futile circles." In clinical terms, worry is abstract, verbal and repetitive. It feels like problem solving but rarely ends in a concrete decision (Borkovec; Watkins). Making the problem concrete is itself therapeutic (Watkins, 2008).[^2]
- **Objective fact-finding.** Carnegie gathers facts "as if for someone else" and argues the other side's case. This resembles *self-distancing*, which reduces emotional reactivity and improves reasoning about one's own problems (Kross and Ayduk, 2017; Grossmann and Kross, 2014). He also warns against seeking only facts that justify our wishes, which is [[Cognitive Bias|confirmation bias]].[^3]
- **Writing it down.** "It is much easier to analyse the facts after writing them down."[^4] Compare [[Expressive Writing]].
- **Decide and act.** Carnegie's executive claimed half his worries vanished once he reached a clear decision, and another 40% once he started acting on it. The figures are anecdotal. Once decided, set a time to start (see [[Implementation Intention]]).[^5]
