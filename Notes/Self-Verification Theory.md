# Definition
> [!info] Self-Verification Theory (Swann)[^1]
> People are motivated to confirm their existing self-views, even negative ones, because a stable self-concept makes the world feel predictable and controllable. They seek, remember and believe feedback that matches how they see themselves, and avoid or discount feedback that doesn't.

# Properties
- **Manson's law of avoidance.** "The more something threatens your identity, the more you will avoid it." It applies to good things as well as bad, which is why people can fear success for the same reason they fear failure: it threatens who they believe they are.[^1]
- **Evidence.**
	- People with negative self-views preferred interaction partners who evaluated them unfavourably (Swann, Stein-Seroussi and Giesler, 1992).
	- Positive self-statements made people with low self-esteem feel worse (Wood, Perunovic and Lee, 2009; see [[Ironic Process Theory]]).
	- Self-verification competes with self-enhancement: positive feedback feels good emotionally, but self-consistent feedback is judged more credible (Kwang and Swann, 2010).
- **Narrow identities.** The narrower and rarer the identity you choose, the more things will threaten it. Defining yourself in ordinary, broad terms makes change less threatening.[^2] Compare [[Anatta]].
- Related: [[Cognitive Dissonance]], [[Self-Esteem]], [[Psychological Entitlement]].
