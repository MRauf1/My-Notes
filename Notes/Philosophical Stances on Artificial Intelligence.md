# Definition
> [!info] Philosophical Stances on Artificial Intelligence[^1]
> The range of positions one may take on the value of human intellectual contributions once AI can replicate or surpass them, ranging between three "straw-man" extremes and a middle ground of complementary coexistence.

# Types
- **Technical retreat** - retreat into purely technical frameworks in which no distinction is made between human- and AI-generated work, so long as the technical specifications of the task are met.[^1]
- **Human exceptionalism** - ascribe an ineffable special status to human intellect and creativity, permanently distinguishing it from any artificial replication however accurate, so that AI is forever a "No True Scotsman" lacking true "soul" or "understanding". This may deny the uncomfortable truth that some human capabilities are not much more sophisticated than the algorithms mimicking them, and risks degenerating into a "god of the gaps" philosophy with an ever-shrinking list of uniquely human qualities.[^2]
- **Total supersession** - hold that all human cognitive abilities will soon be superseded, making questions of human contribution moot; in extreme forms, the exercise of intellect itself is viewed as tedious and to be automated away for leisure. This risks degrading human abilities until humanity cannot monitor, control, or understand the AIs to which it has delegated civilisation.[^3]
- **Complementary coexistence** - a middle ground, exemplified by chess: engines have beaten every grandmaster for decades, yet chess thrives, with players training with engines, revisiting and exploring theory, probing engine weaknesses, and inventing new AI-assisted forms of competition; the question of what chess is and why to play it remains worth asking. Formalised by the [[Copernican Principle|Copernican view of intelligence]].[^4]

# Properties
- Human exceptionalism echoes earlier [[Objections to Machine Intelligence]], such as the [[Heads in the Sand Objection]] and the [[Argument from Consciousness]], and the repeated moving of goalposts for intelligence (cf. [[Turing Test]]).
- The coexistence stance underlies [[Human-Centered Artificial Intelligence]].
