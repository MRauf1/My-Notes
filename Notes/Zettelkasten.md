# Definition

Slip-box system for note-taking and connecting/generating ideas developed by [[Niklas Luhmann]].

Links between notes are what give rise to new ideas and connections between ideas.

Each note should contain only one idea, and is therefore, usually short and concise.

In a sense, Zettelkasten is an extension of one's thinking. A system that promotes research and discovery of new ideas.

# Types of Notes

1) Fleeting Notes
2) Literature Notes
3) Permanent Notes

# Zettelkasten vs. Wikipedia
Unlike Wikipedia-type system that is used for recording information, Zettelkasten is about recording ideas, making connections, and generating new ones.[^1] Although in practice, the two are not all that different, and one could repurpose a Wikipedia-type system to work like a Zettelkasten.

They do both possess similarities though, such as the idea of linking notes. However, they also differ: in Zettelkasten, a note represents a single idea, which is not necessarily the case in Wikipedia.

# Luhmann's Slip-Box (Ahrens)
- Luhmann wrote every note on a small slip with a fixed number and collected them in one place, not by topic. Each new note was filed directly behind a related note or linked to notes anywhere in the system, so the same note could take part in different contexts. An index pointed to one or two entry notes per topic. It is not a personal Wikipedia or a paper database.[^2]
- He translated ideas from texts into his own context rather than copying them.[^2]
- "I, of course, do not think everything by myself. It happens mainly within the slip-box." With it he wrote *The Society of Society* (a 30-year project) and generated more ideas than he could publish.[^3]
- "I only do what is easy. I only write when I immediately know how to do it. If I falter for a moment, I put the matter aside and do something else." Ahrens argues his output came *because* he never forced himself.[^3]
- **Value grows with size.** The slip-box is not a collection to retrieve from but a partner that points you to related notes you weren't looking for, offering lines of developed thought rather than isolated facts. Ahrens claims its usefulness grows "not just linearly but exponentially" with size, much like the brain's interconnectedness.[^4]
- An external structure compensates for the brain's limits. Freeing the brain from organizing notes is the main reason to use one.[^5]
