# Definition
> [!info] Meditation Posture[^1]
> A practice aid for [[Meditation]]: a stable, relaxed posture with the back straight and the head in line with the spine, held so that one can sit for the whole session without moving.

# Properties
- **Threefold purpose**:[^2]
	1. It gives a stable feeling in the body, freeing attention from balance and muscular fatigue.
	2. Physical stillness is mirrored by stillness of mind, which settles concentration.
	3. It lets you sit for a long time without giving in to the meditator's three main enemies: pain, muscular tension, and falling asleep.
- A straight spine is a physiological "position of arousal" that goes with mental alertness.[^1]
- Traditional floor postures (Burmese, half lotus, full lotus, etc.) need a cushion about three inches high, compressed, to raise the spine. Full lotus is considered best but needs considerable flexibility. A chair is fine; personal comfort is the main criterion.[^3]
- Hands are cupped one on the other, palms up, in the lap. Eyes may be open or closed. Clothes should be loose; shoes off.[^4]
- **Handling pain** is a two-stage process. First, remove its cause if possible. Then make any remaining pain the object of meditation (see [[Dukkha]]). If it becomes excruciating, move slowly and mindfully.[^5]
- Leg numbness comes from a pinched nerve, not lack of circulation, and cannot damage tissue. It fades with regular practice.[^6]
