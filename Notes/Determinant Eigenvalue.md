# Definition
> [!abstract] Theorem 1 (Exercise 6.1.9)[^1]
> Suppose $\dim(V) = n$ and $T \in \mathcal{L}(V)$ has $n$ distinct [[Eigenvalue|eigenvalues]] $\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_n$. Then
> $$
> \det(T) = \lambda_1 \cdots \lambda_n
> $$

> [!abstract] Theorem 2 (General Case -- Corollary 6.24)[^2]
> If $\mathbb{F}$ is [[Algebraically Closed Field|algebraically closed]], $A \in M_n(\mathbb{F})$, and $\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_n$ are the eigenvalues of $A$ counted with [[Algebraic Multiplicity of Eigenvalue|algebraic multiplicity]], then $\det(A) = \lambda_1 \cdots \lambda_n$, without requiring $A$ to have $n$ distinct eigenvalues as in Theorem 1. This parallels the analogous general fact for the [[Trace Eigenvalue|trace]].

Algebraic closedness of $\mathbb{F}$ is what guarantees all $n$ eigenvalues (with multiplicity) actually exist in $\mathbb{F}$ to multiply; see [[Algebraic Multiplicity of Eigenvalue]].
