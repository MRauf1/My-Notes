# Definition
> [!info] Being-towards-Death (Heidegger)[^1]
> In *Being and Time*, to be human is above all to exist temporally, between birth and death, certain the end will come yet unable to know when. We don't *have* a limited amount of time; we *are* a limited amount of time. Authentic existence means living in clear-eyed acknowledgment of this finitude: this is it, life is not a dress rehearsal, every choice requires myriad sacrifices, and time may run out today. "I can't entirely depend upon a single moment of the future."

# Properties
- **Falling.** The inauthentic alternative is avoidance and denial: losing ourselves in distraction, busyness and the daily grind so as to forget our predicament. Facing finitude is not morbid; it is the only way to live fully and relate to others as full-fledged humans.[^2]
- **Finitude creates value (Hägglund).** If life never ended, nothing could genuinely matter, because you would never have to decide whether to spend part of your life on something. Only finite things can be valued, and only from that position can one truly care about collective perils such as climate change.[^3]
- **Four thousand weeks** is roughly an 80-year life: $80 \times 365.25 / 7 \approx 4174$ weeks. It is tiny compared with infinity, yet enormous compared with never having been born. "You needn't have happened. But you did happen." (Douglas Harding)[^4]
- **The astonishment of being.** What we fail to appreciate is that there is anything at all rather than nothing. From there, choices become something we *get* to make rather than *have* to make (compare [[Cognitive Reappraisal]]).[^5]
- **Brushes with death** often leave people in a more honest relationship with time: things get "realer", though not reliably happier.[^6] Research on post-traumatic growth finds such reports are common, but self-reported growth correlates poorly with measured change, so the reports may partly be a way of coping (Frazier et al., 2009).
- "Time is the substance I am made of … Time is a river that sweeps me along, but I am the river." (Borges)[^7]
