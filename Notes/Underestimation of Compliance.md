# Definition
> [!info] Underestimation of Compliance[^1]
> People systematically underestimate how willing others are to agree to direct requests for help (Flynn and Lake, 2008). Pausch: "Sometimes, all you have to do is ask… More often than you'd suspect, the answer you'll get is, 'Sure.'"

# Properties
- **Evidence.** Participants who had to get strangers to fill out a questionnaire or lend a phone predicted they would need to ask about twice as many people as they actually did (Flynn and Lake, 2008). The effect generalizes to requests for unethical favors (Bohns, Roghanizad and Xu, 2014).
- **Why.** Requesters focus on the cost of the request to the other person. They neglect the social cost of saying no: refusing feels awkward, so people say yes more than expected.
- **Mentors.** Pausch emailed Fred Brooks asking for 30 minutes. He got 90 and a lifelong mentor.[^1] See [[Brooks's Law]].
- Related: [[Spotlight Effect]], [[Self-Efficacy]].
