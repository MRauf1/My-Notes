# Definition
> [!info] Incommensurability[^1]
> The thesis that successive or competing [[Paradigm (Philosophy of Science)|paradigms]] embody different ways of seeing the world and practising science, so that after a [[Scientific Revolution|revolution]] the scientist's research world seems, here and there, incommensurable with the one previously inhabited, and schools guided by different paradigms are always slightly at cross-purposes.

# Properties
- "When paradigms change, the world itself changes with them": since scientists access the world only through what they see and do, after a revolution they respond to a different world, having learned to see a new gestalt in familiar situations.[^1]
- Paradigms are not corrigible by normal science; crises end not by deliberation and interpretation but by a relatively sudden, unstructured event like a gestalt switch, which gathers up large portions of old experience into a different bundle linked to the new paradigm.[^2]
- The alternative to seeing through one paradigm is not a "fixed" neutral vision but vision through another paradigm ([[Theory-Ladenness of Observation]]).[^3]
- Yet the change is never total: the scientist still looks at the same world, and much language and many instruments persist, the change lying in their relation to the paradigm or their concrete results.[^4]
- Scientific concepts (e.g. "element") gain meaning only within a systematic context of other concepts, procedures, and applications, so they cannot be invented independent of context and change meaning across paradigms.[^5]
