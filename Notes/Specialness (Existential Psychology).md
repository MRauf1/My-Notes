# Definition
> [!info] Specialness (Existential Psychology)[^1]
> The irrational, near-universal belief, held first as a child and then as an adult, in one's personal inviolability: that the rule of mortality applies to others but not to oneself. One of the two basic bulwarks against [[Death Anxiety]], alongside the [[Ultimate Rescuer]].

# Properties
- Consciously no one denies mortality, yet deep down each of us believes, like Ivan Ilyich, that it does not apply to us; when the defense breaks ("My God, I'm really going to die"), one feels lost and oddly betrayed.[^2]
- Accepting death means accepting that one is one of many, that the universe does not acknowledge one's specialness, and that one has "carried counterfeit vouchers".[^3]
- Clinical forms: the compulsive heroic individualist (the Hemingway hero, driven not choosing, with no place for ageing and ordinariness), workaholism (time threatens the belief that one is eternally advancing), and paradoxically suicide from fear of death.[^4]
- Depression often ensues when the belief in an ever-ascending "dominant goal" collapses; death is associated with banality and ordinariness.[^5]
- Decisions threaten specialness by limiting possibility ([[Decision (Existential Psychology)]]).[^6]
- Fusion with a group ("just like everyone else") is its opposite pole; see [[Existential Isolation]].
