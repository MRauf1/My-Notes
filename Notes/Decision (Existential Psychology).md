# Definition
> [!info] Decision (Existential Psychology)[^1]
> The bridge between wishing and action: to decide is to commit oneself to a course of action, so if no action ensues there was no true decision, only flirting with it. Since one constitutes oneself, "decisions are the atoms of the being that one creates", and one cannot not decide.

# Properties
- **Renunciation**: "for every yes there must be a no"; decisions limit possibilities, which brings one closer to death; some "sit at the crossroads" rather than renounce; wisdom finds ways to renounce as little as possible.[^2]
- **Why decisions are hard**: they expose one to groundlessness, challenge [[Specialness (Existential Psychology)|specialness]] and the [[Ultimate Rescuer]], confront one with [[Existential Isolation]] (no one can decide for us), and may be laden with guilt when will was born as counter-will against repressive parents (Rank).[^3]
- The discomfort of freedom is greatest when alternatives are nearly equal, so we unconsciously magnify small differences; afterwards we reduce [[Cognitive Dissonance]] by devaluing the unchosen alternative and filtering information.[^4]
- Procrastination, failure, drinking, being seduced or exploited are decisions; so is staying alive (Nietzsche). Meta-decisions ("deciding not to decide") are decisions to decide passively.[^5]
- The patient must own decisions; a decision made by the therapist is no decision and displaces therapy onto obedience or defiance. A decision sticks when one recognises and discards the payoffs of opposing decisions and discovers their limits.[^6]
- Making a decision reconstitutes one's world and reveals truths previously hidden from oneself.[^7]
- Change is a gradual process of multiple decisions whose groundwork may take months or years.[^8]
- Part of [[Will (Existential Psychology)]].
