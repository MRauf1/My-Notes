# Definition
> [!info] Preconception (Stoicism)[^1]
> (*Prolēpsis*) An innate, universally shared general concept of good and bad, right and wrong, happiness, duty, and so on, which nature instils before we add our own particular views. Preconceptions never conflict with one another; conflict arises only in their application to particular instances.

# Properties
- Everyone agrees that the good is advantageous and choiceworthy; people disagree about whether wealth, pleasure, or health is good.[^1]
- "The cause of all human troubles is the inability to apply preconceptions to particular instances": thinking oneself unwell, poor, or ill-treated when one is not.[^2]
- **Education** is learning to apply natural preconceptions to particular instances in accord with nature, and to recognise what is and isn't up to us ([[Dichotomy of Control]]).[^3]
- Locating the good in externals (e.g. "if it's good for me to have a plot of land, it's good to take my neighbour's") is the source of war, civil strife, and tyranny.[^4]
- Since people's conceptions of what is reasonable differ, the starting point of philosophy is recognising conflicting opinions, investigating why they conflict, distrusting mere belief, and discovering a standard (like a scale for weights or a rule for straightness).[^5]
- Elucidating preconceptions is proved by correctly applying them.[^6]
