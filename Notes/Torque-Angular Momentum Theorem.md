# Definition
> [!info] Torque-Angular Momentum Theorem[^1]
> For any collection of particles, rigid or not, the rate of change of the total [[Angular Momentum]] about any fixed axis equals the total external [[Torque]] about that axis:
> $$
> \begin{align}
> \tau_{\text{ext}} = \sum_i \tau_i = \sum_i \frac{dL_i}{dt} = \frac{dL}{dt}.
> \end{align}
> $$
> In three dimensions, $\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\text{ext}} = d\mathbf{L}/dt$.

# Properties
- Rotational analog of the many-particle form of [[Newton's Second Law]], $\mathbf{F}_{\text{ext}} = d\mathbf{P}/dt$ (see [[Conservation of Momentum]] and [[Center of Mass]]).
- Requires the strong form of [[Newton's Third Law]]: action and reaction are not only equal and opposite but directed along the same line joining the two particles. Then each pair of internal forces has the same lever arm about any axis, so their torques cancel pair by pair.
- Allows the overall rotational motion of a system to be studied without knowing the internal machinery.
- Its special case $\tau_{\text{ext}} = 0$ is [[Conservation of Angular Momentum]].

[^1]: [The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. I, Ch. 18: Rotation in Two Dimensions](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_18.html)
