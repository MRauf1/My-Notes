# Definition
> [!info] Virtuous Circle of Learning (Ahrens)[^1]
> A good workflow creates a positive feedback loop: positive experiences motivate the next task, which makes you better, which makes the work more enjoyable, and so on. Feeling constantly stuck creates the opposite, a vicious circle of demotivation, procrastination, and failure (Fishbach, Eyal, and Finkelstein, 2010).

# Properties
- **Intrinsic reward.** External rewards ("something nice after finishing a chapter") are fragile, short-term fixes. Only when the work itself is rewarding does motivation become self-sustaining (DePasque and Tricomi, 2015).[^2]
- **Feedback.** Nothing motivates more than getting better, which requires timely, concrete feedback. Seeking feedback is the first virtue of a learner ([[Growth Mindset]]). A growth mindset is only half the equation; you also need a system that produces feedback in practice.[^3]
- **Circle of competency.** Expressing ideas in your own words and separating the important from the unimportant both improve with practice, which makes note-taking and reading faster, which yields more learning. [[Permanent Note|Permanent notes]] add a built-in feedback loop, and the slip-box grows more useful as you grow.[^4]
- **Meaningful, well-defined tasks beat willpower.** Where there is no conflict between long- and short-term interests, interesting tasks simply get done. Success comes from smart environments that avoid resistance rather than from willpower (see [[Willpower]]).[^5]
- Students most often fail not from low intelligence but because they stop seeing meaning in what they learn, can't connect it to their goals, or lack control over their own learning (autonomy and meaning, as in self-determination theory).[^6]
- Related: [[Zettelkasten]], [[Flow State]], [[Letting It Happen]].
