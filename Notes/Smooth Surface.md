# Definition
> [!info] Smooth Surface (Parameterized by an Immersion)[^1]
> A (patch of a) smooth surface is described by a smooth map
> $$\begin{align}
> f: M \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^3
> \end{align}$$
> from a region $M \subset \mathbb{R}^2$ of the Euclidean plane to the subset $f(M) \subset \mathbb{R}^3$. The map $f$ is called an *immersion* when its [[Tangent Map|differential]] $df_p$ is injective at every $p \in M$ (so $f(M)$ has a well-defined 2D [[Tangent Plane]] everywhere).

![[Smooth Surface Immersion.png]]

This is the "patch of material floating in space" picture: $M$ is a flat rubber sheet, $f$ bends/stretches it into space, and the [[Tangent Map|differential]] $df$ tells how a little arrow $X$ drawn on $M$ becomes an arrow $df(X)$ on $f(M)$. It is well-suited to the discrete setting (meshes), where the surface is given directly as a subset of $\mathbb{R}^3$.[^1] The more general viewpoint covers abstract surfaces via charts, i.e. a 2-dimensional [[Manifold]].

The whole extrinsic geometry of the surface is generated by two maps: the immersion $f$ and the [[Gauss Map]] $N$. Everything else ([[Induced Metric]], [[Shape Operator]], [[Second Fundamental Form]], curvatures) is built from their differentials $df$ and $dN$.[^2]

![[Gauss Map.png]]

# Properties
- [[Tangent Map|Differential (pushforward)]] $df: TM \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^3$, acting on the [[Tangent Bundle]] $TM$.
- [[Induced Metric]] / [[First Fundamental Form]]: $g(X, Y) = df(X) \cdot df(Y)$.
- [[Surface Normal]], [[Orientable Surface]], [[Gauss Map]], [[Weingarten Map]].
- [[Conformal Map|Conformal]] parameterizations always exist locally; [[Isometric Parameterization|isometric]] ones generally do not.
- [[Normal Curvature]], [[Principal Curvature]], [[Shape Operator]], [[Mean Curvature]], [[Gaussian Curvature]], [[Second Fundamental Form]].
- Special surfaces: [[Developable Surface]] ($K = 0$), [[Minimal Surface]] ($H = 0$); special points: [[Umbilic Point]].
