# Definition
> [!info] Planck Time[^1]
> The Planck time $t_P$ is the time it takes light to travel a distance of one [[Planck Length]] in a vacuum:
> $$t_P = \frac{\ell_P}{c} = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar G}{c^5}} \approx 5.39 \times 10^{-44} \text{ s}$$

# Properties
- Vastly shorter than any duration currently measurable: the shortest time interval measured in a laboratory (around $247$ zeptoseconds, $2.47\times10^{-19}$ s) is still enormously larger than the Planck time.
- Below the Planck time, current physical laws — including general relativity and quantum mechanics — break down and lose their meaning, mirroring the role of the [[Planck Length]] as a fundamental length limit.
- The Planck epoch in cosmology refers to the first Planck time of the universe's existence, before which current physics cannot describe what happened.
- Together with the [[Planck Length]], forms part of the system of [[Planck Units]].

[^1]: [The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. I, Ch. 5: Time and Distance](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_05.html)
