# Definition
> [!info] Cognitive Distancing[^1]
> (Distancing; decentering) In [[Cognitive Therapy]], the process by which a client, by monitoring their automatic thoughts, learns to step back from them and treat them less literally: as mental events or hypotheses rather than facts.

# Properties
- **Stoic counterpart.** Epictetus's first exercise in the *Enchiridion*: question every impression and ask whether it attributes importance to what is under our control or to externals. If it concerns externals, affirm "It is nothing to me" ([[Impression (Stoicism)]], [[Dichotomy of Control]]).[^1]
- **Without distance.** When we are mindless rather than mindful, thoughts merge with experience, so we lose track of how our opinions shape our perceptions and can't see the distortions we impose.[^2]
- **Katharsis.** Plato's original therapeutic sense of *katharsis* ("purification") was separating opinion from perception: winnowing one's own mental acts from perception of the world. Freud later gave the word an entirely different meaning. Distancing corresponds to this and to the Stoic [[Objective Representation (Stoicism)|objective representation]]. It is the natural product of [[Prosoche]].[^3]
- **Self-monitoring.** Monitoring and recording thoughts often produces insight, objectivity and distance on its own (Beck, Emery & Greenberg); see [[Hypomnemata]].[^4]
- **Other people's beliefs.** Considering what others believe, or whether one would apply the same harsh standard to a child or a friend, exposes an inconsistency between standards for oneself and for others (Beck). Epictetus: we view others' setbacks philosophically and should view our own in the same light, or else we apply a double standard. The sage regards his own misfortune as dispassionately as the fate of others.[^5]
- **Evidence.** Decentering is measurable (Experiences Questionnaire). Larger gains in decentering after cognitive therapy predict lower depressive relapse (Fresco, Segal, Buis & Kennedy, 2007).
- Related: [[Mindfulness]], [[Cognitive Reappraisal]], [[View from Above]].
