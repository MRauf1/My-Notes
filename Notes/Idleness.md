# Definition
> [!info] Idleness (Burkeman)[^1]
> Spending at least some leisure time "wastefully", focused solely on the pleasure of the experience, is the only way *not* to waste it: to be truly at leisure rather than covertly engaged in future-focused self-improvement. "From this perspective, idleness isn't merely forgivable; it's practically an obligation."

# Properties
- **Instrumental leisure.** Industrialization made work the point of existence and leisure a way to recover for more work. Labour reformers who won the eight-hour day argued that workers would use the time to improve themselves, which entrenched the idea that leisure needs some justification beyond enjoyment.[^2]
- **The Protestant work ethic (Weber).** Calvinists worked relentlessly to prove, to others and themselves, that they were among the saved. Idleness could then look like evidence of already being damned.[^3]
- **Evidence.** People who believe leisure is wasteful enjoy leisure activities less and report more depression, anxiety and stress (Tonietto et al., 2021).
- Burkeman claims the pressure to use leisure productively grows with wealth.[^4]
- Rest may not feel good at first. Stop expecting it to.[^5] "How can there be play in a time when nothing has meaning unless it leads to something else?" (John Gray)[^6]
