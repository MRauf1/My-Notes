# Definition
> [!info] Conservation of Lepton Number[^1]
> The total lepton number of an isolated system — the number of leptons (the electron, muon, tau, and their neutrinos) minus the number of antileptons (e.g. the positron, the [[Antiparticle]] of the electron) — is conserved to high precision, summed across all three generations.

# Properties
- The lepton family comprises the electron, muon, and tau, each paired with a corresponding neutrino.
- Originally believed to hold separately for each generation (electron lepton number, muon lepton number, etc.); the 1998 discovery of neutrino oscillation showed that individual lepton-flavor numbers are *not* conserved — a neutrino produced in one flavor can later be detected in another — so only the total lepton number summed over all generations is (approximately) conserved.
- Like [[Conservation of Baryon Number]], total lepton number is understood as only an accidental symmetry of the Standard Model, violated in principle by non-perturbative electroweak sphaleron processes, which conserve $B - L$ rather than baryon number and lepton number separately.

[^1]: [The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. I, Ch. 4: Conservation of Energy](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_04.html)
