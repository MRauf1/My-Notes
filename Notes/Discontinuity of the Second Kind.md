# Definition

> [!info] Definition 1 (Discontinuity of the Second Kind)[^1]
> Let $f$ be defined on $(a, b)$ and discontinuous at $x$ ([[Discontinuity]]). If at least one of the [[One-Sided Limit of Function|one-sided limits]] $f(x^+)$, $f(x^-)$ does not exist, the discontinuity is of the second kind.

Also called an essential discontinuity. When a one-sided limit is $\pm\infty$ (e.g. a vertical asymptote), it is called an infinite discontinuity.[^2] Not to be confused with an [[Essential Singularity]] of a complex function.

# Properties
- Complement of [[Simple Discontinuity]] among discontinuities.
- The only possible discontinuities of a [[Derivative]] on an interval ([[Darboux's Theorem]]).
- A [[Monotonic Function]] has no discontinuities of the second kind ([[Monotonic Function One-Sided Limit Theorem]]).
