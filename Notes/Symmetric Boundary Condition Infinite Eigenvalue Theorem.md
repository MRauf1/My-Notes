# Definition
> [!abstract] Theorem (Infinitely Many Eigenvalues)[^1][^2]
> For the eigenvalue problem $X'' + \lambda X = 0$ on $(a, b)$ with any [[Symmetric Boundary Condition]], there are infinitely many eigenvalues. They are real ([[Symmetric Boundary Condition Real Eigenvalue Theorem]]) and form a sequence
> $$
> \begin{align}
> \lambda_n \to +\infty.
> \end{align}
> $$

# Properties
- Contrast with an $N \times N$ matrix, which has at most $N$ eigenvalues ([[Eigenfunction]]).
- Each eigenvalue has finite multiplicity (at most two, since the ODE is second order), and the spectrum is bounded below; asymptotically $\lambda_n \sim (n\pi/(b-a))^2$ for the standard conditions.[^3]
- The corresponding eigenfunctions form a [[Complete Orthogonal Set]], the basis of the [[Eigenfunction Expansion]].
