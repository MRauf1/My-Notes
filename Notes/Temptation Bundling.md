# Definition
> [!info] Temptation Bundling (Milkman)[^1]
> Linking an action you *want* to do with an action you *need* to do, so that the tempting activity is available only while doing the beneficial one. It applies [[Premack's Principle]]. It makes a habit more attractive because the anticipation of reward is what motivates action.

# Properties
- **Evidence.**
	- In the original field experiment, gym-goers whose favorite audiobooks were available only at the gym visited about 51% more often at first. The effect declined over the following weeks (Milkman, Minson and Volpp, 2014).
	- A larger follow-up field experiment found a smaller but significant increase in gym visits, roughly 10–14% (Kirgios et al., 2020).
- Clear combines it with habit stacking: "After [CURRENT HABIT], I will [HABIT I NEED]. After [HABIT I NEED], I will [HABIT I WANT]."[^2]
- It isn't a way to make a habit irresistible, just a simple way to make nearly any habit more attractive than it would otherwise be.[^3]
- **Caveat (overjustification).** Expected tangible rewards undermine intrinsic motivation for activities that are already interesting, though not for uninteresting ones (Deci, Koestner and Ryan, 1999). Bundling therefore suits tasks you don't enjoy rather than ones you already enjoy (see [[Intrinsic Motivation]]).
- Related: [[Law of Craving]], [[Dopamine]].
