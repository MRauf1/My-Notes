# Definition
> [!info] Cooperative Inverse Reinforcement Learning[^1]
> (Russell et al.) A framework in which a human and a machine jointly maximise a single reward function that initially only the human knows, so the machine must infer it from the human's behaviour while acting.

# Properties
- Draws on pedagogy and parenting in both directions: humans should act in ways understandable to machines, and machines should act *legibly* to humans.[^2]
- **Legibility vs. predictability**: behaving predictably presumes the observer knows your goal; behaving legibly presumes they do not, so the two are nearly opposite.[^3]
- Cooperation works best as ongoing interaction, not as separate "learn, then act" phases.[^3]
- Human-team insights transfer, e.g. *cross-training* (temporarily swapping roles), a gold standard in military, industrial, and medical teams.[^4]
- Provides a computational framework for machines acting in tandem with, not in lieu of, humans ([[Human-Machine Partnership]]).[^5]
- Builds on [[Inverse Reinforcement Learning]]; underlies approaches to [[Corrigibility]].
