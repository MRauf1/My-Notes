# Definition
> [!info] Deception Detection[^1]
> People's ability to tell lies from truths. Without special aids it is barely better than chance: about 54% accuracy across 206 studies (Bond and DePaulo, 2006). Pausch claims that "most people who have told a lie think they got away with it… when in fact, they didn't." In the moment, the evidence says they usually *did*.

# Properties
- **Lies are found out later, not in the moment.** Most lies are discovered afterwards, through third parties, physical evidence or contradicting information, rather than through nonverbal cues (Park et al., 2002). Pausch's real point holds over repeated interactions: "You run into people again later… And they tell lots of other people about it." Reputation tracks honesty over time.
- **Cue myths.** Gaze aversion and fidgeting are weak or useless signs of lying (DePaulo et al., 2003). Even trained professionals are near chance.
- **Honesty is less costly than people predict.** People told to be completely honest for several days enjoyed it more and felt more socially connected than they had expected (Levine and Cohen, 2018). This supports Pausch's "Honesty is not only morally right, it's also efficient."[^2]
- **Admitting a mistake.** When the Pausch family honestly told a store how a gift had broken, the store replaced it.[^3] Compare [[Apology]].
