# Definition
> [!info] Planck Length[^1]
> The Planck length $\ell_P$ is the natural unit of length formed from the fundamental constants $\hbar$ (reduced Planck constant), $G$ (gravitational constant), and $c$ (speed of light):
> $$\ell_P = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar G}{c^3}} \approx 1.616 \times 10^{-35} \text{ m}$$

# Properties
- Marks the length scale at which quantum mechanics and general relativity must both become significant, so that a complete theory of quantum gravity would be required to describe physics at or below this scale.
- Probing distances smaller than the Planck length would require concentrating so much energy into so small a region that it would theoretically collapse into a microscopic black hole, obscuring any finer structure.
- Together with the [[Planck Time]], forms part of the system of [[Planck Units]].

[^1]: [The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. I, Ch. 5: Time and Distance](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_05.html)
