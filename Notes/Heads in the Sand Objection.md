# Definition
> [!info] Heads in the Sand Objection[^1]
> The objection to machine intelligence that the consequences of machines thinking would be too dreadful, so we should hope and believe that they cannot. It stems from the wish to believe that humans are in some subtle way superior to the rest of creation, ideally *necessarily* superior, since then there is no danger of losing that commanding position.

# Properties
- One of the [[Objections to Machine Intelligence]].
- Turing considers it too insubstantial to require refutation, since it is an appeal to wishful thinking rather than an argument; consolation is more appropriate than rebuttal.
- Anticipated in Turing's 1948 [[Intelligent Machinery (Turing)|Intelligent Machinery]] as an unwillingness to admit that mankind can have any rivals in intellectual power, which occurs as much among intellectuals as others since they have more to lose; even those admitting the possibility agree its realisation would be very disagreeable, just as with the indisputable possibility of being superseded by another animal species.[^2]
- Closely related to the Theological Objection, which likewise tries to secure a necessary human superiority.
