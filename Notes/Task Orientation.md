# Definition
> [!info] Task Orientation[^1]
> Historians' term for a pre-modern way of living in which the rhythms of life emerge from the tasks themselves (milking, harvesting) rather than being lined up against an abstract timeline. Pre-modern people apparently did not experience time as an abstract entity at all. A farmer's work is infinite, so there is no sense in racing towards a hypothetical moment of completion.

# Properties
- **Clock time.** Once time is measured in the abstract (Lewis Mumford, *Technics and Civilization*), it is natural to treat it as a resource to be bought, sold and used efficiently. Burkeman notes the clock is not solely to blame.[^2]
- **Time and life separated.** Before, time was the medium in which life unfolded; afterwards, it became a thing you *use*. Self-worth becomes bound up with how you use it, and life is spent leaning into the future, never experiencing "deep time".[^3]
- **Industrialization** sharply separated leisure from work, which made leisure instrumental (see [[Idleness]]).[^4]
