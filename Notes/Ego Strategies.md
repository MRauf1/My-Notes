# Definition
> [!info] Ego Strategies (Tolle)[^1]
> The ego's identity is built from thought and emotion, which are fleeting, so its basis is precarious and every ego struggles to survive by protecting and enlarging itself. Whatever behavior it shows, the hidden motive is the same: the need to stand out, be special, be in control; the need for power, attention, and more; and the need for separation, i.e., opposition and enemies. The conceptual "I" cannot survive without the conceptual "other".

# Types
- **Complaining**: a little story the mind makes up and you fully believe. *Resentment* is the emotion that goes with complaining and labeling people. A long-standing resentment is a *grievance*.[^2]
- **Being right**: identifying with a mental position (opinion, judgment, story), which requires making others wrong. The ego confuses opinions with facts and events with its reactions to them, and is "a master of selective perception and distorted interpretation". Once thought and identity merge, defending an opinion feels like defending yourself. Zen: "Don't seek the truth. Just cease to cherish opinions."[^3]
- **Victimhood and specialness**: the stronger the ego, the more other people seem to be the source of your problems. In its extreme (paranoid) form, being persecuted makes one feel like the center of the universe, both victim and hero.[^4]
- **Fighting "evil"**: making it your mission to eradicate evil risks turning you into what you fight.[^5]
- **Illness** can strengthen the ego (illness as identity, slower recovery) or weaken it (people becoming gentler and wiser while ill, until energy and ego return).[^6]
- **Ego at work**: only part of attention is on the work, the rest on oneself. It reacts against obstacles instead of becoming one with the new situation. Cooperation is alien to it except with a secondary motive. Those who work without ego are unusually successful.[^7]
- **Three predominant states of egoic relationship**: wanting, thwarted wanting (anger, resentment, blaming, complaining), and indifference. "Falling in love" is usually an intensification of egoic wanting, an addiction to one's image of the other.[^8]
- **The ambivalent ego**: a shy person both wants and fears attention; the fear is simply stronger. Any conceptual sense of self, positive ("I am the greatest") or negative ("I am no good"), is ego.[^9]
- **Collective ego** (nations, groups) is usually more unconscious than its individual members.[^10]

# Properties
- "The ego isn't wrong; it's just unconscious." It isn't personal; treating it as your personal problem is more ego.[^11]
- Awareness and ego are incompatible. "The recognition of the false is already the arising of the real." When you notice the voice in your head, you realize you are the one aware of it. Only presence can free you of ego, and only now.[^12]
- Thought can at best point to the truth, never be it.[^13]
