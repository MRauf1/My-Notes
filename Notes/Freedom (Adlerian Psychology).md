# Definition
> [!info] Freedom (Adlerian Psychology)[^1]
> "Freedom is being disliked by other people." Being disliked by someone is proof that one is living by one's own principles. It is the **cost** of freedom in interpersonal relationships. Without accepting it, and the possibility of never being recognized, one cannot follow one's own way of living. This is the **courage to be disliked**.

# Properties
- **Not following impulses.** Living like a stone rolling downhill, carried by desires and impulses, is slavery to them. Real freedom is like pushing one's tumbling self back uphill.[^2]
- **Don't fear being disliked.** This does not mean seeking dislike. One simply separates tasks: whether someone likes you is their task. "He should like me after all I've done" is reward thinking that intrudes on their task ([[Separation of Tasks]]).[^3]
- Given a life where everyone likes you and one where some people dislike you, choose the latter. Before being concerned with what others think, follow through with your own being.[^3]
- "The courage to be happy also includes the courage to be disliked." With it, relationships become light.[^4]
- Living in fear of relationships falling apart is unfree: it is living for others. See [[Community Feeling]].[^5]
- There is no freedom in a feeling of contribution gained through recognition. "We are beings who choose freedom while aspiring to happiness." See [[Feeling of Contribution]].[^6]
- Opposes the [[Desire for Recognition]]. Compare [[Freedom (Stoicism)]] and [[Authenticity (Existentialism)]].
