# Definition
> [!info] Meditation Practice Guidelines (Gunaratana)[^1]
> Attitudes for [[Vipassana Meditation]]: don't expect anything; don't strain; don't rush; don't cling to anything and don't reject anything; let go; accept everything that arises; be gentle with yourself; investigate yourself (be empirical); view all problems as challenges; don't ponder (see, don't think); don't dwell on contrasts.

# Properties
- **Don't expect / don't strain.** Novices' expectations are unrealistic and uninformed. Striving after them creates tension, which is the opposite of mindfulness, and leads to frustration. Effort should be relaxed and steady.[^2]
- **Accept.** Treat all experiences as equal. The process of becoming who you will be begins with total acceptance of who you are.[^1]
- **Investigate.** Test every statement against your own experience ([[Empiricism]]).[^1]
- **Problems as challenges.** Difficulties are "grist for the mill". Avoiding unpleasantness in meditation reinforces the habit that makes life unbearable. Kindness to yourself means confronting unpleasantness, not autosuggesting it into something pleasant.[^3]
- **Don't dwell on contrasts.** Comparing self with others is a mental habit that leads to egotism and ill feeling (greed, envy, pride, jealousy). Instead, notice what is universal to all life (e.g., the breath). This leads to kinship, understanding, and not being upset by others' failings.[^4]
- **Schedule.** Set a formal practice time and place. Sitting in the same spot builds an association with deep concentration. A group is an aid, not a crutch.[^5]
- **When.** Morning (once fully awake) and evening (to clear the day's mental rubbish before sleep) are good times. Meditate before reading or watching films, since stories leave lingering mental images. Resolve immediate conflicts first when you can.[^6]
- **How long.** Beginners: 20–30 minutes. After about a year of steady practice: about an hour. A general rule is to sit 5 minutes longer than is comfortable. Decide the length *before* sitting and don't keep checking the clock. Regularity matters more than length; even 10 minutes helps.[^7]
- It is not asceticism or self-mortification. Sitting through excruciating pain to prove something is "a useless exercise in ego".[^8]
- **Self-discipline is patience.** It means seeing that urges have no real power ("they really carry no stick at all") by watching them rise and pass away without getting involved.[^9]
- Mindfulness cannot be forced. It grows through gentle, "effortless effort". Trying to force the mind to settle is repression and does not work.[^10]
- Patience is the key lesson. Changes start right away, but profound effects take years.[^11]
- Advanced meditators tend to be jovial and can laugh at their own failures, so loosen up.[^12]
- Persistent resistance to sitting points to a subtle error in attitude. Regard meditation as a friend and as a form of play.[^13]
- **Daily life.** Ideally practice 24 hours a day: keep the gap between seated meditation and the rest of life as small as possible, and use spare moments for mindfulness. If your practice isn't making everyday emotional reactions clearer and easier to manage, it is shallow.[^14]
