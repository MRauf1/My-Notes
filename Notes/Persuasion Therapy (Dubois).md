# Definition
> [!info] Persuasion Therapy (Dubois)[^1]
> The "rational psychotherapy" of the Swiss neurologist Paul Dubois (1848–1918), an explicitly Stoic-influenced forerunner of [[Cognitive Therapy]]. "Emotions… always follow ideas, so the treatment should go to the root, namely, the erroneous idea the patient has allowed to creep into his mind." The therapist uses rational persuasion to correct those ideas.

# Properties
- **Theory of neurosis.** Dubois held that most neurotic and emotional disorders result from an ongoing, unintentional and irrational [[Autosuggestion|autosuggestion]]. Humans suffer more than animals because they add to brute suffering: "he increases them by imagination, aggravates them by fear, keeps them up by his pessimistic reflections".[^1] (His diagnostic categories of "functional" nervous disorders are historical, not modern nosology.)
- "By rational education of ourselves we modify our ideas and our sentiments."[^2]
- **Reason as arbiter.** Act on good impulses, but leave time for reason to exercise rapid control. When anger, envy or vexation arises, reason should intervene to correct the first impression. This parallels the Stoic treatment of [[Propatheia|first movements]] and [[Assent (Stoicism)|assent]].[^2]
- **Stoic themes.** The fear of death is the root of most other fears. "Do not let us build a second story to our sorrow by being sorry for our sorrow." "He who knows how to suffer suffers less": he accepts the trouble as it is, without adding the terrors of apprehension.[^3]
- **Eudaimonism.** "The sole motive for every action of man is the desire for happiness"; the question is where to look for it ([[Eudaimonia]]).[^4]
- **Determinism as therapy.** Dubois argued: (1) brain activity is wholly determined by antecedent causes; (2) mental activity is wholly determined by brain activity; (3) so mental activity is wholly determined. He used this to cultivate tolerance, forgiveness and resignation to the inevitable ([[Philosophical Empathy]], [[Stoic Determinism]]). Spinoza: "Men think themselves free only because they get a clear view of their actions, they do not think of the motives that determined them."[^5]
- Linked historically to [[Rational Emotive Behavior Therapy]] and [[Cognitive Behavioral Therapy]].
