# Definition
> [!info] Socratic Intellectualism[^1]
> The view, from [[Socrates]] and adopted by the Stoics, that [[Stoic Virtue|virtue]] is knowledge and wrongdoing is ignorance: everyone always wants what is good for them, so no one does wrong or assents to falsehood willingly; they merely judge something false to be true.

# Properties
- The desire for one's own good is, for the Stoics, obvious from birth and the fundamental motivation, shared with animals (cf. [[Oikeiosis]]).[^1]
- Since all actions depend on beliefs, it is critical to get one's belief-set right.[^2]
- Every mistake involves a contradiction: the wrongdoer wants the good but does what isn't good for him. Every rational soul is averse to contradiction but persists while unaware of it; a skilled arguer (protreptic and refutational) shows him the contradiction, after which he abandons it of his own accord.[^3]
- *Protreptic* speech is the ability to show people the conflict they are mired in: they want happiness but look for it in the wrong place.[^4]
- Consequence for conduct: whoever wrongs you acts on what seems right to him, so the one harmed is the one deceived; bearing this in mind removes anger, blame, and hatred.[^5]
- Linked to the [[Socratic Method]] of cross-examination, which removes presumption.[^6]
