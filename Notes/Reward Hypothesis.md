# Definition
> [!info] Reward Hypothesis[^1]
> The hypothesis that all of what we mean by goals and purposes can be well thought of as the maximisation of the cumulative sum of a received scalar reward.

# Properties
- Motivated by the generality of the [[Reinforcement Learning]] framework; Sutton concedes it is "probably ultimately wrong, but so simple we have to disprove it before considering anything more complicated."[^2]
- Reinforcement learning is less like learning with a teacher than with a critic (Barto): the reward evaluates but does not instruct.[^3]
- Combined with McCarthy's definition of intelligence as "the computational part of the ability to achieve goals in the world", RL offers a possibly universal account of intelligence, likely rediscovered by evolution many times.[^4]
- The alignment problem for RL inverts it: given desired behaviour, how should rewards be structured to produce it ([[Reward Shaping]], [[Value Alignment Problem]])?[^5]
- Challenged by intrinsic motivation ([[Curiosity-Driven Exploration]]), which suggests reward maximisation is not the whole story.
