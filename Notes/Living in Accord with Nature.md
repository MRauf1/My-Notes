# Definition
> [!info] Living in Accord with Nature[^1]
> The Stoic formula for the goal of life. "Nature" is deliberately ambiguous between universal Nature and human nature, which coincide: universal nature is rational and the core of human nature is rationality, so living according to one's rational nature is living according to Nature.

# Properties
- For [[Epictetus]] it means chiefly two things: accepting all that happens, since everything comes from God, who is the same as nature; and keeping one's [[Prohairesis|will]] in its natural state of freedom, attentive only to what is up to us.[^2]
- "Instead of wishing that things would happen as you'd like, wish that they would happen as they do, and then you'll be content."[^3]
- Education's purpose is not to change existing conditions but to keep our intellects in harmony with everything that happens.[^4]
- It is human nature to do good, be helpful, and pray for others' success; a creature fares badly when it does not act according to its nature, so a hard-hearted person harms himself by becoming "a wolf, a snake, or a wasp".[^5]
- Two principles to have at hand: apart from will nothing is good or bad, and we should not try to steer events but follow their lead.[^6]
- Seeing each event in the context of the whole, together with gratitude, moves one to praise providence ([[Stoic Theology]]).[^7]
- "Anyone who's dissatisfied with the circumstances assigned him by fortune is unskilled in the art of living."[^8]
