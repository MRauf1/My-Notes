# Definition
> [!info] Elastic Collision[^1]
> A collision between two bodies in which [[Kinetic Energy]] is conserved, in addition to [[Momentum]], so that the bodies' speeds after the collision equal their speeds before it.

# Properties
- That the total kinetic energy is unchanged is a fact separate from [[Conservation of Momentum]], which alone only guarantees that, e.g., the rebound velocities in a symmetric collision are equal and opposite — not that their magnitude matches the pre-collision speed.
- Occurs when negligible energy is converted into other forms (heat, sound, or vibration) during the collision, e.g. between sufficiently rigid or elastic materials such as steel with spring bumpers.
- Collisions between atoms or molecules in a gas are, to an excellent approximation, perfectly elastic, since such elementary objects have no internal "gears or parts" in which to store leftover energy.
- The complementary case to an [[Inelastic Collision]], which does not conserve kinetic energy.

[^1]: [The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. I, Ch. 10: Conservation of Momentum](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_10.html)
