# Definition
> [!info] Asch Conformity Experiment[^1]
> Solomon Asch's 1950s experiments, in which a participant judged line lengths after a group of confederates had unanimously given a wrong answer. Participants often doubted their own eyes and gave the answer they knew to be incorrect. Conformity occurred on about a third of critical trials, and about three quarters of participants conformed at least once.

# Properties
- **Group size.** Clear says conformity rose with the number of actors.[^1] In Asch's data it rose up to about three or four confederates and then plateaued. A single dissenting ally sharply reduced conformity.
- **Replication.** A meta-analysis of 133 studies found the effect robust, larger in collectivist cultures, and declining in the US since the 1950s (Bond and Smith, 1996).
- **Implication for habits.** When unsure how to act we look to the group, and the normal behavior of the tribe often overpowers the desired behavior of the individual. "Most days, we'd rather be wrong with the crowd than be right by ourselves." Change is attractive when it means fitting in with the tribe and unattractive when it means challenging it.[^2]
