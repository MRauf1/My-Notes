# Definition
> [!info] Power Struggle (Adlerian Psychology)[^1]
> A contest in a relationship where the focus shifts from the *rightness of the assertions* to *who wins*. Personal anger is the typical tool in it: a means of making the other person submit. Being genuinely angered by someone should be read as their challenge to a power struggle. The response is to step down rather than react.

# Properties
- **Personal anger vs righteous indignation.** Personal anger (a grudge) cools quickly and is only a tool for making others submit. Indignation at social injustice lasts and is a different thing.[^2]
- **Escalation.** A power struggle that is lost can move to a **revenge stage**, where almost no solution is possible. Do not be drawn in.[^3]
- **Stepping down is not "bearing it".** Feeling that one is enduring the provocation shows one is still in the struggle. Answer the action without a reaction.[^4]
- **Anger is communication, not a necessity.** One can convey thoughts and be accepted without anger. Once this is understood through experience, anger stops appearing. "It's not that you mustn't get angry, but that there is no need to rely on the tool of anger."[^5]
- **"I am right."** The moment one is convinced one is right, one has entered a power struggle: "I am right" implies "you are wrong", which implies "I must win". The rightness of an assertion has nothing to do with winning. If you think you are right, the matter can end there. Admitting a mistake is not a defeat.[^6]
- When fixated on winning and losing, one loses the ability to choose correctly.[^7]
- Follows from viewing emotions as goal-directed ([[Teleology (Adlerian Psychology)]]) and from vertical relationships ([[Vertical and Horizontal Relationships]]). Compare [[Psychological Reactance]].
- **Anger and violence as cheap communication.** Violence, reprimand included, is a low-cost, easy way to make others submit. It reveals one's immaturity, and children sense this. Adler: "Anger is an emotion that pulls people apart." Communication aims at consensus. See [[Adlerian Education]] and stage 3 of the [[Five Stages of Problem Behavior]].[^kk1]
- **Don't judge by morals alone.** Moral standards change with the era, so judging people only by moral rules is dangerous.[^kk2]
