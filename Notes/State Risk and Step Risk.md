# Definition
> [!info] State Risk and Step Risk[^1]
> A *state risk* is associated with being in a certain state, the total exposure being a direct function of how long the system remains in that state. A *step risk* is a discrete risk associated with some necessary or desirable transition, which vanishes once the transition is complete and is usually not a simple function of how long the transition takes.

# Properties
- Conditional on a fast [[Superintelligence Takeoff|takeoff]], creating superintelligence may be a step risk, whose magnitude depends on preparations rather than on whether it takes milliseconds or hours.[^2]
- Concern with existential state risks favours acceleration, provided there is a realistic prospect of reaching a post-transition era with greatly reduced risk.[^2]
- If a step ahead were known to be catastrophic, slowing development would give more generations a chance to exist; but such pessimism is unwarranted.[^2]
- Present existential state risk appears low, so a decade's delay now costs little state risk but may substantially reduce later step risks by allowing preparation. The main significance of the speed of macro-structural development is how well prepared humanity is when confronting key step risks.[^2]
- Cognitive enhancement may improve preparedness beyond merely compressing the same progress into less time.[^3]
- Informs the [[Differential Technological Development|principle of differential technological development]].
