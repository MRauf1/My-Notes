# Definition
> [!info] AI Effect[^1]
> The tendency, lamented by John McCarthy, that "as soon as it works, no one calls it AI anymore": once a task is solved by a machine, it ceases to be regarded as requiring intelligence.

# Properties
- E.g. grandmaster-level chess was once supposed to require high general intelligence, but turned out to be achievable with a special-purpose algorithm.[^2]
- The best current performance being achieved by a complicated mechanism does not imply that no simpler mechanism could do as well or better; the simpler alternative may simply not have been found.[^2]
- Related to the moving of goalposts for intelligence discussed in [[Philosophical Stances on Artificial Intelligence]].
