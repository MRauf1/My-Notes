# Definition

Person's belief in their own ability to complete a task.[^1]

# Sources of Self-Efficacy
Bandura (1977) identified four sources, in decreasing order of strength:
1. **Mastery experiences**: one's own past successes.
2. **Vicarious experiences**: seeing similar others succeed. Roger Bannister's sub-four-minute mile was followed by many more once it was shown to be possible.[^2]
3. **Verbal persuasion.**
4. **Physiological and emotional states.**

Goggins' *Cookie Jar* deliberately recalls past victories, small ones included, and re-feels them in the middle of a struggle.[^3] It draws on the strongest source, mastery experiences, though the recall technique itself is less studied. Related: [[Steeling Effect]], [[Self-Talk]].

Carnegie's own rule for public speaking is "Do the thing you fear to do and get a record of successful experiences behind you."[^4] This combines mastery experiences with [[Exposure Therapy|exposure]]. Exposure-based treatment is the most effective treatment for public-speaking anxiety.
