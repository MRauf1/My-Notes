# Definition
> [!info] Strategic Programming[^1]
> An approach to software development that prioritizes long-term design quality over the short-term speed of "tactical programming" (getting a feature or fix working as quickly as possible), treating every small increase in [[Software Complexity]] with "zero tolerance."

# Properties
- Tactical programming wins in the short term, but strategic programming wins in the long run, since patient attention to design slows the incremental accumulation of complexity.
- Recommends investing 10-20% of total development time in design, whether by thinking about it, implementing it, or fixing existing design problems.
- When a design mistake is discovered, it should be fixed immediately rather than coded or patched around.
- Applies during modification and extension of existing code, not only during initial implementation; every change is an opportunity to improve the system's design, even by a little.
- [[Design It Twice]] is a concrete practice for producing better initial designs under this approach.

[^1]: [A Philosophy of Software Design](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/283RR677?page=1)
