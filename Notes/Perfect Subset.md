# Definition

> [!info] Definition 1 (Perfect [[Subset]])[^1]
> [[Closed Set]] [[Subset]] $E$ of [[Metric Space]] $(S, d)$ is perfect if every [[Point]] $x$ in $E$ is a [[Limit Point]] of a [[Sequence]] of [[Point]] in $E \setminus \{x\}$

# Properties

> [!abstract] Theorem 2 (Nonempty Perfect Subsets of $\mathbb{R}^k$ are Uncountable)[^2]
> Let $P$ be a nonempty [[Perfect Subset|perfect subset]] of $\mathbb{R}^k$. Then $P$ is [[Uncountable Set|uncountable]].

> [!abstract] Corollary 3[^3]
> Every [[Interval]] $[a, b]$ with $a < b$ is [[Uncountable Set|uncountable]]. In particular, [[Set of Real Numbers|$\mathbb{R}$]] is uncountable.
